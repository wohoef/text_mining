This study aimed to elucidate the involvement of various regulatory cytokines in pseudoexfoliation (PEX) development. A cohort of 140 patients undergoing cataract surgery, diagnosed with early or late-stage pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS) or pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG), participated. Using high-sensitivity ELISA kits, we quantified humor and serum levels of transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), epidermal growth factor (EGF), insulin-like growth factor (IGF), IL-8, and interferon-inducible T cell alpha chemoattractant (ITAC). Our findings suggest that elevated TGF-β and PDGF initiate fibrous tissue production in early XFS, with PDGF's influence persisting into late XFS. In the XFG stage, EGF and IGF become the dominant regulators of this process. Furthermore, ITAC counteracts the angiogenic effects of IL-8 in XFG. In conclusion, local chronic ocular inflammation is associated with the release of multiple profibrotic cytokines (TGF-β, PDGF, EGF, IGF, IL-8), but angiogenesis is inhibited by the action of ITAC.

XFS, a genetic extracellular matrix disease, causes progressive deposition of abnormal fibrillar material in intraocular and extraocular tissues, including the trabecular meshwork.
Cellular stress, including oxidative stress and ischemia/hypoxia, is increasingly recognized as a key factor in the fibrotic process of XFS. Oxidative stress alters local conditions, initiating inflammation. Chronic exposure to these factors is thought to have cumulative, irreversible effects, leading to permanent tissue damage.
Oxidative stress products alter blood vessel endothelium function, leading to tissue inflammation. This chronic inflammation then initiates the synthesis of profibrotic cytokines. This, in turn, triggers a complex cascade of other regulatory cytokine synthesis, including profibrotic, angiogenic, and anti-angiogenic factors, within the tissues. The cumulative outcome of this process is the formation of interconnected fibrous fibers, ultimately resulting in pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS). Fibrous tissue production is a downstream consequence of these earlier stages. Regulatory cytokines play a crucial role in the intricate process of fibrous fiber production and their subsequent accumulation in tissues. Although elevated oxidative stress markers and the presence of angiogenic factors like VEGF, FGF, TGF-β, PDGF, Ang-1, and IL-8 have been observed in aqueous humor, research specifically addressing vasculogenesis and angiogenesis in the anterior segment of the eye remains limited.
This study aimed to elucidate the roles of regulatory cytokines in pseudoexfoliation (PEX) development under stress. By examining cytokine involvement in PEX production and accumulation across different stages, the research seeks to uncover the mechanisms driving XFS progression via cytokine formation, offering potential new therapeutic strategies.
A total of 140 patients undergoing cataract surgery at the Clinic for Ophthalmology in Kragujevac, Serbia, were enrolled. The study received approval from the local Ethics Committee, and all participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.
A comprehensive ophthalmic evaluation was conducted for all participants, encompassing visual acuity, intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement, slit lamp and fundus examinations, and gonioscopy. For patients diagnosed with glaucoma, perimetric examination was also performed. A detailed slit lamp examination, conducted after pupillary dilation, was specifically employed to identify signs of pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS). The 140 patients were stratified into four age-matched groups based on pseudoexfoliation (PEX) deposits and IOP levels. These groups included: early stage XFS (Group I, n=35), late stage XFS (Group II, n=35), pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG) (Group III, n=35), and a control group (Group IV, n=35) without PEX deposition. Patients in the early XFS group exhibited diffuse PEX material on the anterior lens capsule and/or pupillary margin, mild pigment liberation upon dilation with deposition on the iris, lens, and chamber angle, subtle pupillary ruff atrophy, and slightly limited mydriasis. The late stage of XFS was characterized by substantial PEX material deposits on the pupillary margin and/or lens, often forming a "ring" imprint on the anterior lens capsule. These patients also presented with hard pigment deposition on anterior segment structures, including the chamber angle, pronounced pupillary ruff atrophy, and significantly restricted mydriasis. The XFG group comprised individuals with elevated IOP (greater than 20 mmHg), an open chamber angle with PEX, characteristic glaucomatous visual field defects, and optic nerve head changes consistent with glaucoma, alongside PEX material deposits in the anterior segment. Patients with a history of ocular trauma, intraocular inflammation, diabetes mellitus, myopia, or prior ocular interventions such as laser photocoagulation, cryotherapy, or intraocular surgery were excluded from the study.
Aqueous humor and serum samples were collected from patients prior to cataract surgery. These samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C for later examination.
For cytokine analysis, undiluted serum samples were obtained from the cubital vein, and aqueous humor samples, diluted 1:10, were collected from the anterior chamber of selected patients via a single needle stick. Samples were immediately stored at -80°C. High-sensitivity enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits specific for human cytokines were used to measure serum and humor cytokine levels, following the manufacturer's instructions. All measurements were conducted in triplicate.
Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 19.0 software, employing unpaired ANOVA and Mann-Whitney tests. Results were presented as Mean ± SE. Significance was determined using one-sided p-values, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Detailed p-values and patient numbers (n) are provided in the Results section and figure legends.
This study aimed to elucidate the role of specific cytokines in pseudoexfoliation (PEX) production. We categorized these cytokines based on their function: profibrotic (TGF-β, PDGF, EGF, IGF), angiogenetic (IL-8), and antiangiogenetic (ITAC).
Analysis of aqueous humor revealed significant alterations in regulatory cytokine levels across different stages of pseudoexfoliation. Transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) showed a statistically significant increase in the early stage of pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS) compared to patients with pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG), late-stage XFS, and the control group. Platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) levels were significantly elevated in both early and late stages of XFS when compared to XFG patients and the control group. Conversely, epidermal growth factor (EGF) levels were notably higher in XFG patients, showing a significant difference compared to early and late XFS stages, as well as the control group. Insulin-like growth factor (IGF) was undetectable in the aqueous humor of XFS patients (both early and late stages). However, a statistically significant level of IGF was observed in the control group, which was considerably higher than its level in the XFG group. These findings, presented in Figure 1A, highlight distinct cytokine profiles in the aqueous humor corresponding to the progression of XFS to XFG.
In serum samples, transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) levels were significantly higher in the early stage of pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS) compared to later stages and the control group. Specifically, early XFS patients exhibited a statistically significant elevation (p < 0.001) in TGF-β, while levels in late XFS and pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG) patients were considerably lower, with TGF-β not detected in the control group. Platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) levels in XFG patients were significantly lower (p < 0.05) than in the late stage of XFS. However, no significant differences were observed between early XFS and control groups for PDGF. Epidermal growth factor (EGF) levels showed a statistically significant decrease (p < 0.05) in XFG patients when compared to those in the late stage of XFS. No significant differences were found between XFG and either the early XFS stage or the control group. Insulin-like growth factor (IGF) levels were found to be significantly increased (p < 0.05) in XFG patients relative to both the early and late stages of XFS. No significant differences in IGF levels were noted between XFG patients and the control group. These findings, detailed in Figure 1B, highlight distinct patterns of these regulatory cytokines across different stages of the disease.
In patients with pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG), aqueous humor levels of IL-8 were significantly elevated (1132.57 ± 78.56 pg/mL) compared to those in the early stage of pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS) (667.24 ± 87.67 pg/mL) and the control group (666.95 ± 78.49 pg/mL). No significant difference was observed when compared to the late stage of XFS (785.14 ± 59.43 pg/mL). Concurrently, ITAC levels in the aqueous humor of XFG patients were also significantly higher (418.93 ± 22.67 pg/mL) than in early XFS patients (362.46 ± 28.5 pg/mL) and the control group (351.97 ± 18.34 pg/mL).
In patients with late-stage pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS) and pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG), serum levels of IL-8 were significantly elevated (p < 0.001) compared to the control group, where it was undetectable. Conversely, IL-8 was not detected in early-stage XFS patients. Regarding ITAC, a statistically significant increase (p < 0.001) was observed in XFG patients, with levels considerably higher than those in early and late-stage XFS patients and the control group.
Analysis of regulatory cytokines revealed significant correlations. A strong positive correlation was observed between serum IGF levels in patients with XFG and those in the early stage of XFS (r = 0.839, p < 0.001). Furthermore, strong positive correlations were found between serum and aqueous humor ITAC levels in XFG patients (r = 0.718, p < 0.001). A notable positive correlation also existed between serum EGF levels in early XFS patients and serum IGF levels in XFG patients (r = 0.859, p < 0.001). Finally, serum TGF-β levels in XFG patients showed a strong positive correlation with serum PDGF levels in the same patient group (r = 0.865, p < 0.001). These findings highlight interconnectedness among these cytokines across different stages of pseudoexfoliation.
PEX manifestation progresses from XFS to XFG based on deposits, IOP, and optic head changes.
The progression of pseudoexfoliation (PEX) syndrome through its distinct stages, from early pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS) to pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG), can be understood by examining the interplay of various regulatory cytokines involved in the fibrosis process. Prior research has highlighted the significant roles of certain cytokines in this pathological cascade. The initial phase of PEX development involves the local ocular tissues responding to various stressors, including ultraviolet radiation, infections, and oxidative stress. Given the strong association of PEX with aging populations, altered local hypoxic conditions are believed to trigger this complex process. Crucially, inflammation within the ocular tissues serves as the foundational step in the initiation of PEX production. This localized inflammation, exacerbated by aging and compromised oxidative stress mechanisms, subsequently leads to the production of fibrous tissue. Indeed, other studies have also identified inflammation as a pivotal stage in the pathogenesis of PEX. Our investigation underscores the profound involvement of specific regulatory cytokines in both PEX production and the broader fibrosis process. For clarity, we categorized these cytokines based on their functional roles: profibrotic (transforming growth factor beta [TGF-β], platelet-derived growth factor [PDGF], epidermal growth factor [EGF], and insulin-like growth factor [IGF]), angiogenetic (interleukin-8 [IL-8]), and antiangiogenetic (interferon-inducible T cell alpha chemoattractant [ITAC]). In the early stages of PEX production, characterized as the initial phase of fibrotic tissue repair, we observed elevated levels of TGF-β and PDGF in the aqueous humor. Previous research has noted elevated TGF-β levels in PEX patients, though its direct impact on the stage of PEX production was not clearly established. TGF-β is known to stimulate fibroblasts, promoting their infiltration into tissues and subsequently enhancing their proliferation and differentiation, a process further facilitated by PDGF. As XFS progresses into its later stages, PDGF levels remain elevated, continuing to amplify fibroblast proliferation and differentiation. The generalized chronic inflammation and subsequent fibrosis are sustained by increased circulating levels of PDGF, EGF, and IL-8, contributing to the intricate pathological process within the eye. The elevated concentrations of profibrotic cytokines in the serum likely maintain their local action by influencing their levels in the aqueous humor. These regulatory cytokines are instrumental in initiating and accumulating fibrous fiber production within the eye. Specifically, increased TGF-β and PDGF concentrations in the aqueous humor drive fibrous tissue production during the early phase of XFS. This effect is prolonged by PDGF in the late stage of XFS. Ultimately, in the XFG stage, fibrous production becomes predominantly regulated by EGF and IGF, leading to an amplification of fibrous tissue generation. Previous work by Koliakos et al. has reported elevated levels of certain growth factors, supporting this observation. Local chronic inflammation is a key driver in sustaining the process of fibrous tissue production. Both EGF and IL-8 are implicated in maintaining this production, and their local levels in the eye increase during the subsequent development of XFG. Our study confirmed elevated IL-8 levels in the aqueous humor of XFG patients. It is plausible that IL-17 contributes to the local synthesis of IL-8 within the ocular tissues. IL-17 has been shown to modulate IL-8 expression in human fibroblasts, with increased IL-17 concentrations leading to a higher level of IL-8 produced by these cells. Our earlier findings on pro-inflammatory cytokine levels indicated elevated IL-17 in the aqueous humor. We observed increased TGF-β levels in the aqueous humor during the early stage of XFS. TGF-β is known to enhance the production of connective tissue growth factor (CTGF). CTGF, in turn, promotes the local synthesis of EGF and IGF. Elevated CTGF levels have been documented in PEX patients in prior studies. Furthermore, PDGF can stimulate IGF synthesis, and their heightened levels and activity are maintained in circulation. EGF plays a role in controlling fibroblast proliferation, while IGF is involved in myofibroblast differentiation. Our investigation revealed elevated levels of EGF and IL-8 in the aqueous humor of XFG patients. However, IGF was only detected in the serum of these patients. While no direct local action of IGF was observed during the early and late stages of XFS, its presence in the serum throughout all phases of PEX development, with increased activity in the final XFG stage, was noted. These findings suggest that IGF emerges as a principal profibrotic cytokine with significant influence during the terminal phase of XFG development, promoting extensive fibroblast differentiation and sustaining fibrous tissue production. The impact of these cytokines on PEX deposition within the eye can be elucidated through their measured levels and observed correlations. We identified a positive correlation between serum IGF levels in patients with early XFS and those with established XFG. Additionally, a positive correlation was found between serum EGF levels in early XFS patients and serum IGF levels in XFG patients. Our data also indicated lower levels of TGF-β and PDGF in both aqueous humor and serum, alongside a positive correlation between serum TGF-β and PDGF levels in XFG patients. The correlation analyses suggest that the primary influence of profibrotic cytokines like TGF-β and PDGF occurs during the early phase of XFS. This is subsequently followed by a pronounced effect of other regulatory cytokines, namely EGF and IGF, during the subsequent stages of disease progression, culminating in XFG. The sustained elevated levels observed in ocular tissues are likely maintained by their systemic actions in the serum during the late stages of XFS and are preserved into the glaucoma phase.
Chronic injury can trigger endothelial cells to initiate vasculogenesis, the formation of new blood vessels, and angiogenesis, the branching of existing capillaries. Various factors, including VEGF, FGF, TGF-β, PDGF, IL-8, and other chemokines, can promote angiogenesis. Our research observed elevated levels of TGF-β and PDGF in the aqueous humor of patients in the early stages of pseudoexfoliation syndrome (XFS). Furthermore, patients with pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (XFG) showed increased IL-8 levels in both serum and aqueous humor. Despite these pro-angiogenic factors, there is no clear evidence of new blood vessel formation in the eyes of XFS/XFG patients, suggesting the presence of factors that inhibit angiogenesis. Previous studies have not found significant increases in anti-angiogenic factors. However, our study revealed elevated levels of ITAC in both the aqueous humor and serum of XFG patients. ITAC appears to counteract the pro-angiogenic effects of IL-8 in the XFG stage. We also confirmed a positive correlation between serum and aqueous humor ITAC levels in XFG patients. These findings suggest that increased ITAC secretion prevents the final stage of fibrous tissue production involving angiogenesis, resulting in fibrous tissue deposition without the formation of new blood vessels in the body and the eye.
Local conditions like oxidative stress initiate chronic inflammation in the eye, leading to the secretion of profibrotic cytokines (TGF-β, PDGF, EGF, IGF, IL-8). Activated tissue fibroblasts are key in fibrous tissue production. In XFG, accumulated PEX material and reduced blood flow contribute to disease development. Crucially, elevated ITAC levels in both humor and serum prevent vasculogenesis and angiogenesis.
