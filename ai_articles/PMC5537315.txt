Conventional serum biomarkers, namely alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and human chorionic gonadotropin subunit beta (B-HCG), are valuable for diagnosing and monitoring testicular germ cell cancer (TGCC). However, a significant portion of TGCC patients, approximately 20% with non-seminoma (NS) and 80% with seminoma (SE), present with negative results for these protein markers. This limitation highlights the potential of embryonic stem cell microRNAs (miRs) as alternative serum biomarkers. This study aimed to evaluate the added value of the serum-based ampTSmiR test for follow-up in TGCC patients who experienced a relapse. A retrospective analysis of 261 serum samples from six carefully selected TGCC patients with confirmed relapses was conducted. The ampTSmiR test was employed to measure miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p levels, and these results were compared against conventional protein biomarkers. At initial diagnosis, elevated levels of B-HCG, AFP, and LDH were observed in 4/6, 3/6, and 3/6 patients, respectively. In contrast, miR-371a-3p and miR-373-3p were elevated in 4/4 available samples, and miR-367-3p in 3/4. Two cases lacked initial serum samples for miR analysis. Residual disease, missed by histopathology, was identified in one patient solely by miR-371a-3p. Furthermore, miR-371a-3p levels increased two months prior to the detection of an intracranial metastasis. For patients with relapse or residual disease, B-HCG was informative in 3/4 cases, while the ampTSmiR test was informative in all 4/4. Notably, none of the tested biomarkers could detect residual mature teratoma. In conclusion, the ampTSmiR test demonstrates superior sensitivity compared to conventional TGCC protein biomarkers for detecting residual disease and relapses, with the exception of mature teratoma.

Testicular germ cell cancer (TGCC), while uncommon overall, stands as the most prevalent malignancy among young Caucasian males aged 20 to 40, predominantly affecting the testes and constituting 60% of all cancers in this demographic. The incidence of TGCC is on the rise, and the long-term consequences of its systemic treatments are becoming increasingly apparent. Consequently, there is a significant drive to achieve early diagnosis of this disease. Clinically and histologically, TGCC is categorized into two primary subtypes: seminoma (SE) and non-seminoma (NS). Both originate from pre-invasive germ cell neoplasia in situ (GCNIS), which itself arises from transformed primordial germ cells or gonocytes. Non-seminoma tumors can exhibit heterogeneity, encompassing various histological variants such as embryonal carcinoma (EC), yolk sac tumor (YST), choriocarcinoma (CH), and teratoma (TE). Despite a high 5-year survival rate exceeding 96% for TGCC patients, they face a persistent risk of developing a second primary TGCC or experiencing disease progression. This necessitates intensive and prolonged follow-up after initial diagnosis. This follow-up typically involves comprehensive physical examinations and monitoring of conventional serum protein biomarkers, namely B-HCG and AFP, with lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) also used to a lesser extent due to its lower specificity. The presence of serum biomarkers is directly linked to the histology of the primary tumor and any potential metastases. Specifically, elevated B-HCG levels are predominantly associated with the presence of a choriocarcinoma component, while increased AFP levels are indicative of a yolk sac tumor component. Conversely, these markers demonstrate limited sensitivity in detecting pure seminoma and the embryonal carcinoma component of non-seminoma. This explains why a considerable proportion of TGCC patients, particularly those lacking choriocarcinoma and yolk sac tumor components, may present with negative serum biomarker levels at initial diagnosis or become negative during follow-up. Furthermore, false positive results can occur; B-HCG elevations can be attributed to other malignancies, chemotherapy, or marijuana use, while AFP can be elevated due to chronic liver disease, liver damage, or liver cancer. Therefore, there is a clear clinical demand for biomarkers possessing enhanced sensitivity, especially for the detection of pure seminoma and embryonal carcinoma, aiding in the identification of primary tumors or metastases. The advancement of techniques like microRNA (miR) profiling has facilitated the identification of numerous small non-coding RNAs specifically expressed in TGCC. Notably, the expression of miR-371a-3p, miR-372-3p, miR-373-3p, and the miR-302a-d/367-3p cluster has been found to be characteristic of TGCC and embryonic tissues, while being absent in fully somatically differentiated teratoma. These miRs have emerged as promising circulating molecular serum biomarkers for patients with testicular and intracranial germ cell tumors. Research has indicated that miR-371a-3p exhibits superior performance in detecting primary TGCC, although the underlying reasons remain unclear. To date, most studies have concentrated on the utility of these biomarkers in primary diagnosis and during chemotherapy, with less extensive investigation into their role during follow-up. This current proof-of-concept study aimed to assess the levels of miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p in serum samples from a retrospective series of selected TGCC patients, spanning from primary diagnosis through relapse and complete remission. Our findings corroborate the effectiveness of elevated serum miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p levels for primary TGCC diagnosis and demonstrate the efficacy of the ampTSmiR test in detecting TGCT relapse, surpassing the performance of conventional serum biomarkers.
Ethical approval for this study was granted by the institution’s Medical Ethical Committee (MEC 02· 981 and CCR2041), and the research followed the Dutch Federation of Medical Scientific Societies' "Code for Proper Secondary Use of Human Tissue in The Netherlands" (version 2002, updated 2011). Serum samples were gathered between January 2000 and December 2008 from the Daniel den Hoed Cancer Center, Erasmus MC, and preserved at either liquid nitrogen or -80 °C. Patient selection for inclusion was contingent upon the availability of sufficient serum samples and comprehensive clinical follow-up data, with a particular focus on identifying cases with relapse or residual disease. The cohort comprised one seminoma (SE) patient with stage I disease and five non-seminoma (NS) patients. Among the NS patients, one had stage I disease, another stage I with a secondary primary tumor of stage IIA, and three presented with stage IV disease. The median age of these patients at primary diagnosis was 23.5 years, with a mean age of 26.8 years (ranging from 21 to 39). All collected sera underwent visual inspection for hemolysis; one sample exhibited moderate hemolysis, and five showed mild hemolysis, but none were excluded from analysis. Additional clinical information for these patients is detailed in Supplementary Table 1.
The ampTSmiR test involved isolating microRNAs (miRs) from 50 μl of serum. This isolation utilized target-specific anti-miR magnetic beads, a method previously described. The process employed a MagMax™ Express-96 robot along with TaqMan® miRNA ABC Purification Kits, panel A, for efficient miR isolation. To ensure quality control during the isolation of targeted miRs and the subsequent cDNA generation, a non-human spike-in, ath-miR-159a, was added to the serum samples at a concentration of 1 nM. For each cDNA synthesis experiment, four ten-fold dilution series of purified miRs from the TCam-2 seminoma cell line were incorporated. These served as controls for quality assurance, to assess qPCR efficiency, and for inter-plate calibration. Following purification, the miRs were reverse transcribed into miR-specific cDNA using a TAQMAN MICRORNA RT KIT. The generated cDNA was then quantified for miR levels using TaqMan Micro RNA assays. These assays specifically analyzed hsa-miR-371a-3p, hsa-miR-373-3p, and hsa-miR-367-3p, along with the spike-in ath-miR-159a and the reference miR hsa-miR-30b-5p. To enhance both sensitivity and specificity, a 13-cycle pre-amplification step was implemented. The quantification of miR levels was conducted on a TaqMan 7500 Real-Time PCR system, with all equipment and kits sourced from Thermo Fisher Scientific. Normalization of the data was achieved using the endogenous reference miR, miR-30b-5p. To classify samples as positive or negative, a threshold was established based on previously collected data that included both proven TGCC patients and healthy males. This threshold was determined using the Youden index, yielding specific sensitivity and specificity values for each miR: miR-371a-3p demonstrated 89% sensitivity and 90% specificity; miR-373-3p showed 70% sensitivity and 89% specificity; and miR-367-3p achieved 79% sensitivity and 85% specificity. The miR-30b levels and the TCam-2 dilution series were instrumental in comparing and normalizing the generated data. The final data are presented as 2 raised to the power of the difference between the highest dCt in the follow-up series and the dCT of the sample. These values are relative but maintain linearity, with higher values indicating increased miR levels.
The ampTSmiR test's utility in monitoring TGCT patients was evaluated by measuring miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p levels in all available serum samples from selected patients. Standard clinical data, including B-HCG, AFP, and LDH levels, were recorded at each time point. Individual patient cases are detailed below, with illustrations focusing solely on informative protein and miR biomarkers.
A 34-year-old male patient presented with a testicular mass. Initial diagnostic tests revealed significantly elevated levels of conventional serum biomarkers: B-HCG at 124,603 U/L (normal range < 2 U/L), AFP at 722 U/L (normal range 0–10 U/L), and LDH at 744 U/L (normal range < 450 U/L). Concurrently, microRNA (miR) analysis also showed elevated levels of miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p at the time of primary diagnosis. Following these findings, the patient underwent an orchidectomy. Histopathological examination of the removed testis identified a mixed non-seminoma comprising embryonal carcinoma (EC), yolk sac tumor (YCT), choriocarcinoma (CH), seminoma (SE), and both immature and mature teratoma (ITE and MTE). Staging investigations, including CT imaging, revealed enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes (MLN), left supraclavicular lymph nodes (LSLN), retroperitoneal lymph nodes (RPLN), and lung metastases. This led to a diagnosis of non-seminoma (NS) stage IV with a poor prognosis. The patient was subsequently treated with chemotherapy, initially one cycle of vinblastine, ifosfamide, and cisplatin (VIP), followed by stem cell collection and three additional VIP cycles with autologous stem cell transplantation. Five months post-diagnosis, a residual retroperitoneal lymph node was surgically removed via retroperitoneal lymph node dissection (RPLND). Prior to this dissection, neither the conventional protein biomarkers nor the tested miRs showed elevated levels. Histopathological analysis of the excised RPLN metastasis indicated large areas of necrosis and a mature teratoma (MTE) component. Based on these findings, no further treatment was administered. The patient's follow-up has been without relapse, which is consistent with the sustained normal levels of both conventional and miR biomarkers. An isolated elevated level of miR-373-3p was noted eight months after the initial diagnosis, though its clinical significance remains unclear. Ultimately, neither the conventional biomarkers nor the miRs were capable of detecting the RPLN composed solely of teratoma.
A 39-year-old man, with a prior history of orchidopexy at age 14, presented with a testicular mass. Initial conventional biomarker assessments revealed elevated B-HCG (226 U/L), AFP (19 U/L), and LDH (2441 U/L). Concurrently, the ampTSmiR test indicated elevated levels of miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p. Following an orchidectomy, histopathological examination of the affected testis confirmed a mixed non-seminoma (NS) comprising embryonal carcinoma (EC) and yolk sac tumor (YST). Staging via CT imaging identified enlarged left inguinal lymph nodes (ILN), retroperitoneal lymph nodes (RPLN), and lung metastases, leading to a diagnosis of NS stage IV, intermediate risk. The patient underwent four cycles of bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP) chemotherapy, followed by retroperitoneal lymph node dissection (RPLND) and left inguinal lymph node dissection (LND). Histological analysis of the resected lymph nodes showed necrosis and reactive changes, with no viable tumor cells detected. During the initial two months post-surgery and chemotherapy, B-HCG levels showed a gradual decline, while AFP, LDH, and individual miR levels decreased rapidly within the first week of treatment. Notably, miR-371a-3p levels increased after two months before declining again. Seven months after the initial diagnosis, the patient experienced neurological symptoms including dysphasia, right arm paresis, and seizures. A brain MRI revealed a solitary intracranial metastasis (ICM) in the left parietal lobe, which histopathology confirmed as pure EC after surgical resection. At this time, B-HCG was moderately elevated at 4.0 U/L, while AFP and LDH levels remained normal. Both miR-371a-3p and miR-373-3p levels were elevated upon detection of the ICM. The miR-371a-3p levels had been increasing for at least two months prior to the identification of the brain metastasis, suggesting residual disease detection by miR-371a-3p that predated and potentially related to the development of the ICM, unlike the serum protein biomarkers. Following surgical resection of the ICM, the patient received whole-brain radiotherapy with a local stereotactic boost, achieving complete clinical remission. Intriguingly, at 15 months post-relapse, positive ampTSmiR scores for miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p were observed for reasons yet unknown. The patient was subsequently lost to follow-up.
In case 3, a 21-year-old male underwent left orchidectomy. Histopathological and clinical assessments confirmed a pure seminoma (SE) at stage I. At diagnosis, his B-HCG level was 2.0 U/L, with normal AFP and LDH levels. Unfortunately, no serum sample was available for retrospective microRNA (miR) analysis at this initial stage. Prophylactic radiotherapy was administered to the para-aortic lymph nodes (PAOLN). Forty-five months post-diagnosis, a rise in B-HCG to 2.0 U/L was observed, while AFP and LDH remained within normal limits. At this juncture, miR-371a-3p levels were found to be elevated, indicated by a red arrow in Figure 3c, with miR-373-3p and miR-367-3p levels undetectable (Figure 3d and 3e). Seven weeks later, CT imaging revealed a para-iliac lymph node (PILN) metastasis. At this point, B-HCG showed a modest elevation to 3.3 U/L, and LDH was 473 U/L, though AFP remained normal. Concurrently, all tested miR levels were elevated, as shown by a black arrow in Figure 3c-e. In response to this relapse, the patient received three cycles of bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP) chemotherapy. Following the initiation of therapy, both protein and miR biomarker levels demonstrated a rapid decline. A complete remission was achieved, and the patient has maintained an uneventful follow-up for 42 months and remains under surveillance. The elevated levels of B-HCG, miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p collectively indicated the presence of the PILN metastasis. Notably, miR-371a-3p, along with a borderline B-HCG level, were the first indicators of this relapse. Intriguingly, positive miR levels were detected at multiple time points prior to the definitive diagnosis of the PILN, signifying a period where they remained above the undetectable threshold. Further during follow-up, a simultaneous positivity for miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and LDH was observed, with all readings just above their respective threshold levels.
Case 4 involved a 25-year-old male who had undergone orchidopexy. He was initially treated for stage I non-seminoma (NS) with an orchidectomy of his right testis. Histopathological examination confirmed a pure embryonal carcinoma (EC). At the time of his initial diagnosis, his B-HCG level was elevated at 3.4 U/L, while his AFP levels were normal. LDH levels were not reported. No serum samples were available for miRNA analysis at this initial stage. Following his primary treatment, the patient was monitored through active surveillance. Approximately four years after his diagnosis, his B-HCG levels significantly increased to 144 U/L. Concurrently, a retroperitoneal lymph node metastasis was identified via CT imaging. Retrospective analysis of serum samples revealed an elevated miR-371a-3p level at the time of this relapse. However, miR-373-3p levels remained below the established cut-off. The patient subsequently received three cycles of bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP) chemotherapy, leading to a complete remission. His follow-up extended for a total of nine years. Notably, seven years after his initial diagnosis, a single positive score for both miR-373-3p and miR-371a-3p was observed, although these findings did not appear to have any known clinical consequences.
Case 5 involved a 20-year-old male who initially presented with a right testicular mass. He was diagnosed and treated for stage I seminoma (SE) through orchidectomy and radiotherapy of the para-aortic lymph nodes (PAOLN), followed by active surveillance. Six years later, a second primary tumor was identified in his left testis, approximately 30 months after the start of this specific follow-up series. Histopathological and clinical examinations revealed this secondary tumor to be a non-seminoma (NS) with both SE and embryonal carcinoma (EC) components, classified as stage IIA. At the time of this second diagnosis, conventional biomarker levels, including B-HCG, AFP, and LDH, were normal. However, the ampTSmiR test detected elevated levels of miR-371a-3p and miR-373-3p. The patient subsequently received treatment consisting of two cycles of bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP), followed by two cycles of etoposide and cisplatin (EP). Five months after undergoing a second orchidectomy, a retroperitoneal lymph node (RPLN) metastasis was detected via CT imaging. At this point, B-HCG, AFP, and LDH levels remained within the normal range. Among the tested miRs, miR-373-3p showed a level just above the established threshold. During the subsequent follow-up period, two serum samples tested positive for miR-373-3p, although these findings did not correlate with any known clinical consequences. The significance of these intermittent miR elevations remains unclear, and the patient continues to be monitored. It is noteworthy that B-HCG levels were frequently elevated during the patient's follow-up, a pattern attributed to increased follicle-stimulating hormone levels resulting from bilateral orchidectomy. This observation did not extend to the miR levels, which did not exhibit a similar fluctuating pattern. The patient's treatment regimen included two cycles of BEP and two cycles of EP.
A 22-year-old male, with a history of right testicular orchidopexy at age 11, presented with a mass in his left testis. Initial conventional biomarker tests revealed significantly elevated levels of B-HCG (88,915 U/L), AFP (2024 U/L), and LDH (3698 U/L). Concurrently, serum levels of miR-371a-3p, miR-373-3p, and miR-367-3p were also found to be high. Following orchidectomy of the affected testis, histopathological examination identified the testis to be composed of mature teratoma (MTE) and necrosis, consistent with non-seminoma (NS) with an MTE component. Staging investigations via CT imaging indicated lung and liver metastases, along with enlarged para-aortic lymph nodes (PAOLN), leading to a diagnosis of NS stage IV with a poor prognosis. The patient underwent four cycles of bleomycin, etoposide, and cisplatin (BEP) chemotherapy. Seven months post-treatment, residual bilateral retroperitoneal lymph node dissections (RPLND) and inguinal lymph node dissections (LND), along with orchidectomy of the right testis, were performed. Histopathological analysis of these resected tissues confirmed they were composed entirely of pure MTE. At this juncture, serum levels of B-HCG, AFP, and LDH were within normal ranges. Consequently, no further chemotherapy was administered based on the clinical data and conventional biomarker results. However, the patient experienced a late relapse seven years after initial diagnosis, manifesting as lung and RPLN metastases. Histopathological examination of the LND from this relapse revealed a NS composed solely of choriocarcinoma (CH). At the time of this relapse, serum biomarkers were normal, and unfortunately, no serum samples were preserved for retrospective microRNA analysis. Treatment for this relapse involved three cycles of paclitaxel, ifosfamide, and cisplatin (TIP) combined with radiotherapy, but a complete remission was not achieved. A retrospective review of the RPLND performed seven months after the initial chemotherapy identified an overlooked CH component. Supporting this finding, retrospective analysis of serum samples collected prior to the RPLND revealed elevated miR-371a-3p levels in four instances, strongly suggesting the presence of components other than MTE alone.
Testicular germ cell cancer (TGCC) patients face a persistent risk of developing a second primary tumor or experiencing a recurrence, even years or decades after their initial diagnosis and treatment. This necessitates rigorous and prolonged follow-up protocols. Current follow-up strategies, which vary among institutions, typically involve patient history, physical examinations, monitoring of serum tumor markers, and serial radiological imaging. This proof-of-concept study aimed to determine if embryonic microRNA (miR) levels could offer superior prediction of TGCC relapse compared to established serum biomarkers like B-HCG, AFP, and LDH. While these findings require validation in larger, independent patient cohorts currently under investigation, the preliminary results suggest that the ampTSmiR test surpasses conventional biomarkers in identifying primary tumors, residual disease, and relapses. Occasionally, positive miR results were observed without corresponding clinical events, the reasons for which remain unclear. This phenomenon may be linked to the sensitivity and specificity thresholds established for this study, which were derived from prior research comparing TGCC patients with healthy individuals. Consequently, in a clinical setting, a positive miR finding should be re-evaluated within a week, mirroring the standard practice for conventional serum markers. Currently, miR analyses are recommended as an adjunct to conventional serum biomarker assessments, offering significant potential value, particularly for patients who are negative for conventional biomarkers.
In this study of six patients, five presented with positive conventional serum biomarker assays at initial diagnosis. This contrasts with the general population of testicular germ cell cancer (TGCC) patients, where positivity rates are around 60%, making follow-up more challenging. Despite the small sample size, significant conclusions can be drawn. All four cases with available samples showed positive miR scores at primary diagnosis, aligning with prior findings on the ampTSmiR test's sensitivity and highlighting its value for initial diagnosis. Regarding relapses, three out of four patients had elevated B-HCG levels, and one had elevated LDH. However, one patient tested negative for all protein-based biomarkers. Crucially, all four of these cases demonstrated positivity for at least one miR, suggesting the ampTSmiR test's potential benefit in TGCC patient follow-up. Among the three miRs investigated, miR-371a-3p emerged as the most sensitive and specific for relapse detection, consistent with recent findings from larger studies with shorter follow-up periods.
Two patients in our study, cases 1 and 6, had residual retroperitoneal lymph nodes (RPLN) after chemotherapy, which were composed solely of mature teratoma (MTE). As anticipated, conventional serum biomarkers like HCG and AFP were undetectable in these patients, and embryonic microRNAs (miRs) were also absent, consistent with previous findings. This inability of both conventional biomarkers and the ampTSmiR test to detect pure teratoma is clinically significant, as this chemo-resistant tumor type necessitates surgical intervention to prevent progression to non-germ cell malignancy. However, a retrospective analysis of case 6's serum samples showed elevated miR-371a-3p levels prior to RPLN dissection, suggesting the presence of components beyond MTE. Subsequent histopathological review of the biopsy material from this case confirmed the overlooked presence of choriocarcinoma (CH). These findings collectively suggest that further (chemo)therapy might have been indicated.
Diagnosing intracranial germ cell tumors (ICM) typically relies on a combination of clinical presentation, biomarker detection (AFP and B-HCG) in blood and cerebrospinal fluid, neuroimaging, and histological analysis. This study demonstrates that, in addition to B-HCG, miR-371a-3p and miR-373-3p can predict the occurrence of an ICM as a relapse of TGCT. This suggests that these microRNAs can cross the blood-brain barrier and be detected in the peripheral bloodstream, aligning with existing research. Specifically, in case study number 2, elevated miR-371a-3p levels persisted during VIP therapy, dropped briefly post-treatment, and then rose again two months prior to both positive serum protein biomarkers and the identification of the ICM. This pattern strongly suggests that a complete remission had not been achieved.
The ampTSmiR test demonstrates high sensitivity and specificity for identifying TGCC patients at primary diagnosis, residual disease, and relapse. These proof-of-concept findings suggest the need for further evaluation of this molecular biomarker in larger patient cohorts, particularly for those with active disease during follow-up.
