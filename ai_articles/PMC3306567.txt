The role of Notch signaling in human papillomavirus (HPV)-mediated transformation has been a subject of ongoing discussion due to conflicting reports of its tumor-suppressive and oncogenic functions. This study investigated whether these divergent findings could be attributed to gene dosage effects and explored the connection with AP-1, a known downstream target of Notch. To address this, SiHa cervical cancer cells were manipulated to express two distinct levels of intracellular active Notch. Concurrently, non-tumorigenic HPV16-immortalized keratinocytes (FK16A) were treated with siRNAs targeting Fra1 or with non-targeting control siRNAs. The resulting transfectants were then comprehensively analyzed. This analysis included assessing the mRNA expression of Notch, Hes, cJun, cFos, and Fra1. Furthermore, Notch pathway activation was evaluated using luciferase assays, cell viability was measured by MTT assays, and anchorage-independent growth was assessed. AP-1 activity and/or AP-1 complex composition were determined through electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSA). In SiHa cells, two distinct activation states of the Notch signaling pathway were observed. Moderate Notch activation promoted increased cell viability and anchorage-independent growth. In contrast, high-level Notch activation resulted in a decrease in anchorage-independent growth. This shift in phenotypic outcome was found to be associated with alterations in AP-1 activity and complex composition. Moderate Notch expression enhanced AP-1 transcriptional and DNA-binding activity without altering its complex composition. However, high Notch levels induced a change in AP-1 complex composition, shifting from cJun/cFos dimers to cJun/Fra1 dimers, a composition characteristic of non-tumorigenic HPV-immortalized cell lines. Furthermore, silencing Fra1 in non-tumorigenic HPV16-immortalized keratinocytes, which led to an enrichment of cJun/cFos dimers, was correlated with increased colony formation. In conclusion, the functional impact of Notch in HPV-mediated transformation is contingent on its dosage, and this effect is intrinsically linked to changes in AP-1.

The acquisition and maintenance of the transformed state in cervical cancer and its precursor lesions depend on the continuous expression of human papilloma virus (HPV) oncogenes E6 and E7. However, further cellular changes are essential for these HPV-containing lesions to progress to full-blown cervical cancer. Aberrant Notch signaling has been implicated in cervical carcinogenesis by numerous studies. Notch influences various cellular functions, including differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis. When Notch receptors interact with Delta and Jagged/Serrate ligands, they undergo a conformational change. This leads to proteolytic cleavage and the release of the intracellular Notch component (ICNX). Once in the nucleus, ICNX competes with repressors for binding to the transcription factor CBF1. This binding event, along with coactivator recruitment, converts CBF1 from a repressor to an activator, primarily affecting the Hairy enhancer of split (Hes) transcription family.
The role of Notch signaling in cervical cancer is complex, with studies reporting both tumor suppressive and oncogenic effects. Some research indicates low or undetectable Notch levels in cervical cancer cell lines and tissues, where Notch overexpression leads to growth inhibition and apoptosis. Conversely, other studies link increased Notch expression to cervical cancer progression and survival signaling. These conflicting findings may stem from dosage-dependent effects. Previous work has shown that high Notch levels can inhibit proliferation and HPV promoter activity in cervical cancer cells, while moderate Notch expression can promote the transformation of HPV-transfected keratinocytes.
AP-1 transcription factor complexes, which are downstream targets of Notch signaling, are dimeric structures composed of Jun family members (cJun, JunD, JunB) and Fos family members (Fra1, Fra2, cFos, FosB). These complexes bind to TPA response elements (TREs) to regulate gene transcription, impacting cellular processes like differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis. The specific impact of AP-1 on gene expression is influenced by its dimerization partners, promoter elements, co-acting transcription factors, and the cell type. AP-1 also plays a role in HPV transcriptional regulation due to the presence of AP-1 binding sites within the HPV upstream regulatory region. Furthermore, the composition of AP-1 complexes is linked to cellular phenotype in HPV-infected cells. Tumorigenic cervical cancer cells, such as SiHa and HeLa, typically exhibit cJun/cFos dimers, while non-tumorigenic HPV-immortalized keratinocytes and HeLa-fibroblast hybrids are characterized by cJun/Fra1 dimers.
Our prior research established a link between altered AP-1 composition in non-tumorigenic HPV-immortalized keratinocytes and tumorigenic cervical cancer cells, and changes in Notch mRNA expression. Specifically, Notch expression levels rose with hrHPV-induced immortalization of primary keratinocytes. Conversely, Notch expression was significantly lower in cervical cancer cell lines like SiHa and HeLa. This pattern, where Notch expression increases during early transformation but decreases in established cancer cells, suggests its functional impact might be dose-dependent. Therefore, our current study aimed to explore these dose-dependent functional effects of Notch in HPV-containing cells and elucidate its relationship with AP-1.
To investigate the dosage-dependent effects of Notch signaling, experiments were conducted using the cervical cancer cell line SiHa, sourced from the American Type Culture Collection. SiHa cells were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum and standard antibiotics. For comparison, HPV16-immortalized keratinocytes (FK16A) were cultured in serum-free keratinocyte medium. Transfection procedures were employed to manipulate Notch expression and AP-1 components. cDNA encoding the intracellular domain of Notch (ICNX), a generous gift from Dr. Warren Pear, was cloned into the pcDNA3.1hygro expression vector. Moderate and high levels of Notch expression were achieved by transfecting SiHa cells with different concentrations of this ICNX cDNA. Empty vector transfections served as controls for both expression levels. To specifically target Fra-1, a pool of Fra-1 specific siRNAs was used. Non-targeting siRNAs were employed as a negative control to account for off-target effects. Transfections for both cDNA and siRNA were performed using Lipofectamine or Dharmafect2, respectively, following manufacturer guidelines. Luciferase reporter assays were utilized to assess Notch pathway activation and AP-1 activity. A Hes1 promoter-driven luciferase construct, provided by Dr. Marc Vooijs, was used to measure Notch signaling output. Additionally, a URR-luciferase construct, obtained from Dr. Felix Hoppe-Seyler, was employed. Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were quantified using the Dual Luciferase assay system.
To analyze mRNA expression, RNA was extracted using RNA-B reagent. Following DNase treatment to remove contaminating DNA, the RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using a specific reverse primer. Quantitative RT-PCR was then employed to measure the expression levels of AP-1 family members, with primers and probes detailed in Table 1. To ensure accurate quantification and account for variations in RNA quality and quantity, RT-PCR for the housekeeping gene snRNP U1A was also performed. A standard curve, generated from serial dilutions of cDNA from cell lines known to highly express the target genes (primary keratinocytes, FK16A, or SiHa), was used for quantification. Notch and Hes1 RT-PCRs utilized SYBR Green PCR Master Mix, while AP-1 member RT-PCRs employed Universal PCR Master Mix.
Nuclear extracts were prepared and their protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay kit. An AP-1 consensus oligonucleotide was synthesized and purified. This oligonucleotide was then labeled with [γ-32P]ATP and purified via a polyacrylamide gel. Binding reactions were conducted, followed by the addition of specific antibodies for cJun, Fra1, and cFos. These complexes were then separated on a 5.5% non-denaturing polyacrylamide gel.
Cell viability was measured using MTT dye reduction after seeding cells in triplicate in 96-well plates post-transfection. Measurements were taken after five days, with control conditions normalized to 100%.
Anchorage independent growth was assessed by plating 5000 cells in semi-solid agarose. Colonies larger than 50 cells were counted and photographed after three weeks.
Statistical significance was determined using Excel's T-test, with p-values ≤0.05 considered significant.
To investigate the dose-dependent role of Notch in HPV-induced transformation, we manipulated Notch expression levels in SiHa cervical cancer cells, a line known for its low Notch levels. We introduced two concentrations of cDNA encoding the active intracellular Notch (ICNX), termed moderate and high. Moderate ICNX transfection led to a 46-fold increase in Notch mRNA, while high concentrations resulted in a 191-fold increase (Fig. 1a). This approach allowed us to examine the impact of varying Notch signaling activation on cellular phenotypes within the same cell line. To assess how Notch overexpression impacts pathway activation, SiHa cells were co-transfected with ICNX and a luciferase construct regulated by the Hes1 promoter. Notch was found to dose-dependently induce Hes1 transcription, increasing it 15-fold with moderate expression and 34-fold with high expression compared to untransfected cells. This increase in Hes1 transcription, confirmed by elevated Hes1 mRNA levels in Notch transfectants, validated functional Notch activity. However, Hes1 mRNA levels did not differ between moderate and high Notch expression groups. This suggests that regulatory mechanisms beyond those detected by reporter assays, such as chromatin modifications or post-transcriptional regulation, may limit further Hes1 mRNA upregulation.
The functional impact of introducing Notch was investigated in SiHa cells by transfecting them with two concentrations of intracellular active Notch (ICNX) or an empty vector control. Cellular viability was assessed using an MTT assay. Moderate Notch expression led to a significant increase in cell viability compared to controls (p = 0.0002). In contrast, high Notch expression did not affect cell viability. Anchorage-independent growth was also evaluated. Moderate Notch levels showed a trend towards increased colony formation, although this was not statistically significant. However, high Notch expression significantly reduced colony-forming capacity (p = 0.05). These findings, illustrated in Figure 2, demonstrate that the functional outcomes of Notch overexpression in SiHa cells are dependent on the expression level.
The study then investigated the impact of Notch overexpression on AP-1 transcriptional activity, a known downstream target of Notch signaling. By co-transfecting cells with ICNX cDNA and a luciferase construct regulated by a TPA response element (TRE), researchers measured AP-1 activity. These experiments demonstrated that AP-1 activity increased in a dose-dependent manner with increasing Notch expression levels (Fig. 3a). The figure legend indicates that this section of the study also examined the mRNA expression of AP-1 family members (cJun, Fra1, and cFos) and the composition of AP-1 complexes using EMSA, comparing moderate and high Notch expression to empty vector controls.
Transcription of AP-1 family members is regulated by TPA response elements (TREs), to which AP-1 complexes bind. To investigate how elevated Notch expression influences AP-1 member mRNA levels, quantitative RT-PCR was employed. Moderate Notch overexpression led to increased cJun mRNA, with minimal changes observed in cFos and Fra1. At high Notch expression levels, cJun mRNA remained elevated, though less so than with moderate expression. A slight increase in cFos mRNA was also noted, while Fra1 mRNA levels were unaffected.
Electrophoretic Mobility Shift Assays (EMSAs) were conducted to investigate the relationship between Notch overexpression and changes in AP-1 DNA binding activity and complex composition. In SiHa cells transfected with an empty vector control, AP-1 complexes were found to comprise cJun/Fra1, cJun/cFos, and potentially cJun/cJun dimers. Moderate Notch expression led to increased DNA binding, signifying elevated AP-1 activity consistent with luciferase assay results, but did not alter complex composition. In contrast, high Notch expression resulted in no observable increase in DNA binding activity. However, a decrease in the cFos signal and an increase in the Fra1 signal were noted, indicating a shift in complex composition towards cJun/Fra1 dimers. This alteration in AP-1 complex composition is significant because cJun/cFos dimers are associated with a tumorigenic phenotype, whereas cJun/Fra1 dimers are linked to a non-tumorigenic state.
Non-tumorigenic, HPV16-immortalized keratinocytes exhibit a cJun/Fra1 AP-1 complex. To investigate the functional impact of altered AP-1 complexes in these cells, Fra1 was depleted in FK16A cells using Fra1-specific siRNAs, with non-targeting siRNAs serving as controls.

Fra1 silencing led to an increase in cFos mRNA, while cJun mRNA expression remained largely unaffected (Fig. 4a). Analysis of AP-1 complex composition revealed that reduced Fra1 incorporation resulted in a greater prevalence of cJun/cFos dimers (Fig. 4b). Consequently, Fra1-silenced HPV16-immortalized keratinocytes exhibited enhanced colony formation in soft agarose (Fig. 4c), without a corresponding increase in proliferation. Control transfections with non-targeting siRNAs showed no impact on complex composition, proliferation, or anchorage-independent growth. These findings underscore the significance of AP-1 complex composition shifts in HPV-mediated transformation.
The role of Notch signaling in cervical cancer development has been a subject of ongoing debate, with conflicting reports on its effects. A proposed model suggests these opposing outcomes are dose-dependent. This hypothesis was previously supported by studies using single doses of Notch expression in various cell lines and by examining two levels of Notch overexpression in HeLa and HaCat cells, which influenced DNA synthesis and HPV promoter activity. However, direct experimental validation was lacking. Our study aimed to directly test this hypothesis by investigating the impact of two distinct Notch expression levels on multiple cellular phenotypes within the same cell line. We generated moderate and high expression levels of intracellular active Notch in the cervical cancer cell line SiHa, confirming two distinct pathway activation states through Hes luciferase assays. Moderate Notch activation enhanced cell viability and anchorage-independent growth. In contrast, high Notch activation displayed tumor-suppressive characteristics, notably reducing colony formation. These results align with and provide experimental support for the dose-dependent model, offering a potential explanation for the previously contradictory findings. Furthermore, we demonstrated that these differing phenotypic outcomes, observed with moderate versus high Notch expression, are linked to a shift in the composition of the transcription factor AP-1, a known downstream target of Notch. In tumorigenic SiHa cells, cJun/cFos heterodimers are typically more prevalent than in non-tumorigenic HPV-immortalized cell lines. Moderate Notch activation in SiHa cells boosted AP-1 activity, as indicated by TRE-driven luciferase assays and EMSA-detected DNA binding, without altering AP-1 complex composition. Conversely, high Notch activation induced a change in AP-1 composition, favoring cJun/Fra1 dimers.
The relationship between Notch signaling and AP-1 family members has been explored in various studies, but its role in AP-1 complex composition remained unexamined. Previous research in HeLa cells showed that Notch overexpression boosted cJun protein levels, aligning with our observation of increased cJun mRNA in SiHa cells under moderate Notch expression. However, contrasting findings exist regarding c-Fos and overall AP-1 activity. Some studies reported that Notch overexpression reduced c-Fos expression and AP-1 activity in HeLa cells. In contrast, our research demonstrated increased AP-1 activity with both moderate and high Notch expression, accompanied by a slight rise in c-Fos mRNA. Furthermore, a study by Talora et al. found no effect on AP-1 activity in SiHa, HeLa, and CaSki cells following Notch overexpression. This lack of consistent results across studies highlights that individual AP-1 member expression or reporter-based AP-1 activity alone cannot fully elucidate the connection between Notch and AP-1 in HPV-driven transformation. Therefore, considering the composition of the AP-1 complex is crucial for a comprehensive understanding.
In HPV16-immortalized keratinocytes, cJun/Fra1 complexes are prevalent, highlighting the significance of AP-1 complex composition for phenotypic outcomes. Fra1 silencing via siRNA reduced AP-1 DNA binding and Fra1's presence in EMSA, yet increased colony formation. This shift towards cJun/cFos dimers enhanced anchorage-independent growth, consistent with findings in HeLa cells by Soto et al.
Differences observed in previous studies may stem from the specific cell lines employed. For instance, CaSki cells exhibit significantly higher levels of both Notch and Fra1 compared to SiHa and HeLa cells. In CaSki cells, Notch expression is crucial for their tumorigenic nature, with its silencing inhibiting growth. Conversely, excessive Notch levels negatively impact the growth and survival of HeLa cells, mirroring the effects seen in SiHa cells. The precise mechanisms governing Notch expression and activity remain incompletely understood. Evidence suggests that HPV E6/E7 oncogenes can elevate Notch expression in primary cells, consistent with our prior observation of increased Notch mRNA in HPV-immortalized cells relative to primary keratinocytes. Conversely, Notch signaling, likely via AP-1, appears to regulate E6/E7 expression. Furthermore, Notch activity is influenced by cell-cell interactions through ligand availability. Notably, Jagged and Delta-like 1 ligands, which modulate Notch signaling, display similar expression patterns to Notch across different transformation stages—elevated in HPV-immortalized cells and reduced in cancer cell lines. These ligands may therefore play a role in regulating Notch within HPV-positive cells.
Notch expression increases during early transformation stages, from primary cells to immortalized and anchorage-independent cells, facilitating initial transformation steps. Conversely, after transformation is achieved, Notch expression decreases, seemingly promoting tumorigenicity. Our findings reveal Notch's dose-dependent function: moderate levels promote transformation, while high levels alter AP-1 composition towards cJun/Fra1 dimers, reducing anchorage-independent growth. This highlights Notch's complex, context-dependent role in HPV-mediated cervical carcinogenesis.
