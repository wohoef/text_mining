New copolymers incorporating the benzo[1,2-d:4,5-d′]bis(thiazole) (BBT) unit have been synthesized. These polymers were designed with comonomers including bisthienyl-diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), dithienopyrrole (DTP), benzothiadiazole (BT), benzodithiophene (BDT), and 4,4′-dialkoxybithiazole (BTz). A key feature of these polymers is their conjugation pathway, which is orthogonal to the typical 2,6-substitution of the BBT unit. This orthogonal pathway promotes intramolecular non-covalent interactions between neighboring monomer units' heteroatoms. The strength and number of these interactions can be adjusted to control molecular planarity and, consequently, tune the band gap. The synthesized 4,8-BBT materials demonstrated promising electronic properties, achieving a p-type organic field-effect transistor mobility of up to 2.16 × 10−2 cm2 V−1 s−1 with pDPP2ThBBT, and a power conversion efficiency of 4.45% in solar cells with pBT2ThBBT.

The past few decades have seen extensive research into organic materials for electronic devices, driven by their advantages over inorganic counterparts like silicon. These advantages include inherent tunability, solubility, and flexibility, enabling the creation of devices with precisely controlled properties that can be processed from solution onto flexible substrates. Significant progress in charge carrier mobility has been made in the development of materials for organic field-effect transistors (OFETs). These materials are finding applications in areas such as biosensors, active matrix light-emitting diodes (AMOLEDs), and as a cost-effective, flexible alternative to amorphous silicon for radiofrequency identification tagging (RFIDs). For organic materials to be practically useful, their mobility must approach that of amorphous silicon, which exhibits a hole mobility (μ) between 0.1 and 1 cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹. While numerous organic materials now boast mobilities exceeding 10 cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹, many high-performing candidates necessitate pre-treatment of substrates, such as SiO₂, to control polymer chain self-assembly or enhance wettability. Additionally, high-temperature thermal annealing is often required to achieve the necessary morphology for high mobility. These additional processing steps increase fabrication time, cost, and complexity. Consequently, there is a strong demand for materials that can achieve high mobility with minimal processing.
Organic semiconductors are frequently investigated for organic photovoltaic (OPV) devices due to their capacity for broad solar spectrum absorption. When paired with appropriate acceptor materials, these organic solar cells can achieve power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) exceeding 12%. To optimize performance, minimizing the band gap, enhancing carrier mobility, and promoting bulk self-assembly are crucial. This is often achieved by incorporating alternating conjugated donor and acceptor units into the polymer backbone, creating a push–pull effect. This structural design is readily accessible through the copolymerization of functionalized donor and acceptor monomers. The presence of multiple heteroatoms within these units offers additional benefits, including planarization and robust interchain packing, facilitated by π–π stacking and non-covalent interactions like heteroatom or weak hydrogen bonding. Specifically, the incorporation of thiazole units, as opposed to thiophene, contributes to these advantages. Thiazole also leads to deeper highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) and lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) energy levels, ultimately improving device performance in both organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) and OPVs. Furthermore, intermolecular non-covalent interactions within the bulk material play a significant role in enhancing charge carrier properties.
We previously introduced a benzobisthiazole (BBT) unit featuring a thiazole ring and an orthogonal 4,8-conjugation pathway, contrasting with the more common 2,6-substitution. While 2,6-BBT units are extensively studied, 4,8-substituted versions remain less explored. However, the 4,8-substitution offers a superior framework for promoting intramolecular non-covalent interactions that planarize neighboring heterocycles. By strategically choosing the heteroatoms and their positions within these flanking rings, the planarity, solubility, and energy gap of the resulting molecules and polymers can be precisely controlled.
Non-covalent interactions occur when the contact distance between two atoms, typically heteroatoms, is less than the sum of their van der Waals radii. Previous investigations utilizing X-ray crystallography and computational simulations have revealed that incorporating thiophene units adjacent to the BBT heterocycle (as depicted in Fig. 2, where X = CH and Y = S) leads to a non-rigid, twisted molecular structure. In the orientation shown in Fig. 2, C–H⋯N hydrogen bonding is counteracted by repulsive S⋯S interactions. Furthermore, S⋯N interactions are hindered by steric clashes between C–H and S groups when the flanking heterocycles are rotated 180 degrees. In contrast, substituting thiophene with thiazole moieties (Fig. 2: X = S, Y = N) results in a highly planarized structure due to four intramolecular S⋯N non-covalent interactions, with a maximum torsion angle of only 5.1° across the connecting C–C bond. Similarly, furan units (Fig. 2: X = CH, Y = O) also promote significant planarity, exhibiting a maximum torsion angle of 4.1°, facilitated by S⋯O non-covalent interactions. These findings, among others, demonstrate that S⋯N and S⋯O non-covalent contacts provide comparable favorable interactions. Conversely, S⋯S interactions are repulsive, and N⋯O interactions are weak or insignificant. While intramolecular hydrogen bonding with BBT nitrogen atoms might contribute when X = CH, the crucial role of heteroatom-heteroatom interactions in influencing molecular structure has been definitively established.
This study details the synthesis of novel BBT copolymers, utilizing the 4,8-substitution pathway for conjugation, and evaluates their semiconducting performance in OFET and OPV devices. Comonomers chosen for this series include bisthienyl-diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), dithienopyrrole (DTP), benzothiadiazole (BT), benzodithiophene (BDT), and 4,4′-dialkoxybithiazole (BTz). These selections were based on their established utility in high-performance OFET and OPV applications, their capacity for tuning HOMO and LUMO energy levels to modify the band gap, and their potential to influence polymer planarity via non-covalent heteroatom interactions.
To synthesize the target BBT-containing polymers, Stille or Suzuki cross-coupling polymerizations were employed. These reactions involved alkylated 4,8-dibromo-BBT and functionalized monomers including bisthienyl-diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), dithienopyrrole (DTP), benzothiadiazole (BT), benzodithiophene (BDT), and 4,4′-dialkoxybithiazole (BTz). Following the main polymerization, thiophene units were added to the polymer ends through further Stille or Suzuki cross-coupling reactions using tributyl(thiophen-2-yl)stannane or thiophen-2-ylboronic acid, followed by 2-bromothiophene. For polymers with a biheterocyclic bridge between donor/acceptor units, random copolymers were formed by incorporating 2,5-bis(trimethylstannyl)thiophene or 2,5-bis(trimethylstannyl)furan as a third monomer. The molecular weights of the synthesized polymers, determined by gel permeation chromatography (GPC) in chloroform or o-dichlorobenzene, varied between 14.4 and 96.0 kg mol−1. A key factor limiting molecular weight for each polymer was its solubility, as all precipitated from solution during polymerization. Interestingly, pDPP2ThBBT and pDPPThFBBT exhibited similar solubility despite their differing molecular weights. This similarity in pDPPThFBBT is attributed to reduced rotational freedom caused by intramolecular S⋯O and C–H⋯N interactions facilitated by the furan rings adjacent to the BBT unit. Conversely, the thiophene groups in pDPP2ThBBT lead to unfavorable S⋯S interactions and a more twisted structure, allowing the polymer chain to remain soluble for longer periods before precipitation. Variations in molecular weight among pDPPThBBT, pBT2ThBBT, and pBDTBBT, all of which contain thiophene units flanking the BBT core and thus unfavorable S⋯S interactions, are likely a result of differing alkyl chain lengths influencing solubility limits during polymerization, along with variations in polymerization conditions employed.
The absorption spectra of the 4,8-BBT copolymers were measured in both o-dichlorobenzene solution and as thin films, revealing broad absorption that extended up to 900 nm. This broad absorption corresponded to optical band gaps ranging from 1.36 to 2.00 eV, determined from the onset of the longest wavelength absorption in the solid state. A consistent observation across all 4,8-BBT copolymers was a red shift in their longest wavelength absorbance onset when transitioning from solution to thin film, attributed to enhanced intermolecular interactions. However, the magnitude of this red shift varied. The three DPP-containing copolymers (pDDPThBBT, pDDP2ThBBT, and pDPPThFBBT) and pBTzBBT exhibited only a minor red shift (≤35 nm), suggesting a rigid and aggregated structure even in solution. Conversely, pBT2ThBBT, pDTPBBT, and pBDTBBT displayed a more pronounced red shift (up to 95 nm) in their film form. This greater shift in the latter polymers indicates increased order due to aggregation and molecular packing in the solid state, partly explained by the presence of thiophene groups flanking the BBT unit, which leads to a more twisted and less conjugated structure in solution.
The DPP-containing copolymers (pDDPThBBT, pDDP2ThBBT, and pDPPThFBBT) exhibited the smallest optical band gaps among all the 4,8-BBT copolymers, ranging from 1.36 to 1.43 eV, and displayed very similar optical characteristics. This dominance of the DPP unit's strong electron-accepting nature significantly influenced the optical properties and led to red-shifted absorption. In contrast, copolymers with weaker acceptor units, BTz and BT, showed slightly wider band gaps (1.53 eV for pBTzBBT and 1.58 eV for pBT2ThBBT). The widest optical band gaps were observed in copolymers incorporating electron-donating units, specifically 1.84 eV for pDTPBBT and 2.00 eV for pBDTBBT.
When dissolved, pBT2ThBBT, pBDTBBT, and pDTPBBT displayed absorption profiles similar to their 2,6-BBT counterparts, with absorption onsets either comparable or slightly blue-shifted. In thin film form, pDTPBBT and its 2,6-substituted analogue, PBTDTP, maintained similar absorption characteristics and nearly identical optical band gaps of 1.84 and 1.85 eV, respectively. In contrast, the solid-state absorption of pBT2ThBBT and pBDTBBT showed a red-shift compared to their 2,6-analogues, leading to reduced optical band gaps. Specifically, the BT-containing polymers exhibited band gaps of 1.58 eV versus 1.7 eV, and the BDT-containing polymers showed 2.00 eV versus 2.13 eV. Furthermore, pBT2ThBBT possessed a considerably wider absorption window than its 2,6-analogue, PBBTzBT-HD, enhancing its photon absorption capabilities.
Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to determine the electronic properties of the 4,8-BBT copolymers in both solution and thin film states. These analyses, conducted on platinum disc electrodes, revealed electronic band gaps ranging from 1.26 to 2.24 eV, which aligned well with the optical band gap measurements. While cyclic voltammograms for all copolymers were documented, pBT2ThBBT did not exhibit a reduction peak within the tested potential range (down to -2.0 V), precluding the electrochemical determination of its LUMO level.
The DPP-containing 4,8-BBT copolymers displayed electrochemical band gaps of 1.39 eV for pDPPThBBT and 1.43 eV for pDPP2ThBBT, reflecting their similar HOMO and LUMO energy levels. pDPPThFBBT exhibited a slightly lower band gap of 1.26 eV. This difference is attributed to the furan groups flanking the BBT unit, which possess lower resonance stabilization energy and facilitate increased conjugation via planarizing S–O interactions. Overall, the electrochemical findings for these DPP-based copolymers align with optical data, confirming that the potent electron-accepting DPP unit dictates their electronic properties. Nevertheless, the incorporation of thiophene or furan flanking units allows for further refinement of this behavior.
Copolymers with weaker electron acceptor units, like pBTzBBT, exhibited slightly wider electrochemical band gaps (1.69 eV). Conversely, those with electron-donating units, pDTPBBT and pBDTBBT, showed significantly wider band gaps (2.11 and 2.24 eV, respectively). Notably, pBDTBBT possessed a smaller electrochemical band gap (2.24 eV) compared to its 2,6-analogue (PBTHDDT, 2.37 eV), aligning with observed bathochromic shifts in absorption.
To fabricate bottom gate/bottom contact (BGBC) organic field-effect transistors (OFETs), n-doped silicon substrates from the Fraunhofer Institute für Photonische Mikrosysteme IPMS were utilized. These substrates were equipped with a 200 nm thermally grown SiO2 dielectric layer and pre-fabricated gold source/drain electrodes, featuring channel lengths of 2.5, 5, 10, and 20 μm, and a channel width of 1 cm. Unless specified otherwise, polymer solutions underwent pre-stirring at 50 °C for a minimum of three hours before being spin-coated at 2000 rpm while still hot. After any necessary annealing, the devices were dried under vacuum (5 × 10−2 mbar). Performance measurements were then conducted using a Keithley 4200 semiconductor characterisation system within a dry, nitrogen-filled atmosphere. Carrier mobilities were determined in the saturation region using the standard equation, which relates source-drain current (Ids) to mobility (μ), gate voltage (Vgs), channel length (L), channel width (W), and insulator capacitance per unit area (Ci).
To optimize device performance, pDPP2ThBBT was chosen for its favorable solubility and high molecular weight, factors known to enhance charge carrier mobility. Studies involved varying annealing temperatures, from as-cast to 200 °C, and testing different solvents: chloroform, chlorobenzene, and o-dichlorobenzene. Solutions at a concentration of 10 mg ml−1 were applied to prefabricated substrates using the established spin-coating method, with the same device evaluated at each annealing step. To ensure fair material comparison and minimize additional processing, techniques like self-assembled monolayers or processing additives were intentionally omitted. Comprehensive data for pDPP2ThBBT-based devices are presented in Table 2 and Figure 5, with complete device information available in the ESI.
For pDPP2ThBBT, o-dichlorobenzene proved to be the optimal solvent for OFET fabrication, yielding superior device performance compared to chlorobenzene or chloroform across all annealing temperatures. Annealing beyond 100 °C led to a decline in performance, attributed to poor film morphology. This degradation is likely due to the polymer's inherent rigidity, causing modest annealing to induce crystallization and grain boundaries. Consequently, other 4,8-BBT copolymers were processed using o-dichlorobenzene and annealed at 100 °C. Their corresponding output and transfer characteristics are presented in Table 3 and Figures S34–S39 (ESI†).
The hole mobilities observed across the series of polymers exhibited a wide range, spanning four orders of magnitude. Among these, organic field-effect transistors (OFETs) fabricated with pDPP2ThBBT demonstrated the highest hole mobility, reaching 2.16 × 10⁻² cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹. These devices also presented a moderate on/off current ratio (Ion/Ioff) of 10³ and a low threshold voltage of 5 V, characteristic of p-type operation. However, they did display increased hysteresis in both output and transfer characteristics, potentially stemming from charge trapping within the bulk film or suboptimal device architecture. In contrast, pDPPThFBBT achieved a slightly lower hole mobility of 2.03 × 10⁻³ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹. This reduction may be attributed to its lower molecular weight and a larger threshold voltage of −2 V for p-type devices. The likely cause is a non-ohmic contact between the gold electrode and the shallow highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level of pDPPThFBBT (−4.86 eV), leading to inefficient charge injection. Atomic force microscopy (AFM) revealed that thin films of pDPP2ThBBT and pDPPThFBBT deposited on OFET substrates formed very smooth, uniform layers with root mean square (RMS) roughness values of 0.55 nm and 0.34 nm, respectively, and grain boundaries of 0.1 μm or less. In comparison, pDPPThBBT exhibited a significantly rougher film morphology (RMS roughness of 2.82 nm), with domains extending up to 5 μm and grain boundaries exceeding 1 μm. This poor film quality, coupled with the relatively low molecular weight of pDPPThBBT, is believed to be responsible for its considerably lower hole mobility of 3.23 × 10⁻⁶ cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹.
To address the limited solubility of pBTzBBT, solutions for OFET fabrication underwent a three-hour pre-stirring at 100 °C before hot spin-coating to prevent material precipitation. Despite this, pBTzBBT yielded the most promising OFETs among those studied, exhibiting a respectable hole mobility of 2.16 × 10−3 cm2 V−1 s−1. Critically, these devices achieved a high Ion/Ioff ratio of 105 and a low driving voltage of −2 V. Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) revealed a rough film morphology for pBTzBBT (RMS roughness of 15.63 nm), likely a consequence of its poor solubility even after pre-treatment. Consequently, refining device preparation or enhancing polymer solubility through longer alkyl chains could further improve the performance of solution-processed OFETs based on this material.
The incorporation of the BBT unit significantly impacts OFET performance, as demonstrated by pBT2ThBBT. While exhibiting modest mobility (3.60 × 10−3 cm2 V−1 s−1), this value is more than two orders of magnitude greater than a comparable literature example using only BT units. This enhancement underscores the improved crystallinity facilitated by the BBT unit's potential for non-covalent interactions and two-dimensional conjugation within the polymer backbone.
Many of the synthesized 4,8-BBT copolymers, exhibiting visible light absorption, narrow band gaps, and efficient charge transport, were evaluated as donor materials in bulk heterojunction (BHJ) organic photovoltaic (OPV) devices. However, the poor solubility of pBTzBBT prevented the preparation of suitable blend solutions with fullerene acceptors for OPV fabrication. Consequently, the other synthesized polymers were used as donors. These were blended with [6,6]-phenyl-C71-butyric acid methyl ester (PC71BM) and incorporated into OPV devices following a standard ITO/PEDOT:PSS/BHJ/Ca/Al architecture. The outcomes of these experiments are presented in Table 4.
The DPP-containing BBT copolymers, pDPP2ThBBT and pDPPThFBBT, showed comparable power conversion efficiencies (PCEs) of 0.89% and 0.87%, respectively. This represents a slight enhancement over pDPPThBBT (0.50% PCE). These improved PCEs are linked to higher fill factors (FFs) of 60% and 55%, respectively, compared to pDPPThBBT's 50% FF. The FFs for these DPP-based copolymers correlated directly with their hole mobilities, aligning with prior research.
The pBT2ThBBT polymer demonstrated the highest power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 4.45% when utilized in a 1:1 blend with PC71BM, enhanced by the inclusion of 3% diiodooctane (DIO) as an additive. This notable PCE was primarily driven by a very high short-circuit current density (JSC) of 14.32 mA cm−2, alongside moderate open-circuit voltage (VOC) and fill factor (FF) values of 0.65 V and 48%, respectively. The impressive JSC achieved by this device, which is comparable to that of PTB7, positions pBT2ThBBT as a strong contender for high-efficiency organic photovoltaic (OPV) applications. Further insights into the performance of the optimal OPV device, including its J–V characteristics, external quantum efficiency (EQE), and the absorption spectra of its active layer components (pBT2ThBBT and PC71BM), are presented in Fig. 7. The enhanced photocurrent observed in this polymer, relative to others, is likely attributable to its broad absorption profile, as illustrated in Fig. 3, and a higher extinction coefficient compared to other BBT polymers. Additionally, time-resolved fluorescence studies revealed an exciton diffusion length of approximately 10 nm for pBT2ThBBT, exceeding that of many donor–acceptor polymers. This extended exciton diffusion length, compared to values like 4–5 nm for PTB7, contributes significantly to improved photon harvesting within the pBT2ThBBT:PC71BM blend. While pBT2ThBBT achieved a higher initial power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 4.45% compared to its 2,6-BBT counterpart (2.37%) in a conventional device structure, the analogue's PCE could be enhanced to 3.84% and 6.53% with inverted structures and ZnO layers. This suggests potential for further improvement in pBT2ThBBT devices.
For the first time, copolymers incorporating the 4,8-benzobisthiazole (BBT) unit have been synthesized, featuring comonomers such as bisthienyl-diketopyrrolopyrrole (DPP), dithienopyrrole (DTP), benzothiadiazole (BT), benzodithiophene (BDT), and 4,4′-dialkoxybithiazole (BTz). These newly developed copolymers exhibit optical band gaps ranging from 1.36 to 2.00 eV, which are comparable to or narrower than their 2,6-BBT counterparts, and these values align well with their electrochemical band gaps (1.26–2.24 eV). When fabricated into bottom-gate/bottom-contact organic field-effect transistors (OFETs), these materials achieved record hole mobilities among similar 2,6- or 4,8-BBT polymers, reaching up to 2.16 × 10−2 cm2 V−1 s−1 with pDPP2ThBBT. OFETs made from pBTzBBT demonstrated a highly desirable Ion/Ioff ratio of 105 and a low driving voltage of −2 V, despite exhibiting poor film quality as determined by atomic force microscopy. Further optimization of this material and device structure is anticipated to yield efficient, low-cost, solution-processed OFETs. In organic photovoltaic (OPV) devices, the 4,8-BBT copolymers served as donor materials with fullerene acceptors. The most promising material, pBT2ThBBT, achieved a high short-circuit current exceeding 14 mA cm−2 and a power conversion efficiency of 4.45%. Future enhancements in pBT2ThBBT device performance are expected through the adoption of an inverted device structure and the incorporation of a ZnO electron transport layer.
