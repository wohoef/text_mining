The EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP Panel) has developed various guidance documents. These documents serve to supplement regulations governing feed additive authorization and assist applicants in preparing technical dossiers. While many of these guidance documents have undergone updates, some components now require technical revision, as indicated by accumulated experience. In response to this need, EFSA tasked the FEEDAP Panel with identifying which current guidance documents require updating. The Panel approached this by reviewing insights gained from assessments conducted since the last significant revisions of individual documents. Each Standing Working Group within the FEEDAP Panel was asked to pinpoint issues encountered during additive assessments that highlighted areas needing reconsideration within the guidance. Furthermore, the Panel considered overarching guidance from the EFSA Scientific Committee and advancements in assessment tools from EFSA and other international bodies. The collected information revealed several broad areas within the existing guidance that warrant revision. The Panel specifically recommends prioritizing the revision of the environmental risk assessment guidance, as it no longer aligns with other EFSA outputs. Following this, the Panel proposes updating the three safety-related guidance documents (covering target animals, consumers, and users), and then the guidance on efficacy. Concurrently, the data requirements for additive characterization will be reviewed and adjusted. The FEEDAP Panel emphasizes the crucial role of involving industry, consumer associations, and experts from Member State risk assessment bodies early in the guidance revision process.

Detailed rules for preparing, presenting, assessing, and authorizing feed additive applications are outlined in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008. This regulation implements the broader framework for community authorization of animal nutrition additives, which is established by Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003.
To assist applicants in preparing and submitting technical dossiers for animal feed additive authorization, the FEEDAP Panel developed guidance documents. These documents supplement Regulation (EC) No 429/2008, aligning with the authorization framework established by Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003.
The FEEDAP Panel's guidance documents have generally undergone updates since their initial adoption, ranging from minor adjustments due to rule changes to extensive revisions. However, some documents remain unreviewed. EFSA has tasked the FEEDAP Panel with identifying which of these documents, listed in Table 1, require updating.
The EFSA has tasked its FEEDAP Panel with a multi-stage process for updating guidance documents. First, the Panel must pinpoint which existing guidance documents require revision, drawing upon recent scientific advancements and insights from past feed additive assessments. Subsequently, these identified documents are to be updated, a process that can be phased according to established priorities and available resources. Furthermore, given the importance and scope of certain revisions, the Panel is to explore preparatory information sessions or public consultations for draft guidance. Feedback gathered from these initiatives will be crucial and must be incorporated into the final versions of the guidance documents as appropriate.
This document focuses solely on the initial term of reference. The remaining two terms will be tackled once the identified guidance documents undergo their necessary updates.
To address the initial mandate, the FEEDAP Panel drew upon insights from numerous applications processed since the last major guidance revisions. This involved tasking each Standing Working Group with identifying problems encountered during assessments, thereby highlighting areas within the existing guidance that required re-evaluation for applicants.
The Panel will incorporate overarching guidance from the EFSA Scientific Committee and advancements in assessment tools from EFSA (like food consumption databases and statistical reporting) and other international bodies (such as EMA, ECHA, WHO/FAO, OECD) when updating the guidance documents.
To determine if existing guidance required modification, the Panel also reviewed input from external parties like the European Commission and industry, gathered through meetings, calls, and correspondence regarding FEEDAP opinions.
Information gathered revealed fundamental elements in applicant guidance requiring reconsideration and potential revision. Key issues, structured according to Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 429/2008, are detailed below.
A clear definition of active substances and their relationship with blended formulations (additives, premixtures, preparations) is required.
The compositional data requirements for certain additive categories, such as X-ray diffraction for clays or FTIR for chelates, need clearer definition to ensure adequate description.
When assessing safety, the data required must account for new manufacturing and processing methods, such as nanomaterials and ultrafiltration.
Manufacturers' descriptions should incorporate HACCP plans and external audit evidence.
For additives without an authorization holder, the necessity of certain data, like purity/impurity information (unless part of specifications) and particle size/dusting potential, warrants re-evaluation, especially when a specific formulation is absent.
The assessment of fermentation products across various additive categories requires a consistent approach. This includes evaluating aspects like GMM versus non-GMM products, antimicrobial resistance, presence of production strain DNA, endotoxin production in Gram-negative bacteria, secondary metabolite production, and purity relative to inclusion rate.
The ongoing evolution of antibiotic resistance in clinical bacterial strains and fluctuating antibiotic efficacy necessitate a re-evaluation of both the tested antibiotic panel and the microbial species for which cutoff values are suggested.
Botanical safety assessments require revision. This should draw upon direct experience with botanical preparations and evaluations of individual compounds within botanical extracts.
Current statistical analysis of tolerance study data for target animal safety relies on detecting differences, which fails to establish effect equivalence. Consequently, future statistical evaluations of these studies should prioritize equivalence assessment.
To minimize animal use, safety studies for target species should incorporate experimental designs that reduce animal numbers. This includes using benchmark approaches, selecting relevant endpoints, combining studies, and testing multiple substances concurrently.
To minimize animal testing, the established method of inferring target animal safety from laboratory animal data, currently used for sensory and food-authorized additives, could extend to other additive types. This expansion necessitates careful evaluation of the toxicological data and endpoints derived from such studies.
To better reflect contemporary European breeding advancements and production methods, the default body weight and feed intake values for target species require revision. This necessitates re-evaluating target animal categories for safety and efficacy, considering the additive's purpose. Consequently, study durations should be adjusted.
Re-evaluating the safety of food-authorized additives for target animal species requires reconsidering the comparison between human and animal exposure levels.
Toxicological studies for fermentation products require reassessment, focusing on the production strain's nature and history, and the overall production process.
To assess consumer exposure to residues from animal products, the food consumption model needs updating. It should incorporate the latest pan-European food consumption data from EFSA and potentially include country-specific and population-group data.
When assessing user safety for additives without an authorization holder, the focus might be narrowed to systemic effects tied to the active substance. This approach would exclude formulation-specific aspects not covered by the authorization.
Harmonization is needed between ECHA's user safety approach for individual compounds and the approach for holder-specific additives.
The environmental risk assessment guidance requires complete revision. This is due to new methodological developments and extensive documentation from EFSA's Scientific Committee and other organizations like EMA and OECD.
The guidance should incorporate specific endpoints for experimentally demonstrating efficacy across all additive categories, including recently introduced ones.
Efficacy trials for coccidiostats require a review of their number and type.
The FEEDAP guidance documents require alignment with EFSA's statistical reporting guidelines. Furthermore, a thorough description of statistical methods for various assessments (e.g., target animal safety, toxicology, residue determination, efficacy dose titration) is needed. The utility of meta-analysis in efficacy assessment also warrants re-evaluation.
Guidance documents should integrate validated, internationally recognized methods, such as OECD guidelines for user safety, comet assay, and QSAR for genotoxicity prediction.
The identified issues necessitate significant revisions to the listed guidance documents.
Substantial revisions are necessary for several guidance documents. These include those detailing dossier preparation for various feed additive categories like technological, sensory, nutritional, zootechnical, and coccidiostat/histomonostat additives. Additionally, technical guidance on target animal tolerance and efficacy, microbial studies, consumer and user safety, and environmental risk assessment requires updating. Revisions are also needed for technical guidance concerning data extrapolation between species, food-authorized additives, bacterial antibiotic susceptibility, and additives for non-food-producing animals.
Updates to other guidance documents might necessitate revisions to the guidance for renewing feed additive authorizations and assessing biomasses for animal nutrition.
Beyond the major issues, specific guidance documents will also address minor aspects. All updates will incorporate new methodologies and improved study conduct and reporting.
The Panel determined that certain guidance documents do not currently require revision. The guidance for re-evaluation is deemed current because all pertinent applications have already been submitted. Similarly, updates are not considered necessary for the safety assessment of *Bacillus* spp. and *Enterococcus* spp., given their recent adoption/update and the absence of new justifying information. Furthermore, the guidance on the compatibility of zootechnical additives with antimicrobial substances also does not need immediate updating.
The overall structure of existing guidance might require alteration, contingent on revision outcomes. For instance, separating current tolerance and efficacy guidance could be beneficial, potentially leading to dedicated guidance for additive characterization.
Given the outdated nature of current environmental risk assessment guidance, the Panel prioritizes its revision. A dedicated working group will be formed to undertake this essential update.
Following the environmental risk assessment, the FEEDAP Panel will prioritize revising the three guidance documents addressing safety for target animals, consumers, and users, subsequently tackling the efficacy guidance. Concurrently, the working group responsible for guidance updates, potentially augmented with specialized expertise, will review and adjust the data requirements for additive characterization. These changes could either be integrated into existing additive category guidance or necessitate the development of a new, distinct guidance document.
The FEEDAP Panel will assess if all current guidance documents are necessary. They may consider reducing their number, potentially by removing those specific to additive categories.
The remaining guidance documents will be updated later, either by the current working group or by specially formed ad hoc groups, as needed.
Early engagement with industry, consumer groups, and risk assessment experts from Member States is deemed crucial by the FEEDAP Panel for the guidance revision process. To facilitate this, the Panel plans to distribute this document to stakeholders before an information session in July 2016. This will allow stakeholders to provide written feedback beforehand or contribute to discussions during the session, with all input informing the guidance document revisions.
Public consultations are planned for guidance documents undergoing substantial revisions, particularly those crucial for assessing all additive categories.
