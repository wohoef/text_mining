The European Commission requested a scientific opinion from the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) regarding the safety and efficacy of Diarr‐Stop S Plus®. This product is a mixture comprising the sodium salt of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (Na2EDTA), an extract rich in tannins from *Castanea sativa*, and thyme and oregano oils. It is intended for use in feed for pigs destined for fattening at a recommended dose of 1,000 mg/kg of feed. The primary purpose of its application is to mitigate dysentery caused by *Brachyspira hyodysenteriae*, thereby enhancing animal performance. The FEEDAP Panel concluded that Diarr‐Stop S Plus® is safe for pigs for fattening at the recommended dosage. Furthermore, the individual active components of the additive did not present safety concerns for consumers when present in feed at the specified levels. Given the absence of expected interactions between constituents that could affect consumer safety, the use of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® in feed for fattening pigs is deemed safe for consumers, with no withdrawal period required. Regarding user safety, the additive is classified as a skin and eye irritant. However, the FEEDAP Panel could not definitively assess its potential for dermal sensitization. Due to its particle size, inhalation exposure for users is considered unlikely. A comprehensive risk assessment for terrestrial and aquatic environments could not be completed due to insufficient data on the ecotoxicological effects of Na2EDTA. Critically, none of the efficacy studies submitted, which were all conducted on commercial premises, met the minimum requirements for experimental design. Consequently, the FEEDAP Panel was unable to reach a conclusion on the efficacy of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® for use in feed for pigs for fattening.

Under Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Community authorises feed additives. Article 4(1) specifies that anyone seeking authorisation for a feed additive, or a new use of one, must submit an application following Article 7 of the regulation.
Pharmatéka Bt. requested European Commission authorization for Diarr‐Stop S Plus®, a product containing Na2EDTA, Castanea sativa extract, thyme oil, and oregano oil. This product is intended for use as a feed additive for pigs for fattening, classified as a zootechnical additive and a gut flora stabiliser.
Following Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission transmitted Pharmatéka Bt.'s application for Diarr‐Stop S Plus® to EFSA. This application, submitted under Article 4(1) for feed additive authorization, was supported by a technical dossier directly provided by the applicant. EFSA confirmed the validity of the submitted documents on November 14, 2011.
Following verification of the applicant's submitted particulars and documents, EFSA is required by Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 to conduct an assessment. This assessment aims to determine if the feed additive meets the conditions outlined in Article 5. EFSA will then provide an opinion on the safety of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® (containing Na2EDTA, C. sativa mill, thyme oil, and oregano oil) for target animals, consumers, users, and the environment. This opinion will also address the product's efficacy under the proposed conditions of use.
Diarr‐Stop S Plus® is a mixture of Na2EDTA, a tannin-rich extract from *C. sativa*, thyme oil, and oregano oil.
Diarr‐Stop S Plus® has no prior assessment as a feed additive within the EU or globally. Nevertheless, its individual constituents have undergone evaluation for various food and feed applications. Calcium disodium EDTA is recognized as an authorized food additive in the EU (E 385). The Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives (JECFA) established an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 2.5 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day for this substance. While an ADI for EDTA or its sodium salts specifically has not been set, a value of 1.9 mg/kg bw per day for EDTA itself can be derived from the calcium disodium EDTA ADI. This derived value was subsequently employed in determining safe levels of ferric sodium EDTA for human iron supplementation.
Tannic acid, also known as hydrolysable tannin, is recognized by the Council of Europe and is included in the EU's list of food flavoring substances, identified by FLAVIS number [16.080]. It is also present in the EU Register of Feed Additives. The FEEDAP Panel has previously issued an opinion on the safety and efficacy of tannic acid as a feed flavoring for all animal species. Additionally, the Panel had an earlier, inconclusive opinion regarding a tannin-rich extract from *C. sativa* when used as a zootechnical additive for piglets and rabbits intended for fattening.
The assessment of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® as a feed additive relies on data provided by the applicant in a technical dossier. This dossier was compiled according to the requirements of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 and Regulation (EC) No 429/2008, adhering to relevant EFSA guidance documents, to support the authorization request.
To produce its opinion, the FEEDAP Panel analyzed the applicant's data alongside information from prior EFSA risk assessments, other expert bodies, scientific publications, and reports.
EFSA verified the EURL report concerning methods for controlling Diarr-Stop S Plus® in animal feed. The Executive Summary of this report is available in Annex A.6.
The FEEDAP Panel's assessment of Diarr‐Stop S Plus®'s safety and efficacy adhered to the principles established in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008. This approach was guided by several key documents. These included guidance on zootechnical additives, technical guidance for tolerance and efficacy studies in target animals, and technical guidance for assessing the environmental safety of feed additives. Furthermore, the panel followed guidance for establishing the safety of additives for consumers and guidance on studies concerning the safety of the additive for users and workers.
Diarr‐Stop S Plus® is a preparation composed of sodium ethylenediaminetetraacetate (Na2EDTA), a tannin-rich extract from *C. sativa*, thyme oil, and oregano oil. It is designed as a zootechnical additive, categorized as a gut flora stabiliser. Its intended use is to positively impact the intestinal microbiota in pigs for fattening, specifically by decreasing the occurrence of dysentery caused by *Brachyspira hyodysenteriae*, thereby enhancing performance.
Diarr‐Stop S Plus® is a straightforward mixture comprising Na2EDTA, a tannin-rich extract, and two essential oils. It also includes an emulsifier (glyceryl polyethyleneglycol ricinoleate) and glucose and wheat flour as carriers. The manufacturing process involves first combining the essential oils with the emulsifier. This mixture is then incorporated with glucose, the tannin extract, Na2EDTA, and wheat flour. All constituent components are sourced commercially and blended on-site. Table 1 details the intended composition and specifications for the primary components.
Analysis of six to nine batches confirmed the levels of active components in Diarr‐Stop S Plus®. While individual compounds and tannin were directly measured, the major components of the botanical extracts were quantified instead. Dry matter content across seven batches varied from 88.2% to 94.4%. Additional analysis of three batches, used for shelf-life studies, revealed mean concentrations: EDTA at 23.4% (range 22.9–23.8%), tannic acid at 7.3% (range 7.2–7.5%), thymol at 0.67% (range 0.64–0.68%), and carvacrol at 0.47% (range 0.46–0.49%). Other terpenoids from the essential oils, including linalool (0.55%), p‐cymene (0.60%), and γ‐terpinene (0.02%), were also quantified.
The additive Diarr-Stop S Plus® has established action limits for heavy metals and arsenic. Analysis of three batches confirmed that lead, mercury, cadmium, and arsenic levels were all below these specified limits. Microbial contamination testing also revealed low counts for total aerobic bacteria and Enterobacteriaceae, with Escherichia coli, Staphyloccocus aureus, and Clostridium perfringens below 10 CFU/g. Salmonella sp. was absent in 25 CFU/g, and fungal counts were under 100 CFU/g. Furthermore, tests on three batches for aflatoxin B1, deoxynivalenol, and dioxins showed results within acceptable ranges.
Particle size analysis, using sieving and laser diffraction on multiple batches, revealed a mean particle diameter of approximately 700 μm. Crucially, no particles smaller than 100 μm were detected. Information regarding the additive's dusting potential was not provided.
Disodium ethylenediamine tetraacetate, also known as Na2EDTA, has CAS numbers 6381-92-6 and 139-33-3, and EINECS number 205-358-3. Its IUPAC name is Disodium{[2-(bis-carboxymethyl-amino)-ethyl]-carboxymethylamino}-acetate. With the empirical formula C10H14N2Na2O8, it has a molecular weight of 372.24.
Na2EDTA, a white powder, dissolves in water (100 g/L at 20°C) and has a bulk density of 700 g/L. Its free acid acts as a hexadentate ligand and chelating agent, especially for divalent cations.
The Na2EDTA in the additive contains at least 99.0% Na2EDTA, with nitrilotriacetic acid, a manufacturing byproduct, limited to under 0.1%.
A tannin-rich extract from sweet chestnut (C. sativa) is produced via aqueous extraction. It is commercially available and specified to contain 75.3% hydrolysable tannins, 17% non-tannin substances, and 7.1% moisture. Analysis of a commercial batch confirmed a tannin content of 75.6%, with 1.2% ash and 6% moisture.
Thyme oil, derived from *Thymus vulgaris* via steam distillation, is a pale yellow to reddish-brown liquid insoluble in water. Its primary constituent is the monoterpene thymol, a compound with the CAS number 89-83-8, the formula C10H14O, and a molecular weight of 150.22. Thyme oil typically contains 35% to 55% thymol. Analysis of nine batches of the additive revealed an average of 7.78 g/kg of thyme oil, indicating that the oil incorporated into the additive had a mean thymol concentration of 55%.
Thyme oil also contains monoterpenes and related compounds, including p-cymene (15–28%), terpinene (5–10%), linalool (4–6.5%), carvacrol (1–4%), myrcene (1–3%), and terpinen-4-ol (0.2–2.5%). Certificates of analysis were not provided.
Obtained by steam distillation from *Origanum vulgare* (CAS 8007-11-2), oregano oil is a yellowish to dark brownish liquid that does not mix with water. Its primary constituent is carvacrol (60-80%), also known as 5-isopropyl-2-methylphenol or 2-methyl-5-(1-methylethyl)phenol (CAS 499-75-2, C10H14O, molecular weight 150.22). The oil typically contains 2-5% of its structural isomer, thymol. Analysis of carvacrol in the final product indicated a mean concentration of approximately 80% in the oil used for formulation.
The stability of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® was assessed by storing three batches in sealed containers under different temperature and humidity conditions for up to 18 months. During this period, no significant loss of Na2EDTA, tannic acid, thymol, or carvacrol was detected. However, minor components from the essential oils, specifically p‐cymene and linalool, experienced a reduction of over 50% by the conclusion of the experiments.
Na2EDTA content remained stable in pelleted feed after three months of storage at 25°C/60% RH, with levels showing minimal change (24.2% vs 23.4%).
Stability data for Diarr‐Stop S Plus® in premixes or when incorporated into feed were not provided.
Homogeneous distribution of Diarr-Stop S Plus in pig feed was evaluated. Three batches of the additive were incorporated into pig feed at a rate of 1,000 mg/kg. From each batch, eight samples were collected from various locations within the mixing vessel: top, bottom, middle, and randomly. Analysis of Na2EDTA concentration in these samples allowed for the calculation of coefficients of variation (CV). Across the three batches, the average CV was 1.5%, with values ranging from 0.9% to 1.8%. This low average CV demonstrates that the additive distributes uniformly within the feed.
Diarr-Stop S Plus® is recommended for pigs for fattening, weighing at least 35 kg, at a dose of 1,000 mg/kg of complete feed. No withdrawal period is anticipated.
A tolerance trial involved 12 sows and their litters, divided into three treatment groups based on parity, farrowing date, and litter size. The experiment spanned two phases: suckling (days 8-30) and rearing (days 31-79). A basal diet of maize, wheat, barley, and soybean meal was supplemented with Diarr‐Stop S Plus® at three levels: 0 mg/kg (control), 1,000 mg/kg (1x recommended dose), or 10,000 mg/kg (10x recommended dose). Diets were fed ad libitum. Analysis confirmed Na2EDTA content in the diets, showing 260 mg/kg for the 1x dose and 2,510 mg/kg for the 10x dose. Piglet weights and litter feed intake were recorded on days 8 and 30 of age.
Piglets weighing over 6 kg at weaning were moved to rearing pens. Within each treatment group, piglets were divided into five pens, with each pen containing three males and three females. Litters from the same treatment were combined. To ensure comparable starting weights for the rearing phase across the three treatment groups (control: 7.4 ± 0.7 kg; 1x dose: 8.2 ± 1.0 kg; 10x dose: 8.2 ± 1.6 kg), the sex ratio was adjusted based on weaning weights. The rearing phase continued for 49 days, concluding when the piglets reached 79 days of age. Throughout this period and at the trial's conclusion, individual piglet weights were recorded, and feed consumption was measured per pen.
Haematological and biochemical parameters were measured from blood samples taken from all 90 animals on days 1 and 49 of the rearing period. Data analysis utilized analysis of variance (ANOVA), with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.
During the suckling period, six piglets died, with crushing being the primary cause. One death occurred in the control group, one in the 1× treatment group, and four in the 10× group. No significant differences were noted in daily weight gain, averaging around 220 g/day. Similarly, final weaning weights showed no significant variation, with control animals averaging 6.7 kg, the 1× group 7.9 kg, and the 10× group 7.4 kg. Feed intake was recorded but not statistically analyzed due to the significant nutritional contribution from the sow.
During the rearing phase, no significant differences in weight gain, feed intake, or measured haematological and biochemical parameters were observed across the treatments. Furthermore, no mortality was reported during this growth period.
A combined study investigating tolerance and residues was conducted with 36 pigs intended for fattening. These pigs, a crossbreed of Hungarian Large White and Hungarian Landrace, began the study at an average weight of 29 kg. The animals were divided into three treatment groups, with six males and six females housed individually in each. These groups received a basal diet of maize, wheat, barley, and soybean meal supplemented with Diarr‐Stop S Plus®. The supplementation levels were either 0 mg/kg (control), 1,000 mg/kg (the recommended dose), or 10,000 mg/kg (ten times the recommended dose) of complete feed. It's noted that the actual doses of the supplement in the feed were not confirmed by analysis. Throughout the 92-day experimental period, animals were fed their respective diets ad libitum. Their general health was monitored, and both animals and feed were weighed every two weeks. Biological samples, including blood, urine, and faeces, were collected from each pig at the trial's commencement, again after six weeks (when pigs were approximately 59 kg), and finally at the study's conclusion (around 102 kg). A specific design was employed for the groups receiving Diarr‐Stop S Plus®. Four pigs in these groups were fed the treated diets until the experiment's end. Another four received the treated diets until two days prior to slaughter, and the remaining four were fed the treated diets until four days before the end. These latter groups were switched to the control feed once the treated feed was withdrawn. Blood, urine, and faecal samples were collected at the beginning of the trial, after 1.5 months, and at the end. Upon sacrifice, carcasses were evaluated, and samples from various organs and tissues, including liver, kidney, intestines (both large and small), back fat, thigh muscle, and rib bone, were collected from all animals. The concentration of EDTA was measured in all collected biological and tissue samples as part of the metabolism and residue determination. Statistical analysis of the data was performed using ANOVA, with significance set at p < 0.05.
During the experiment, no animals died. Final weight and average daily weight gain showed no significant differences between groups. However, feed intake was notably lower in the 10x group compared to the control. This reduction in feed intake led to a significantly improved feed-to-gain ratio in the 10x group.
In the tolerance study involving pigs for fattening, samples of rib bones and liver were collected. Bone analysis focused on calcium, zinc, and lead content, while liver analysis included copper, manganese, zinc, and lead. Trace element levels remained unaffected by the additive, except for manganese in the liver. Pigs receiving the additive, regardless of dose, showed a significant reduction in liver manganese levels (4.01, 3.66, and 3.41 mg/kg for the control, 1x, and 10x doses, respectively; p < 0.05).
Intestinal morphology, including villi length and width, endothelium thickness, propria layer infiltration, and epithelioglandular cells in Lieberkühn's crypts, was analyzed. No treatment-related differences were observed in any measured parameters.
In vitro studies with Na2EDTA, but not the full additive, were provided. These investigated the survival and proliferation of *B. hyodysenteria* strains and their impact on other gut bacteria.
While the provided tolerance study for pigs for fattening lacked blood haematology and biochemistry data, the FEEDAP Panel considered all available information. This included data from weaned piglets, identified as the most sensitive growth stage, and pigs for fattening. Both groups exhibited no adverse effects even when fed diets containing ten times the recommended dose of Diarr‐Stop S Plus®. Consequently, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that the additive is safe for pigs for fattening at the recommended dose of 1,000 mg/kg of complete feed.
Consumer safety is assessed by examining each component of the additive individually, as no toxicological studies on the additive itself were provided.
Studies in rats and mice have established a no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of 500 mg/kg body weight per day for Na2EDTA and Na3EDTA. In a 90-day study, doses of 2,500 mg/kg body weight per day, and in a preceding range-finding study, higher doses led to adverse effects including diarrhea, reduced body weight, decreased packed cell volume (PCV) and hemoglobin (Hb), and parakeratosis in the esophagus and forestomach. Animal studies indicated no negative impacts on reproductive performance at EDTA doses up to 250 mg/kg body weight per day. Teratological effects observed at high EDTA doses (1,000 mg/kg body weight per day and above) were primarily linked to disruptions in zinc homeostasis and developmental consequences. These effects could be mitigated by supplementing the diet with excess zinc, as noted in the European Union Risk Assessment Report (EU-RAR, 2004).
While EDTA showed no genotoxicity in somatic cells, a micronucleus test with spermatids yielded a positive result. This suggested potential aneugenic effects during late spermatogenesis. However, this effect was only observed at extremely high doses, near the LD50. Given that aneuploidy induction follows a threshold mechanism, the risk of such effects at lower doses is considered negligible.
A combined tolerance and residue study involved pigs for fattening divided into three groups: a control, a group receiving Diarr‐Stop S Plus® at the proposed 1,000 mg/kg feed, and a group receiving 10 times that dose (10,000 mg/kg feed). Following slaughter without any prior withdrawal period, EDTA was detected in all analyzed tissues at ng/g levels. The liver consistently showed the highest concentrations for both dosages, measuring 436 ng/g for the proposed dose and 1,382 ng/g for the higher dose. Kidney, meat, and fat also contained EDTA, with concentrations of 220 and 707 ng/g (kidney), 163 and 522 ng/g (meat), and 31 and 172 ng/g (fat), respectively. A withdrawal period of 2 or 4 days before slaughter significantly reduced these tissue EDTA concentrations, generally by half or more.
The FEEDAP Panel has no safety concerns for consumers regarding the EDTA component of Diarr-Stop S Plus. This conclusion is based on comparing the established Acceptable Daily Intake (ADI) for calcium disodium EDTA (2.5 mg/kg bw/day) and the derived EDTA moiety value (1.9 mg/kg bw/day) with the estimated maximum consumer exposure of 9 μg/kg bw/day.
The FEEDAP Panel previously issued an opinion on a tannin-rich extract from the same source used in Diarr‐Stop S Plus®. In that assessment, it was concluded that larger molecular fragments of tannins are unlikely to be absorbed. Only free or released gallic and ellagic acids are available for uptake, and these can be detected in serum and urine, either as conjugates or simple methoxyl derivatives of gallate. Humans are routinely exposed to hydrolysable tannins and their breakdown products. While the extent to which these products deposit in animal tissues cannot be definitively determined, it is considered likely to be minimal.
In a previous assessment for feed flavour use, the FEEDAP Panel reviewed toxicological studies on hydrolysable tannins. In vitro genotoxicity tests showed potential, but in vivo studies found no genotoxicity. Furthermore, oral exposure did not indicate carcinogenicity.
A 90-day subchronic study involving repeated doses of tannic acid administered to rats through their drinking water (at concentrations of 0.025–0.4%) was available solely as an abstract. This report indicated liver necrosis in male rats subjected to treatment, though female rats did not exhibit this effect. The study's authors deemed this finding incidental, citing the lack of liver lesions in females during the 90-day study and in both sexes during a carcinogenicity study. Consequently, they proposed the highest dose as a no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL). This NOAEL, when converted using EFSA Scientific Committee's default values, corresponds to approximately 360 mg/kg body weight per day. However, the FEEDAP Panel cannot confirm this NOAEL due to the absence of the complete study report.
In livestock, hydrolysable tannins are broken down in the digestive tract, with absorbed products eliminated through established pathways. Consequently, tannin or its metabolites are unlikely to accumulate in animal tissues or products. Even if accumulation did occur, existing toxicity studies suggest that residues of tannin or its metabolites would not cause adverse effects in consumers of animal products.
Thymol and carvacrol, key components of the oils in Diarr‐Stop S Plus, were previously evaluated as feed flavorings. The FEEDAP Panel determined they are safe for consumers in pig feed up to a maximum concentration of 12 mg/kg.
When Diarr‐Stop S Plus is used at its maximum recommended dose of 1,000 mg/kg of feed, the resulting levels of thymol and carvacrol are 8.6 mg/kg and 7.2 mg/kg of feed, respectively. Even considering the highest values due to batch-to-batch variation, these concentrations remain below the safe upper limits for consumers established by the FEEDAP Panel. Furthermore, other components from the essential oils, when the additive is used at the maximum dose, would be present at the following levels in the feed: linalool at 5 mg/kg, γ‐terpinene at 0.2 mg/kg, and p‐cymene at 6 mg/kg. The FEEDAP Panel has previously evaluated these specific compounds for their use as feed flavourings. For linalool, γ‐terpinene, and p‐cymene, the Panel concluded that their inclusion in feed at concentrations up to 20 mg/kg, 1.5 mg/kg, and 25 mg/kg, respectively, would be safe for consumers.
When evaluated individually, the active components of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® did not present safety concerns at the recommended feeding levels. Consequently, and with no anticipated interactions affecting consumer safety, the product is deemed safe for consumers at the proposed 1,000 mg/kg feed inclusion rate for pigs for fattening, without requiring a withdrawal period.
Diarr‐Stop S Plus® was tested for skin irritation using a reconstructed human epidermis model (OECD guideline 439). Results indicated potential for skin irritation, leading to the conclusion that it should also be considered an eye irritant. Information regarding the additive's sensitisation properties was not provided.
Due to a lack of inhalable particles (<100 μm), exposure of the lower respiratory system to the additive is unlikely.
The FEEDAP Panel concluded Diarr-Stop S Plus is a skin and eye irritant. Inhalation exposure is unlikely, but dermal sensitization could not be assessed due to lack of data.
The majority of Diarr‐Stop S Plus®'s components are naturally occurring. As these substances are not expected to significantly increase in concentration in the environment due to the additive's use, further environmental assessment is not required according to EFSA guidance. Therefore, the tannin extract, essential oils, and excipients are excluded from detailed environmental risk assessment.
As EDTA is the primary component of Diarr‐Stop S Plus® and is not naturally found, its addition to feed at the recommended level introduces 240 mg of Na2EDTA per kilogram. This prompted an initial environmental risk assessment, which utilized this dosage along with the physicochemical properties detailed in Table 2.
Calculations based on EFSA guidance (2008) for the use of 240 mg Na2EDTA/kg feed for pigs for fattening resulted in a predicted soil concentration (PECsoil) of 4,308.9 μg/kg. This value significantly exceeds the 10 μg/kg threshold of concern. Furthermore, predicted groundwater (PECgw) and surface water (PECsw) concentrations were calculated at 64.9 μg/L and 21.66 μg/L, respectively, both surpassing the 0.1 μg/L threshold. These findings necessitate a phase II environmental risk assessment for the affected compartments.
According to the European Union Risk Assessment Report (EU-RAR, 2004) concerning EDTA, Na2EDTA's intake into the body and discharge into the environment are comparable to H4EDTA and Na4EDTA, suggesting similar toxicological mechanisms. While the fate and behavior of EDTA are expected to be consistent across its different salt forms, their ecotoxicological profiles diverge. Crucially, the applicant has not provided any new data regarding the ecotoxicological effects of Na2EDTA. Without this information, a Predicted No Effect Concentration (PNEC) calculation for Na2EDTA is not possible. Therefore, a comprehensive risk assessment for terrestrial and aquatic environments cannot be finalized due to the lack of appropriate ecotoxicological data for Na2EDTA.
The FEEDAP Panel found that the four efficacy studies submitted for Diarr‐Stop S Plus® were conducted on commercial farms without replication. As these studies did not meet the minimum requirements for demonstrating efficacy, the Panel could not conclude on the additive's effectiveness for pigs for fattening.
The FEEDAP Panel determined no specific post-market monitoring plan is needed beyond existing Feed Hygiene Regulation and Good Manufacturing Practice requirements.

Authorization for Diarr‐Stop S Plus is requested under Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, specifically Article 4(1). It is classified as a zootechnical additive, functioning as a gut flora stabiliser. The application targets its use in feed for pigs intended for fattening.
Diarr-Stop S Plus is a brown powder composed of wheat meal, dextrose/meritose, and glycerol polyethyleneglycol ricinoleate. It contains four active substances: sodium ethylenediaminetetraacetate (Na2EDTA) at 19–29%, tannin/tannic acid from Castanea sativa at 5–15%, thymol from thyme oil at 0.6–0.9%, and carvacrol from origanum oil at 0.5–0.8%.
The applicant proposes using Diarr‐Stop S Plus directly in feedingstuffs at a level of 900 to 1,100 mg/kg of complete feed.
A single-laboratory validated method using high-performance liquid chromatography with ultraviolet detection (HPLC-UV) was submitted by the applicant for determining sodium ethylenediaminetetraacetate (Na2EDTA) in the feed additive. Validation and verification studies reported a relative standard deviation for repeatability (RSDr) between 1.7% and 5.0%. Intermediate precision (RSDip) ranged from 4.4% to 5.0%, with recovery rates (Rrec) between 100% and 106%.
The applicant utilized the ring trial validated ISO 9648 method, employing spectrophotometry, to determine tannin/tannic acid content in the feed additive. A relative standard deviation for repeatability (RSDr) of 6.7% was reported for a tannin/tannic acid content of 5.8%. This precision aligns well with the ISO method's reported performance.
A method for determining thymol and carvacrol in the feed additive was submitted by the applicant. This method, based on gas chromatography with flame ionization detection (GC-FID), was validated and verified in a single laboratory. Performance characteristics reported included a repeatability relative standard deviation (RSDr) between 1.9% and 9.2%, and an intermediate precision relative standard deviation (RSDip) between 7.7% and 12.6%. The method achieved a recovery rate (Rrec) of 93% and a limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.005 mg/kg for both analytes.
The EURL recommends the applicant's validated HPLC-UV and GC-FID methods, along with the ISO method, for quantifying Na2EDTA, thymol, carvacrol, and tannin/tannic acid in the Diarr-Stop S Plus feed additive. This recommendation is based on available experimental evidence.
Experimental determination of Diarr‐Stop S plus in feedingstuffs is not achievable. Consequently, the EURL cannot evaluate or recommend any method for official control of this additive in feed.
No further testing or validation of methods by the consortium of National Reference Laboratories, as per Article 10 of Commission Regulation (EC) No 378/2005, is deemed necessary.
