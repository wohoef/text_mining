MicroRNAs (miRNAs) with altered expression are implicated in numerous human cancers, including oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), through their regulation of target genes. While miR-223 has been observed to be upregulated in gastric and ovarian cancers, its specific role in OSCC remained undetermined. This study revealed that miR-223 is overexpressed in OSCC tissues, as confirmed by qRT-PCR. Subsequent investigations into miR-223's biological mechanisms in OSCC demonstrated its role in promoting cell proliferation and migration, evidenced by MTT and Transwell assays. Concurrently, FBXW7 expression was found to be decreased in OSCC, and its re-expression suppressed OSCC cell proliferation and migration. Importantly, the promotional effects of miR-223 on OSCC cells were reversed by FBXW7 mimic. In conclusion, miR-223 enhances OSCC cell proliferation and migration by downregulating FBXW7, presenting a potential therapeutic avenue for OSCC.

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), a prevalent oral malignancy, accounts for approximately 90% of oral cancers. Despite current treatments, over half of OSCC patients succumb within five years due to complications, highlighting a dismal prognosis. Consequently, in-depth research is crucial to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of OSCC development and identify potential therapeutic strategies.
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are conserved, non-coding small molecules originating from higher eukaryotic genomes. They exert their regulatory function by binding to the 3' untranslatable region (3'UTR) of target genes. Aberrant miRNA expression has been linked to numerous cancers, including oral cancer. For instance, miR-381-3p, found at decreased levels in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), inhibits OSCC cell proliferation. MiR-155 functions as an oncogene in OSCC, influencing cell cycle and apoptosis. Furthermore, miR-373-3p promotes tongue squamous cell carcinoma metastasis by targeting DKK1. While prior research indicated miR-223 is upregulated in oral cancer, its precise biological role in OSCC remained unclear.
FBXW7, initially identified in drosophila as CDC4, is a cell cycle regulatory protein now recognized as a crucial tumor suppressor gene. Its critical role in human cell cycle regulation, growth, and differentiation means that its loss can accelerate cancer cell proliferation and worsen patient prognosis. Previous research has linked FBXW7 deficiency to poor outcomes in colorectal, esophageal, and gastric cancers. More recently, FBXW7 has emerged as a microRNA target in various cancers. For example, miR-223 has been shown to inhibit FBXW7 in acute lymphoblastic leukemia, while miR-27a promotes lung cancer growth by suppressing FBXW7, underscoring FBXW7's tumor-suppressive function in human tumorigenesis. Similarly, miR-23a targets FBXW7 in colorectal cancer. However, the specific regulation of FBXW7 by miR-223 in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) has not been previously investigated.
This study found miR-223 over-expressed in OSCC, promoting proliferation and migration. We also demonstrated that miR-223 directly targets FBXW7 in OSCC regulation.
Fifty paired OSCC tumor and adjacent normal tongue tissue samples were collected from surgically treated patients at Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital. All patients provided informed consent, and samples were stored at -80°C. Patients had not received prior treatment. The study received approval from the Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval no. 2017-18).
Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) cell lines, including SCC-15, SCC-4, and HSC-3, were acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). OECM1 cells were kindly provided by the National Defense Medical Center. CGHNC9 cells and normal keratinocyte cells (CGHNK2) were obtained from Chang Gung Memorial Hospital. Normal keratinocytes were cultured in Keratinocyte Serum Free Medium (K-SFM). The oral cancer cell lines were maintained in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, penicillin, and streptomycin, and incubated at 37°C under a 5% CO2 atmosphere.
OSCC cells were transfected with purchased miR-223 mimic, inhibitor, or their respective controls using Lipofectamine 2000. Following the manufacturer's protocol, cells were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 48 hours.
To assess protein levels, cells were lysed using RIPA buffer supplemented with proteinase inhibitors and PMSF. Protein concentration was then determined using a BCA reagent kit. Equal amounts of total protein (50 μg) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto a PVDF membrane. Following a 2-hour blocking step with 5% skim milk at room temperature, primary antibodies targeting FBXW7, Caspase-8, and GADPH were applied and incubated overnight at 4°C. After three washes with TBST, secondary antibodies were added for a 2-hour incubation at room temperature. Protein signals were detected using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit, with GADPH serving as the loading control.
Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol reagent. Subsequently, cDNA was synthesized using the All-in-OneTM miRNA First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed using a TaqMan PCR kit, with all reactions conducted in triplicate. Specific primers were utilized for miR-223, FBXW7, PCNA, U6, and GAPDH. U6 and GAPDH served as internal controls. The 2ΔTM⁢ΔCt method was employed to determine the expression levels of miR-223 and FBXW7.
Cell proliferation was assessed using the MTT assay. Cells, following various transfections, were plated in 96-well plates and incubated for 0, 24, 36, 48, and 96 hours. Subsequently, MTT reagent was added to each well. After a 4-hour incubation at 37°C, the medium was replaced with DMSO. Following a 10-minute incubation, absorbance was measured at 490 nm to quantify cell viability.
To assess cell migration, a transwell assay was employed. This involved placing a transwell chamber, featuring an 8 μm pore size polycarbonate membrane, within a 24-well plate to create distinct upper and lower compartments. One hundred thousand OSCC cells were introduced into the upper chamber. The lower chamber was filled with RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum, serving as a chemoattractant. Following a 24-hour incubation period at 37°C, cells that had migrated to the lower chamber were stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 30 minutes. Microscopic imaging captured the migrated cells. Importantly, MTT assays confirmed that the cell lines exhibited no significant proliferation within the 24-hour timeframe of the transwell assay.
To investigate FBXW7 as a target of miR-223, a luciferase reporter assay was performed. Wild-type and mutated miR-223 target sites within the FBXW7 3'UTR were cloned into a luciferase vector. OECM1 cells were then transfected with either a control mimic or miR-223 mimic. Following transfection, luciferase activity was measured after 48 hours using a dual-luciferase reporter assay system.
Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS v.19.0, with GraphPad Prism 5.02 for graph presentations. All experiments were performed in triplicate. Data are presented as mean ± SD. Student's t-test or Tukey's post hoc test was used for evaluation, with P < 0.05 indicating statistical significance.
To investigate the role of miR-223 in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), we quantified its expression in OSCC cell lines and paired tumor tissues using real-time PCR. Our findings, illustrated in Figure 1A and B, revealed elevated miR-223 levels in OSCC samples compared to normal controls. Concurrently, we observed a decrease in FBXW7 expression in OSCC cell lines and tissues (Figure 1C and D). A regression analysis of 50 OSCC specimens demonstrated a negative correlation between FBXW7 and miR-223 expression (Figure 1F). Moreover, FBXW7 expression was linked to patient prognosis (Figure 1E). Table 1 further indicated a significant association between high miR-223 expression and advanced clinical stage and larger tumor size, suggesting a correlation between miR-223 and OSCC progression.
In OSCC patients, miR-223 expression was significantly correlated with clinical stage and tumor size, but not age or gender. Statistical analysis utilized the χ2 test, with P<0.05 considered significant.

To investigate the functional role of miR-223 in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), OECM1 and SCC15 cells were transfected with either miR-223 mimics or inhibitors, alongside control molecules. Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) confirmed successful alterations in miR-223 expression levels (Fig. 2A and B). Subsequent MTT assays revealed that the re-expression of miR-223 enhanced cell viability, whereas its inhibition reduced it in both cell lines (Fig. 2C and D). Further analysis by qRT-PCR indicated that miR-223 mimic upregulated PCNA expression, while miR-223 inhibitor downregulated it (Fig. 2E and F). Western blot analysis demonstrated that miR-223 mimic suppressed apoptosis, and miR-223 inhibitor promoted it (Fig. 2G). Collectively, these findings suggest that elevated miR-223 levels promote OSCC cell proliferation and inhibit apoptosis.
The role of miR-223 in promoting OSCC cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis was investigated. Following transfection of OECM1 and SCC15 cells with miR-223 mimic or inhibitor, miR-223 expression levels were confirmed via qRT-PCR (Figures 2A and 2B). MTT assays revealed that miR-223 mimic enhanced cell viability, while miR-223 inhibitor reduced it in both cell lines (Figures 2C and 2D). Furthermore, miR-223 mimic increased PCNA mRNA levels, indicating increased proliferation, whereas miR-223 inhibitor decreased PCNA expression (Figures 2E and 2F). Western blot analysis demonstrated that miR-223 mimic suppressed Caspase-8 protein levels, suggesting reduced apoptosis, while miR-223 inhibitor led to increased Caspase-8 expression (Figure 2G). These findings collectively indicate that elevated miR-223 promotes OSCC cell proliferation and suppresses apoptosis.
Transwell assays revealed that miR-223 promoted OSCC cell migration. Specifically, miR-223 mimic increased migration, while miR-223 inhibition reduced it in OECM1 and SCC15 cells.
The transwell assay demonstrated that miR-223 promotes OSCC cell migration. Specifically, miR-223 mimic enhanced migration in OECM1 and SCC15 cells, while miR-223 inhibitor suppressed it. These findings were consistent across triplicate experiments.
FBXW7 was identified as a potential target of miR-223 in OSCC cells through bioinformatics analysis. Luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-223 directly targets the 3' untranslated region of FBXW7. Specifically, miR-223 mimic significantly reduced FBXW7 expression, while miR-223 inhibitor increased it. This effect was observed at both mRNA and protein levels, as demonstrated by qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses. The experiments were repeated three times to ensure reliability.
The study investigated whether FBXW7 could counteract the pro-proliferative and pro-migratory effects of miR-223 in OSCC cells. Initially, FBXW7 was overexpressed in OECM1 cells, confirming its mRNA expression levels (Fig. 5A). Subsequent MTT assays demonstrated that increased FBXW7 expression inhibited the viability of both OECM1 and SCC15 cells over a 96-hour period (Fig. 5B, C). Similarly, transwell assays showed that FBXW7 overexpression reduced the migratory capacity of these OSCC cell lines (Fig. 5D, E). To assess FBXW7's ability to reverse miR-223's actions, OSCC cells were co-transfected with miR-223 mimic and an FBXW7 vector. The results from MTT assays indicated that FBXW7 overexpression significantly attenuated the promotion of cell proliferation induced by miR-223 mimic in OECM1 and SCC15 cells (Fig. 5F, G). Furthermore, transwell migration assays revealed a decrease in the number of migrating cells in OECM1 and SCC15 cells co-transfected with both miR-223 mimic and FBXW7, compared to cells treated with miR-223 alone (Fig. 5H, I). These findings collectively suggest that FBXW7 can reverse the oncogenic effects of miR-223 on OSCC cell proliferation and migration.
Potential targets for miR-223 were identified using Target Scan Human 7.1, with FBXW7 emerging as a likely candidate (Fig. 4A). To investigate this interaction, the impact of miR-223 mimic and inhibitor on FBXW7 expression in OECM1 cells was assessed via luciferase reporter assays. Transfection with a wild-type FBXW7 3'-UTR demonstrated that miR-223 mimic substantially reduced FBXW7 expression by approximately 71.5%, while miR-223 inhibitor enhanced it. Conversely, a mutant FBXW7 3'-UTR showed no effect from either mimic or inhibitor (Fig. 4B). Further analysis confirmed that miR-223 mimic decreased both FBXW7 mRNA and protein levels, whereas miR-223 inhibitor increased them (Fig. 4C and D).
To elucidate FBXW7's role in OSCC proliferation and migration, we first overexpressed FBXW7, observing significant inhibition of cell proliferation and migration in OECM1 and SCC15 cells (Fig. 5A-D). Subsequently, we investigated OSCC cells co-transfected with an FBXW7 vector and miR-223 mimic. The results demonstrated that FBXW7 overexpression substantially reversed the pro-proliferative effects of miR-223 in both cell lines (Fig. 5E). Furthermore, co-transfection with FBXW7 and miR-223 led to a reduced number of migrating cells in OECM1 and SCC15 compared to cells transfected with miR-223 alone (Fig. 5F).
MicroRNAs are increasingly recognized for their significant roles in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) progression. While prior miRNA microarray studies indicated a downregulation of miR-223 in OSCC tissues compared to normal counterparts, subsequent expression and functional data remained limited. This study sought to elucidate the biological function and underlying mechanisms of miR-223 in OSCC. Our findings revealed an elevated expression of miR-223 in OSCC, contrasting with a downregulation of FBXW7. Moreover, we demonstrated a negative correlation between miR-223 and FBXW7 mRNA levels. Crucially, FBXW7 was found to counteract the pro-proliferative and pro-migratory effects of miR-223 in OSCC cells.
Aberrant expression of miR-223 has been observed across various cancers, indicating its involvement in tumorigenesis and progression. While miR-223 is upregulated in cancers such as gastric, colorectal, non-small cell lung, and pancreatic cancers, it is downregulated in cervical, prostate, nasopharyngeal carcinomas, and acute myeloid leukemia. Consistent with previous findings in oral cancer [7, 8], our research confirms that miR-223 expression is elevated in OSCC cells compared to normal counterparts. This aligns with studies demonstrating miR-223's oncogenic role, such as its promotion of ovarian cancer cell proliferation and invasion by targeting MAFB [29], and its ability to enhance gastric cancer cell proliferation and migration [30]. Based on this evidence, we hypothesized that miR-223 acts as an oncogene in OSCC, influencing cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Our experimental results support this, showing that miR-223 mimic enhanced proliferation and migration, while miR-223 inhibition had the opposite effect in OECM1 and SCC15 cell lines.
To elucidate the role of miR-223 in OSCC, bioinformatics analysis identified FBXW7 as a potential target. This interaction was subsequently confirmed as direct through luciferase reporter assays. While miR-223 expression was elevated in OSCC, FGFR2 levels were reduced. FBXW7 has been established as a tumor suppressor in cancer progression. Previous research has linked FBXW7 to other miRNAs in various cancers; for instance, it's a target of miR-367 in non-small cell lung cancer and miR-92a in osteosarcoma, where its suppression promotes tumor growth. Notably, FBXW7 is downregulated in breast cancer and is targeted by miR-223 to influence proliferation and migration. Similarly, miR-223 regulates testicular germ cell tumor proliferation and apoptosis via FBXW7. These findings collectively highlight FBXW7's tumor-suppressive function and its role as a miRNA target. Our study demonstrated that FBXW7 expression is decreased in OSCC, and its re-expression inhibits OSCC cell proliferation and migration. Furthermore, FBXW7 overexpression counteracted the pro-proliferative and migratory effects of miR-223. In clinical samples, FBXW7 levels were inversely correlated with miR-223 expression, supporting FBXW7's role as a downstream mediator in miR-223-driven OSCC.
In conclusion, miR-223 is upregulated in OSCC and functions as an oncogene. Our findings demonstrate that miR-223 promotes OSCC cell proliferation and migration by targeting FBXW7. This newly identified miR-223/FBXW7 axis offers a deeper understanding of oral cancer mechanisms and presents a potential therapeutic target for OSCC.
