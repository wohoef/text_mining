l-Tryptophan, a feed additive manufactured via fermentation using a genetically modified *Escherichia coli* strain, was previously assessed by the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP Panel). Their initial opinion stated that l-tryptophan produced by *E. coli* CGMCC 7.59 is safe for non-ruminant species when incorporated into feed at suitable levels. However, the Panel advised against administering unprotected l-tryptophan orally to ruminants due to potential toxicity from ruminal bacterial metabolites. Concerns were also raised regarding the safety of l-tryptophan for target animals when administered through drinking water. Furthermore, in the absence of sensitization data, the product was deemed a potential dermal sensitizer, and the presence of endotoxins coupled with its dusting potential posed an inhalation risk to users. Following this, the European Commission requested a new opinion from EFSA on the safety of l-tryptophan produced by an enhanced manufacturing process, considering additional characterization data. The FEEDAP Panel reaffirmed its reservations about using unprotected tryptophan in ruminants and its administration to target species via drinking water. Importantly, improvements in the manufacturing process have reduced endotoxin levels and dustiness, mitigating the inhalation risk for handlers. Nevertheless, the potential for dermal sensitization persists.

New data on the additive's physical properties and manufacturing process were provided.
Previously, the endotoxin activity in three batches of the l-tryptophan product was between 0.36 and 1.12 IU/mg. The applicant reported improvements to the downstream processing to lower the final product's bacterial endotoxin levels. This involved modifying the ceramic membrane filtration, reducing the pore size from 300 kDa to 20 kDa, and implementing an additional purification technique. Subsequent analysis of three new product batches, using the European Pharmacopoeia 2.6.14 method, showed bacterial endotoxin activity ranging from 0.33 to 0.67 IU/mg.
To reduce dustiness, the production process was modified to increase the final product's crystal size. This involved lowering the evaporation temperature from 75°C to 72°C, reducing the evaporation rate from 2.5 to 2.0 m³/h, extending evaporation time from 12 to 15 hours, and prolonging cooling time from 3 to 5 hours.
New data on particle size and dustiness have been provided for the improved final product. Laser diffraction analysis of three batches showed that 97.8% of particles were less than 100 µm, 73.7% were less than 50 µm, and 13.5% were less than 10 µm in diameter. In contrast, the former product had approximately 4% of particles less than 50 µm. The dusting potential of the new product, measured by Stauber–Heubach analysis of three batches, ranged from 0.81 to 1.21 g/m³. This is a significant reduction compared to the dusting potential of the previous product, which ranged from 3.9 to 4.2 g/m³ in January 2015.

Previous opinions detailed endotoxin inhalation effects and exposure limits. Calculations for maximum acceptable exposure levels from feed additives were also provided.
According to EFSA guidance (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b), the probable exposure time for additives in premixtures is estimated at a maximum of 40 periods daily, each lasting 20 seconds, totaling 800 seconds per day. Applying an uncertainty factor of 2, the maximum inhalation exposure duration is 1,600 seconds, or 0.444 hours per day. Assuming a respiration volume of 1.25 m³/h, the total inhalation volume exposed to potentially endotoxin-containing dust is 0.556 m³ per day. To maintain a limit of 900 IU of endotoxin within this volume, the dust generated from the product must not exceed 1,619 IU/m³. The calculation of endotoxin content in dust requires specific measurements.
To assess the potential respiratory risk from endotoxins in the product, two primary measurements are necessary: the product's dusting potential (in g/m³) and the endotoxin activity of the dust itself, measured in IU/g using the Limulus amoebocyte lysate assay. In the absence of specific dust data, the endotoxin content of the product can be utilized. The endotoxin concentration in the dust (c IU/m³) is calculated by multiplying the endotoxin content of the additive (a IU/g) by its dusting potential (b g/m³).
