An application to modify existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance penthiopyrad was submitted by France and Bulgaria, the evaluating Member States (EMS), in accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. The proposed modifications aimed to accommodate intended uses of penthiopyrad, leading to suggested increases in MRLs: from 0.2 to 0.3 mg/kg for barley and oat, and from 2 to 4 mg/kg for apricots and peaches. Following Article 8 of the same regulation, France and Bulgaria prepared evaluation reports, which were then submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA). EFSA reviewed the data and deemed it sufficient to support MRL proposals of 0.3 mg/kg for barley and oat, and 4 mg/kg for apricots and peaches. The availability of adequate analytical enforcement methods was confirmed. Based on its risk assessment, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of penthiopyrad on these crops would not lead to consumer exposure exceeding toxicological reference values, thus presenting no likely risk to consumer health.

An application to modify existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance penthiopyrad was submitted to the European Union. Bulgaria, acting as an evaluating Member State (EMS), received a request from DuPont EOOD concerning apricots and peaches. Concurrently, France, another EMS, received a similar application from DuPont Solutions S.A.S for barley and oat. These applications aimed to increase the MRLs to accommodate intended uses of penthiopyrad. Specifically, proposals were made to raise the MRLs for barley and oat from 0.2 mg/kg to 0.3 mg/kg, and for apricots and peaches from 2 mg/kg to 4 mg/kg. Following Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, both Bulgaria and France prepared evaluation reports. These reports were subsequently submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on September 2, 2015.
EFSA's assessment relies on evaluation reports from the Member States, the draft assessment report (DAR) under Council Directive 91/414/EEC, and its addendum. It also considers the peer review conclusions for penthiopyrad's risk assessment, the findings on confirmatory data for the PAM metabolite, the Commission's review report, the JMPR Evaluation report, and prior EFSA opinions on penthiopyrad.
During the peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC, the toxicological profile of penthiopyrad was evaluated. This assessment yielded an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.1 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.75 mg/kg bw. Experts also determined that the metabolite 753-A-OH exhibited similar toxicity to the parent compound. However, insufficient information was available regarding the toxicity of the PAM metabolite. Consequently, confirmatory data were requested and subsequently assessed by EFSA, leading to the derivation of an ADI of 0.0024 mg/kg bw per day and an ARfD of 0.024 mg/kg bw for PAM.
During the peer review, penthiopyrad's metabolism in primary crops, including fruits, leafy vegetables, cereals, and oilseeds, was examined after foliar application. These studies led to the proposal of penthiopyrad as the residue definition for enforcement. For risk assessment purposes, two distinct residue definitions were suggested: penthiopyrad and 753-A-OH (expressed as penthiopyrad), and the PAM metabolite. EFSA has determined that the metabolism of penthiopyrad in these primary crops is adequately understood for the current applications, and the established residue definitions are appropriate.
Based on supervised residue trials, EFSA finds sufficient data to propose MRLs of 0.3 mg/kg for barley and oat, and 4 mg/kg for apricots and peaches. Analytical methods are adequate for monitoring penthiopyrad residues at a limit of quantification of 0.01 mg/kg.
During the peer review, studies on penthiopyrad's behavior under standard hydrolysis conditions confirmed its stability. Consequently, the residue definitions used for raw agricultural commodities (RAC) remain applicable. Processing factors (PF) for penthiopyrad were also established for apple, tomato, barley, and wheat. Although data on the PAM metabolite's behavior during processing was not available, it was deemed unnecessary for the current applications. This decision was based on the theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) of PAM for the crops in question being less than 10% of the acceptable daily intake (ADI).
During the peer review, the presence of penthiopyrad residues in rotational crops, including leafy vegetables, root crops, and cereals, was examined. Due to a similar metabolic profile to primary crops, the same residue definitions were recommended. However, the data available from the peer review were insufficient to determine the extent of penthiopyrad residue uptake by succeeding crops from the soil. Consequently, EFSA maintains the prior recommendation that Member States should account for the potential presence of residues in subsequent crops when authorizing penthiopyrad uses, as no new studies were submitted for this application.
The assessment considered the potential for penthiopyrad residues to transfer from animal feed to food products of animal origin, given that cereals are used as feed. Investigations into the nature of penthiopyrad residues in livestock were conducted during the peer review. For enforcement purposes, the residue definition was established as penthiopyrad and PAM, both expressed as penthiopyrad. For risk assessment, two distinct residue definitions were established: penthiopyrad and PAM separately. The calculated dietary burden for livestock exceeded the 0.1 mg/kg dry matter (DM) trigger value for penthiopyrad across all relevant animal species. PAM metabolite's dietary burden surpassed this 0.1 mg/kg threshold solely in ruminants. Feeding studies involving dairy cows and laying hens, which examined penthiopyrad and PAM intake, were submitted and reviewed. These studies found no residues above the limit of quantification (LOQ) at the feeding levels closest to the expected exposure. Consequently, EFSA concluded that no modifications to the existing Maximum Residue Levels (MRLs) for animal commodities are necessary.
Using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) revision 2, separate risk assessments were conducted for penthiopyrad and its metabolite PAM. No long-term or acute consumer intake concerns were identified for either substance. The highest chronic intake reached 7% of the ADI for penthiopyrad and 16% for PAM. For acute exposure, the highest calculated risk was 16% of the ARfD for penthiopyrad in peaches and 1.3% for PAM in apricots.
EFSA's assessment indicates that proposed penthiopyrad use on apricots, peaches, barley, and oats will not lead to consumer exposure exceeding toxicological reference values, thus posing no health risk.
EFSA recommends modifying current EU Maximum Residue Levels (MRLs) for penthiopyrad. For apricots and peaches, the MRL would increase from 2 to 4 mg/kg, supported by data from Southern Europe (SEU) trials, which were adjusted by a factor of 0.76. No consumer health risks were identified for these uses. Similarly, for barley and oats, the proposed MRL is raised from 0.2 to 0.3 mg/kg, based on data from both Northern (NEU) and Southern Europe (SEU) and extrapolation from barley data for oats. These proposed changes are deemed safe for consumers, with no need to alter MRLs for animal products. The enforcement residue definition remains penthiopyrad.
Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 governs the establishment of EU pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs). Under Article 6, any party with a legitimate interest or seeking authorization for a plant protection product, as per Council Directive 91/414/EEC (now repealed by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009), must submit an application to a Member State to modify an existing MRL. This process must follow the provisions outlined in Article 7 of the Regulation.
DuPont Solutions (France) S.A.S applied to the evaluating Member State France (EMS-FR) to change penthiopyrad's existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) in barley and oat. Separately, DuPont Bulgaria EOOD submitted an application to the evaluating Member State Bulgaria (EMS-BG) for modifications to penthiopyrad's existing MRLs in apricots and peaches.
The European Commission and EFSA were notified of these applications. Subsequently, the EMS evaluated them according to Article 8 of the Regulation.
The evaluation reports were submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on September 2, 2015.
EFSA registered two applications concerning penthiopyrad: EFSA-Q-2015-00498 for barley and oats, and EFSA-Q-2015-00499 for apricots and peaches. To streamline the process, EFSA consolidated these applications into a single reasoned opinion.
To accommodate intended uses, France proposed increasing penthiopyrad MRLs for barley and oats from 0.2 to 0.3 mg/kg. Bulgaria proposed raising MRLs for apricots and peaches from 2 to 4 mg/kg.
EFSA requested that both Bulgaria and France consider the toxicological data for the PAM metabolite and re-evaluate the application accordingly. Bulgaria was also asked to provide further details on residue trials. Subsequently, EFSA assessed the toxicological data for a penthiopyrad metabolite during the peer review. Following this, Bulgaria and France updated their evaluation reports in September 2016.
EFSA assessed the application and evaluation report as per Article 10 of the Regulation.

Codex Alimentarius has set Codex maximum residue limits (CXLs) for various commodities. For the crops in question, these limits are 0.2 mg/kg for oat and barley, and 4 mg/kg for stone fruits like apricots, peaches, and nectarines.
The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) conducted its assessment by reviewing evaluation reports from Bulgaria and France (2016). These reports were based on the Draft Assessment Report (DAR) and its addendum, prepared under Directive 91/414/EEC (United Kingdom, 2012, 2013). EFSA also considered the peer review conclusions on penthiopyrad's pesticide risk assessment (EFSA, 2013a), the results of confirmatory data on the PAM metabolite (EFSA, 2016), the European Commission's review report (European Commission, 2013), and the Joint Meeting on Pesticide Residues (JMPR) Evaluation report (FAO, 2012). Additionally, prior EFSA opinions on penthiopyrad (EFSA, 2012) were taken into account. This evaluation adhered to the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and Authorisation of Plant Protection Products, as stipulated by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011, and followed current guidance documents for consumer risk assessment of pesticide residues (European Commission, 1996-2011; OECD, 2011).
During the peer review, analytical methods for determining penthiopyrad residues in plant products were evaluated. Method DFG-S19, utilizing LC-MS/MS, was validated for the parent compound in various commodity types, including acidic, high water, and high oil content matrices, achieving a 0.01 mg/kg limit. An independent laboratory validation (ILV) for acidic and high water commodities was also provided.
A single residue method using HPLC-MS/MS was validated to determine penthiopyrad and its PAM metabolite. This method, which includes a hydrolysis step, achieved a validation level of 0.01 mg/kg. It is suitable for matrices with high water, acid, or oil content, as well as dry commodities like those with high starch or protein. The peer review also evaluated this method.
EFSA confirms that sufficiently validated analytical methods are available to monitor the proposed MRLs for these high-water and high-starch commodities.
During the peer review, analytical methods for penthiopyrad residues in animal products were assessed. A validated LC-MS/MS method could determine penthiopyrad and its metabolite PAM in milk, eggs, bovine meat, and liver at 0.01 mg/kg, but not in fat. However, a separate HPLC-MS/MS method is available for quantifying both penthiopyrad and PAM in all animal matrices down to the 0.01 mg/kg LOQ.
Validated analytical methods exist for monitoring penthiopyrad and PAM residues in food of animal origin.

Ten residue trials were submitted by the Bulgarian evaluating Member State (EMS-BG) from Southern Europe (SEU) concerning stone fruits, including five on apricots, four on peaches, and one on nectarines. These trials took place between 2011 and 2012. The application rate used in these trials (2 x 450 g/ha) exceeded the proposed Good Agricultural Practice (GAP) of 2 x 345 g/ha, falling slightly outside the acceptable ±25% deviation. Four of the submitted trials were subsequently declined. Analysis of the fruit pulp samples focused on penthiopyrad and its metabolites, 753-A-OH and PAM, with results reported for the whole fruit. Penthiopyrad residues were found to range from 0.26 to 2.59 mg/kg, while 753-A-OH residues varied between 0.01 and 0.067 mg/kg. PAM was detected in only one sample at 0.011 mg/kg, with all other samples showing no detection or levels below the limit of quantification (LOQ). Given the higher application rates in the residue trials compared to the proposed GAP, a proportionality approach was employed, scaling down the results by a factor of 0.76. Notably, two trials showed higher residue levels at a 7-day pre-harvest interval (PHI) than the proposed 3-day PHI. As no justification for excluding these as outliers was provided, the residue concentrations measured at the 7-day PHI were included in the calculation. This led to the proposed Maximum Residue Level (MRL) of 4 mg/kg for apricots and peaches.
Eighteen residue trials on barley, conducted in Northern and Southern Europe between 2007-2013, were reported by the EMS-FR. Analysis of barley grain and straw for penthiopyrad, 753-A-OH, and PAM revealed penthiopyrad residues in grain from 0.01 to 0.2 mg/kg. The metabolites 753-A-OH and PAM were detected at levels up to 0.029 mg/kg and 0.048 mg/kg, respectively.
Penthiopyrad residues in barley straw reached up to 4.7 mg/kg, with metabolites 753-A-OH and PAM found at levels up to 0.42 and 0.94 mg/kg, respectively.
A Mann-Whitney test (U < 5%) indicated similarity between data from Southern and Northern Europe. Consequently, the data were combined to propose an MRL of 0.3 mg/kg. The applicant also requested extrapolation of this MRL to oats, which is permissible under EU guidance.

Residue stability of penthiopyrad and its metabolites in plants during storage was evaluated. Studies showed that residues remain stable for 18 months when stored at -20°C across all crop matrices. Given that the evaluated samples were stored frozen for up to 10 months, storage stability is confirmed.
The analytical methods employed for sample analysis were deemed sufficiently validated and fit for purpose by the EMS, as reported by Bulgaria and France in 2016.
EFSA proposes MRLs of 4 mg/kg for apricots and peaches (SEU outdoor use) and 0.3 mg/kg for barley and oat (EU outdoor use), deeming the supporting data sufficient.
Supervised residue trials analyzed stone fruits and barley for penthiopyrad and metabolite 753-A-OH, both included in the risk assessment residue definition. Conversion factors (CF) were calculated from these trials, excluding samples with residues near the limit of quantification.
For risk assessment, the EMSs (Bulgaria and France) propose the following overall conversion factors (CFs): 1.02 for stone fruits (apricots and peaches), 1.3 for cereal grains (barley and oat), and 1.2 for cereal straw (barley and oat).
Penthiopyrad's stability under standard processing conditions like pasteurization, boiling, and sterilization was evaluated during the peer review, concluding the parent compound is hydrolytically stable. While experimental data for metabolite 753-A-OH was lacking, its structural similarity to the parent compound suggested comparable behavior during processing. Consequently, the residue definition for processed commodities remains the same as for raw agricultural commodities.
Processing factors for certain apple, tomato, barley, and wheat products were established during the peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC, based on studies assessing penthiopyrad residue levels in processed goods.
Processing studies for the PAM metabolite were requested during the peer review, but none are available. However, because the theoretical maximum daily intake of PAM for the crops in question is below the 10% ADI trigger value, dedicated processing studies are not considered necessary for this application. Future applications will require further investigation into the behavior of the PAM metabolite during processing.
The potential for residues in succeeding crops from penthiopyrad use on primary crops necessitates assessment, as cereals are often part of crop rotations. Laboratory studies indicate moderate to high soil persistence for penthiopyrad, with a DT90 exceeding 1,000 days. Metabolite DM-PCA also showed significant persistence (maximum 558 days). Furthermore, two other metabolites, 753-A-OH and 753-T-DO, had DT90 values above the 100-day threshold, reaching 172 and 158 days respectively.
Field studies showed that penthiopyrad degraded more rapidly, with a maximum DT90 of 169 days. For metabolites PAM, PCA, and DM-PCA, only DT50 values were determined, reaching up to 45, 78, and 476 days, respectively. These values indicated that the DT90 trigger value would be surpassed for all these compounds. Consequently, additional research on rotational crops is necessary.
During the peer review, studies on penthiopyrad residues in rotational crops were evaluated. It was determined that the metabolic pathway in these crops mirrors that of primary crops, making the same residue definitions applicable. Field studies on rotational crops, using a total seasonal application rate of 800 g/ha (1.3 times the intended GAP for barley and oat), revealed that DM-PCA, PCA, and parent penthiopyrad were the primary residues. However, the peer review could not definitively conclude if these studies accurately reflected residue levels after multiple years of penthiopyrad application. Consequently, the potential for DM-PCA accumulation with consecutive annual use remains a possibility.
As no new studies were submitted, EFSA reiterates its prior recommendation. Member States should implement risk mitigation measures during authorization to minimize penthiopyrad residues and its metabolites in rotational crops.

During the peer review, penthiopyrad's metabolism in lactating goats and laying hens was studied. For enforcement, residues are defined as penthiopyrad and PAM combined, expressed as penthiopyrad. For risk assessment, two distinct definitions are used: penthiopyrad and the PAM metabolite.
During the peer review, PAM's metabolism in livestock wasn't studied independently. However, as PAM is a metabolite of penthiopyrad, the existing studies on the parent compound were deemed sufficient to cover its metabolism.
Penthiopyrad, a fat-soluble compound, did not accumulate in fat during metabolism studies. The metabolite PAM has a lower log Po/w value. EFSA determined that penthiopyrad's metabolism in livestock is sufficiently understood for this application.
In the peer review, livestock feeding studies were assessed. Dairy cows received doses of 0.15, 0.48, and 1.65 mg/kg body weight daily for 28 days. These doses, equivalent to 8.4, 24.1, and 74.6 mg/kg dry matter, represented 2N, 7N, and 21N of the maximum dietary burden for cattle.
Laying hens were dosed for 28 days at 0.4, 1.2, and 4 mg/kg body weight. These levels equated to 5.85, 17.54, and 58.46 mg/kg dry matter, representing 27N, 81N, and 269N of the maximum dietary burden.
Penthiopyrad and its metabolites remained below 0.01 mg/kg in all examined animal matrices at feeding levels mirroring the maximum dietary burden. EFSA determined that penthiopyrad residues exceeding the limit of quantification are improbable in animal products, thus no adjustments to current MRLs for animal commodities are required.
Consumer risk was assessed using EFSA's PRIMo model, which incorporates European food consumption data for various population subgroups. This assessment considered two residue definitions for penthiopyrad.
EFSA calculated chronic exposure using STMRs from apricot, peach, and barley residue trials. Accepted Codex Maximum Residue Limits and median values from prior EU legislation were also incorporated into the assessment.
For acute exposure assessment, the focus was solely on the commodities in question. This involved assuming consumption of large portions of food items, as documented in national surveys, which contained residues at the highest observed levels from supervised field trials. A factor to account for uneven residue distribution among consumed items was incorporated into the calculations for peaches and apricots. EFSA recommends modifying current Maximum Residue Levels (MRLs) for penthiopyrad. For apricots and peaches, the MRL would increase from 2 to 4 mg/kg, supported by data from Southern Europe and a scaling factor of 0.76, with no identified consumer health risks. Similarly, MRLs for barley and oats are proposed to rise from 0.2 to 0.3 mg/kg, based on data from Northern and Southern Europe, also without identified consumer health risks or the need for changes in animal commodity MRLs. These proposals are justified by the intended uses and the availability of enforcement methods for penthiopyrad.
This document defines several abbreviations and acronyms relevant to pesticide residue assessment. Key terms include: * **a.s.** (active substance), **ADI** (acceptable daily intake), and **ARfD** (acute reference dose) relate to toxicological safety values. * **MRL** (maximum residue level) is the central regulatory limit being discussed. * **EMS** (evaluating Member State) and **RMS** (rapporteur Member State) denote roles in the evaluation process. * **GAP** (good agricultural practice) and **PHI** (preharvest interval) describe agricultural usage. * **STMR** (supervised trials median residue) and **HR** (highest residue) are used in residue data analysis. * **PRIMo** (Pesticide Residues Intake Model) is a tool for exposure assessment. * **LOQ** (limit of quantification) and **LC–MS/MS** (liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry detector) are analytical terms. * **SEU** (southern Europe) and **NEU** (northern Europe) specify geographical regions. * **DT50** and **DT90** refer to residue dissipation rates in soil.
