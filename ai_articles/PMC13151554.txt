Austria, acting as the evaluating Member State (EMS), submitted an application under Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 to amend the current maximum residue level (MRL) for fluazifop-P in pumpkin seeds. The proposed MRL is 5 mg/kg, intended to align with the planned application of fluazifop-P. Austria prepared an evaluation report, as stipulated by Article 8 of the same regulation, and sent it to the European Commission, which then forwarded it to EFSA. EFSA has determined that the provided data adequately support the proposed MRL of 5 mg/kg for pumpkin seeds. Furthermore, suitable analytical methods are available for monitoring fluazifop-P residues in this commodity. Based on its risk assessment, EFSA concluded that the proposed use of fluazifop-P on pumpkin seeds will not lead to consumer exposure exceeding established toxicological reference values, thus posing no apparent risk to consumer health.

Austria, acting as the evaluating Member State, submitted an application under Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 to amend the current maximum residue level (MRL) for fluazifop-P in pumpkin seeds. This modification aims to align with the intended use of the active substance, for which Austria proposed an MRL of 5 mg/kg. An evaluation report, prepared according to Article 8 of the same regulation, was subsequently sent to the European Commission and forwarded to EFSA on February 5, 2016.
EFSA's assessment relies on the evaluating Member State's report, the draft assessment report and its addenda from Directive 91/414/EEC, the updated Commission review report, and the conclusions from the fluazifop-P risk assessment peer review. Previous EFSA reasoned opinions, including one on the review of existing MRLs under Article 12, were also considered.
During the peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC, fluazifop-P's toxicological profile was evaluated. This assessment provided sufficient data to establish an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.01 mg/kg body weight per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.017 mg/kg body weight.
Studies on fluazifop-P metabolism in various primary crops, including fruits, leafy vegetables, roots, and oilseeds/pulses, following foliar application have been conducted. These studies established the residue definition (RD) for both enforcement and risk assessment purposes as the "sum of all constituent isomers of fluazifop, its esters, and its conjugates, expressed as fluazifop (sum of isomers)." EFSA has determined that this metabolism data is sufficient for the proposed use on pumpkin seeds, and the established RDs are applicable.
Based on supervised residue trials, EFSA proposes a 5 mg/kg MRL for fluazifop-P in pumpkin seeds. Analytical methods are adequate for monitoring residues down to 0.01–0.05 mg/kg.
Processing studies for pumpkin seeds were not submitted, nor were they deemed necessary due to their minimal contribution to consumer intake. However, processing factors established for rapeseed during the peer review are applicable to pumpkin seeds.
Investigations into fluazifop-P residues in rotational crops were conducted during the peer review and the Article 12 MRL review. Available data indicated that the residue definition established for primary crops also applies to rotational crops. Consequently, significant residue levels are not anticipated in subsequent crops if the compound is used in accordance with proposed good agricultural practices (GAP). Residues in animal products were not evaluated, as pumpkin seeds are not used for livestock feed.
Using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) revision 2, the consumer risk assessment found no long-term or acute intake concerns. The highest chronic intake of fluazifop-P reached 42% of the ADI (WHO Cluster E). Residues from the considered crops contributed negligibly to overall consumer exposure, less than 0.1% of the ADI. Maximum acute exposure was calculated at 12% of the ARfD.
EFSA has concluded that the proposed use of fluazifop-P on pumpkin seeds poses no consumer health risk, as exposure levels remain below toxicological reference values. Consequently, EFSA recommends amending the existing Maximum Residue Levels (MRLs). The proposed MRL for pumpkin seeds is 5 mg/kg, replacing the current 10 mg/kg, based on extrapolation from rapeseed trials.
The proposed EU MRL for pumpkin seeds is 5 mg/kg, based on extrapolation from Northern European rapeseed trials. This represents a reduction from the current 10 mg/kg MRL, although a decrease to the limit of quantification (0.01 mg/kg) has been approved but not yet implemented. The proposed MRL is set at the analytical limit of quantification.
European Union rules for setting pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs) are established by Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. This regulation, specifically Article 6, mandates that any entity with a legitimate interest or seeking authorization for a plant protection product must submit an application to a Member State to modify an existing MRL. This process must adhere to the provisions outlined in Article 7 of the same regulation.
An application to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for fluazifop-P in pumpkin seeds was compiled by Austria, the evaluating Member State (EMS). Following notification to the European Commission and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), the application underwent evaluation per Article 8 of the Regulation. Austria submitted its completed evaluation report to both bodies on February 5, 2016.

EFSA's evaluation for the fluazifop-P MRL modification in pumpkin seeds relied on several key documents. These included the evaluation report from the evaluating Member State (Austria, 2016), the draft assessment report (DAR) from France (2007), and its subsequent addenda and reports (France, 2010, 2012). Additionally, EFSA considered the European Commission's revised review report for fluazifop-P (2013), the conclusions from the pesticide risk assessment peer reviews (EFSA, 2010, 2012), and prior EFSA reasoned opinions on the substance (EFSA, 2015a,b). The assessment adhered to the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and Authorisation of Plant Protection Products, as established by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. Furthermore, the evaluation incorporated current guidance documents pertinent to consumer risk assessment for pesticide residues, issued by the European Commission and the OECD.
During peer reviews and an Article 12 MRL review, analytical methods for determining fluazifop-P residues in plant commodities were assessed.
During the MRL review, a validated LC-MS/MS method capable of determining fluazifop-P, its esters, and conjugates was submitted. This method, which includes a hydrolysis step, achieves a limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg in various matrices, including those with high acid, water, or oil content, as well as dry commodities. Furthermore, prior to this, the peer review had documented additional analytical techniques. These included HPLC-MS/MS, HPLC-UV, and GC-MS, which were reported for fluazifop-P determination in both oily and non-oily commodities, with LOQs ranging from 0.01 to 0.05 mg/kg.
EFSA determined that validated analytical methods exist for fluazifop-P in pumpkin seeds, as they are a high-oil commodity.
Since pumpkin seeds are not used for animal feed, residues in food of animal origin were not evaluated in this application.

In the peer review, fluazifop-P's metabolism in primary crops was assessed across fruit, root/tuber, leafy, cereal, and pulse/oilseed groups. Table 3 provides an overview of these metabolism studies.
Following metabolism studies, the residue definition for monitoring and risk assessment was established as the sum of all fluazifop isomers, esters, and conjugates, expressed as fluazifop. This definition, agreed upon during the peer review, differs from the current EU regulation's definition, which specifies fluazifop acid (free and conjugates).
EFSA determined that fluazifop-P metabolism is adequately understood for pumpkin seed use. The residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment, established during peer review and confirmed by the Article 12 MRL review, are deemed applicable.
To support the proposed Good Agricultural Practice (GAP), ten residue trials from Northern Europe (NEU) on rapeseed, conducted in Germany between 1983 and 1998, were provided. While multiple formulations were tested at most sites, only one residue value per location was used for MRL calculations. Three trials were excluded because the application occurred at growth stage BBCH 50, exceeding the proposed GAP limit of BBCH 21. Furthermore, the trials used higher application rates (375 and 750 g/ha) than intended. Consequently, residue levels were adjusted proportionally to the nominal application rate of 250 g/ha.
Based on seven residue trials at 375 g/ha and a scaling factor of 0.67, an MRL of 5 mg/kg was proposed. This MRL was then extrapolated to pumpkin seeds, following the applicant's suggestion and EU guideline 7525/VI/95.

During the peer review, fluazifop-P-butyl residue stability in plant matrices was evaluated. It was found to be stable for 28 months at ≤ -18°C in high water content samples, and for 18 months in high oil content and dry commodities. Since trial samples were stored for no more than 17 months under validated conditions, the residue data is considered valid concerning storage stability.
The EMS confirmed that analytical methods for residue trials were validated and suitable for their intended purpose.
EFSA deems data adequate to propose a 5 mg/kg MRL for pumpkin seeds in Northern Europe, extrapolating from rapeseed trials.
Standard hydrolysis studies simulating the effects of pasteurisation, boiling, and sterilisation on fluazifop‐P residues were not provided. However, during the Article 12 MRL review, it was concluded that the active substance is stable under hydrolysis conditions. This conclusion was based on the observation that, even under the severe hydrolytic conditions of analytical methods, fluazifop conjugates and esters do not hydrolyse beyond the stable fluazifop moiety. Consequently, the submission of standard hydrolysis studies was deemed unnecessary.
Processing studies for oilseed rape, including cake and oil, were evaluated during the peer review, yielding processing factors (PFs) applicable to pumpkin seeds. No new studies were submitted for this application, and they were deemed unnecessary.
The potential for fluazifop-P residues to affect subsequent crops grown in rotation with pumpkins was examined. Previous peer review studies revealed that fluazifop-P degrades significantly in soil, with compound X, a primary soil metabolite, becoming the dominant residue in rotational crops due to soil uptake. Since compound X was deemed not to pose a toxicological concern, the residue definition for rotational crops was established to align with that of primary crops.
To assess the impact of fluazifop-P residues on subsequent crops, rotational crop field trials were conducted. These trials involved sowing wheat, lettuce, and carrot in rotation with oilseed rape treated at 375 g/ha, or on bare soils treated at 375–475 g/ha of fluazifop-P-butyl. Plant-back intervals of 1, 2, 4, and 6 months were observed. Residue levels of total fluazifop and total compound X (both free and conjugated) remained below the limit of quantification (LOQ) in all tested crops. An exception was observed in carrot foliage, where compound X reached levels of 0.03–0.13 mg/kg. Given that the annual application rate for pumpkin seeds is capped at 250 g/ha, EFSA anticipates no significant residues in rotational crops, assuming adherence to proposed good agricultural practices (GAPs).
As pumpkin seeds are not used for livestock feed, fluazifop-P residues in animals were not evaluated for this application.
EFSA's PRIMo model, version 2, was used for consumer risk assessment, incorporating European food consumption data for various population subgroups.
A thorough long-term exposure assessment for fluazifop-P was conducted as part of the Article 12 MRL review, considering current EU usage patterns. Food items with no reported uses during this review were excluded from the exposure calculations, based on the assumption of no fluazifop-P application on those crops. Following the identification of some acute risk exceedances, risk mitigation measures were advised (scenario 2). EFSA then utilized the risk assessment from scenario 2 to update the consumer risk assessment, incorporating median residue levels (STMR) for pumpkin seeds.
To assess acute consumer exposure to fluazifop-P in pumpkin seeds, a scenario was modeled using national food consumption data. This involved assuming consumption of a large portion of pumpkin seeds containing the highest observed residue levels from field trials. A variability factor was incorporated to account for uneven residue distribution across individual food items. The specific input values utilized for this dietary exposure calculation are detailed in Table 5.
Toxicological reference values for fluazifop-P were compared against the estimated exposure. Intake calculation results from the EFSA PRIMo, a key supporting document, are publicly available as background material for this reasoned opinion.
Long-term consumer intake concerns were not identified for any European diet within the EFSA PRIMo, assuming proposed MRL amendments from the Article 12 review. The highest chronic intake reached 42% of the ADI (WHO cluster E). Pumpkin seed residues contributed insignificantly, less than 0.1% of the ADI for a German child.
No acute consumer risk was identified for pumpkin seeds. The highest calculated acute exposure was 12% of the ARfD.
EFSA has concluded that the proposed use of fluazifop-P on pumpkin seeds poses no consumer health risk, as exposure levels are unlikely to exceed toxicological reference values.
