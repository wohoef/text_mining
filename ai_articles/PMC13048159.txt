For infertile women diagnosed with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR), both clomiphene citrate (CC) and progestin-based ovarian stimulation protocols are utilized. However, the comparative efficacy of these approaches has not been definitively established. This study sought to evaluate the outcomes of progestin-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS) against CC-primed ovarian stimulation (CPOS) in this patient population. A retrospective, before-and-after self-controlled design was employed, analyzing data from 50 infertile women with DOR. These participants had previously undergone a failed in vitro fertilization/intracytoplasmic sperm injection-frozen embryo transfer cycle using CPOS, subsequently switching to PPOS at the Reproductive Medicine Center of Changzhou Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital. The findings indicated that the PPOS protocol effectively suppressed the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge. Furthermore, PPOS demonstrated superior results for DOR patients, leading to a greater number of retrieved oocytes, mature MII oocytes, normally fertilized oocytes, cleaved embryos, and high-grade embryos. The protocol also resulted in more cryopreserved embryos, a higher pregnancy rate, and an increased live-birth rate, alongside a reduction in miscarriage rates. In conclusion, this research suggests that the PPOS protocol is more effective than CPOS for patients with DOR. It not only controls the LH surge but also enhances both the quantity and, notably, the quality of oocytes.

Controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) enables the simultaneous growth and maturation of multiple oocytes, a crucial step for in vitro fertilization (IVF) and embryo transfer. Over decades of research and clinical application, various COH protocols have emerged, broadly categorized into conventional pituitary downregulation and other methods. Women undergoing IVF can exhibit poor, normal, or high ovarian responsiveness to gonadotropin (Gn). Patients with poor ovarian responses (PORs) face the lowest likelihood of pregnancy through IVF. Diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) is a primary contributor to poor response. DOR is identified by elevated follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), reduced estrogen production, low anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), and a low antral follicle count (AFC), which can be influenced by age or occur independently. Consequently, there is a global effort among clinicians and researchers to enhance both the quantity and quality of oocytes retrieved, especially for individuals with DOR.
The success of in vitro fertilization (IVF) treatment is significantly influenced by the controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) protocol used, particularly for patients with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR). While the conventional pituitary downregulation protocol is standard and effective for many, it proves inefficient and costly for DOR patients due to its lengthy duration, higher gonadotropin (Gn) requirements, increased risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS), and reduced overall efficacy. In response, clomiphene citrate (CC)-primed ovarian stimulation (CPOS) has emerged. Developed by Fauser et al., CC works by inhibiting estrogen's negative feedback, leading to increased follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH) release from the hypothalamus. CPOS offers advantages such as a shorter treatment period, reduced Gn usage, lower costs, and improved patient comfort, making it a patient-friendly option. Studies have indicated that CPOS can achieve pregnancy outcomes comparable to conventional COH protocols in patients with poor ovarian response (POR). Consequently, CPOS has become a popular choice for POR patients, despite potential risks like early LH surges and the cessation of menstrual cycles.
A novel controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) protocol, known as progestin-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS), was recently introduced by Kuang and colleagues. This protocol utilizes progestin to prevent the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge triggered by estrogen, thereby avoiding the risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS). PPOS has demonstrated success across a range of ovarian conditions, including normal ovarian reserve and polycystic ovary syndrome. Previous research by Kuang et al. indicated that PPOS could prevent premature ovulation in patients with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) without compromising oocyte quality, using natural cycles as a comparison. Despite these findings, the optimal protocol for DOR patients between CPOS and PPOS remained undetermined. Therefore, this study retrospectively examined data from DOR patients who experienced suboptimal stimulation with CPOS and subsequently transitioned to the PPOS protocol. A comparative analysis of clinical outcomes, encompassing hormone levels, oocyte retrieval, embryo fertilization and development, and overall clinical results, was conducted between the two protocols within the same patient cohort.
Between January 2016 and June 2017, a retrospective study was performed at the Changzhou Maternal and Child Health Care Hospital. Fifty women undergoing IVF/ICSI cycles for infertility were included. The study adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki for medical research and received approval from the hospital's Ethics Committee. All participants gave informed consent following counseling on infertility treatments and standard IVF procedures. Diminished ovarian reserve (DOR) was diagnosed in patients meeting at least two of the following criteria: FSH levels between 10-25 IU/L, an FSH/LH ratio of 2 or higher, an antral follicle count (AFC) of 5 or less, or an AMH level between 0.5-1.1. Participants were required to have a DOR diagnosis and have previously failed to conceive with the CPOS protocol, leading to a switch to PPOS. The study then compared clinical outcomes between these two treatment protocols within each patient.
In the CPOS protocol, clomiphene citrate (CC) at 50 mg daily, along with gonadotropins (Gn) at 150-225 IU, was administered starting from menstrual cycle day 3 until the day of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) administration. The initial Gn dosage was determined by factors such as patient age, baseline FSH levels, antral follicle count (AFC), and prior stimulation plans. Follicular development was monitored through regular ultrasound examinations and serum hormone tests, with Gn dosage adjusted accordingly. Ovulation was triggered with triptorelin (0.1 mg) and hCG (4000 IU) once a dominant follicle reached a diameter greater than 18 mm. Oocyte retrieval took place 36 to 38 hours after this trigger.
The PPOS protocol involved administering Medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) and gonadotropin (Gn) from menstrual cycle day 3. The rest of the procedure mirrored the CPOS protocol.
Following oocyte retrieval, all follicles exceeding 10 mm in diameter were collected. Standard insemination or ICSI was performed within six hours, contingent on semen parameters. Embryos were assessed on day three for cell number, homogeneity, and fragmentation, adhering to Cummins et al.'s specifications. Grade I-III embryos were cryopreserved using vitrification on day three. Any remaining embryos underwent extended culture, with only blastocysts exhibiting good morphology being frozen on days five or six. The cryopreservation process has been detailed elsewhere. All participants in this study received frozen embryo transfers. Endometrial preparation involved hormone replacement therapy, commencing with ethinyl estrogen (25 μg tid) for 14 days. This was followed by a transition to oral progesterone (8 mg estradiol and 40 mg dydrogesterone) and vaginal progesterone capsules (200 mg bid). Post-conception, exogenous estrogen and progesterone supplementation continued until 10 weeks of gestation.
Hormone levels (FSH, LH, E2, and progesterone) were measured using chemiluminescence. Blood samples were drawn on menstrual cycle day 3, the day before ovulation trigger, and on the day of the trigger. Sensitivity limits were: FSH 0.06 mIU/mL, LH 0.09 mIU/mL, E2 10 pg/mL, and P 0.1 ng/mL.
Statistical analysis of acquired data was performed using SPSS version 20.0. Constituent ratio data were compared using the χ2 test. Normally distributed data were analyzed with the paired student’s t test, while the Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test was applied to data that did not meet normality criteria. A p-value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.
Patients in the study had an average infertility duration of 4.22 years and an average interval of 117.9 days between their unsuccessful CPOS cycle and the PPOS initiation. The average patient age was 36.48 years, with a BMI of 22.85. Baseline hormone levels included FSH of 9.60 mIU/mL, LH of 4.92 mIU/mL, E2 of 26.78 mIU/mL, and an AFC of 3.88.
While Gn duration was comparable, the total Gn dose was lower in CPOS compared to PPOS. On the day of ovulation trigger, LH levels were significantly higher in CPOS. No significant differences were observed in FSH, E2, or P levels between the two treatment groups at this time.
PPOS treatment resulted in a significantly higher number of dominant follicles, retrieved oocytes, MII mature oocytes, normally fertilized oocytes, cleaved embryos, and high-grade embryos compared to CPOS. Furthermore, PPOS significantly reduced miscarriage rates. While cancellation, implantation, and clinical pregnancy rates showed no significant differences between the protocols, nine live-births were achieved with PPOS, whereas none occurred with CPOS.
This retrospective study compared the clinical outcomes of two controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (COH) protocols, clomiphene-primed ovarian stimulation (CPOS) and progestin-primed ovarian stimulation (PPOS), in patients with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR). The findings indicated that PPOS effectively suppressed the luteinizing hormone (LH) surge and led to improved results. Specifically, the PPOS protocol resulted in a greater number of dominant follicles, retrieved oocytes, MII mature oocytes, normally fertilized oocytes, cleaved embryos, and high-grade embryos. Furthermore, PPOS was associated with a higher rate of live births and a reduced miscarriage rate. These outcomes align with a prior study that highlighted PPOS's advantage in enhancing oocyte utilization and the production of high-quality embryos in older infertile women when compared to the classic long protocol.
A prior study on PPOS in patients with low gonadotropin responsiveness reported increased numbers of dominant follicles, retrieved oocytes, mature oocytes, normally fertilized oocytes, and viable embryos compared to natural cycles. This earlier research also noted a lower miscarriage rate, though clinical pregnancy, implantation, live-birth, and miscarriage rates did not reach statistical significance. In our current study, while clinical pregnancy and implantation rates were not significantly different between protocols, we observed significant improvements in miscarriage and live-birth rates with PPOS. This suggests that the enhanced oocyte quality resulting from PPOS contributes to successful live births in patients with diminished ovarian reserve. The self-controlled design of our study, with its reduced variables, allows for a more conclusive interpretation of findings from a limited sample size. Consequently, our results underscore PPOS's crucial role in improving folliculogenesis and oocyte quality.
Kuang et al. have proposed mechanisms for MPA's inhibition of the early LH surge. Clinical studies indicate that progestin administration can significantly suppress LH surges when estrogen levels are low. The progestin receptor (PR) is recognized as vital for the estrogen-induced LH surge. Estrogen promotes PR expression in the hypothalamus, while progestin reduces its own receptor levels. Consequently, elevated progestin without sufficient estrogen leads to a low PR state, disrupting the positive feedback loop of estrogen and resulting in reduced LH release.
The precise mechanisms by which medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) enhances oocyte quantity and quality are not yet fully understood. One hypothesis, proposed by Kuang et al., suggests that the use of human menopausal gonadotropin (hMG) during the later stages of MPA treatment might lead to an increased yield of retrieved oocytes and embryos. While hMG was administered in both the PPOS and CPOS protocols in this study, a higher dose was used with PPOS. Despite this, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) levels on the day of ovulation triggering remained comparable between the two treatments. Previous research indicates that increased gonadotropin (Gn) use offers no significant advantage for patients with diminished ovarian reserve (DOR), particularly those with a low antral follicle count (AFC). Therefore, it is unlikely that the observed improvements in oocyte retrieval and quality in the PPOS group are solely due to higher Gn administration.
Luteinizing hormone (LH) is essential for androgen synthesis and secretion, which are precursors to estrogen (E2) production. Research indicates that androgen levels influence folliculogenesis, with low levels promoting it and high levels inhibiting it. Therefore, maintaining LH levels within an optimal range is crucial for successful IVF/ICSI outcomes, as both excessively low and high LH can be detrimental. In this study, neither treatment protocol resulted in a premature LH surge. Furthermore, E2 levels at ovulation were comparable between the two protocols, suggesting that the elevated LH observed with the CPOS protocol did not lead to increased E2 synthesis. Interestingly, a recent study found that clomiphene citrate (CC) increased LH levels during PPOS treatment without impacting IVF/ICSI results, implying that the benefits of PPOS on oocytes may not be directly linked to LH levels. Another recent publication highlighted differences in follicular fluid lipids between PPOS and a short protocol, which could be associated with improved IVF/ICSI outcomes. Further research is required to elucidate the precise mechanisms by which medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) enhances folliculogenesis.
This self-controlled study concluded that PPOS effectively suppresses LH surges and enhances oocyte quantity and quality in DOR patients via improved folliculogenesis, making it an ideal stimulation protocol for this group.
