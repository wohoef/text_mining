Transforming acidic coiled-coil protein 2 (TACC2), a member of the TACC protein family, plays a key role in stabilizing spindles and regulating microtubule dynamics by interacting with centrosome and microtubule-associated molecules. While TACC2 has been implicated in the tumorigenesis of various cancers, its clinical significance in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains largely unknown. This study aimed to investigate TACC2 expression in HCC and assess its clinical significance and prognostic relevance. To achieve this, we examined TACC2 mRNA and protein expression in paired HCC and adjacent non-cancerous tissues using quantitative PCR (qPCR) and western blot. Additionally, immunohistochemistry was performed on 106 postoperative HCC samples. Our findings revealed elevated expression of TACC2 protein and mRNA in HCC tissues. Immunohistochemical analysis further indicated that high TACC2 expression in HCC tissue was significantly associated with capsular extension, tumor recurrence, and reduced overall and disease-free survival. Cox regression analysis identified high TACC2 expression as an independent prognostic factor for HCC patients. Consequently, TACC2 emerges as a potential candidate biomarker for predicting HCC recurrence and prognosis.

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the fifth most prevalent cancer globally and the third leading cause of cancer-related death. Despite advancements in diagnosis and treatment, HCC incidence has been rising. Even with curative intent surgery, patient outcomes are inconsistent, with a high rate of recurrence. This aggressive disease often presents at advanced stages, with only 30–40% of patients diagnosed early enough for curative therapy, while the remainder receive palliative care. Consequently, the long-term prognosis for patients undergoing potentially curative hepatic resection remains poor, with 5-year survival rates between 17% and 53%. This underscores a critical need for novel biomarkers to facilitate early diagnosis and predict recurrence in HCC.

Transforming acidic coiled-coil (TACC) proteins are recognized for their role as centrosome and microtubule binding proteins, crucial for spindle stabilization and regulating microtubule dynamics during cell division. The mammalian TACC family, including TACC1, TACC2, and TACC3, localizes to the centrosome and interacts with microtubules, influencing cell growth and differentiation. However, abnormal TACC protein expression can disrupt gene regulation via interactions with chromatin remodeling complexes, potentially contributing to tumorigenesis. Research has indicated TACC2 overexpression in various cancers. For instance, it promotes androgen-mediated growth in prostate cancer and is linked to breast cancer cell proliferation and poorer prognoses. High TACC2 levels have also been associated with reduced event-free survival in infant acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Conversely, TACC2 has been identified as a potential tumor suppressor in breast cancer, leading to ongoing debate about its precise role and expression patterns across different cancer types.
Previous research on TACC2 in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is limited. To address this, we examined HCC data from the Oncomine database, identifying TACC2 as a candidate gene for a novel prognostic marker. This study's objective is to elucidate TACC2's role and expression patterns in HCC and assess its clinical significance and prognostic value.
A cohort of 106 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients who underwent surgical resection between 2006 and 2007 at Sun Yat-sen Cancer Center in Guangzhou, China, formed the basis of this study. All participants provided written informed consent, and the study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Sun Yat-sen University. Pathologists verified both the cancer and adjacent non-cancerous specimens, ensuring the latter were free of malignant cells. Patients did not receive neoadjuvant therapy, though some received adjuvant treatments like transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) or radiofrequency ablation (RFA). Individuals with metastatic disease or unclear resection margins were excluded. Comprehensive clinicopathological data, including age, sex, tumor characteristics (size, location, number), cirrhosis status, lymph node status, pathological differentiation, TNM staging, and capsulation, were meticulously recorded from patient medical records and are detailed in Table 1. Patient follow-up occurred every three months postoperatively for the initial year, then every six months thereafter, extending over an eight-year period for the cohort.
To assess TACC2 protein levels, western blot analysis was conducted on eight pairs of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) tissues and their corresponding adjacent non-cancerous tissues. Protein quantification was performed using a BCA protein assay kit. Subsequently, 40 μg of tissue protein lysates were prepared with loading buffer and heated to 95°C for 10 minutes. These lysates underwent electrophoresis on a 9% SDS-polyacrylamide gel and were then transferred to a PVDF membrane. Following a 1-hour blocking step with 5% skim milk at room temperature, primary antibodies against TACC2 (1:1,000 dilution) and β-actin (1:3,000 dilution), both from Proteintech Group, were applied to determine their respective expression levels.
To quantify TACC2 mRNA levels, RNA was isolated from frozen tissue samples using TRIZOL reagent. Following the manufacturer's protocol, complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized. Quantitative real-time PCR was then employed to amplify TACC2 and the internal control gene, β-actin, using specific primers. The relative expression of TACC2 was determined by normalizing it to β-actin, resulting in a 2-ΔCt value. The PCR amplification involved an initial 5-minute step at 95°C, followed by 40 cycles of 15 seconds at 95°C and 30 seconds at 60°C. All reactions were performed in triplicate.
To assess TACC2 expression, immunohistochemical (IHC) staining was performed on 106 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) specimens. Initially, paraffin-embedded HCC tissues were sectioned to 4-μm thickness and subjected to deparaffinization using xylene, followed by rehydration with ethanol and distilled water. Endogenous peroxidase activity was then quenched by immersing the sections in 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 minutes at room temperature. Antigen retrieval was achieved by boiling the sections in Citrate Antigen Retrieval Solution (pH 6.5) for 4 minutes in an electric pressure cooker. Subsequently, the sections were incubated overnight at 4°C in a moist chamber with a primary antibody against TACC2 (diluted 1:800; Proteintech). The following day, a secondary antibody was applied at room temperature for 30 minutes. Staining was completed using 3,3'-diaminobenzidine (DAB) for 10 minutes, followed by counterstaining with 10% Mayer's hematoxylin. After dehydration and mounting, the stained sections were evaluated independently by two pathologists. A negative control was included, where the primary antibody was substituted with normal rabbit serum.
The cutoff value for TACC2 expression was determined by ROC analysis (AUC=0.772, threshold=5).
TACC2 expression levels were categorized by staining intensity as 0 (none), 1+ (weak), 2+ (moderate), or 3+ (strong). To establish a definitive threshold for TACC2 expression, a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed. This analysis yielded a statistically significant cutoff score (AUC = 0.772, P< 0.001, 95% CI 0.638–0.832), representing the optimal balance between sensitivity and specificity. Each tumor's final IHC score was an average of repeated measurements. Tumors scoring above this established cutoff were classified as having high TACC2 expression, while those scoring at or below the cutoff were designated as having low TACC2 expression.
Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS v-20 and Prism software. The chi-square test was employed to assess the relationship between TACC2 expression and clinicopathological variables, with a p-value less than 0.05 considered significant. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the duration from surgery until death, while disease-free survival (DFS) was measured from surgery to cancer progression or metastasis. Kaplan-Meier analysis, with log-rank testing for comparison, was used to calculate cumulative survival times. Additionally, Cox proportional hazard regression analysis was utilized to evaluate the impact of various clinicopathological features and TACC2 expression on patient survival.

In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) samples, TACC2 immunoreactivity was predominantly observed in the cytoplasm of tumor cells. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) revealed high TACC2 expression in cancer cells, contrasting with negative or weak expression in adjacent normal cells from the same patient specimens. The intensity of TACC2 staining varied from negative to strong, as illustrated in Figure 5. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis established a cutoff score, categorizing patients into low (IHC score ≤ 5) and high (IHC score > 5) TACC2 expression groups. A significant association between TACC2 expression levels and survival status was identified, with 47 out of 106 HCC cases exhibiting high TACC2 expression.
TACC2 expression was notably higher in advanced clinical stages (III/IV) compared to earlier stages (I/II), with statistically significant findings (p=0.042). Specifically, TACC2 was expressed in 61.5% of stage III/IV patients versus 38.8% in stages I/II. A strong correlation was also observed between elevated TACC2 levels and disease recurrence (56.9% vs. 17.6%, p<0.001). Furthermore, TACC2 expression was more prevalent in cases with incomplete or no capsule (53.8% and 51.4%, respectively) compared to complete encapsulation (23.3%, p<0.023). Patients who had died from HCC also showed higher TACC2 expression (62.1%) than those who were alive (15%, p<0.001). However, no significant correlation was found between TACC2 expression and age, gender, T status, tumor size, cirrhosis, or pathological grading. While not statistically significant, a trend towards higher TACC2 expression was observed in larger tumors, higher-grade tumors (G3/G4), and multiple tumors.
At the study's conclusion, 106 patients had been followed for a median of 62.4 months (range: 2-125 months), with 66 cancer-related deaths recorded. Analysis using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and Cox proportional hazards models revealed that patients with high TACC2 expression experienced significantly shorter overall survival (OS) (41.3 months vs. 89.2 months, P < 0.001) and disease-free survival (DFS) (27.4 months vs. 71.9 months, P < 0.001) compared to those with low TACC2 expression. These findings indicate that high TACC2 expression is associated with a poorer prognosis in HCC patients.
Cox regression analysis confirmed the prognostic significance of TACC2 expression. Initially, a univariate analysis assessed the impact of various clinical characteristics—such as age, gender, histological grade, T status, cirrhosis, AFP, tumor size, location, staging, and extracapsular extension, alongside TACC2 expression—on overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) in HCC patients. This revealed that pathological differentiation, T status, staging, tumor nodules, capsule status, tumor location, and TACC2 expression significantly influenced OS. Consequently, these factors were subjected to multivariate analysis. The results identified tumor location and TACC2 expression as independent prognostic factors for OS. For DFS, univariate analysis indicated an impact from pathological differentiation, T status, staging, tumor nodules, and tumor location, in addition to TACC2 expression. In the subsequent multivariate analysis for DFS, tumor nodules and TACC2 emerged as independent prognostic factors.
In a univariate analysis of overall survival for hepatocellular carcinoma patients, several factors showed statistical significance. These included differentiation, tumor status, clinical stage, tumor nodules, capsule status, tumor location, and TACC2 expression. However, upon multivariate analysis, only tumor location and TACC2 expression remained as independent prognostic factors. Specifically, TACC2 expression, when high (score > 5), was associated with a significantly increased risk of mortality (HR = 3.075, p = 0.001) compared to low expression.
Table 3 presents the results of univariate and multivariate COX regression analyses for disease-free survival in hepatocellular carcinoma patients. In the univariate analysis, several factors including differentiation, tumor status, clinical stage, tumor nodules, tumor location, and TACC2 expression were found to be significant. However, upon multivariate analysis, tumor nodules (HR 1.960, p=0.030), tumor location (HR 0.566, p=0.020), and TACC2 expression (HR 2.796, p=0.001) emerged as independent prognostic factors for disease-free survival.
This study is the first to establish a link between TACC2 expression, clinicopathological features, and survival outcomes in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients. We observed elevated TACC2 protein and mRNA levels in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues, with immunohistochemistry confirming higher cytoplasmic TACC2 expression in HCC cells. Crucially, increased TACC2 protein levels were significantly associated with tumor recurrence and reduced overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). These findings suggest TACC2 plays a role in HCC progression and holds potential as an independent prognostic indicator for patient survival.
Current treatment options for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), including surgery, ablation, embolization, transplantation, and chemotherapy, have not significantly improved patient outcomes, with high recurrence rates and poor prognoses. While α-Fetoprotein (AFP) is the sole serum biomarker for HCC diagnosis, its low sensitivity, especially in early stages, necessitates the development of new tools. There is an urgent need for standardized methods to assess molecular biomarkers and validate their potential for early disease detection, prediction of progression, and anticipation of recurrence.
TACC family members exhibit limited overlap in their functions. Unlike TACC3, TACC2 is predominantly found in post-mitotic tissues such as the brain, heart, muscle, and kidney. Detecting TACC2 protein has proven difficult, unlike TACC3. This could be due to significantly lower TACC2 RNA levels compared to TACC3, or potentially greater TACC2 protein instability and alternative low-level expression in vivo.
The TACC protein family plays a role in regulating centrosome and microtubule interactions, potentially linking TACC genes to cancer development. Aberrant TACC levels at the centrosome can disrupt crucial cellular processes like pronuclear fusion, nuclear migration, and chromosome segregation. While TACC1 and TACC3 have been shown to act as oncogenes in various cancers, with TACC1 implicated in mammary and prostate tumorigenesis, and TACC3 upregulated across numerous cancer types, the role of TACC2 is more complex. Previous research has presented TACC2 as both an oncogene and a tumor suppressor depending on the cancer type. However, this study's findings, indicating TACC2 overexpression at both protein and mRNA levels, align with an oncogenic role. This overexpression was associated with disease recurrence and a poorer prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma.
The role of TACC2 in cancer progression and development has been investigated in several studies, often linked to its function in mitotic spindle formation during mitosis. However, findings regarding TACC2's oncogenic or tumor-suppressive capacity have varied across different cancer types. For instance, Chen et al. identified TACC2 as a tumor suppressor protein, AZU-1, which was found to be downregulated in breast cancer cell lines and primary tumors. In their research, normal levels of TACC2/AZU-1 protein were associated with reduced malignancy. Conversely, other studies have reported TACC2 overexpression. Shan Cheng et al. observed TACC2 overexpression in breast cancer patients, correlating with a poorer prognosis. Similarly, research by Yoshiaki Onodera et al. highlighted TACC2's crucial role in breast cancer cell proliferation, with TACC2 status significantly linked to recurrence and worse prognosis. Kenichi Takayama et al. also found TACC2 overexpression in prostate cancer, suggesting it promotes tumor progression by regulating the cell cycle during mitosis, and high TACC2 immunoreactivity was associated with a poor prognosis. While Schuendeln et al. demonstrated that TACC2 is not essential for normal development and its deficiency does not lead to cancer in animal models (unlike TACC3 deficiency, which caused embryonic lethality), the current study's findings of TACC2 overexpression correlating with poor prognosis in HCC align with the latter group of studies, but contrast with the findings of Chen et al.
Our immunohistochemical analysis revealed high TACC2 expression in 44% of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) samples, with negligible levels observed in adjacent normal tissue. Further correlation analysis demonstrated a significant association between TACC2 expression and several clinicopathological parameters, including clinical stage (p=0.042), capsulation (p=0.023), recurrence (p<0.001), and overall survival (p<0.001). These findings align with established knowledge that clinical stage, capsulation, and recurrent disease are key determinants of patient outcomes. Specifically, higher TACC2 levels were noted in patients who succumbed to HCC compared to those who survived, indicating a link between elevated TACC2 and disease recurrence and diminished survival. Subsequent Cox multivariate analysis identified increased TACC2 expression as an independent prognostic factor for both disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS), correlating with tumor location and tumor nodules. These multivariate analyses underscore the significance of TACC2 status in predicting patient prognosis. However, further research is warranted to elucidate the precise mechanisms by which TACC2 contributes to HCC development and to explore its potential as a therapeutic target.
High TACC2 expression in HCC patients serves as a warning sign, suggesting a need for prioritized management and aggressive adjuvant therapy to mitigate recurrence and enhance survival. Our findings collectively indicate that TACC2 is a predictive factor for HCC patient survival and may function as a novel marker for predicting recurrence and prognosis.
