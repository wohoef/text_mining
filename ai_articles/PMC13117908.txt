This study investigated irisin's role in combating obesity and type 2 diabetes. Irisin, a hormone released by skeletal muscles, promotes the conversion of white adipose tissue (WAT) into a "brown-like" state, a process known as "browning." This browning is linked to reduced body fat accumulation and weight loss in mammals. Our research in mice revealed that recombinant irisin lowered body weight and enhanced glucose regulation. We found that irisin increased the expression of uncoupling protein-1 (UCP-1), a key regulator of brown fat's heat-generating capacity. This UCP-1 upregulation appears to be mediated by irisin's activation of the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) and extracellular signal–related kinase (ERK) signaling pathways, as inhibiting these pathways abolished irisin's effect on UCP-1. Furthermore, irisin stimulated the production of betatrophin, a hormone known to boost pancreatic β-cell proliferation and improve glucose tolerance. These findings suggest irisin could be a therapeutic target for obesity and type 2 diabetes by inducing WAT browning through the p38 MAPK and ERK pathways.

Obesity, the most prevalent metabolic disorder globally, arises from an imbalance where energy intake surpasses expenditure, leading to excessive adipose tissue. This condition significantly elevates the risk of developing type 2 diabetes. Consequently, boosting energy expenditure offers a promising strategy for obesity prevention. While the precise mechanisms governing energy expenditure remain somewhat elusive, adipocytes are recognized as key players in regulating vertebrate energy balance and nutrient flow.
Adipose tissue exists in two primary forms: white adipose tissue (WAT) and brown adipose tissue (BAT). While white adipocytes primarily function in energy storage, brown adipocytes are specialized for energy consumption. Research indicates that alterations in BAT activity significantly influence adaptive thermogenesis and glucose homeostasis. The thermogenic capacity of brown fat is largely attributed to mitochondrial uncoupling protein-1 (UCP-1). This protein uncouples the electron transport chain from energy production, converting the chemical energy from food into heat. UCP-1 expression is controlled by various transcriptional factors, notably peroxisome proliferator–activated receptor γ coactivator-1α (PGC-1α), which can be activated by cold exposure or β-adrenergic signaling. In rodent studies, the "browning" of WAT depots has demonstrated protective effects against diet-induced metabolic conditions such as obesity and diabetes.
Irisin, a polypeptide hormone of 111 amino acids and approximately 22 kDa, is secreted by skeletal muscles, with levels increasing during exercise. Its production involves the upregulation of fibronectin type III domain containing 5 (Fndc5) by PGC-1α and exercise. Fndc5, a skeletal muscle transmembrane protein, is then proteolytically cleaved at amino acid positions 30 and 140 to yield irisin. Previous research indicates that irisin overexpression, achieved via adenoviral vectors, can boost total body energy expenditure, lead to slight weight loss, and enhance glucose tolerance in mice on high-fat diets. Circulating irisin levels are influenced by age and skeletal muscle mass, with young male athletes exhibiting significantly higher concentrations than middle-aged obese women. Individuals with type 2 diabetes have notably lower circulating irisin compared to non-diabetic controls. Furthermore, plasma irisin levels show a negative correlation with blood urea nitrogen in chronic kidney disease patients and a positive association with aerobic performance in heart failure patients. Given its role in energy expenditure, irisin is recognized as a thermogenesis-stimulating protein in brown adipocytes, similar to liver fibroblast growth factor 21 and cardiac natriuretic peptides. Despite these findings, the precise molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways through which irisin induces a "brown-like" adipocyte phenotype remain largely unexplored.
This study investigated the effects of recombinant human irisin (r-irisin) on fat cell browning and its underlying mechanisms. We developed an efficient system for expressing and purifying r-irisin using *Pichia pastoris*. This r-irisin was then administered to mice via intraperitoneal injection and applied to primary and 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes in cell culture. *In vivo* administration of r-irisin successfully reduced body weight, enhanced brown fat-specific gene expression in subcutaneous white adipose tissue, and improved glucose tolerance. *In vitro* experiments revealed that irisin stimulated brown fat-specific gene expression by activating the p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK) and extracellular signal-related kinase (ERK) signaling pathways. These findings suggest a direct role for irisin in promoting fat browning and improving metabolic health.
A 360 bp human irisin cDNA, designed and synthesized by Life Technologies, was cloned into the pPICZαA plasmid's EcoR1/Xba1 sites. Pichia pastoris X-33 was then transformed with this linearized pPICZαA-irisin plasmid, following the Pichia Easycomp Transformation Kit manual. Yeast culture and protein expression induction were conducted as previously described.
Recombinant irisin (r-irisin) was purified from the supernatant using a two-step process. Initially, r-irisin was precipitated with 60% saturated ammonium sulfate. The resulting precipitate was then dissolved in Buffer A (containing HEPES, glycerol, KCl, EDTA, and dithiothreitol) and dialyzed against this buffer. Following dialysis, the sample was applied to a concanavalin A–agarose column. Proteins were eluted from the column using Buffer A with 1.5 mol/L KCl, after an initial wash with 0.2 mol/L KCl in Buffer A. Western blotting was used to analyze the eluted fractions. The purified irisin was subsequently dialyzed against 10% glycerol in PBS and stored at −80°C.
To confirm the glycosylation sites at Asn7 and Asn52 in human irisin protein, we generated mutant irisin cDNA clones, N7H and N52H. Using a site-directed mutagenesis kit, the pPICZαA-irisin plasmid served as a template for PCR with specific point-mutation primers. Following Dpn1 digestion, the resulting PCR fragments were introduced into *Escherichia coli* Top10. The cDNA from these clones was then purified using a Qiagen plasmid midi-purification kit. Each mutant cDNA underwent complete sequencing to verify that only the intended base changes were present. The specific primers used for these mutations were: N7H forward (5′-tccatccgctccagtgcacgttactgttagacacttgaaggc) and reverse (5′-tgtctaacagtaacgtgcctggagcggatggagagaattc); and N52H forward (5′-gattcatccaagaggtgcacactactactagatcctgtgctttg) and reverse (5′-gatctagtagtagtgtgcacctcttggatgaatctcaacattc).
Adipocytes were isolated from male Sprague-Dawley rats (200g) using a previously established protocol. Subcutaneous fat pads were first minced, then digested with 1 mg/mL type I collagenase at 37°C for 90 minutes in a water bath shaker. Following digestion, the material was filtered through a mesh and centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 10 minutes. The resulting floating cell layer, containing the adipocytes, underwent three washes with PBS. Finally, these isolated adipocytes were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM)/F12, enriched with 10% FBS and 100 units/mL penicillin-streptomycin, within a 5% CO2 atmosphere at 37°C.
To investigate the effects of recombinant irisin, murine 3T3-L1 preadipocytes, obtained from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank, were differentiated into mature adipocytes. The differentiation process began with culturing preadipocytes in a basic medium, consisting of DMEM supplemented with 10% bovine calf serum and penicillin-streptomycin, at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator until confluence was reached on day 1. Adipocyte differentiation was then initiated by introducing an induction medium, which included 0.5 mmol/L isobutylmethylxanthine, 0.25 μmol/L dexamethasone, and 10 μmol/L insulin, in addition to the basic medium. On day 3, two days post-induction, the medium was replaced with basic medium containing only insulin for an additional two days. Subsequently, on day 5, the cells were returned to basic medium for another two days. Recombinant irisin was introduced to the 3T3-L1 cells on day 3 of differentiation, and this stimulation continued for a total of four days. By day 7, the mature 3T3-L1 adipocytes, both with and without irisin treatment, were prepared for various analyses. These included Oil Red O staining to confirm adipocyte differentiation, RNA extraction for reverse transcription-quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR), immunocytochemistry (ICC) using anti–UCP-1 antibodies, and Western blotting.
To investigate irisin binding, 3T3-L1 adipocytes were differentiated for seven days. Cells were briefly exposed to irisin, then washed and incubated with an anti-irisin antibody, followed by a FITC-conjugated secondary antibody. Confocal microscopy then visualized irisin presence. In a separate experiment, cells were treated with either a luciferase-irisin fusion protein or luciferase alone. Immunocytochemistry with an anti-luciferase antibody was subsequently performed to detect binding. These methods aimed to identify irisin's interaction with the adipocyte surface.
Gene expression was quantified using the 2−∆ct method, with β-actin RNA serving as an internal control. Total RNA was extracted from treated and untreated cells using Trizol reagent. Subsequently, 2 μg of this RNA underwent reverse transcription with the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit. Real-time PCR, performed in triplicate using SYBR Green Master Mix and gene-specific primers (Table 1), then measured relative gene expression.
Immunocytochemistry (ICC) for UCP-1 protein expression in 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes involved several steps. First, cells were fixed with ice-cold methanol for 10 minutes at 4°C, followed by three washes with PBS. To prevent non-specific binding, cells were then blocked with hydrogen peroxide solution for 10 minutes at room temperature. Subsequently, a primary UCP-1 antibody (1:500; Sigma-Aldrich) was applied, and cells were incubated overnight at 4°C. After another washing step, cells were exposed to a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG antibody for 30 minutes at room temperature. A final PBS wash preceded a 30-minute incubation with avidin-biotinylated HRP. Diaminobenzidine served as the chromogen for ICC, while hematoxylin counterstained the nuclei. Dark brown cytoplasmic staining indicated positive UCP-1 protein expression.
Proteins from cell lysates were separated by 12% SDS-PAGE, with equal amounts loaded per well. Following electrophoresis, the separated proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. These membranes were then blocked for one hour at room temperature using 5% fat-free milk. Subsequently, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies (1:1,000 dilution) targeting irisin, UCP-1, ERK1/2, phospho-ERK1/2, AKT, phospho-AKT, JNK, phospho-JNK, p38 MAPK, and phospho-p38 MAPK. A rabbit anti-GAPDH antibody (1:10,000) served as a loading control. After washing with Tris-buffered saline containing Tween, the membranes were incubated for one hour at room temperature with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Immune complexes were then detected using the ECL method, and the resulting immunoreactive bands were quantified densitometrically with an Alpha Imager 2200.
To investigate the effects of irisin, C57BL/6 mice were fed a high-fat diet (60% fat) for ten weeks, reaching an initial body weight of 35–43g. These obese mice were then randomly assigned to one of two groups, each containing eight animals: a control group and a recombinant irisin (r-irisin) treated group. For 14 days, the r-irisin group received daily intraperitoneal injections of purified r-irisin at a dose of 0.5 µg/g body weight. The control group received daily injections of saline at an equivalent volume. At the conclusion of the 14-day treatment period, mouse body weights were measured. Prior to euthanasia, some mice underwent an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test. Subsequently, subcutaneous fat tissues were collected from the mice for immunohistochemistry and total RNA extraction, which was used for RT-qPCR analysis.
Data are presented as mean ± SE from at least three independent experiments, each performed in triplicate. Statistical analyses involved one-way ANOVA for multiple groups, followed by Student's t-tests for pairwise comparisons, utilizing SPSS 17.0. A p-value below 0.05 indicated statistical significance.
To produce human irisin in large quantities, an optimized cDNA sequence, tailored for yeast codon usage, was designed and synthesized. This sequence, encoding recombinant irisin (r-irisin), was then integrated into the pPICZαA plasmid. Subsequently, this plasmid was introduced into *Pichia pastoris* cells. From 50 colonies grown on zeocin-containing YPD, eight were chosen to assess their capacity for r-irisin protein expression. Analysis of the secreted r-irisin at different time points, using SDS-PAGE and Coomassie blue staining, revealed three distinct protein bands with molecular weights of 25, 22, and 15 kDa. Western blotting confirmed that all three bands corresponded to human irisin. Further validation of the protein identity for each band was achieved through MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry. This process successfully established a high-yield yeast expression system for human irisin.
The secreted irisin protein exhibited two higher molecular weights (25 and 22 kDa) compared to native irisin, suggesting glycosylation. To confirm this, purified recombinant irisin (r-irisin) was treated with PNGase F, an enzyme that removes N-linked glycans. Western blot analysis of the enzyme-treated r-irisin revealed a single band at a lower molecular weight (15 kDa). This finding confirmed that the majority of the secreted r-irisin produced by *P. pastoris* was indeed glycosylated, specifically at its two putative N-glycosylation sites, asparagine 7 (Asn7) and asparagine 52 (Asn52).
To confirm irisin's two glycosylation sites, individual mutations were introduced at Asn7 or Asn52. Each mutant recombinant irisin (N7H or N52H) exhibited two bands at 22 and 15 kDa. The 22 kDa band, sensitive to PNGase-F treatment, likely indicates partial glycosylation of the mutant irisin at one site. These experiments thus confirm that human irisin possesses two glycosylation sites, specifically at Asn7 and Asn52.
Our investigation into recombinant irisin's (r-irisin) browning effect on adipocytes utilized both 3T3-L1-derived and primary rat adipocytes as experimental models, with or without r-irisin treatment. Gene expression analysis revealed that 20 nmol/L r-irisin significantly upregulated several brown cell markers in 3T3-L1 adipocytes (P < 0.01). These markers included uncoupling protein-1 (UCP-1), peroxisome proliferator–activated receptor γ (PPARγ) coactivator-1α (PGC-1α), transmembrane protein 26 (TMEM26), early B cell factor 3 (Ebf3), elongation of very long chain fatty acids-like 3 (Elovl3), cell death-inducing DNA fragmentation factor (Cidea), and cytochrome c oxidase, subunit VII α (Cox7α). UCP-1, a unique mitochondrial protein, is responsible for converting chemical energy into heat. PGC-1α, a potent transcriptional coactivator, is a key regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis, encompassing UCP-1 and Fndc5 in brown fat cells. The elevated expression of TMEM26, a beige cell marker, alongside brown cell markers like Cidea and Cox7α, suggests that r-irisin may facilitate the conversion of white adipose cells into beige-like and brown adipocytes. Furthermore, r-irisin treatment of adipocytes also led to a significant increase in betatrophin (ANGPTL8) gene expression (3.53 ± 0.97–fold, P < 0.01). Betatrophin, a recently discovered pancreatic β-cell growth hormone, is predominantly expressed in the liver and fat of mice. This finding implies that irisin's ability to enhance betatrophin expression could partially account for its observed improvement in insulin resistance.
Recombinant irisin's ability to boost UCP-1 expression at the protein level was confirmed in 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes through both Western blotting and immunocytochemistry (ICC). These findings were observed in adipocytes whose differentiation was verified by Oil Red O staining. Quantitative ICC analysis of r-irisin-treated 3T3-L1 adipocytes showed a significant 2.78-fold increase in UCP-1-positive cells, characterized by multilocular lipid droplets, compared to saline-treated controls (P < 0.01). Furthermore, treating primary cultured rat adipocytes with varying concentrations of r-irisin (2, 20, and 100 nmol/L) led to a notable increase in UCP-1 and PGC-1α gene expression. Given that 20 nmol/L r-irisin resulted in a fourfold increase in UCP-1 mRNA compared to untreated cells, this concentration was selected for subsequent experiments.
We investigated the impact of irisin's glycosylation on its biological activity by treating 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes with irisin proteins mutated at either the Asn7 or Asn52 site. Our findings revealed that these mutated irisin proteins significantly diminished the upregulation of browning markers UCP-1 and PGC-1α, an effect observed at both gene and protein levels. Specifically, UCP-1 expression was substantially lower in cells treated with mutated irisin compared to those receiving wild-type irisin. This suggests that the posttranslational glycosylation of irisin, particularly when secreted from yeast, plays a crucial role in enhancing its "browning" effect, as evidenced by the increased expression of UCP-1 and PGC-1α. However, it is important to acknowledge that other mechanisms, beyond altered glycosylation, might also contribute to the reduced biological activity seen with the N7H and N52H mutant irisin.
While irisin's role in "browning" is recognized, its specific molecular signaling mechanisms remain largely undefined. Our investigation revealed that p38 MAPK and ERK pathways are central to this process. In both primary rat and 3T3-L1 adipocytes, recombinant irisin treatment significantly elevated phosphorylated p38 MAPK (P-p38) and phosphorylated ERK (P-ERK) levels. These increases were observed rapidly, peaking between 10 and 20 minutes post-treatment before declining by 30 minutes. Densitometric quantification confirmed the statistical significance of these elevated protein levels, highlighting the involvement of these pathways in irisin's browning effect.
To investigate the role of p38 MAPK and ERK pathways in irisin's browning effects, 3T3-L1 adipocytes were exposed to irisin alongside specific inhibitors. Irisin typically promotes the phosphorylation of both p38 and ERK. However, when the p38 MAPK inhibitor SB203580 or the ERK inhibitor U0126 was introduced, this phosphorylation was progressively diminished as inhibitor concentrations increased. Importantly, the total protein levels of p38 and ERK remained unchanged, indicating a specific inhibition of their activation. Furthermore, the irisin-driven increase in UCP-1 expression was significantly curtailed by either inhibitor individually. When both SB203580 and U0126 were applied concurrently, the irisin-mediated upregulation of UCP-1, both at the mRNA and protein levels, was almost entirely abolished. These results strongly suggest that the browning effect induced by irisin, characterized by enhanced UCP-1 expression, is critically dependent on the activation of both the p38 MAPK and ERK signaling pathways.
While irisin was known to enhance glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity in high-fat diet-fed mice, the underlying mechanisms remained unclear. This study investigated irisin's impact on key signaling pathways, specifically AKT and JNK, within adipocytes. Our findings revealed that irisin did not significantly affect AKT phosphorylation in 3T3-L1 adipocytes across various time points. This contrasts with insulin, which strongly promotes AKT phosphorylation. Furthermore, no additive or synergistic effects were observed when insulin and irisin were combined, suggesting that the AKT signaling pathway is not involved in irisin's browning action. Similarly, irisin treatment had no discernible effect on the phosphorylation of c-Jun NH2-terminal kinases (JNKs) in adipocytes, indicating that the JNK signaling pathway also does not mediate the irisin-induced browning process. These results collectively suggest that irisin's beneficial effects on glucose homeostasis and its browning capabilities operate independently of both the AKT and JNK signaling pathways.
While a specific membrane receptor for irisin on adipocytes has not yet been definitively identified, our findings suggest its probable existence, particularly given irisin's activation of P-p38 and P-ERK signaling pathways. Traditional methods for identifying ligand-receptor binding, which involve radioactively labeled ligands, present considerable drawbacks due to handling hazards and environmental concerns associated with radioisotope disposal. Consequently, we employed live cell binding techniques to investigate irisin's interaction with the cell membrane. In our experiments, 3T3-L1 adipocytes were briefly exposed to recombinant irisin (r-irisin). Subsequent immunofluorescence imaging, following sequential incubation with an anti-irisin antibody and a FITC–anti-rabbit IgG antibody, revealed green fluorescence exclusively on the cell membrane of irisin-treated cells (Fig. 6A), indicating r-irisin binding. To further substantiate this observation, we introduced either a luciferase-irisin fusion protein or luciferase alone (as a control) into the cell culture medium. Visualization of irisin binding was then achieved using an antiluciferase antibody. As depicted in Fig. 6B, cells incubated with the luciferase-irisin fusion protein displayed clear green fluorescence along the membrane, with increasing intensity, whereas no discernible membrane signal was observed in luciferase-treated control cells. These collective data strongly imply the presence of an as-yet-unidentified irisin receptor on the cell membrane of 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes.
To investigate the therapeutic potential of recombinant irisin (r-irisin), a 14-day study was conducted where both normal and high-fat diet (HFD)-fed mice received daily injections of r-irisin. This treatment led to a significant increase in the mRNA levels of several brown adipocyte marker genes, including UCP-1, PGC-1α, Cox7α, Elovl3, PPARα, and Cidea, within the adipose tissues of normal mice (P < 0.01). Similar enhancements in gene expression were also observed in the adipose tissue of HFD-fed mice. Furthermore, r-irisin administration significantly elevated the expression of betatrophin (ANGPTL8) in the adipose tissue of treated mice compared to controls (P < 0.05), consistent with findings in 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes. These molecular changes in gene expression were accompanied by observable histological alterations in subcutaneous adipose tissues, specifically an increased presence of UCP-1-positive, multilocular adipocytes, indicative of a "browning" effect. Clinically, the r-irisin treatment resulted in a reduction in body weight in HFD-fed mice after 14 days when compared to saline-treated control mice. Importantly, r-irisin significantly improved glucose tolerance in HFD-fed mice, evidenced by a faster recovery in intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests and markedly reduced fasting insulin levels. These findings collectively suggest that in vivo r-irisin treatment in HFD-fed mice can effectively decrease body weight, stimulate the expression of brown-specific genes, and ameliorate HFD-induced insulin resistance, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic agent.
Irisin, a myokine derived from Fndc5 protein cleavage, promotes brown fat-like development and thermogenesis in white adipose tissue both in vitro and in vivo. This discovery offers new avenues for understanding adipocyte roles in obesity, diabetes, and related metabolic disorders. However, the specific molecular mechanisms and cellular signaling pathways governing irisin's "browning" effect remain unclear.
Our research involved creating a recombinant human irisin protein, optimized for yeast expression, using the *Pichia pastoris* system. This system is well-suited for producing proteins with posttranslational modifications. The resulting irisin protein, when expressed in yeast, exhibited a primary band around 22 kDa, consistent with previously observed glycosylated irisin. Through PNGase-F digestion and site-directed mutagenesis, we identified and confirmed two glycosylation sites in human irisin at asparagine 7 and asparagine 52. Given that glycosylation is a common characteristic of secreted proteins in both mice and humans, we investigated the biological impact of these glycosylation sites. We mutated these sites to assess the effect on irisin's function. Our findings demonstrated that the posttranslational glycosylation of secreted irisin significantly enhances the expression of UCP-1 and PGC-1α compared to mutated irisin. This suggests that irisin produced in a yeast system is functionally superior to that from bacterial expression systems. This enhanced activity highlights the importance of proper glycosylation for irisin's biological role.
Adipose tissue contains two distinct types of brown adipocytes. Classical brown fat cells originate from a myf5, muscle-like lineage. The second type, termed beige or "brown-in-white" (brite) cells, are UCP-1 positive brown adipocytes found within white adipose tissue (WAT). These beige cells emerge in response to various stimuli, including chemical or hormonal signals, environmental shifts, cold exposure, and specific genetic manipulations. Our current research demonstrates that recombinant irisin (r-irisin) promotes the development of these beige adipocytes. Specifically, r-irisin treatment significantly increased the expression of thermogenic genes, such as UCP-1, PGC-1α, Cox7α, Ebf3, and Elovl3, in both primary and cell line-derived adipocytes. Furthermore, we observed an elevated expression of TMEM26, a recognized marker for beige cells, when r-irisin was introduced to cultured 3T3-L1 preadipocytes during their differentiation. This finding aligns with previous work by Wu et al., which indicated that beige cell precursors are particularly responsive to irisin's browning effects. Therefore, our results suggest that irisin plays a role in increasing the population of beige-like cells.
Irisin, a muscle-derived signal, promotes adipose tissue browning, influencing energy expenditure. Understanding the molecular pathways behind this browning effect is crucial. Previous research by Boström et al. (12) implicated PPARα in UCP-1 expression, but other mechanisms are likely involved. PGC-1α, a key regulator of mitochondrial biogenesis and function, is a cold-inducible protein that governs adaptive thermogenesis in brown adipose tissue (BAT), with its expression modulated by cAMP/PKA/p38 MAPK signaling pathways (35,36). Additionally, molecules like AMPK and PPARδ have been proposed as exercise mimetics (37,38). Our findings demonstrate that irisin significantly elevates P-p38 MAPK and P-ERK levels in both primary and 3T3-L1-derived adipocytes. This effect, which leads to irisin-induced UCP-1 expression, was entirely negated by inhibiting p38 and ERK phosphorylation (Fig. 5). Therefore, we propose that the p38 and ERK signaling pathways are central to the development of brown adipocytes induced by irisin.
Our study provides initial evidence for a potential irisin-specific receptor on adipocyte membranes. While further research is needed to fully characterize and validate this receptor, its existence is suggested by our findings.
Recombinant irisin enhances thermogenic gene expression both in vitro and in vivo. This stimulation of brown cell-specific genes in adipocytes occurs through the p38 and ERK signaling pathways. The mechanism likely involves a specific membrane receptor on adipocytes. These findings offer initial experimental support for recombinant irisin's potential therapeutic application in treating obesity, diabetes, and related metabolic disorders.
