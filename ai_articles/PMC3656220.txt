This study aimed to investigate the expression of ERM (ezrin, moesin) and Rho (RhoA, RhoB, Cdc42) proteins in breast cancer (BC) patients. The research focused on understanding how the subcellular localization of these proteins relates to clinicopathological characteristics and patient survival. Using immunohistochemistry, the expression and specific subcellular distribution of ERM/Rho proteins were analyzed in a cohort of 85 stage II ductal BC patients who were followed for 15 years. Key findings revealed that enhanced immunoreactivity of all examined proteins was linked to the presence of lymph node metastases. Specifically, nuclear ezrin localization correlated with lymph node metastases and histological dedifferentiation. Conversely, nuclear RhoA and Cdc42, along with perinuclear RhoB, were strongly associated with the absence of nodal metastases. A reduced RhoA reactivity in the tumor's stromal compartment was also linked to the presence of lymph node metastases. No correlation was found between ERM/Rho protein expression and ER, PgR, or HER-2 reactivity in BC cells. While ERM/Rho protein expression did not predict overall patient survival, RhoB overexpression in the tumor stroma was associated with a better prognosis. The study concluded that the ERM/Rho immunoprofile, coupled with an assessment of their specific subcellular localization, could be valuable tools for predicting lymph node metastases in ductal BC patients.

Breast cancer (BC) remains a significant global health concern, contributing substantially to morbidity and mortality among women. In 2008 alone, over 1.38 million new cases were diagnosed, resulting in approximately 500,000 deaths worldwide. Current BC treatment strategies involve a multimodal approach, encompassing endocrine therapy, surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted molecular therapies. Established prognostic and predictive factors include disease stage, characterized by tumor size, nodal status, and distant metastases. Additionally, the expression levels of estrogen and progesterone receptors (ER and PgR), the HER-2 status, and Ki-67 expression are crucial indicators. While extensive gene expression studies have identified distinct BC subtypes, practical clinical application is often limited. Consequently, a simplified classification system has been proposed, relying on immunohistochemical assessment of ER and PgR, HER-2 evaluation, and the Ki-67 proliferation index.
The ERM protein family, comprising ezrin, radixin, and moesin, displays distinct tissue-specific expression. Ezrin is primarily found in epithelial cells, while moesin is present in endothelia, and radixin in hepatocytes. These ERM proteins act as crucial structural and regulatory components, linking the membrane to the cytoskeleton and influencing cell morphology. They also play a role in cellular processes such as polarity, motility, adhesion, and survival, all of which are implicated in cancer development and progression. ERM proteins can interact with molecules like CD43, CD44, ICAM-1, ICAM-2, EGFR, and MET, which are linked to tumor progression. While ezrin overexpression has been associated with poor outcomes in various cancers, the significance of moesin immunoreactivity in tumors requires further investigation.
The Ras homologous (Rho) family, including RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42, comprises low molecular weight GTP-binding proteins. These proteins are key regulators of signal transduction pathways that influence actin cytoskeleton remodeling. Early research on RhoA's role in cellular development demonstrated that its overexpression promotes actin polymerization by activating diaphanous-related formins (DRFs) and Rho-activated kinases (ROCKs). This coordinated action of RhoA, DRFs, and ROCKs leads to the formation of stress fibers, essential for maintaining cell polarity and shape. While RhoB's precise functions are less detailed, studies indicate it is downregulated in human tumors, with its expression inversely correlating with tumor progression. Cdc42 has been implicated in enhancing cellular motility through its involvement in filopodia formation.
The role of ERM and Rho protein expression in breast carcinogenesis and their effect on long-term survival remain largely unknown. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic significance of ERM (ezrin, moesin) and Rho (RhoA, RhoB, Cdc42) protein expression using immunohistochemistry in 85 stage II ductal breast cancer patients. We examined how the subcellular localization of these proteins related to patient clinicopathological characteristics. Furthermore, we investigated the correlation between ERM/Rho protein reactivity in breast cancer specimens and the status of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PgR), and HER-2. Given the ongoing debate regarding treatment protocols for stage II breast cancer, identifying novel immunohistochemical markers is crucial for stratifying patients and enabling personalized treatment based on tumor aggressiveness.
This study utilized tissue samples from 85 patients who underwent radical treatment for stage II invasive ductal breast cancer. These diagnoses were made between 1993 and 1994 at the Lower Silesian Oncology Centre in Wroclaw, Poland. The patient cohort had a median age of 55.6 years. Patient selection was based on tissue availability, and the cohort was not specifically stratified for preoperative or pathological prognostic factors. All patients received surgical treatment, specifically Madden mastectomy, with or without subsequent adjuvant therapy. The study established cancer-specific overall survival (CSOS) and disease-free survival (DFS) rates for all participants. The inclusion of 85 patients was determined by the single series available from the institution, a 15-year follow-up period, and the highly homogeneous nature of the selected tumors. These tumors were characterized as invasive ductal breast cancer, grading G2 and G3, and clinical stage II according to the UICC, all treated with Madden mastectomy. The relatively younger age of the patient group can be attributed to lower average life expectancy in Poland during the 1993-1994 period compared to Western Europe. Comprehensive details regarding the patient cohort are presented in Table 1. The Institutional Review Board of the Wroclaw Medical University, Poland, provided approval for this research.
Tumour specimens were preserved in 10% buffered formalin and embedded in paraffin. Two pathologists reviewed all haematoxylin and eosin stained sections. The study's inclusion of a homogeneous group of clinical stage II BC patients, as detailed previously, influenced the median tumour size. This approach was adopted partly because a population-based BC screening program was not available at the study's inception, leading to variations in primary tumour sizes compared to other regions, such as European countries. Tumour staging was conducted using the TNM classification system [17], and tumour grading was determined by the Bloom-Richardson protocol, incorporating the Elston and Ellis [18] modification, as presented in Table 1.
To conduct the study, tissue samples were retrospectively analyzed using immunohistochemistry. These samples, collected for routine diagnostic purposes, were formalin-fixed and paraffin-embedded. Freshly prepared 4 μm sections were utilized. The immunohistochemical procedure, consistent with previous studies [19–21], involved specific antibodies diluted in Antibody Diluent, Background Reducing (DakoCytomation, Poland). These included anti-ezrin (1:150, clone 4A5, mouse monoclonal, Chemicon International), anti-moesin (1:100, Sc-6410, goat polyclonal, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), anti-RhoA (1:100, Sc-179, rabbit polyclonal, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), anti-RhoB (1:100, Sc-180, rabbit polyclonal, Santa Cruz Biotechnology), and anti-Cdc42 (1:50, Sc-87, rabbit polyclonal, Santa Cruz Biotechnology). Additionally, antibodies for oestrogen and progesterone receptors were employed: an optimally pre-diluted mouse monoclonal antibody (clone 1D5, DakoCytomation) for ER and an optimally pre-diluted mouse monoclonal antibody (clone PgR636, DakoCytomation) for PgR. HER-2 detection utilized a semi-quantitative diagnostic test (HercepTest™Kit, K5207, DakoCytomation). All tissue sections were incubated with the primary antibodies for one hour at room temperature. Following this, biotinylated antibodies were applied for 15 minutes at room temperature, followed by a streptavidin-biotinylated peroxidase complex (LSAB+, HRP, DakoCytomation) for another 15 minutes at room temperature. The chromogen used was NovaRed (Vector Laboratories, UK) for 10 minutes at room temperature. Meyer’s haematoxylin served as the counterstain for all sections. To ensure accuracy, control reactions were performed for each case, substituting the specific antibody with a Primary Mouse Negative Control (DakoCytomation, Poland).
Two pathologists independently assessed the intensity of the immunohistochemical reactions. To quantify protein expression, a modified semi-quantitative ImmunoReactive Score (IRS) was employed, incorporating both the intensity of the color reaction and the percentage of positive cells. This scoring system, with a final range of 0 to 12, was adapted by the authors to specifically consider the percentage of positive cells, as detailed in Table 2. Scores from 0 to 2 on the IRS scale were categorized as negative for expression. Beyond the predominant cytoplasmic localization observed for most proteins, additional subcellular distributions were noted for all analyzed proteins except moesin. Ezrin exhibited both membranous and nuclear localization. RhoA and Cdc42 were also found within the cell nuclei, while RhoB was detected in the cell membrane and the perinuclear region. Furthermore, all investigated proteins were present in the stromal compartments of the tumors and within their blood vessels. Stromal and blood vessel reactivity were evaluated using a three-step scale (SS for stromal score, BVS for blood vessel score), which considered the intensity of the color reaction and the proportion of stained tissue or blood vessels. This scale ranged from 0 (no reaction) to 3 (strongly positive reaction).
Oestrogen and progesterone receptor expression was assessed using standard methods. Staining intensity (0-3 scale) and the percentage of positive cells (0-5 scale) were recorded, with the Allred score calculated to combine these values. HER-2 status was determined via an FDA-approved scoring system (0, 1+, 2+, 3+), where 0 indicated no immunostaining, 1+ weak staining in less than 30% of tumor cells, 2+ complete membranous reactivity in at least 10% of cells, and 3+ uniform intense membranous staining in at least 30% of tumor cells.
Statistical analyses were conducted using Statistica 9.1 software. Disease-free survival (DFS) was calculated from primary surgical treatment to relapse or death, with censored data for patients without recurrence. Cancer-specific overall survival (CSOS) was measured from primary surgery to cancer-related death, also with censored data for survivors. Associations between ERM/Rho protein expression and clinicopathological factors were examined using the χ2 and Spearman rank correlation tests. Overall survival rates were determined using Kaplan-Meier curves and the log-rank test. A P value less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.
In all analyzed breast cancer (BC) cases, ezrin protein expression was detected, with an average ImmunoReactive Score (IRS) of 8.88. The primary location of ezrin was cytoplasmic, as depicted in Figure 1a. However, co-expression of ezrin was also observed in the cellular membrane in 20 cases (23.53%) (Figure 1b) and within the nucleus in 27 cases (31.76%) (Figure 1c). Moesin, on the other hand, showed only a cytoplasmic reaction, with a mean IRS of 3.69 (Figure 1d). Microscopic examination further revealed the presence of both ezrin and moesin expression within the stromal compartment of the BC tumor specimens and in their blood vessels, as detailed in Table 3.
All breast cancer cases examined showed expression of RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 proteins. The primary location for these proteins was the cytoplasm. RhoA and Cdc42 were also found in the nucleus in a significant number of cases (70 cases for RhoA, 6 cases for Cdc42). RhoB exhibited additional localization in the cellular membrane (14 cases) and the perinuclear region (24 cases). Consistent with the ERM proteins, RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 were also detected in the stromal areas of the tumors and within their blood vessels.
No association was found between ERM/Rho protein expression and ER, PgR, or HER-2 reactivity within breast cancer cells. However, a significant correlation was identified between reduced ezrin expression in tumor blood vessels and positive progesterone receptor (PgR) status (P = 0.028). Additionally, a trend was observed linking low ezrin expression in tumor blood vessels with HER-2 over-expression, though this did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.059). These findings suggest potential indirect relationships between ERM/Rho protein localization in the vascular stroma and certain hormone receptor and HER-2 statuses, even in the absence of direct correlation within the cancer cells themselves.
Examining ezrin and moesin protein expression in relation to patient clinicopathological factors revealed significant links between their expression within tumor compartments and the presence of lymph node metastases (P = 0.047 for ezrin, P = 0.038 for moesin). While a trend suggested a link between higher ezrin immunoreactivity and UICC stage (IIA vs. IIB), this did not reach statistical significance. No significant associations were found between ezrin or moesin expression and tumor grade, size, or patient age at diagnosis. However, nuclear ezrin expression was notably more prevalent in patients with lymph node metastases (47.22%) compared to those without (18.37%, P = 0.004). Furthermore, nuclear ezrin was associated with Grade 3 breast cancers (P = 0.015), supporting a potential role for nuclear ezrin localization in tumor progression. Expression of ezrin and moesin in stromal compartments and tumor blood vessels showed no significant associations with clinicopathological parameters, though a slight correlation was noted between increased stromal ezrin and postmenopausal status (P = 0.042).
In the tumour cells, elevated levels of RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 were linked to the presence of lymph node metastases. Specifically, increased RhoB expression showed a significant correlation with UICC stage IIB. Further investigation into RhoA's sub-cellular distribution revealed that nuclear localization was associated with both fewer nodal metastases and lower tumor grading. Similarly, Cdc42's presence in the nucleus and RhoB's perinuclear localization were independently correlated with the absence of lymph node metastases. However, the expression of these Rho proteins did not demonstrate significant associations with other clinicopathological factors such as tumor size, overall grade, age at diagnosis, or menopausal status.
Intriguing findings emerged from the expression analysis of Rho proteins within the stromal compartments and tumor blood vessels of breast cancer specimens. A decrease in RhoA reactivity in the tumor stroma was linked to the presence of lymph node metastases (P = 0.011). Additionally, low Cdc42 levels in the stromal compartment correlated with postmenopausal status (P = 0.018). Conversely, heightened Cdc42 reactivity in tumor blood vessels showed a significant association with premenopausal status (P = 0.026). Older patients, in contrast, displayed reduced Cdc42 expression in these blood vessels.
Survival analyses indicated that ezrin and moesin protein expression did not hold prognostic significance in breast cancer patients. Neither the ImmunoReactive Score (IRS) nor the sub-cellular localizations of these proteins impacted cancer-specific overall survival (CSOS) or disease-free survival (DFS). Kaplan-Meier curves showed no significant differences in DFS for enhanced ezrin, RhoA, and Cdc42 immunoreactivity. However, patients with RhoB over-expression in the stromal compartment demonstrated an increased CSOS (P = 0.0106).
While ERM/Rho protein expression generally didn't predict survival, RhoB over-expression in the stromal compartment was linked to a better patient outcome (P = 0.0106). Sub-cellular RhoA distribution and overall RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 expression did not show significant correlations with patient survival.
This study examined ERM (ezrin, moesin) and Rho (RhoA, RhoB, Cdc42) protein expression in stage II invasive ductal breast cancer. We assessed how these proteins' subcellular localization related to clinicopathological features and patient survival over 15 years. Furthermore, we investigated the connection between ERM/Rho reactivity in BC specimens and the status of steroid receptors and HER-2.
Proper sub-cellular localization of ERM and Rho proteins is crucial for their function. While all analyzed proteins were predominantly cytoplasmic, our study identified other localizations. Ezrin was found in the nucleus and cell membrane, while moesin remained cytoplasmic. RhoA and Cdc42 exhibited nuclear localization, and RhoB was present in the cell membrane and perinuclear region. Interestingly, nuclear RhoA and Cdc42, along with perinuclear RhoB, were significantly linked to the absence of lymph node metastases in univariate analyses, though this did not translate to survival outcomes. Conversely, nuclear ezrin localization correlated with the presence of nodal metastases. Furthermore, nuclear ezrin was strongly associated with grade 3 breast cancer, suggesting a role for its nuclear distribution in carcinogenesis.
Previous research has identified various subcellular localizations for ERM and Rho proteins. Ezrin has been reported in cytoplasmic, membranous, nuclear, and apical compartments. Moesin is primarily found in the cytoplasm, with one study also noting its presence in the cell membrane, a finding not replicated in this study. RhoA and RhoB have predominantly been observed in the cytoplasm, though one report also indicated a cell membrane localization for RhoA. Cdc42 expression has thus far been exclusively documented in the cytoplasm.
Our investigation revealed that elevated expression of ERM and Rho proteins within the tumor compartment of ductal breast cancer (BC) significantly correlated with the presence of lymph node metastases, reinforcing their involvement in BC progression. This finding aligns with previous research by Sarrio et al. [10], which indicated that cytoplasmic ezrin expression might serve as a negative prognostic indicator, strongly associated with lymph node metastases. Their work further elucidated that variations in ezrin localization, rather than overall protein levels, were linked to more aggressive BC behavior. Specifically, a shift in ezrin distribution from the apical membrane to the entire membrane or cytoplasm was associated with BC cell de-differentiation and adverse clinical outcomes in invasive BC [10]. It is understood that cytoplasmic ezrin exists in a dormant, inactive state, unable to interact with cellular membrane proteins and the actin cytoskeleton [7]. Consequently, disruptions in ezrin's sub-cellular distribution could lead to the dysregulation of its cytophysiological functions. Our study also observed complete membranous ezrin expression in 23.53% of ductal BC cases. However, due to the limited sample size, a definitive prognostic value could not be established. A potential biochemical explanation for this phenomenon involves ezrin redistribution to the entire cellular membrane, possibly influenced by external growth factors. Upon stimulation, ezrin becomes active, enabling it to bind to membrane proteins and the actin cytoskeleton, forming ezrin-rich structures like filopodia, which are known to enhance motility, invasion, and metastatic potential [31, 32]. To our knowledge, this study is the first to document nuclear ezrin localization in BC cells (observed in 31.76% of cases), suggesting a potential role for ezrin as a transcriptional factor in this context. In agreement with our findings, Charafe-Jauffret et al. [23] reported moesin immunoreactivity in the cytoplasm of BC cells, along with expression in the stromal compartment and tumor blood vessels, though the significance of this latter observation was not deeply explored.
The Rho family of proteins, including RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42, are integral to numerous cellular functions such as migration, cell cycle progression, exocytosis, and apoptosis. These GTP-binding proteins are critical regulators of cellular motility and adhesion, thus playing a significant role in cancer development. Existing research across various cancer types has established a strong link between Rho GTPases and carcinogenesis. Our investigation revealed that elevated expression of RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 in breast cancer (BC) tumors was significantly associated with the presence of lymph node metastases. Furthermore, a higher immunoreactivity of RhoB specifically correlated with UICC stage IIB BC. These findings align with previous research by Fritz et al. [35], who observed significantly increased levels of RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 in BC samples compared to healthy breast tissue. While Fritz et al. noted an increase in RhoA and RhoB reactivity with tumor proliferation and grading, they did not explore correlations with nodal status or patient survival. Studies in other cancers have also highlighted the involvement of RhoA. In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, RhoA over-expression has been linked to advanced TNM stage and invasion of lymphatic and blood vessels, with enhanced RhoA reactivity predicting lower 5-year survival rates [27]. Similarly, in hepatocellular carcinoma, strong RhoA expression is a predictor of shorter survival [28]. However, contrasting findings exist; Dittert et al. [26] found that RhoA expression in pancreatic adenocarcinoma did not correlate with TNM classification and, in fact, was associated with a better prognosis. They also noted stromal expression of RhoA, though its clinicopathological relevance was not assessed. Pan et al. [36] reported that high RhoA mRNA expression correlated with advanced TNM classification and low histological differentiation, while RhoB and Cdc42 mRNA levels showed no such correlation. In retinoblastoma, Cdc42 reactivity, as measured by immunohistochemistry and Western blotting, lacked prognostic value and did not correlate with tumor invasion or differentiation [37]. Within our study, while elevated Cdc42 expression was associated with nodal metastases, a more nuanced observation emerged from qualitative analysis: nuclear localization of Cdc42 emerged as a positive prognostic indicator. This highlights the complexity of Rho protein function, where not only overall expression but also specific subcellular localization can influence clinical outcomes.
This study presents an initial comprehensive analysis of ERM and Rho protein family members in ductal breast cancer. The findings suggest that ERM/Rho immunoprofiles, particularly when considering detailed sub-cellular localization, could aid in predicting lymph node metastasis in these patients. No correlations were observed between ERM/Rho expression and ER, PgR, or HER-2 status in breast cancer cells. While ERM/Rho protein expression generally did not predict patient survival, RhoB over-expression in the tumor's stromal compartment was linked to a better prognosis. Further research with larger patient cohorts is necessary to validate these results and fully elucidate the clinicopathological implications of ezrin, moesin, RhoA, RhoB, and Cdc42 expression in breast cancer development.
