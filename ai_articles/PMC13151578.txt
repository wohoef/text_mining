The EFSA Panel on Food Contact Materials, Enzymes, Flavourings and Processing Aids (CEF Panel) has issued a scientific opinion regarding the safety of polyacrylic acid, sodium salt, cross-linked (FCM substance No 1015). This substance is designed for use as a liquid absorber in the packaging of various fresh or frozen foods, including meat, poultry, seafood, fruits, and vegetables. The Panel determined that specific migration tests were unnecessary because the substance exhibits a high capacity for liquid absorption. It was observed that if the polyacrylic acid, sodium salt, cross-linked material is contained within a pad, preventing direct food contact, and its absorption limit is not surpassed, then no migration into the food is anticipated. Consequently, consumers are not expected to be exposed to the substance through the consumption of packaged food. Therefore, the CEF Panel concluded that employing this cross-linked polyacrylic acid, sodium salt, in absorbent pads for fresh or frozen meat, poultry, fish, fruits, and vegetables does not pose a safety risk. Crucially, these absorbent pads must be utilized under conditions where the active substance's absorption capacity remains unexceeded and direct contact with the food is strictly avoided.

Specific regulations for active and intelligent food contact materials are outlined in Commission Regulation (EC) No 450/2009. These rules supplement the broader requirements for all food contact materials set forth in European Parliament and Council Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004. Active materials and articles are specifically designed to enhance or preserve the quality and shelf life of packaged food. Their function involves intentionally integrating components that either release or absorb substances from the food or its surrounding environment.
The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) must conduct a safety evaluation of substances enabling active/intelligent material functions. Following this assessment, the Commission will add these substances to a positive list, adhering to established regulatory procedures.
Industry applications for active and intelligent substances in food contact materials are first submitted to Member State authorities. These authorities then forward the applications, accompanied by a technical dossier, to EFSA for safety evaluation. The dossier must adhere to EFSA's specific guidelines for such submissions (EFSA CEF Panel, 2009).
McAirlaid's Vliesstoffe GmbH, Germany, applied to EFSA, via the Bundesamt für Verbraucherschutz und Lebensmittelsicherheit, for evaluation of cross-linked polyacrylic acid, sodium salt.
Under Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004, the EFSA is mandated to evaluate risks associated with the proposed use of substances in food contact materials. This involves conducting a safety assessment and issuing a scientific opinion on their suitability.
A dossier was submitted by the applicant to support the authorization of cross-linked sodium polyacrylate for active food contact materials. This dossier included non-toxicological data and information for evaluation.
The safety evaluation dossier included non-toxicological data on identity, physicochemical properties, intended use, authorization, migration, and residual content. No toxicological data were provided.
For active or intelligent substances in food contact materials, EFSA's safety assessment follows the general methodology outlined in the Scientific Committee on Food (SCF) guidelines (EC, 2001). These guidelines, originally for monomers and additives in plastics, provide the framework for evaluating such substances, as detailed by the EFSA CEF Panel in 2009.
The safety evaluation adhered to the principles outlined in Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004, concerning food contact materials. This regulation advises applicants to follow the SCF guidelines (EC, 2001) when preparing a dossier for the safety assessment of a substance intended for FCM authorization, detailing the necessary data. The applicant's submitted dossier for evaluation conformed to these SCF guidelines.
Safety assessment methodology involves characterizing the substance, its impurities, and degradation products. It also includes evaluating exposure via migration and determining the necessary toxicity data. This framework ensures a thorough safety review before authorization.
Assessing the safety of ingested migrating substances requires integrating toxicological hazard data with human exposure estimates. Exposure is determined by studying migration into food or simulants, assuming a daily intake of up to 1 kg of food from the contact material.
The amount of toxicological data needed for a substance's safety assessment depends on migration exposure. Higher migration levels necessitate more extensive toxicological information, with three distinct tiers defining these data requirements.
The required toxicological data for safety assessment varies with migration levels. Low migration (< 0.05 mg/kg food) needs limited data. Moderate migration (0.05-5 mg/kg food) requires a reduced dataset, while high migration (5-60 mg/kg food) necessitates extensive data.
The SCF guidelines (EC, 2001) provide comprehensive details on necessary data.
The EFSA Scientific Committee's guidance and its transparency principles for risk assessment informed this evaluation.
The applicant states that cross-linked sodium polyacrylate is designed for absorbent pads in meat, fish, and fruit packaging. Its purpose is to soak up liquids released by these foods.
The absorbent pads, designed for use at room temperature or colder, feature an outer layer of perforated plastic, such as polyethylene. Their core comprises the active substance blended with virgin fluff pulp (cellulose) for absorption.
Under Regulation (EU) 10/2011, cellulose (FCM substance No 553) is approved as a starting material and additive for food-contact plastics, without specific restrictions.
Cross-linked sodium polyacrylate serves as the active component. This substance, identified as FCM Substance No. 70, is approved for use in food-contact plastics under Regulation (EU) No 10/2011. Its authorization includes a specific migration limit of 6 mg/kg, measured as acrylic acid.
Analysis by gel permeation chromatography revealed no oligomeric species below 1,000 Da in this high molecular weight polymer. However, residual solvents and polymerization inhibitors were identified.
This substance demonstrates stability during processing and use. Its absorption capacity, determined using a saline solution, is approximately 34 grams per gram.
A perforated, liquid-permeable plastic layer prevents the substance from directly touching food.
The cross-linker, present in minimal amounts, possesses a high molecular weight. Should any unreacted residues persist within the absorber gel, they are not expected to migrate under the intended time and temperature conditions, provided direct food contact with the active substance is prevented.
Given its high liquid absorption, the substance and its residual components are not anticipated to migrate. This holds true when used as intended, provided the pad's absorption capacity isn't surpassed.
The cross-linked polymer is created from two approved components: acrylic acid, an authorized monomer, and sodium hydroxide, an authorized additive/polymer production aid. Acrylic acid has been deemed non-genotoxic through both in vitro and in vivo studies (ECB, 2002; Johannsen et al., 2008). It is regulated under EU No 10/2011 with a specific migration limit of 6 mg/kg, expressed as acrylic acid. Furthermore, the non-cross-linked form of this polymer is also an authorized food contact material additive under the same regulation.

This conclusion stems from its structural similarity to other substances previously assessed as non-genotoxic. For instance, 1,1,1-trimethylolpropane trimethacrylate, a multifunctional acrylate, was deemed non-genotoxic based on in vivo studies, including a mouse bone marrow micronucleus test and a rat hepatocyte unscheduled DNA synthesis (UDS) test (SCF, 2001). Furthermore, trimethylolpropane triacrylate showed no genotoxic effects in vitro (bacterial mutation test) or in vivo, as it did not significantly increase micronuclei frequency in mouse peripheral blood (NTP, 2005; Surh et al., 2014). Reports also indicate that various mono- and multifunctional acrylates and methacrylates do not induce genotoxic effects in vivo (Johannsen et al., 2008).
Given the expected absence of migration for the active substance, its residual components, or impurities under specified use conditions, toxicological data are deemed unnecessary.
The CEF Panel determined that cross-linked sodium polyacrylate poses no consumer safety risk when incorporated into liquid absorbent pads. This applies to the packaging of fresh or frozen meats, fish, fruits, and vegetables, at or below room temperature. Crucially, the pads must prevent direct contact between the substance and food, and their liquid absorption capacity must not be surpassed.
McAirlaid's Vliesstoffe GmbH submitted a dossier for cross-linked sodium polyacrylate in June 2014. Subsequent submissions for missing and additional information followed in April and December 2015, respectively.
