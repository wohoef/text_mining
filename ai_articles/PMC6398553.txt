This study investigated the role of α2δ1 in laryngeal cancer, focusing on its expression and impact on cancer cell behavior, given that cancer stem cells (CSCs) drive tumor development, recurrence, and spread. We initially observed α2δ1 expression across different laryngeal tissue types. Immunofluorescence staining indicated α2δ1 positivity in 81.25% (13/16) of laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) tissues, 43.75% (7/16) of paracancerous tissues, and only 12.5% (2/16) of normal tissues. Further experiments explored the characteristics of α2δ1-positive cells. Quantitative RT-PCR demonstrated that α2δ1+ LSCC cells exhibited elevated levels of genes linked to stem cell function and drug resistance compared to α2δ1- cells. Functional assays reinforced these findings: α2δ1+ cells displayed superior sphere-forming efficiency and enhanced invasive and migratory capabilities in Matrigel assays. Moreover, exposure to cisplatin or paclitaxel led to a significant increase in the proportion of α2δ1+ cells in TU686 and TU212 cell lines. In vivo tumor xenograft studies confirmed that α2δ1+ cells possessed substantially greater tumorigenicity. These results collectively suggest that a distinct α2δ1+ subpopulation within laryngeal cancer exhibits CSC-like properties, including robust self-renewal, capacity for tumor growth, differentiation, migration, invasion, and resistance to chemotherapy, positioning them as a potential therapeutic target for LSCC.

Laryngeal cancer is a prevalent head and neck malignancy, ranking as the second most common. Annually, 110,000 to 130,000 new cases are identified worldwide. From 2008 to 2012, China reported a crude incidence of 1.86/100,000 and a mortality rate of 1.01/100,000. Despite standard treatments like radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and surgery, a definitive strategy for advanced laryngeal cancer management remains elusive.
Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are key drivers of tumor development and differentiation, possessing significant self-renewal capabilities that fuel the tumorigenic process. Their role in initiating and advancing cancer makes them attractive therapeutic targets [4]. In laryngeal cancer, a small subset of CSCs has been identified as highly tumorigenic, capable of differentiating into cells that promote tumor growth and recurrence [4]. These laryngeal CSCs are typically identified by markers such as aldehyde dehydrogenase isoform 1 (ALDH1) [5], CD133 [6], and CD44 [7, 8]. However, the diverse nature of laryngeal CSCs may contribute to the overall heterogeneity observed in laryngeal cancer [9]. The α2δ1 subunit of calcium channels is known to regulate calcium oscillation amplitude [10] and has been linked to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [11]. A specific subpopulation of HCC cells expressing α2δ1 exhibits stem cell-like characteristics, showing enhanced invasiveness, self-renewal, and elevated expression of stem cell-related genes compared to unpurified cells [11, 12]. Furthermore, inhibiting the α2δ1 gene, particularly when combined with doxorubicin, has been shown to impede HCC oncogenesis [11]. More recently, research by Yu et al. indicated that α2δ1-expressing non-small cell lung cancer cells also display CSC-like properties and may contribute to resistance against chemotherapy [13].
This study investigated α2δ1's role in laryngeal cancer, a previously unexplored area. We analyzed α2δ1+ expression in laryngeal cancer tissues and assessed its impact on the migration and tumorigenic potential of laryngeal cancer cells.
This research was structured into three main phases. Initially, clinical samples from 16 laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) patients, who underwent laryngectomy without prior neoadjuvant therapy, were analyzed. This involved comparing α2δ1 expression in their primary tumors against adjacent non-cancerous tissues. The second phase utilized two LSCC cell lines, TU212 (more aggressive) and TU686 (less aggressive). Here, α2δ1+ cells were isolated via flow cytometry, and their characteristics were contrasted with α2δ1- cells. These comparisons included assessing key cancer stem cell markers (BMI1, SOX2, EPCAM, OCT4, CNTTB, KLF4, NANO, ABCG2, MDR1), sphere formation, differentiation potential, migratory and invasive capabilities, and sensitivity to cisplatin/paclitaxel. Additionally, the impact of α2δ1 knockdown via shRNAs on these features in α2δ1+ cells was investigated. The final phase involved in vivo xenograft experiments in NOD/SCID mice, where the tumor-forming and growth potential of α2δ1+ versus α2δ1- TU686 cells was evaluated through limiting dilution assays.
Sixteen male patients, who had not received prior treatment, provided laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) tissue specimens through surgical resection. These archived samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. All subjects gave written informed consent, and the Beijing Friendship Hospital's ethics committee approved the study protocol (no. 2017-P2-187-01).
To prepare for microscopic examination, frozen tissue sections were first methanol-fixed for 30 seconds. Following this, a 5% nonfat milk in PBS solution was used for blocking. The sections were then treated overnight at 4°C with a 1:100 dilution of α2δ1 monoclonal antibody (ThermoFisher Scientific, Catalog no. MA3-921), which was omitted in negative controls. Subsequently, FITC-conjugated goat-anti-mouse IgG was applied. Nuclei were visualized using DAPI counterstaining (Polysciences). Finally, slides were mounted in a 90% glycerol/PBS solution containing 2.5% 1,4-diazabicyclo (2,2,2) octane and observed under a Leica SP5 confocal microscope.
The human laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) cell lines, TU212 and TU686, were sourced from Shanghai Huiying Biological Technology. These cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 environment, utilizing RPMI 1640 medium (Invitrogen) enriched with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin (Invitrogen).
To isolate α2δ1+ cells, TU212 and TU686 cells were first washed with PBS, then separated using 0.25% trypsin. These cells were subsequently incubated for 30 minutes at 4°C with a 1:100 dilution of FITC-conjugated α2δ1 monoclonal antibody (Catalog No. MA3-921, ThermoFisher Scientific). A Facscan flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson) was then employed to sort the α2δ1+ cells. Additionally, TU686 cells were exposed to either 6 µmol/L cisplatin or 0.2 µmol/L paclitaxel for 72 hours. Following this treatment, flow cytometry was used to determine the proportion of α2δ1+ cells, with analysis performed using FlowJo and FACSCanto II software.
To analyze gene expression, total RNA was first isolated from both α2δ1+ and α2δ1- cells using Trizol reagent (Invitrogen). Subsequently, quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was employed to measure the mRNA levels of specific genes: BMI-1, SOX2, EpCAM, OCT4, CTNNB, KLF4, NANOG, ABCG2, and MDR1. This process utilized an ABI7500 PCR machine and SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Toyobo Co. Ltd., Osaka, Japan). β-actin served as the internal control for normalization, and the 2-ΔΔCt method was used to determine expression differences. Primer sequences for these genes are detailed in Table 1.
To silence human α2δ1, specific 29-mer shRNAs (shRNA53 and shRNA56) were employed. These shRNA sequences, along with a scrambled control shRNA, were obtained from Origene. The U6 promoter-driven shRNA expression cassettes were then integrated into the plenti6 lentiviral shuttle vector (Invitrogen). Lentiviral particles were subsequently packaged, used for infection, and blasticidin-resistant cell populations were selected following established protocols.
To assess sphere formation, α2δ1+ and α2δ1- cells were cultivated in 96-well plates designed for ultralow attachment. The culture medium consisted of DMEM, 1% methylcellulose, B27, 20 ng/mL bFGF, and 20 ng/mL EGF. Every other day, 200 μL of fresh supplementary medium was added. After four weeks, spheroids measuring 100 μm or larger were quantified using a microscope. Sphere-formation assays also utilized α2δ1+ cells from passage two, as well as α2δ1+ cells that had undergone treatment with shRNA53 and shRNA56.
To assess differentiation, α2δ1+ cells were cultured for one week in RPMI 1640 with 10% FBS at 37°C and 5% CO2. Subsequently, flow cytometry determined the proportion of α2δ1+ cells.
Figure 1 illustrates α2δ1 expression in primary LSCC tissues. Panel A displays representative immunofluorescence images from a T3N0M0 laryngeal cancer patient, showing α2δ1 (green) and DAPI-stained nuclei (blue) in laryngeal, paracancerous, and normal adjacent tissues. Arrows highlight α2δ1-positive cells. Panel B summarizes α2δ1 expression across these tissue types.
To assess the migratory and invasive capabilities of α2δ1+ cells, 5 × 10^4 cells were placed on an 8 µm porous membrane (BD Biosciences) pre-coated with 2 mg/mL Matrigel. Following a 48-hour incubation at 37°C, the cells were rinsed thrice with PBS and then fixed using 4% neutral formaldehyde before undergoing a 5-minute hematoxylin stain. The quantity of cells that had migrated through the membrane or invaded the Matrigel was determined by counting in 10 magnified fields (×20). SPOT imaging software (Nikon) was utilized for image capture and analysis.
NOD/SCID female mice, aged 4-6 weeks, were acquired from Huafu Kang Experimental Animal Co., Ltd (Beijing, China). These animals were housed in a SPF facility. All animal procedures adhered to the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and received approval from the Animal Care and Use Committee at Peking University Cancer Hospital.
For tumor xenograft assays, cells were prepared by suspending them in a 1:1 mixture of RPMI 1640 and Matrigel. Each mouse received injections of 102 and 103 cells into its right and left flanks. Tumor development was observed weekly. After 20 weeks, mice were euthanized with a urethane overdose. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula V=L×W2× 0.5, with L and W denoting the largest and smallest diameters.
Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 (2-sided). Differences were analyzed using Student’s t-test, χ2 test, and one-way ANOVA. Tumorigenic cell frequency was determined via extreme limiting dilution analysis, utilizing the ELDA web tool. All data processing was performed with SPSS 17.0 software.

Immunofluorescence staining showed α2δ1 was present in 13 of 16 LSCC tissue samples (81.25%). In contrast, only 7 of 16 paracancerous tissues (43.75%) and 2 of 16 normal laryngeal tissues (12.5%) were positive for α2δ1. LSCC tissues contained numerous α2δ1+ cells, while normal tissues had almost none, and paracancerous tissues had few.
Using FACS, TU686 cells were separated into α2δ1+ and α2δ1- populations. Quantitative RT-PCR analysis revealed that the α2δ1+ subpopulation exhibited significantly elevated expression of several genes compared to α2δ1- cells (P<0.05). This included stem cell-associated markers like EpCAM (3.5-fold), OCT4 (2.3-fold), and NANO (1.6-fold), as well as drug resistance genes such as BMI1 (4.5-fold) and SOX2 (3.8-fold).
The ability of α2δ1+ cells to form spheres was significantly greater than that of α2δ1- cells. This self-renewal capacity was even more pronounced in second-passage α2δ1+ cells compared to first-passage cells. Conversely, reducing α2δ1 expression via specific shRNAs markedly suppressed sphere formation in α2δ1+ cells.
When α2δ1+ cells from TU212 and TU686 lines were cultured for a week, their proportions significantly dropped (from 96.85% to 73.27% for TU212, and 92.49% to 3.08% for TU686). This indicates that α2δ1+ cells underwent differentiation into α2δ1- cells.
Matrigel assays revealed that α2δ1+ cells demonstrated superior migratory and invasive capabilities compared to α2δ1- cells.
After 72 hours of exposure to cisplatin (6 µmol/L) or paclitaxel (0.2 µmol/L), the proportion of α2δ1+ cells in TU686 cultures significantly increased. Flow cytometry revealed that treated cells had a higher percentage of purified α2δ1+ cells (cisplatin: 7.87%; paclitaxel: 10.87%) compared to the untreated control (1.72%) (Fig. 6).
In vivo experiments demonstrated that a small number (102-103) of purified α2δ1+ cells from TU212 and TU686 cell lines consistently induced tumor formation in nearly all recipient mice (Fig. 7A–E). Conversely, α2δ1- cells rarely formed tumors, and when they did, these were only small nodules, or they were entirely non-tumorigenic (Fig. 7A–E). This enhanced tumorigenicity of α2δ1+ cells was further validated in passage 2 cells (Fig. 7F and G), where α2δ1- cells from passage 2 failed to produce any discernible tumors within 12 weeks (Fig. 7F and G). Additionally, silencing α2δ1 expression in α2δ1+ TU686 cells using shRNA53 and shRNA56 significantly reduced tumor xenograft development (Fig. 7H–J).

The α2δ1 subunit, a component of voltage-gated calcium (Cav) channels, plays a role in various cellular processes including synaptic input integration, calcium-dependent signaling, neuronal migration, and the release of neurotransmitters and hormones. Previous research has established α2δ1 expression in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Studies on HCC cells demonstrated that reducing α2δ1 levels led to tumor-initiating cell apoptosis and hindered tumor formation and self-renewal. Conversely, increased α2δ1 expression promoted sphere formation, suggesting that α2δ1+ HCC cells exhibit stem cell-like characteristics. Our current investigation similarly identified α2δ1 expression within a subpopulation of laryngeal cancer cells (TU212 and TU686), indicating that these α2δ1+ LSCC cells also possess stem cell-like properties.
Our investigation revealed that α2δ1 serves as a marker for a cancer stem cell (CSC) subpopulation in laryngeal cancer. Immunofluorescence staining of 16 laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) samples demonstrated a significantly higher presence of α2δ1+ cells in cancerous tissue compared to adjacent non-cancerous and normal tissues, aligning with observations in other cancers. Furthermore, quantitative PCR analysis of α2δ1+ TU686 cells indicated elevated expression of several stem cell-associated genes (BMI-1, SOX2, EpCAM, OCT4, CTNNB1, ABCG2). These findings collectively suggest that α2δ1+ cells exhibit CSC-like properties, including self-renewal, differentiation capacity, and resistance to conventional chemotherapy.
The study also identified an α2δ1+ subpopulation within both TU212 and TU686 cell lines. When cultured for four weeks in serum-free, growth factor-enriched, non-adherent conditions, these purified α2δ1+ cells demonstrated the capacity to form undifferentiated cancer spheres. This sphere-forming ability was considerably diminished in α2δ1- cells under identical conditions. Furthermore, individual α2δ1+ cells could generate spheres with almost unlimited growth potential, allowing for expansion through serial passages. These findings suggest that α2δ1 is vital for sphere formation in TU212 and TU686 cells, aligning with prior research showing that α2δ1 Cav channel overexpression boosts sphere formation in HCC cells. Given that self-renewal is a hallmark of cancer stem cells, these observations imply that the α2δ1+ cells in TU212 and TU686 lines possess CSC-like properties.
The α2δ1+ subpopulation in TU212 and TU686 cells demonstrated enhanced differentiation, migration, and invasion capabilities compared to α2δ1- cells. Notably, α2δ1+ cells could differentiate into both α2δ1+ and α2δ1- cells, a property absent in α2δ1- cells. These observed characteristics align with known features of cancer stem cells.
Our findings, mirroring a prior study on α2δ1+ lung cancer cells, demonstrate that exposing TU686 cells to cisplatin and paclitaxel elevates the proportion of α2δ1+ cells. This suggests that laryngeal cancer cells expressing α2δ1 exhibit resistance to these chemotherapeutic drugs. We hypothesize that cisplatin and paclitaxel induce apoptosis or necrosis in α2δ1- cells, while α2δ1+ cells remain unaffected by their tumor-killing action. Consequently, the relative abundance of α2δ1+ cells among the surviving cell population increases, effectively enriching for cancer stem cells. Future research will investigate this hypothesis further by examining whether the shift in α2δ1+ cell ratio is dependent on the duration and dosage of chemotherapeutic agents. The observed resistance of α2δ1+ cells to cisplatin/paclitaxel aligns with the known characteristic of cancer stem cells to resist standard therapies.
The role of α2δ1 in modulating the amplitude of calcium oscillations in cancer stem cells has been established in prior research. While spontaneous calcium oscillations are observed in excitable tissues like muscles, nerve cells, embryonic stem cells, and immature dendritic cells, their precise regulatory mechanisms and biological functions are largely unknown. These calcium oscillations, a significant form of calcium signaling, can influence specific gene expression, with their impact tied to the intensity and duration of calcium transients. This process may involve transcription factors remaining at high nuclear levels, allowing them to bind to enhancer sites and initiate transcription. We propose that α2δ1 might contribute to an "amplitude-encoding" signal in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC), thereby preserving its stem cell characteristics.
The tumorigenic potential of α2δ1+ cells was evident in vivo, as as few as 100 purified cells were sufficient to initiate tumor growth in xenograft models. Conversely, α2δ1- cells rarely formed tumors, or only produced small nodules. Further in vivo experiments, involving serial inoculations of re-sorted cells from α2δ1+-derived tumors, reinforced the finding that only α2δ1+ cells possessed tumorigenic capabilities. Furthermore, reducing α2δ1 expression in purified α2δ1+ TU686 cells through shRNA significantly inhibited sphere formation and diminished the number of tumorigenic cells. These findings highlight α2δ1's crucial role in LSCC sphere formation and tumorigenicity, both in vitro and in vivo, suggesting its potential as a marker for LSCC cancer stem cells. However, its precise involvement in LSCC development, progression, and spread warrants additional investigation.
This study identified an α2δ1+ subpopulation in laryngeal cancer possessing cancer stem cell-like properties. Further research is needed to elucidate the precise mechanisms by which α2δ1 mediates these CSC-like effects in laryngeal cancer.
