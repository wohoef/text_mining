Previous research into thymidylate synthase (TS) expression in colorectal cancers (CRCs) has not yielded consistent prognostic or predictive insights. This study aimed to evaluate the prognostic value of TS expression in patients with Astler-Coller stage B2 and C CRCs, specifically those defined by a p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype and undergoing 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-based adjuvant therapy. A group of 189 CRCs was analyzed for TS, p21WAF1, and p53 expression using immunohistochemistry on tissue microarrays. The study then assessed the impact of these markers on disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) through univariate and multivariate analyses. The results indicated that TS expression could stratify patients with colon cancer, but not rectal cancer, who possessed immunophenotypes other than the p21WAF1+/p53- profile. These patients were divided into subgroups with either a worse prognosis (P&P TS+) or a better prognosis (P&P TS-) for both DFS and OS, as demonstrated by univariate and multivariate analyses (P = 0.006 and P = 0.005 for univariate; P = 0.0004 and P = 0.002 for multivariate). Conversely, the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype was consistently linked to a favorable prognosis, regardless of TS expression levels. The strong correlation between the P&P TS+ immunophenotype and poorer DFS and OS suggests that TS expression may hold predictive value for 5FU-based adjuvant therapy in colon cancer patients with this specific immunophenotype. Furthermore, the findings suggest that TS expression is most relevant as a prognostic or predictive marker in colon cancers with immunophenotypes other than p21WAF1+/p53-. In this particular subgroup, elevated TS expression is associated with unfavorable DFS and OS. Consequently, combining TS expression assessment with p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotyping in colon cancers could potentially enhance the selection of patients who would benefit from 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy.

Despite advancements in colorectal cancer (CRC) systemic treatment with new drugs, fluoropyrimidine analogs continue to be vital components in chemotherapy regimens for enhancing therapeutic outcomes. Nevertheless, the appropriate use of adjuvant therapy for stage II CRC patients remains a subject of debate. Some suggest that 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-based adjuvant chemotherapy offers a cure for only a small fraction of stage II CRC patients, highlighting the necessity for predictive markers of 5FU response. The mechanism of 5FU-based therapy involves inhibiting thymidylate synthase (TS), a crucial enzyme in nucleotide synthesis, which then triggers p53-dependent apoptosis via cell cycle regulators. This understanding, supported by in vitro studies, prompted clinical trials to investigate TS's role in 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy. However, these trials have yielded inconsistent and unsatisfactory results, failing to offer clear prognostic or predictive information, leading to TS studies in CRC being described as a "never-ending story." A potential explanation for this lack of definitive findings is the infrequent consideration of other important cell cycle regulatory proteins, besides p53, in studies aiming to identify patient subgroups where TS might be relevant.
This study investigated the prognostic value of thymidylate synthase (TS) expression in patients with Astler-Coller stage B2 and C colorectal cancers (CRCs) who received 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-based adjuvant chemotherapy, considering their p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype. Our results indicate that TS expression is a relevant prognostic and predictive marker specifically within a subset of colon cancers that do not exhibit the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype. In this particular subgroup, elevated TS expression was linked to poorer disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS).
A cohort of 189 consecutive patients undergoing potentially curative resection for sporadic colorectal cancer (CRC) formed the study group. These patients had no prior chemotherapy, no distant metastases confirmed by imaging and intraoperative assessment, and a histopathologic diagnosis of invasive adenocarcinoma, Astler-Coller stage B2 or C, with clear resection margins. All participants received the same adjuvant 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-based therapy.
The study group comprised 189 patients whose histological slides were re-examined. Table 1 provides a summary of the clinico-pathological characteristics of these 189 tumors and patients. The mean age of the participants was 59.1 years, with a range from 33 to 81 years and a median of 60 years. Surgeries were performed at the Pomeranian Medical University teaching hospitals and the Regional Oncological Center in Szczecin, Poland. Surgical procedures included either resection with lymphadenectomy or total mesorectal excision for rectal cancers. All 189 patients, consisting of 76 with stage B2 and 113 with stage C disease, received identical adjuvant chemotherapy. This regimen involved six 5-day courses of bolus 5-fluorouracil (5FU) infusions at 425 mg/m², administered every four weeks, alongside leucovorin (20 mg/m²).
Radiotherapy was administered to a portion of the rectal cancer patients. Specifically, 40 of the 93 rectal cancer patients received postoperative radiotherapy (50.4 Gy), and 32 received preoperative radiotherapy (5 × 5 Gy). Among the 76 Astler-Coller stage B2 tumors, 36 were located in the rectum, with 15 (42%) receiving preoperative and 10 (28%) receiving postoperative radiotherapy. Crucially, no statistically significant differences in the expression of TS, p53, or p21WAF1 were observed between rectal cancers from patients who underwent preoperative radiotherapy and those who did not. Therefore, patients who received preoperative radiotherapy were included in the study.
Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery until death from cancer or the last follow-up. Disease-free survival (DFS) was the time until the first metastasis or local recurrence. The study cohort was followed for a median of 51 months (mean 51.1 ± 23.8 months, range 7–120 months). During this period, 43 patients (22.8%) died from their disease, while 115 (60.8%) remained alive and disease-free. A total of 74 patients experienced recurrences. Four deaths unrelated to cancer were treated as censored observations.
Tumor tissue was preserved in buffered 10% formalin and then embedded in paraffin. Histopathological diagnoses were made from 4 μm thick sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Tissue microarrays were prepared by taking a single 0.6 mm core from a carefully selected, histologically uniform, representative region at the outer invasive edge of each colorectal cancer, as previously detailed.
Following deparaffinization and rehydration, endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched. Antigen retrieval was achieved by immersing the slides in pH 9.0 buffer and applying heat in a pressure cooker. Subsequently, the slides were incubated for 30 minutes with monoclonal antibodies targeting p21WAF1 (1:25 dilution), TS106 (1:50 dilution), and p53 (1:50 dilution). Immunostaining was conducted using a Dako EnVision kit, following the manufacturer's protocol. The EnVision™ + Peroxidase Anti-mouse Polymer system was employed for its sensitivity, attributed to signal amplification via HRP-labeled polymers, which surpasses methods lacking this amplification layer. The reaction was visualized with diaminobenzidine substrate-chromogen, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining. Positive and negative controls were incorporated. All immunohistochemical procedures for the 189 tumors were performed concurrently under identical conditions, utilizing four slides containing tissue cores from every tumor.
To evaluate DNA mismatch repair (MMR) protein status, immunohistochemistry was conducted on 185 colorectal cancer (CRC) specimens, as four samples were insufficient for analysis. This method, using antibodies against MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and PMS2, is considered equivalent to microsatellite instability (MSI) testing. Defective DNA mismatch repair (dMMR) was identified by the absence of nuclear staining for these proteins, provided stromal cells showed positive staining. The staining procedures were carried out using a Benchmark XT automated stainer from Ventana, following the manufacturer's instructions.
Two observers, PD and WD, independently evaluated the immunohistochemical staining of each tumor core. They were unaware of the clinical and pathological information associated with the samples. Any discrepancies in their assessments were resolved through consensus. For each tissue microarray core, all tumor cells were counted. The percentage of tumor cell nuclei exhibiting clear staining was then recorded. p21WAF1 expression was categorized as negative if fewer than 1% of tumor nuclei were positive (p21-), and positive if 1% or more showed nuclear immunoreactivity (p21+). A histoscore system was employed for the assessment of p53 and TS expression. This system, previously demonstrated to be effective and reproducible for immunohistochemical analysis, incorporated both the average intensity of staining and the pattern of expression. The proportion of positive cells was estimated and assigned a score from 1 to 6, representing ranges from 1% to 4% (score 1) up to 80% to 100% (score 6). Simultaneously, the average intensity of positively stained tumor cells was scored from 0 (no staining) to 3 (strong staining). A final histoscore was derived by multiplying the percentage score by the intensity score, resulting in a range from 0 to 18. To stratify p53 expression, a histoscore of 7 was established as the cutoff, distinguishing between low (≤ 7, p53-) and high (>7, p53+) expression. This cutoff was chosen because the histogram of p53 values exhibited a local minimum at this point, effectively dividing the patient population into two distinct groups. Likewise, TS expression was stratified into low (< 2, TS-) and high (≥ 2, TS+) based on a similar local minimum observed in the histoscore histogram.
To analyze the relationships between tumor immunophenotype and other categorical variables, Fisher's exact test was employed. Associations with patient age were examined using the Mann-Whitney test. Survival analyses were conducted with the Kaplan-Meier method for univariate assessments, with log-rank tests determining differences between groups. Both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were utilized to evaluate factors influencing overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). The models incorporated variables such as age, gender, tumor site, Astler-Coller stage, histological grade, and the expression of the studied tumor markers. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. Analyses were performed using STATISTICA version 10, adhering to REMARK guidelines.
The expression levels of p53, TS, and p21WAF1 were evaluated using immunohistochemical staining of tumor cell nuclei. High nuclear p53 expression (p53+) was observed in 60.3% of the 189 samples analyzed, with a histoscore greater than 7. The distribution of these p53 histoscores is detailed in Figure 2. Similarly, high TS expression (TS+) was detected in the nuclei of tumor cells in 67.2% of the samples (127 out of 189), characterized by a histoscore of 2 or higher, as illustrated in Figure 3. Nuclear p21WAF1 expression (p21+) was present in 66.7% of the specimens. Furthermore, the study identified the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype in 50 out of 185 samples, representing 26.5% of the assessed cases. The presence of loss of DNA mismatch repair (MMR) proteins was noted in 31 out of 185 samples, accounting for 16.8%. Representative images of p53, TS, and p21 immunostaining are provided in Figure 1.
The study examined the correlation between TS, p53, and p21WAF1 expression levels and various patient and tumor characteristics, including age, gender, tumor grade, stage, site, and preoperative radiotherapy. Results indicated that high TS expression was more prevalent in colon tumors than rectal tumors (75.0% vs. 59.1%, P = 0.03). Males exhibited higher p53 expression more often than females (68.6% vs. 50.6%, P = 0.02). Stage C colorectal cancers showed a higher frequency of p21WAF1 expression compared to stage B2 (72.6% vs. 57.9%, P = 0.04). Furthermore, the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype was observed more frequently in females than in males (34.5% vs. 19.6%, P = 0.03). No other significant associations were identified.
In a univariate analysis of all patients with Astler-Coller stage B2 and C colorectal cancer, high thymidylate synthase (TS) expression correlated with significantly worse overall survival (OS) (P = 0.038, HR = 2.26). Disease-free survival (DFS) showed a trend in the same direction but did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.082, HR = 1.59). Conversely, p21WAF1 expression was linked to significantly better OS (P = 0.0076, HR = 0.44) and a borderline improvement in DFS (P = 0.076, HR = 0.66). High p53 expression, however, was associated with significantly worse DFS (P = 0.029, HR = 1.74), though its association with OS did not reach significance (P = 0.099, HR = 1.73). The p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype, characterized by p21WAF1 expression and low p53, demonstrated a strong association with both significantly better OS (P = 0.0057, HR = 0.23) and DFS (P = 0.0023, HR = 0.36). For patients with combined phenotypes other than p21WAF1+/p53- (referred to as P&P, encompassing p21WAF1+/p53+, p21WAF1-/p53+, and p21WAF1-/p53-), univariate analysis indicated that high TS expression was significantly associated with worse DFS (P = 0.02, HR = 1.90) and OS (P = 0.009, HR = 2.99).
Initially, loss of mismatch repair (MMR) protein expression was linked to improved disease-free survival (DFS), though not overall survival (OS). However, a subsequent multivariate analysis, which incorporated factors such as sex, age, Astler-Coller stage, tumor grade, tumor site, TS expression, and the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype, revealed different independent predictors for both DFS and OS. In this comprehensive analysis, Astler-Coller stage, high TS expression, and the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype emerged as significant factors. Notably, the association between loss of MMR protein expression and DFS lost its statistical significance within this multivariate model. The table presents the results of this multivariate analysis for stage B2 and C colorectal cancer patients undergoing 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy.
Survival analyses using Kaplan-Meier curves revealed that patients possessing the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype demonstrated the most favorable disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). Within this specific subgroup, thymidylate synthase (TS) expression levels did not significantly impact survival outcomes. However, for patients with the "other" immunophenotype (referred to as P&P), which encompasses all combinations except p21WAF1+/p53-, TS expression played a crucial role in stratifying survival. These P&P patients were divided into two distinct groups based on their TS levels. Those with high TS expression (P&P TS+) experienced significantly worse DFS and OS compared to patients with low TS expression (P&P TS-). Further comparisons showed that the P&P TS+ group had notably poorer survival rates than other immunophenotype combinations, including the p21WAF1+/p53- group, regardless of TS expression. These survival differences were statistically significant across DFS and OS. These observations were corroborated by multivariate analysis, which confirmed that the P&P TS+ immunophenotype was independently associated with a substantially worse DFS and OS when compared to all other immunophenotype categories. This indicates that the combination of the P&P phenotype and high TS expression is a strong indicator of a poorer prognosis in patients receiving 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy.
Therapeutic approaches differ for colon and rectal cancers. To determine if tumor site influenced our findings, patients with colon and rectal tumors were analyzed independently.
In patients with colon cancer, similar to the overall cohort, thymidylate synthase (TS) expression did not significantly stratify outcomes for those with the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype, a group already associated with the best disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). However, for colon tumors exhibiting phenotypes other than p21WAF1+/p53- (referred to as the P&P group), TS expression emerged as a significant stratifying factor for both DFS and OS, as illustrated in Figures 6 and 7. Specifically, within the P&P colon cancer subgroup, patients with high TS expression (P&P TS+) demonstrated a worse DFS (P = 0.006) and OS (P = 0.005) compared to those with low TS expression (P&P TS-). Further analysis revealed that the survival curves for the P&P TS+ group significantly differed from other immunophenotype combinations. This included comparisons with the p21WAF1+/p53- TS+ group (DFS: P = 0.0005; OS: P = 0.005) and the p21WAF1+/p53- TS- group (DFS: P = 0.007; OS: P = 0.003). These findings were consistently supported by multivariate analyses, which indicated that the P&P TS+ immunophenotype was associated with a substantially worse DFS (P = 0.0004, Hazard Ratio = 4.93) and OS (P = 0.002, Hazard Ratio = 10.55) when compared to all other immunophenotype combinations. This association remained robust even after excluding cases with loss of mismatch repair (MMR) protein expression from the analysis, with DFS showing a P = 0.0008 (HR = 4.80, 95% CI 1.92–12.03) and OS showing a P = 0.004 (HR = 8.82, 95% CI 1.99–39.03). This suggests that TS expression, in conjunction with the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype, plays a critical role in predicting outcomes for patients with colon cancer receiving 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-based adjuvant chemotherapy, particularly in those not exhibiting the favorable p21WAF1+/p53- profile. The data strongly suggest that TS expression is a predictive marker for 5FU-based adjuvant therapy in this specific subgroup of colon cancer patients.
For rectal cancer patients (n=93), the P&P/TS+ immunophenotype showed no significant correlation with disease-free survival (DFS) or overall survival (OS), as confirmed by both univariate and multivariate analyses. Preoperative radiotherapy did not significantly alter the prevalence of the P&P/TS+ immunophenotype. However, a significant interaction was observed between tumor site (colon vs. rectum) and the P&P/TS+ immunophenotype's association with DFS and OS in multivariate analysis. This interaction suggests that TS expression, when considered alongside the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype, could only stratify prognostic subgroups in colon cancer patients receiving adjuvant 5FU therapy, but not in rectal cancer patients.
For patients with stage C colorectal cancer (CRC), 5-fluorouracil (5FU)-based adjuvant chemotherapy has demonstrated a 41% reduction in recurrence risk and a 33% decrease in overall mortality. However, its impact on stage II CRC is more modest, with a mere 3–6% improvement in 5-year survival rates. Even in advanced CRC, survival gains from various treatments, including 5FU combinations, have been limited, despite initial improvements in response rates. This suggests that a significant proportion of patients may not benefit from 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy and are consequently exposed to its toxic side effects. Therefore, identifying specific patient subgroups who will and will not benefit from this treatment is crucial. Our prior research indicated that p21WAF1 expression in colorectal tumor cells could identify Astler-Coller stage B2 patients who significantly benefit from 5FU-based chemotherapy, thereby improving patient selection for adjuvant therapy. This current study further demonstrates that analyzing TS expression alongside p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotyping enables the identification of prognostic subgroups within CRC patients receiving 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy.
In our study, 67.2% of colorectal cancer (CRC) samples exhibited high nuclear thymidylate synthase (TS) expression, aligning with previous findings by Wong et al. (69%) [18]. TS, a crucial enzyme in DNA synthesis, is a primary target of 5-fluorouracil (5FU) [19] and possesses oncogenic properties [20]. The therapeutic action of 5FU is multifaceted, involving TS inhibition and direct disruption of DNA and RNA synthesis via the incorporation of 5-fluoronucleotides [2]. While in vitro studies using CRC cell lines have indicated that TS expression may predict the effectiveness of 5FU-based chemotherapy [21], clinical trials investigating its predictive or prognostic value in CRC patients have yielded conflicting results. Meta-analyses suggest that high TS expression has a moderately adverse effect on CRC patient survival [22] and a slight negative correlation with 5FU response [23]. Consequently, it has been concluded that TS expression alone is insufficient as a predictive marker in clinical practice [23]. Despite its established role as a 5FU target, TS has not become a clinically useful predictive marker for 5FU response [24–26]. Research using CRC cell lines has also suggested that resistance to 5FU chemotherapy is not solely attributable to TS or p53 expression [27]. More recent findings emphasize the necessity of incorporating cell cycle-related factors when searching for predictive markers that benefit 5FU-based CRC chemotherapy [6, 28]. This suggests that identifying CRC subgroups responsive to 5FU treatment likely requires considering multiple factors beyond just TS.
Multivariate analysis revealed that thymidylate synthase (TS) expression, when considered in isolation, did not significantly correlate with disease-free survival (DFS) or overall survival (OS). However, this changed when the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype was incorporated into the analysis. Specifically, in colon cancer patients whose tumors displayed an immunophenotype other than p21WAF1+/p53-, TS expression became a significant prognostic and predictive marker. In this particular subgroup, high TS expression was linked to unfavorable DFS and OS. This suggests that the combined assessment of these markers can offer prognostic and predictive insights into the efficacy of 5FU-based chemotherapy. For colon cancer patients with phenotypes other than p21WAF1+/p53-, a low TS expression appears to indicate a greater likelihood of benefiting from 5FU-based adjuvant therapy. Conversely, high TS expression in this group suggests a poorer prognosis, even with treatment, implying a lack of benefit from 5FU. In contrast, for colon cancer patients with the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype, which represents the most favorable prognosis, TS expression did not stratify outcomes regarding DFS or OS. Therefore, TS expression is unlikely to serve as a prognostic or predictive marker for 5FU-based adjuvant therapy within this specific subgroup.
For rectal cancer patients receiving adjuvant 5FU, TS expression, in conjunction with the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype, did not stratify them into distinct prognostic subgroups. Radiotherapy, which is often administered to rectal tumors, may influence these findings. Additionally, the observed site-specific differences might contribute to this lack of stratification. Further research with larger patient cohorts is necessary to resolve these uncertainties.
Previous research on the association between the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype and survival in colorectal cancer (CRC) has yielded inconsistent findings. While some studies align with our observations, reporting the poorest survival in patients with p21WAF1-/p53+ tumors and the best in those with p21WAF1+/p53- tumors, others have indicated a favorable prognosis for low p21WAF1 expression combined with high p53 expression. A study by Ropponen et al. even found no correlation between the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype and survival. These discrepancies may stem from variations in patient cohorts, including tumor stage and adjuvant therapy types, as well as the limited sample sizes within subgroups. However, they could also highlight the complex, multifaceted role of the p53-p21WAF1 cell cycle checkpoint pathway, which is crucial for maintaining genomic stability. Our findings suggest that the efficacy of 5FU-based treatment in CRC patients is influenced by the status of this pathway. Patients with tumors exhibiting a functional p53-p21WAF1 pathway (p21WAF1+/p53-) demonstrated the best survival outcomes, irrespective of TS expression. This implies that 5FU-based adjuvant therapy, targeting TS, did not improve their prognosis, likely because they already possessed the most favorable outlook. Conversely, in cases where the p53-p21WAF1 pathway was compromised (in the "P&P" subgroup, encompassing p21WAF1+/p53+, p21WAF1-/p53+, or p21WAF1-/p53- tumors), only patients with low TS expression appeared to benefit from this type of adjuvant treatment.
Our investigation revealed that both the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype and the loss of MMR protein expression served as prognostic indicators for disease-free survival (DFS) in univariate analyses. However, upon multivariate analysis, only the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype maintained statistical significance. This suggests that the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype is a more robust predictor of DFS compared to MMR protein loss in patients with stage B and C Astler-Coller colorectal cancer receiving adjuvant 5FU therapy. This finding aligns with other research where, despite both mutant TP53 and MSI-H showing prognostic value in univariate analyses, only TP53 remained significant in multivariate assessments. Collectively, these results highlight the critical role of the p53-p21WAF1 cell cycle checkpoint pathway in determining the prognosis for colorectal cancer patients undergoing 5FU-based treatment.
This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. Firstly, its retrospective nature and relatively small patient cohort necessitate confirmation of the findings through prospective randomized trials. Secondly, the selection of cut-off levels for marker expression requires further discussion. For p21WAF1, a threshold of ≥1% positive tumor nuclei was employed, consistent with our prior research, and this level was associated with improved disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) in colorectal cancer (CRC) patients. However, universally accepted cut-off values for nuclear p53 and thymidylate synthase (TS) expression are not established. Consequently, we examined local minima identified in the histograms of their expression levels, selecting those that demonstrated the strongest associations with survival outcomes. The assessment of p53 expression in CRC has utilized a variety of threshold levels across different studies. For instance, 14 reports on the prognostic significance of p53 accumulation employed cut-offs ranging from >0% to >20%, resulting in p53-positive tumor percentages between 30% and 63%. Other studies have used thresholds of 25% [37], >50% [38], or ≥50% [39], identifying 63%, 48.5%, and 43% of tumors as p53-positive, respectively. Our chosen cut-off for p53 (histoscore > 7) identified high nuclear p53 expression in 60.3% of samples, which falls within the reported range. Similarly, diverse scoring methods and threshold levels exist for immunohistochemical TS expression in CRC. These include arbitrary grading of staining intensity from 0 to 3 or 4, dichotomized into low (0-1) and high (3-4) levels, as reviewed in multiple studies. Other approaches involve dichotomizing TS expression based on arbitrary percentages of stained cells, such as 10%, 15%, 20% [22], or ≥30% [42]. Histoscores have also been calculated [18, 43]. Across these reports, the proportion of cases with high TS expression ranges from 14% to 80%. Our finding of high TS expression in 67.2% of CRCs is consistent with this spectrum. The lack of standardized cut-off levels highlights the empirical nature of their selection in such studies, with a histoscore approach potentially offering a more nuanced reflection of biological associations than simple percentage counts.
The precise percentage of tumor cells that must express these markers to impact patient survival with 5FU-based adjuvant therapy remains unclear. Consequently, cut-off levels for positivity are typically determined empirically, with common thresholds including 5%, 10%, 20%, or 50%. However, a histoscore, as utilized in this study, appears to offer a more accurate reflection of biological associations compared to simply counting positive cells. Evidence from Wong et al. [18] suggests that assessing TS expression via a histoscore revealed significant differences in response to 5FU-based therapy in CRC patients, a distinction not observed when only the percentage of positive nuclei was considered. Their study employed a median histoscore as a threshold, a strategy similar to our approach of using a cut-off near the median. Further complicating the issue, a comprehensive review by Munro et al. [44] encompassing 168 reports and survival data from over 18,000 patients found no consistent relationship between the criteria used to define immunohistochemical positivity for p53 abnormalities and clinical outcomes. For instance, the survival rate difference remained virtually identical whether the cut-off was set at >1% or >10% of positive cells.
Utilizing tissue microarrays for immunohistochemistry offers a significant advantage by allowing simultaneous assessment of numerous tumors under standardized conditions, thereby enhancing result reproducibility. However, a key limitation of this method is the potential for small tissue cores to not fully represent the entire tumor. Consequently, meticulous selection of a typical and representative tumor area is paramount during tissue microarray construction. In this study, we utilized a single core from a carefully chosen, histologically uniform region at the outer invasive margin of each colorectal cancer (CRC), characterized by high mitotic activity. This methodology yielded high expression rates for p53 (60.3%), TS (67.2%), and p21WAF1 (66.7%) in CRCs, figures consistent with existing literature ranges for these proteins (30%–63% for p53, 15%–80% for TS, and 16%–87% for p21WAF1).
The optimal number of tissue cores for establishing associations between biomarkers and clinicopathological parameters is a subject of debate. Research by Hoos et al. [45] indicated that correlations between phenotypes and clinical outcomes showed no significant difference whether full tissue sections or triplicate 0.6-mm core tissue microarrays were used. This finding was further supported by studies showing similar correlations when only a single 0.6-mm core tissue microarray was employed [46, 47]. Zhang et al. [46] reported over 97% concordance rates between tissue microarrays and full sections for multiple markers, leading them to conclude that "a tissue microarray with a single core per specimen ensures full biological representativeness to identify associations between biomarkers and clinicopathological parameters, with no significantly associated sampling bias." The degree of concordance between tissue microarrays and whole sections is generally higher when tumor cells exhibit lower heterogeneity concerning the proteins being studied. Investigations into the intratumoral variation of TS nuclear expression in colorectal cancers (CRCs) by Wong et al. [18] revealed it to be relatively minor, with a maximum TS histoscore difference of 15 within a range of 0 to 300. Similarly, another report noted only slight intratumor heterogeneity of TS expression [42]. Nevertheless, the meticulous selection of representative tumor regions is paramount in the construction of tissue microarrays. Zhang et al. [46] emphasized that a 0.6-mm biopsy from a typical and representative tumor area offers a dependable and efficient method for large-scale analysis of cancer tissues on tissue microarray platforms, proving valuable for extensive clinicopathological studies by preserving the molecular profile of clinically relevant markers.
Our findings indicate that for patients with stage B2 and C colon cancer receiving 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy, the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype is linked to a favorable prognosis, irrespective of thymidylate synthase (TS) expression levels. Furthermore, TS expression plays a role in stratifying patients with colon cancer who do not exhibit the p21WAF1+/p53- immunophenotype. These patients can be divided into two groups based on TS levels: those with high TS expression face a worse prognosis, while those with low TS expression have a better outlook. The significant correlation between the "P&P TS+" immunophenotype and poorer disease-free survival (DFS), observed in both univariate and multivariate analyses, suggests that TS expression may predict treatment response to 5FU-based adjuvant therapy in colon cancer patients with this specific immunophenotype. Consequently, future clinical trials investigating the predictive value of TS expression in colon cancer for 5FU-based adjuvant chemotherapy should incorporate the p21WAF1/p53 immunophenotype of tumor cells. Given the retrospective nature of this study, these conclusions necessitate confirmation through additional prospective randomized studies.
