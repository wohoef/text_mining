While EGFLAM has been identified as a potential biomarker in several cancer types, its role in glioblastoma (GBM) remained unexplored. This study aimed to elucidate EGFLAM's functional significance in GBM. We began by analyzing EGFLAM expression and its clinical relevance in GBM patients using data from the TCGA and Oncomine databases. Subsequently, we investigated the biological impact of EGFLAM in GBM cells through a series of experiments, including qRT-PCR, CCK-8 assays, colony formation assays, wound healing assays, transwell assays, and western blot analysis. Our database analysis revealed that elevated EGFLAM expression in GBM patients was associated with a poorer prognosis. Furthermore, silencing EGFLAM in U87 cells demonstrably suppressed proliferation, migration, and invasion, a process regulated by the downregulation of the PI3K/AKT pathway. These findings collectively suggest that EGFLAM holds promise as a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target for GBM.

Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), the most aggressive brain tumor, accounts for half of all gliomas and is characterized by a dismal prognosis. Survival rates are exceptionally low, with only five percent of patients living beyond five years post-diagnosis, according to the CBTRUS. Standard treatment involves surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, primarily temozolomide (TMZ). However, the diffuse nature of glioma infiltration compromises surgical effectiveness, and GBM often develops resistance to radiotherapy. While TMZ has offered some improvement over radiotherapy alone, median survival remains poor, at just 14-16 months. Consequently, targeted therapy has emerged as a promising new avenue for GBM treatment. This underscores the critical need for novel and effective biomarkers to guide targeted therapies, ultimately aiming to enhance patient survival and quality of life.
The EGFLAM gene, situated on chromosomal region 5p13.2-p13.1, is expressed in various tissues, including the brain, endocrine tissues, and muscle tissues. Known also as Pikachurin, its protein product localizes with dystrophin and dystroglycan at ribbon synapses, playing a crucial role in the interaction between photoreceptor ribbon synapses and bipolar dendrites. EGFLAM has been implicated in impaired photoreceptor synaptic function, particularly in congenital muscular dystrophies like muscle-eye-brain disease, which stems from defective α-dystroglycan glycosylation. Beyond its neurological and muscular roles, EGFLAM has emerged as a novel gene biomarker in several cancers. Research by Yamada et al. identified EGFLAM as a susceptibility locus for aortic aneurysms. Furthermore, Gu et al. observed a significant decrease in EGFLAM methylation in ovarian cancer tissues compared to benign ovarian diseases, suggesting its potential as a specific hypomethylated tumor marker for ovarian cancer. These diverse findings prompted our investigation into EGFLAM's potential as a valuable biomarker and therapeutic target for glioblastoma multiforme (GBM).
We assessed EGFLAM's aberrant expression and clinical significance in databases, then investigated its in vitro biological function and underlying mechanism.
Gene expression data for 169 GBM and 5 normal brain tissues were sourced from the TCGA database. Additionally, clinical-pathological characteristics of GBM patients were acquired from the same TCGA database.
EGFLAM expression in 22 GBM and 3 normal brain tissues was further analyzed using the Oncomine database, following all relevant regulations.
Human cell lines, including HEB, U87, U251, and HS683, were utilized. HEB cells were sourced from Sun Yat-Sen University, while U87 and U251 cells came from Shanghai Institutes for Biological Sciences. HS683 cells were obtained from ATCC. All cell lines were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% serum, penicillin (100 U/ml), and streptomycin (0.1 mg/ml) at 37°C with 5% CO2.
To quantify EGFLAM expression, total RNA was isolated from cells using TRIZOL reagent. The concentration and purity of the RNA were assessed with a Nanodrop spectrophotometer. Subsequently, RNA was converted to cDNA via reverse transcription, following the manufacturer's instructions for the Reverse Transcription Kit. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was then performed using SYBR Select Master Mix. Data acquisition was conducted on a QuantStudio™ 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System. Amplification involved an initial denaturation at 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 30 seconds, 60°C for 40 seconds, and 72°C for 1 minute. GAPDH served as the internal control, and all samples were analyzed in triplicate. Relative expression levels were determined using the 2-ΔΔCT method. The specific primer sequences used for EGFLAM were: forward, 5’-AGGAAGGAGGGCTATGAC TGT-3’, and reverse, 5’-AGGCAGTAGTTCCCACAC TCT-3’. For GAPDH, the primers were: forward, 5’-GGAGCGAGATCCCTC CAAAAT-3’, and reverse, 5’-GGCTGTTGTCATACT TCTCATGG-3’.
U87 cells were plated in six-well plates at a density of 1 × 10⁵ cells/well and incubated for 24 hours. Subsequently, cells were transfected with either a specific siRNA targeting EGFLAM (si-EGFLAM) or a non-specific control siRNA (si-con) using Lipofectamine 2000. Following a 24-hour transfection period, the cells were harvested for subsequent experiments.
To assess colony formation, transfected U87 cells were plated at 200 cells per well in six-well plates. Following a 14-day incubation period, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 minutes and subsequently stained with 0.1% crystal violet for another 30 minutes. The number of colonies was then counted and imaged using a microscope. Colony-forming efficiency was determined by calculating the ratio of the number of clones to the number of seeded cells, multiplied by 100%. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate.
To assess cell viability, transfected U87 cells were cultured in 96-well plates for 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours. At each time point, CCK-8 solution was added to the wells and incubated for 2 hours at 37°C. Absorbance was then measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. These experiments were independently repeated in triplicate.
To assess the impact of EGFLAM on GBM cell migration, U87 cells were prepared for a wound healing assay. Transfected cells were seeded into six-well plates at a density of 5 × 10⁵ cells/ml and cultured to full confluence in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% serum for 24 hours. A wound was then introduced into each plate using a 100 μl pipette tip. Following three PBS washes, cells were incubated in serum-free medium for an additional 24 hours. Wound widths were subsequently measured in five random microscopic fields per well using an Olympus BX51 microscope at 200× magnification. Each experiment was conducted in triplicate.
To assess the impact of EGFLAM on glioblastoma cell migration and invasion, Transwell chambers, both uncoated and coated with Matrigel, were utilized. For migration experiments, transfected U87 cells, suspended in serum-free medium, were introduced into the upper chamber. Invasion assays involved placing these cells into the upper chamber that had been pre-coated with Matrigel. In both assay types, the lower chambers were filled with medium supplemented with 10% serum. Following a 24-hour incubation period, cells that had not migrated or invaded were cleared from the upper chambers using cotton swabs. Subsequently, the remaining migrated or invaded cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde for 30 minutes, stained with 0.1% crystal violet for 20 minutes, and then rinsed with PBS. The number of these cells was quantified and visualized across five random microscopic fields per well using an Olympus BX51 microscope at 200x magnification. Each experiment was independently replicated three times.
Following transfection, U87 cells were lysed for 30 minutes in RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA kit according to the manufacturer's instructions. Equal protein amounts were then heated and loaded onto SDS-PAGE for separation. Proteins were subsequently electrotransferred to a PVDF membrane, which was then blocked with 5% non-fat milk. The blocked membranes were incubated overnight with primary antibodies targeting phosphoinositide-3 kinase (PI3K), phosphorylated PI3K (p-PI3K), AKT, phosphorylated AKT (p-AKT), and phosphorylated P70S6K (p-P70S6K), along with GAPDH as a loading control. After three washes in TBST buffer, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit secondary antibodies for 1 hour at room temperature. Following another wash with blocking solution, the proteins were visualized using ECL detection. Signal intensity on each membrane was quantified using Quantity One software and normalized against GAPDH.
Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS (version 22.0) and GraphPad Prism v7.0. Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Comparisons between two groups utilized the Student's t-test, while ANOVA was employed for comparisons involving more than two groups. The Chi-square test assessed the association between EGFLAM expression and clinical characteristics. Patient survival was analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method with the long-rank test for comparisons. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All experiments were replicated at least three times.
EGFLAM expression was found to be elevated in Glioblastoma (GBM). Analysis of the TCGA database revealed higher EGFLAM levels in GBM tissues compared to normal brain tissues (A). This finding was further supported by data from the Oncomine database (B). Additionally, Kaplan-Meier analysis indicated that patients with high EGFLAM expression had a worse overall prognosis (p=0.005) (C).
EGFLAM's role in glioblastoma (GBM) was investigated by analyzing its expression in TCGA data from 169 GBM and 5 normal brain tissues. The results demonstrated a significant upregulation of EGFLAM in GBM tissues compared to normal brain tissues, as shown in Figure 1A (p<0.05).
Further analysis using the Oncomine database, with 22 GBM and 3 normal brain tissues, confirmed higher EGFLAM expression in GBM tissues compared to normal brain tissues (Fig. 1B, p<0.05).
The relationship between EGFLAM expression and GBM progression was investigated using clinical-pathological data from the TCGA database. A chi-square test was employed to analyze this association. GBM patients were categorized into high and low EGFLAM expression groups based on the median expression level. Results indicated a significant correlation between EGFLAM expression and GBM subtype (p=0.003). However, no significant associations were found with age, gender, or Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) (all p>0.05).
EGFLAM expression was found to be elevated in GBM cells. Quantitative RT-PCR analysis of EGFLAM levels in HEB, U87, U251, and HS683 cell lines revealed significantly higher expression in U87 cells compared to the normal HEB cell line. Consequently, the U87 cell line was selected for subsequent experiments involving EGFLAM knockdown. Following transfection with siRNA, EGFLAM expression was confirmed to be significantly reduced in the si-EGFLAM group compared to the control (si-con) group, as assessed by both qRT-PCR and western blot analysis.
Analyzing overall survival using the Kaplan-Meier method and long-rank test revealed that GBM patients with elevated EGFLAM levels experienced poorer survival outcomes (p=0.005, Fig. 1C). These findings indicate that EGFLAM holds potential as a prognostic biomarker for GBM.
The biological role of EGFLAM in glioblastoma (GBM) cells was investigated. Initially, EGFLAM expression was quantified in a normal human astrocyte cell line (HEB) and several glioma cell lines (U87, U251, HS683) using qRT-PCR. Results indicated significantly higher EGFLAM levels in U87 cells compared to HEB cells (p<0.01). Consequently, the U87 cell line was chosen for subsequent experiments.
EGFLAM expression correlated with GBM subtype (p=0.003). However, no significant associations were found with age, gender, or KPS.
To investigate EGFLAM's role in GBM cells, U87 cells were transfected with siRNA to silence EGFLAM. Following 24-hour transfection, EGFLAM expression was assessed via qRT-PCR and western blot. Results demonstrated a significant reduction in EGFLAM levels in the si-EGFLAM group compared to the si-con control. This knockdown achieved approximately 90% reduction in EGFLAM expression. These findings further supported the observation of elevated EGFLAM levels in GBM cell lines.
EGFLAM promotes U87 cell proliferation. Knocking down EGFLAM reduced cell viability, as shown by CCK-8 assays (A). Colony formation assays also revealed a decrease in colony numbers following EGFLAM knockdown (B). These results indicate that EGFLAM enhances the proliferative capacity of U87 cells.
EGFLAM's role in promoting U87 cell migration and invasion was investigated. A wound healing assay demonstrated that silencing EGFLAM inhibited cell migration. Furthermore, Transwell assays revealed a significant suppression of both migration and invasion in cells with reduced EGFLAM expression compared to the control group. These findings highlight EGFLAM's contribution to the invasive capabilities of U87 glioblastoma cells.
Western blot analysis was employed to investigate the PI3K/AKT pathway. The expression levels of PI3K, p-PI3K (Tyr199), AKT, p-AKT (Ser473), and p-P70S6K (Ser371) were measured. Following normalization to GAPDH, results indicated that silencing EGFLAM in U87 cells (si-EGFLAM group) significantly reduced the phosphorylation of PI3K, AKT, and P70S6K compared to the control group (si-con group), suggesting EGFLAM induces PI3K/AKT pathway activation.
CCK-8 assays revealed that EGFLAM knockdown significantly reduced U87 cell viability at 72 and 96 hours, indicating EGFLAM promotes GBM cell proliferation.
Colony formation assays further assessed U87 cell proliferation. Knockdown of EGFLAM significantly reduced colony formation viability (Fig. 3B and C, p<0.01). These findings indicate that EGFLAM promotes GBM cell proliferation.
To investigate EGFLAM's role in GBM cell migration and invasion, wound healing and Transwell assays were employed. Wound healing assays demonstrated that U87 cells with EGFLAM knockdown exhibited significantly slower wound closure compared to control cells, indicating EGFLAM promotes migration.
Transwell assays demonstrated that EGFLAM knockdown significantly reduced invading and migrating GBM cells. These findings collectively indicate that EGFLAM promotes GBM cell migration and invasion.
Western blot analysis was employed to investigate the underlying mechanism of EGFLAM's influence on cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, focusing on key PI3K/AKT pathway proteins. While total PI3K and AKT expression remained unchanged between the si-EGFLAM and si-con groups, the levels of phosphorylated PI3K, AKT, and P70S6K were significantly reduced in the si-EGFLAM group. These findings demonstrate that EGFLAM effectively promotes the activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway.
Our study demonstrates that EGFLAM is elevated in GBM samples and associated with a poorer prognosis. Silencing EGFLAM suppressed GBM cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, likely through PI3K/AKT pathway inactivation. These findings suggest EGFLAM's significant role in GBM progression and its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.
The EGFLAM gene has garnered increasing attention across various research domains. Initially identified for its localization within the synaptic cleft of photoreceptors, adjacent to ribbon synapses, EGFLAM plays a crucial role in the formation of normal photoreceptor ribbon synapses and visual perception [10, 14]. Subsequent research has linked EGFLAM to a range of diseases. For example, it has been recognized as a cell biomarker for theca interna and granulosa cells [15], a susceptibility locus for aortic aneurysms [9], and a hypomethylated biomarker in ovarian cancer [13]. Motivated by these findings, this study aimed to explore EGFLAM's potential as a novel biomarker and therapeutic target for glioblastoma (GBM). Analysis of TCGA data revealed significantly elevated EGFLAM expression in GBM tissues compared to normal brain tissues, a finding corroborated by data from the Oncomine database. Furthermore, bioinformatics analysis demonstrated a positive correlation between EGFLAM expression and poor prognosis in GBM patients. Experimental investigations confirmed EGFLAM's upregulation in GBM U87 cells, and the knockdown of EGFLAM effectively suppressed GBM cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Collectively, these results suggest that EGFLAM holds promise as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target for GBM.
The PI3K/AKT pathway is a crucial intracellular signaling cascade, with its key proteins—PI3K, AKT, and P70S6K—playing vital roles in regulating cellular proliferation, migration, and invasion. This pathway is frequently dysregulated in numerous cancers, including glioblastoma (GBM) [17, 18]. The development of GBM often involves molecular alterations in epidermal growth factor (EGF) and EGF receptor (EGFR), leading to the activation of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [17, 19]. Consequently, inhibiting this pathway represents a promising therapeutic strategy for GBM. Despite extensive research into PI3K/AKT pathway inhibitors, clinical trials have faced setbacks due to issues with toxicity, pharmacodynamics, and selectivity [17]. Therefore, identifying novel and safe inhibitors for targeted GBM therapy remains a critical need. Our findings demonstrate that EGFLAM knockdown significantly reduced the levels of phosphorylated PI3K, AKT, and P70S6K, indicating that EGFLAM effectively induces PI3K/AKT pathway activation. This study represents an initial investigation, and further animal experiments and clinical trials are necessary to elucidate the detailed mechanisms, pharmacodynamics, and toxicology of EGFLAM's effects on GBM.
This study is the first to report EGFLAM's role in promoting GBM cell proliferation, migration, and invasion through PI3K/AKT pathway activation. EGFLAM also predicts poor prognosis, suggesting it may serve as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for GBM.
