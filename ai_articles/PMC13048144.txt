Meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS) is a significant contributor to severe respiratory distress in term and near-term newborns. Effective management of severe MAS hinges on reducing inflammation. Phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors are recognized for their capacity to relax airway smooth muscles and mitigate alveolar inflammation. This research sought to evaluate the impact of various intravenous (IV) PDE inhibitors and corticosteroids on MAS. To achieve this, MAS was experimentally induced in newborn piglets through the instillation of human meconium. These piglets were then randomly allocated into five distinct groups, each comprising five animals. The groups were: a control group receiving sham treatment, a dexamethasone (Dex) group treated with IV dexamethasone (0.6 mg/kg), an aminophylline (Ami) group receiving IV aminophylline (6 mg/kg followed by a continuous infusion of 0.5 mg/kg/h), a milrinone (Mil) group treated with IV milrinone (50 μg/kg followed by a continuous infusion of 0.75 μg/kg/h), and a rolipram (Rol) group administered IV rolipram (0.8 mg/kg). The experimental protocol extended for a total of four hours. The findings indicated that all four treatment groups demonstrated improved oxygenation from three hours post-treatment initiation onwards, when contrasted with the control group. Notably, the rolipram group experienced a significant increase in heart rate (p < 0.05) and a comparatively lower blood pressure during the initial two hours of the experiment. In terms of inflammatory markers, the dexamethasone group exhibited significantly reduced levels of interleukin (IL)-1β in lung tissue compared to all other groups (p < 0.05). Furthermore, IL-6 levels were significantly lower in the dexamethasone group when compared to the aminophylline and milrinone groups (p < 0.05). Histological examination of lung tissue revealed less pronounced inflammation and atelectasis in the dexamethasone group relative to the other treatment groups. However, lung injury scores did not show any significant differences between the groups. In conclusion, the administration of IV corticosteroids or any class of PDE inhibitors offered some advantages in enhancing oxygenation in MAS. Nevertheless, PDE inhibitors did not prove superior to IV corticosteroids. The phosphodiesterase type 4 (PDE4) inhibitor, rolipram, was associated with adverse cardiovascular effects. Further research is warranted before the clinical application of IV corticosteroids and PDE inhibitors for MAS.

Meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS) frequently leads to severe respiratory failure in near-term and full-term neonates. Meconium aspiration can trigger several respiratory complications. Key mechanisms of MAS involve direct lung injury from meconium, airway obstruction causing impaired gas diffusion, atelectasis, and pneumonitis. It can also result in persistent pulmonary hypertension, secondary pneumonia, significant pulmonary inflammation, and alveolar epithelial cell apoptosis.
Effective treatment of severe meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS) in neonates necessitates reducing pulmonary inflammation alongside standard respiratory support. This underscores the need for therapies that can decrease inflammation without causing significant adverse effects, ultimately improving outcomes. While corticosteroids are recognized for their anti-inflammatory properties, their application in acute lung injury remains a subject of debate. Research indicates that dexamethasone, administered prophylactically, can lessen structural damage in MAS, and IV corticosteroids have demonstrated a capacity to mitigate inflammatory responses in animal models of meconium instillation. Nevertheless, the use of corticosteroids in neonates is complicated by potential acute side effects, including hyperglycemia, sepsis, hypertension, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, gastrointestinal issues, growth impairment, and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis suppression, contributing to the controversy surrounding their use.
Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) offer anti-inflammatory benefits distinct from corticosteroids' primary side effects. Phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors, a class of NSAIDs, have demonstrated efficacy in reducing inflammation in various conditions. These inhibitors are categorized as either selective or nonselective. Nonselective PDE inhibitors, such as xanthine derivatives like theophylline and aminophylline, are commonly employed in asthma treatment. While researchers have explored various NSAIDs, including nonselective PDE inhibitors, for meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS) and other lung injuries, concerns exist regarding their narrow therapeutic window and associated side effects. Studies, such as one by Mokra et al., have indicated potential adverse cardiovascular effects like elevated blood pressure and heart rate in meconium-instilled animals treated with nonselective PDE inhibitors, suggesting they may not be ideal for MAS management.
Ongoing research explores PDE inhibitors for neonatal lung injuries. Studies have shown benefits using PDE3 inhibitors in meconium-instilled animal models and PDE4 inhibitors in preventing mortality and inflammation in hyperoxia-induced lung injuries. These findings suggest that selective PDE3 and PDE4 inhibitors hold potential for treating acute neonatal pulmonary diseases. Nevertheless, further investigation into their therapeutic efficacy and optimal administration methods is necessary before clinical application.
This study hypothesized that intravenous PDE inhibitors, similar to corticosteroids, could reduce inflammation in meconium-injured neonatal lungs and improve outcomes. Consequently, the research aimed to compare the short-term therapeutic and adverse effects of IV corticosteroids and various PDE inhibitors in a MAS animal model.
Human meconium from healthy neonates was collected, pooled, homogenized, and diluted to a 20% saline slurry. It was then frozen at -20°C for later use.
For this study, 25 neonatal piglets (less than two weeks old) were prepared using established procedures for inducing meconium-induced lung injury. Initially, piglets received inhaled isoflurane for anesthesia, followed by intramuscular injections of atropine (0.1 mg/dose) and ketamine (10 mg/kg/dose). Local anesthesia was administered with 2% lidocaine hydrochloride. Throughout the experiment, sedation and paralysis were maintained via continuous infusions of ketamine (5 mg/kg/h), midazolam (0.5 mg/kg/h), and cisatracurium (0.2 mg/kg/h).
Following a tracheotomy, a 3.5-mm-diameter uncuffed tracheal tube was inserted. Controlled mechanical ventilation was then initiated using a volume-controlled ventilator. The established settings included a tidal volume of 9 mL/kg, an initial respiratory rate of 40 breaths/min, an inspiratory:expiratory ratio of 1:1, positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) of 5 cm H2O, and a fractional concentration of inspired oxygen (FiO2) of 1.0. These ventilator parameters remained constant, with the exception of the respiratory rate, which was adjusted by 2 to 5 breaths/min if arterial blood gas analysis indicated a partial pressure of carbon dioxide (pCO2) exceeding 50 mmHg and a pH below 7.25. For medication administration and anesthesia, a 3.5 Fr umbilical vessel catheter was placed in the right femoral vein. Additionally, another catheter was inserted into the right femoral artery to facilitate continuous monitoring of arterial blood pressure and for blood sampling. Throughout the experiment, a continuous infusion of 0.33% saline in 5% dextrose was administered at a rate of 5 mL/kg/h. Piglet body temperature was consistently maintained between 38°C and 39°C using a servo-controlled heating blanket.
Throughout the experiment, continuous monitoring of the piglets' physiological parameters was conducted. Mean arterial blood pressure (MBP), oxygen saturation (SpO2) via pulse oximetry, and anal temperature were recorded using an HP M1205A OmniCare Model. Cardiac activity was continuously assessed with an electrocardiograph. Respiratory flow was measured using a Fleisch No. 3 heated capillary tube pneumotachograph connected to an MP-45-16 differential pressure transducer. All pressure transducers underwent calibration with water or mercury manometers. Signals for respiratory flow and airway pressure were captured by a PowerLab 16/30 data acquisition system. Tidal volume readings were obtained by integrating the flow signals. Respiratory system compliance (Crs) was calculated on a breath-by-breath basis using an online computer system featuring a DA100C analog–digital converter and Chart & Scope software. Additionally, arterial blood samples were collected for blood gas analysis.
After establishing baseline measurements, each piglet received 6 to 8 aliquots of 1 mL/kg of 20% meconium slurry through a tracheal tube using an 8 Fr feeding tube. This procedure aimed to decrease arterial partial pressure of oxygen (pO2) to below 150 mmHg (at an FiO2 of 1.0) and reduce respiratory system compliance (Crs) to less than two-thirds of its baseline. These targets ensured the induced lung injury was of moderate to severe intensity, consistent with acute respiratory distress syndrome definitions.
Twenty-five meconium-instilled piglets were randomly assigned to five groups (n=5 each).
The experiment lasted for 4 hours. Throughout this time, piglets exhibiting metabolic acidosis (pH < 7.20, base deficit >8 meq/L) received sodium bicarbonate. Bradycardic piglets (HR < 100 beats/min) were given epinephrine, and those with significant hypotension (MBP < 40 mmHg) were treated with saline. No cardiac massage was performed if a piglet's heart stopped beating before the experiment concluded.
Blood gas analysis was performed hourly on 0.2 mL arterial samples. Throughout the experiment, cardiopulmonary profiles were continuously monitored. At the study's conclusion, 1 mL of blood was collected for serum analysis of cytokines, which was then stored at -80°C.
The piglets were euthanized with potassium chloride after deep anesthesia.
Following euthanasia, each piglet's chest was opened for gross examination of the lungs and thorax. Within five minutes of death, mechanical ventilation was ceased, and positive airway pressure (5 cm H2O PEEP) was applied. The trachea and right bronchus were then clamped. The airways and right lungs were subsequently incised and fixed in 4% formaldehyde for histological analysis. Lung tissues were prepared for paraffin embedding, and 4 μm thin sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin for examination under light microscopy. Lung injury was quantified using a scoring system from 0 to 4, based on the proportion of the microscopic field covered by injury. To ensure accuracy and minimize variability, at least five fields from each lung tissue sample were evaluated.
Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid was collected from the left lungs by lavaging with 4 mL/kg of 37°C 0.9% saline. This collected BAL fluid was then stored at -80°C for later analysis. Following this, the left lungs underwent perfusion with a phosphate buffer solution. This perfusion was initiated by inserting a catheter into the pulmonary artery and creating an opening in the left atrium, continuing until the outflow fluid was clear. Subsequently, two approximately 1 cm diameter pieces of lung tissue, encompassing the middle and lower lobes, were excised. These tissue samples were weighed and then homogenized at 4°C using MagNA Lyser Green Beads. The homogenization process involved a ratio of 0.1 g of tissue to 1 mL of cell lysis buffer containing 100 mM phenylmethysulfonyl fluoride. The homogenized samples were then subjected to multiple centrifugation steps. Initially, they were centrifuged twice at 6500 rpm for 20 seconds, followed by a spin down at 3000 rpm for 30 minutes. A further centrifugation at 14,000 rpm for 30 minutes at 4°C was performed. The resulting final supernatant was stored at -80°C until it was ready for analysis.
Proinflammatory cytokines IL-1β, IL-6, and IL-8 were quantified in duplicate using a porcine-specific ELISA kit. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm with a microplate spectrophotometer. Sample concentrations were determined after subtracting background absorbance from standards and unknowns.
To assess potential increases in inflammatory tissue activity, Phospholipase A2 (PLA2) enzyme activity was measured in lung tissue homogenates. This was done using a porcine-specific PLA2G1B ELISA Kit, following the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance was read at 450 nm. Sample concentrations were determined after subtracting background absorbance from standards and unknowns.
CRP levels were measured in piglet serum, lung tissue, and BAL fluid using a specific ELISA kit. Absorbance was read at 450 nm with a microplate spectrophotometer, following the manufacturer's instructions.
Data were presented as mean ± SEM or median (interquartile range). Statistical analysis and graph generation were performed using SigmaPlot 12.0. Within-group comparisons of pre- and post-injury cardiopulmonary data utilized the paired t test. To compare data across the five groups, one-way ANOVA or the Kruskal-Wallis test was employed, with the Student-Newman-Keuls test used for post hoc pairwise comparisons when needed. A p-value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.
The study found no significant differences in piglet age, weight, or meconium volume across the five groups. Similarly, arterial blood gas values, respiratory system compliance (Crs), mean blood pressure (MBP), and heart rate (HR) did not differ significantly between groups either before or after lung injury. However, within each group, significant changes were observed post-injury, including decreased pO2, increased pCO2, and reduced Crs.
At hours 3 and 4, the control group exhibited significantly lower pO2 and a higher alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient (A-aDO2) compared to the treatment groups. Throughout the 4-hour experiment, partial pressure of carbon dioxide (pCO2) and pH levels did not differ significantly between groups. Furthermore, lung compliance and peak inspiratory pressure remained comparable across all groups during the experimental period.
Cardiovascular monitoring revealed that the rolipram (Rol) group experienced significantly elevated heart rate from 30 minutes to 2 hours post-treatment compared to other groups. While rolipram also led to relatively lower blood pressure, this difference was not statistically significant.
Analysis of serum inflammatory markers revealed differences in IL-1β, IL-6, and CRP levels across the groups at the experiment's conclusion, though these were not statistically significant. Similarly, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid showed a trend towards reduced IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and CRP in the dexamethasone group, but this did not reach statistical significance. However, examination of lung tissue demonstrated a significant reduction in IL-1β levels in the dexamethasone group compared to all other groups. Furthermore, dexamethasone treatment resulted in significantly lower IL-6 levels in lung tissue when compared to the aminophylline and milrinone groups. No significant differences were observed in lung tissue IL-8, CRP, or PLA2 levels among the groups.
Histological examination of the lungs showed severe inflammation, hemorrhage, and atelectasis in the control group, particularly in dependent areas. While the treatment groups also exhibited damage in dependent lung regions, the dexamethasone (Dex) group displayed reduced inflammation and atelectasis compared to controls. Despite these observations, lung injury scores did not significantly differ across the groups.
Intravenous administration of PDE inhibitors or corticosteroids improved oxygenation in treated piglets compared to controls. However, significant evidence of reduced pulmonary inflammation was not observed. The PDE4 inhibitor group experienced transient tachycardia and relatively lower blood pressure.
Phosphodiesterases (PDEs) comprise 11 enzyme subtypes that regulate intracellular cyclic nucleotides like cyclic AMP and cyclic guanosine monophosphate. These enzymes are found in various cell types and are implicated in respiratory diseases. Elevated cyclic AMP levels are linked to airway smooth muscle relaxation and anti-inflammatory effects. Consequently, PDE inhibitors are thought to contribute to airway smooth muscle relaxation and the suppression of cellular inflammation and other immune responses, potentially aiding in the management of severe respiratory conditions. Selective PDE inhibitors offer more targeted actions and fewer adverse effects than their nonselective counterparts.
Selective phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors, specifically PDE3 and PDE4 inhibitors, are recognized for their efficacy in managing airway diseases. PDE3 inhibitors, by selectively blocking the PDE3 enzyme, exert a positive inotropic effect and are frequently employed in the treatment of acute heart failure and cardiogenic shock. This enzyme also plays a primary role in regulating cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) levels within airway smooth muscles. Conversely, PDE4 is a critical isoenzyme found in inflammatory cells such as macrophages, monocytes, lymphocytes, dendritic cells, and neutrophils, where it is instrumental in inhibiting inflammatory cell activation. Research by Currie et al. has detailed the immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties of PDE4. Consequently, PDE4 inhibitors hold significant promise for enhancing smooth muscle relaxation, reducing fibroblast activity, suppressing the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and decreasing mucus secretion in airway cells. Further supporting the potential of PDE4 inhibitors, Patel et al. found that PDE4 inhibitors, unlike PDE3 inhibitors, can augment the expression of anti-inflammatory mitogen-activated protein kinase phosphatase 1 in airway smooth muscle when stimulated by beta-agonists. This suggests that PDE4 inhibitors might be more potent in addressing pulmonary inflammation, warranting further investigation into their effectiveness in resolving inflammation associated with meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS). Previous studies have also indicated positive outcomes with the use of either PDE3 or PDE4 inhibitors in patients with asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.
Combination therapies involving different phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors, or PDE inhibitors with other drugs, have been explored for pulmonary diseases. For instance, combining PDE3 and PDE4 inhibitors demonstrated a synergistic effect in treating acute pulmonary inflammation. Additionally, a PDE4 inhibitor paired with a β2 agonist effectively reduced the release of pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic mediators from lung fibroblasts. However, no studies have yet investigated such combination strategies for meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS), presenting an avenue for future research.
During the initial two hours of the experiment, the rolipram (Rol) group exhibited significantly higher heart rates compared to the other groups. While their blood pressure was comparatively lower, this difference was not statistically significant. The intravenous administration route allowed for clear observation of systemic side effects. Future research should consider how drug dosage and administration route impact both pulmonary outcomes and cardiovascular responses. Given that the lungs are the primary site of inflammation in MAS animal models, delivering anti-inflammatory agents directly into the lungs via intrapulmonary administration could theoretically reduce inflammation without increasing systemic side effects. Adjusting the PDE4 inhibitor dosage in subsequent studies may help achieve beneficial pulmonary effects while mitigating adverse cardiovascular responses.
Beyond IV anti-inflammatory drugs like PDE inhibitors and corticosteroids, other therapies could enhance MAS outcomes. Surfactant lavage, known to reduce pulmonary inflammation and improve lung function, is one such method. Future research could explore combining surfactant lavage with diluted surfactant and PDE inhibitors for treating moderate to severe MAS.
Investigating the administration route for anti-inflammatory drugs in MAS is crucial. While rolipram, a PDE4 inhibitor, improved oxygenation, its systemic side effects limit its clinical applicability. Further research is needed on optimal IV dosages, direct intratracheal administration, and potential combination therapies to determine rolipram's role in MAS.
When comparing corticosteroids to PDE inhibitors, dexamethasone (Dex) showed a trend towards reduced pulmonary inflammation and fewer proinflammatory cytokines than the other treatment groups. Aminophylline (Ami) resulted in lower serum CRP levels but offered no other advantages over the control group, suggesting further research into its use may be unnecessary. Milrinone (Mil) and rolipram (Rol) did not demonstrate significant reductions in inflammatory markers or histological inflammation, and rolipram carried a risk of adverse cardiovascular effects. While dexamethasone exhibited the lowest lung tissue IL-6 levels overall, this reduction was only statistically significant when compared to aminophylline and milrinone. Consequently, additional research is needed to fully understand the advantages and disadvantages of PDE inhibitors in severe meconium aspiration syndrome.
Several limitations were identified in this study. The small sample size of five piglets per group, coupled with significant data variability, may have impacted the statistical significance of the findings. Furthermore, the observation period was limited to four hours post-lung injury, capturing only acute outcomes. While improved oxygenation was observed in all treatment groups from hour three onwards, the long-term maintenance of this benefit remains to be determined. Finally, the drug dosages used may not have been optimal for treating severe pulmonary dysfunction, and the efficacy of intravenous versus intratracheal administration warrants further exploration. These factors necessitate additional research to fully address these questions.
Intravenous corticosteroids and various phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitors showed potential to improve oxygenation in severe meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS). However, this study did not find conclusive evidence of benefits from IV PDE3 or PDE4 inhibitors. Drug dosage and administration route likely impact outcomes. Further research into different dosages, administration methods, and combination therapies is needed to determine the role of selective PDE inhibitors in treating MAS.
