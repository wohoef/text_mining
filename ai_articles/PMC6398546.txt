This study investigated the role of miR-539 in Wilms' Tumor (WT) pathogenesis, building on prior research linking miR-539 to the carcinogenesis of other cancers. To achieve this, miR-539 expression was quantified in 42 WT tissues and the SK-NEP-1 cell line using qRT-PCR. Western blotting was employed to assess the protein levels of E-cadherin, N-cadherin, Vimentin, Notch 1, Notch 3, and JAG1. The functional impact of miR-539 was examined in SK-NEP-1 cells through MTT and Transwell assays. Additionally, the interaction between miR-539 and JAG1 was validated using a dual luciferase assay. The findings revealed a significant decrease in miR-539 expression within WT tissues. This downregulation was found to correlate with NWTS-5 stage, lymph node metastasis, and histological type. Moreover, lower miR-539 levels were associated with a reduced overall survival rate in WT patients. In vitro experiments demonstrated that overexpressing miR-539 inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion of SK-NEP-1 cells. The research also established JAG1 as a direct target of miR-539. Ultimately, miR-539 was shown to impede WT development by suppressing JAG1 and Notch1/3 expression, thereby blocking epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT). The study concluded that miR-539 inhibits WT progression by downregulating JAG1 and Notch1/3.

Wilms' tumor (WT) is a malignant embryonal tumor originating from renal blastocytes, representing about 87% of childhood renal tumors. The disease typically affects children aged 3 to 4 years, with 80% of cases occurring before the age of five. The tumor was initially described by Rance in 1814 and later characterized by Max Wilms, after whom it was named. Treatment for WT involves surgical removal at all stages. Radiotherapy is also effective, with postoperative application improving outcomes. Preoperative chemotherapy can reduce tumor size, decrease surgical risks, and enhance complete resection rates. Prognosis is influenced by factors such as histological type, tumor stage, patient age, and biological characteristics. While most WT patients have a favorable prognosis, with 4-year survival rates ranging from 54.8% to 90% after treatment, there are no specific preventative measures. Consequently, early detection and intervention are crucial for improving patient outcomes.
The involvement of microRNAs (miRNAs) in cancer diagnosis and treatment is a growing area of research. Numerous miRNAs have been identified as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors, playing a role in the development of various cancers, including Wilms' Tumor (WT). For example, elevated miR-190b has been shown to enhance cell invasion and migration while suppressing apoptosis in WT by downregulating PTEN [1]. Additionally, miR-21 has demonstrated prognostic significance in WT patients [6]. The miR-539 has been observed to have diverse functions across different diseases and cancers. In some contexts, such as failing hearts, miR-539 is upregulated [23], and it promotes osteoblast activity and osteoclast apoptosis [33]. Conversely, a study on osteosarcoma found that decreased miR-539 levels were linked to a poorer prognosis [22]. Furthermore, miR-539 has been shown to inhibit glioma cell proliferation and invasion by targeting DIXDC1 [25]. Despite these findings, the specific role of miR-539 in the progression of Wilms' Tumor has not yet been investigated.
Jagged-1 (JAG1), an oncogene, is frequently overexpressed in cancers, correlating with disease progression and unfavorable outcomes. Its expression is known to be modulated by various microRNAs, including miR-26a and miR-524. JAG1 is implicated in human cancer development via the Notch signaling pathway, a complex system that can act as both a promoter and suppressor of cancer. This pathway comprises Notch receptors (Notch 1-4) and ligands like Jagged-1. Dysregulation of the Notch pathway is also linked to kidney disease. Despite these known roles, the specific interplay between miR-539 and the JAG1-Notch1/3 signaling axis in Wilms' Tumor (WT) has not been previously elucidated.
This study investigated miR-539's function in Wilms' Tumor (WT), observing its abnormal expression in WT tissues. We analyzed the interaction between miR-539 and the JAG1-Notch1/3 signaling pathway. These findings aim to identify novel therapeutic targets for WT treatment.
Human Wilms' Tumor (WT) tissues and adjacent normal tissues were collected from 42 patients at The Affiliated Yantai Yuhuangding Hospital of Qingdao University, with informed consent. Patients had not undergone prior treatment. Tissues were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C. The study received approval from the hospital's Ethics Committee.
The SK-NEP-1 human Wilms' Tumor cell line, obtained from ATCC, was utilized for miRNA research in WT. These cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS and maintained at 37°C with 5% CO2.
SK-NEP-1 cells were transfected with miR-539 mimic/inhibitor or JAG1 plasmid using Lipofectamine 2000, following manufacturer instructions.
To quantify miR-539 expression in Wilms' Tumor (WT) tissues, total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent. Subsequently, cDNA was synthesized via reverse transcription with the TaqMan® MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit. Quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was then conducted on an ABI 7500 Fast Real-Time PCR system, utilizing TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix. U6 and GAPDH served as internal controls for miR-539 and JAG1, respectively. Expression levels were determined using the 2-Δ⁢Δ⁢Ct method.
To verify the interaction, the 3'-UTR of JAG1, either wild-type or mutant, was cloned into a pGL3 luciferase vector. SK-NEP-1 cells were then co-transfected with these constructs and miR-539 mimics. Luciferase activity was subsequently measured using a dual luciferase assay.
To assess cell proliferation, SK-NEP-1 cells were seeded in 96-well plates. Following transfection with miR-539 mimic or inhibitor, cells were incubated for 24 to 96 hours. Subsequently, MTT reagent was added and incubated for 4 hours at 37°C. Absorbance was then measured at 490 nm using a spectrophotometer.
Cell migration and invasion were assessed using Transwell chambers with 8-μm pore size membranes. SK-NEP-1 cells, either transfected with miR-539 mimics/inhibitor or a negative control (NC), were incubated in serum-free medium for 24 hours. Subsequently, 2 × 10⁴ cells were placed in the upper chamber, while medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. After a 24-hour incubation at 37°C, non-migrated cells were removed. The remaining migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.5% crystal violet. For invasion assays, the procedure was identical, with the exception that the upper chamber was pre-coated with 1 mg/ml Matrigel. The number of migrated or invaded cells was then quantified using a microscope.
Protein extraction was performed using RIPA lysis buffer. Following separation by SDS-PAGE, proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes and blocked with 5% skim milk. Membranes were then incubated overnight at 4°C with antibodies against EMT markers (E-cadherin, N-cadherin, vimentin), Notch pathway markers (Notch 1, Notch 3), JAG1, and GAPDH. After incubation with secondary antibodies, protein expression levels were detected using ECL.
miR-539 expression in Wilms' tumor tissues showed significant associations with NWTS-5 stage (P=0.041), lymph node metastasis (P=0.049), and histological type (P=0.0201). Age and gender did not show significant correlations. These findings suggest miR-539's role in WT progression.
miR-539 expression was downregulated in Wilms' Tumor (WT) tissues (Figure 1A). Low miR-539 levels correlated with shorter overall survival in WT patients (Figure 1B).
Overexpression of miR-539 was shown to inhibit Wilms' Tumor (WT) cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Initially, miR-539 expression in SK-NEP-1 cell lines was assessed (A). Subsequent qRT-PCR confirmed altered miR-539 levels in cells treated with mimics or inhibitors (B). MTT assays revealed that miR-539 overexpression suppressed cell proliferation (C), while its inhibition promoted it (D). Transwell assays demonstrated that miR-539 upregulation reduced cell migration (E) and invasion (F), whereas its knockdown had the opposite effect.
JAG1 was identified as a direct target of miR-539. Binding sites were observed, and luciferase assays confirmed this interaction. A negative correlation between miR-539 and JAG1 expression was found, with miR-539 mimics decreasing JAG1 levels in SK-NEP-1 cells.
Statistical analyses, including Chi-squared tests and Tukey's one-way ANOVA, were performed using SPSS 19.0 and Graphpad Prism 6. Differences in survival were assessed via Kaplan-Meier analysis and log-rank tests. A P-value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.
Quantitative RT-PCR revealed that miR-539 expression was significantly reduced in Wilms' Tumor (WT) tissues, approximately by half compared to normal tissues, indicating its downregulation (Fig. 1A). This aberrant expression correlated with NWTS-5 stage (P=0.041), lymph node metastasis (P=0.049), and histological type (P=0.0201, Table 1). Furthermore, lower miR-539 levels were linked to a reduced overall survival rate in WT patients (P=0.0113, Fig. 1B), suggesting a poorer prognosis. These findings collectively indicate miR-539's involvement in WT tumorigenesis and its prognostic significance.
The SK-NEP-1 cell line was used to examine miR-539 expression, revealing its downregulation in these cells, mirroring findings in WT tissues. To understand miR-539's functional role in WT, SK-NEP-1 cells were transfected with miR-539 mimics or inhibitors. Transfection with mimics successfully increased miR-539 levels, while inhibitors decreased them, as confirmed by qRT-PCR. Subsequent MTT and Transwell assays demonstrated that overexpressing miR-539 suppressed SK-NEP-1 cell proliferation. Conversely, reducing miR-539 levels enhanced proliferation. Similar inhibitory effects were observed for cell migration and invasion upon miR-539 upregulation, with the opposite occurring when miR-539 was silenced. These in vitro findings collectively indicate that miR-539 functions as a suppressor in Wilms' Tumor.
JAG1 expression was elevated in Wilms' Tumor (WT) tissues and correlated with poorer patient survival. To further investigate the interaction between miR-539 and JAG1, JAG1 expression was assessed in SK-NEP-1 cells treated with a JAG1 vector and miR-539. The JAG1 vector reversed the inhibitory effects of miR-539 on cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, as demonstrated by MTT and Transwell assays. These findings confirm that miR-539 inhibits WT development by targeting JAG1.
MiR-539 overexpression suppressed EMT markers (E-cadherin, N-cadherin, Vimentin) and Notch1/3 expression in WT cells. Conversely, miR-539 inhibition promoted EMT and increased Notch1/3.
The interaction between miR-539 and JAG1 was investigated. Bioinformatic prediction via TargetScan indicated a potential binding site for miR-539 within JAG1's 3'-UTR (Fig. 3A), suggesting JAG1 as a target. This hypothesis was validated through a luciferase reporter assay. The assay demonstrated that miR-539 mimics significantly reduced the luciferase activity of wild-type JAG1 (Wt-JAG1), while activity remained unaffected in the mutant JAG1 construct (Mut-JAG1) (Fig. 3B). Further analysis of WT tissues revealed a strong negative correlation between miR-539 and JAG1 expression (P<0.0001, R2=0.6257; Fig. 3C). In vitro experiments using SK-NEP-1 cells confirmed these findings. Overexpression of miR-539 led to a decrease in JAG1 expression (Fig. 3D), whereas inhibition of miR-539 resulted in elevated JAG1 levels (Fig. 3E). Collectively, these results establish that miR-539 directly targets JAG1 and exerts negative regulation over its expression in Wilms' Tumor.
The role of JAG1 in Wilms' Tumor (WT) was investigated by first examining its expression levels in WT tissues. Results showed a significant increase in JAG1 expression in WT tissues compared to normal tissues (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, elevated JAG1 levels were associated with a poorer prognosis for WT patients (P= 0.0217, Fig. 4B). To further elucidate the interaction between miR-539 and JAG1, SK-NEP-1 cells were co-transfected with miR-539 mimics and a JAG1 vector. This experiment demonstrated that the JAG1 vector could restore JAG1 expression, which had been reduced by miR-539 mimics (Fig. 4C). Crucially, the proliferation-inhibiting effects of miR-539 were diminished when JAG1 was upregulated (Fig. 4D). Similar findings were observed for cell migration and invasion (Fig. 4E, 4F), indicating that miR-539's inhibition of these processes in WT cells is mediated by its targeting of JAG1.
To further elucidate the regulatory mechanism of miR-539 in Wilms' Tumor (WT), its impact on Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition (EMT) and Notch1/3 expression was investigated. The findings revealed that increasing miR-539 levels in SK-NEP-1 cells led to enhanced E-cadherin expression while suppressing N-cadherin and Vimentin. This indicates that miR-539 overexpression inhibits EMT in WT. Conversely, reducing miR-539 levels promoted EMT. Additionally, the study explored the involvement of the Notch pathway, known to be influenced by JAG1, a ligand that interacts with Notch receptors like Notch-1, Notch-2, and Notch-3. Western blot analysis demonstrated that miR-539 mimics decreased the expression of Notch-1 and Notch-3, whereas a miR-539 inhibitor increased their levels in SK-NEP-1 cells. These results collectively suggest that miR-539 inhibits EMT and suppresses Notch1/3 expression in WT, implying a role for the Notch pathway in WT development.
Wilms' tumor (WT), a malignant renal tumor predominantly affecting children, has been shown to be influenced by various microRNAs (miRNAs) in its progression and development. While some miRNAs, such as miR-19b, have been reported to promote WT cell proliferation and migration through the PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway, others, like miR-613, have been found to attenuate these processes by targeting FRS2. Our study revealed that the overexpression of miR-539 also inhibited WT cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. We observed a reduction in miR-539 expression within WT tissues, and its aberrant expression was linked to NWTS-5 stage, lymph node metastasis, and histological type. Moreover, lower miR-539 levels correlated with a poorer prognosis in WT patients. Collectively, these findings establish miR-539 as a tumor suppressor gene in WT.
miR-539 is recognized for its inhibitory role across various human cancers, achieved by regulating specific target genes. This aligns with findings in Wilms' Tumor (WT), where miR-539 also demonstrates suppressive effects. Previous research has shown miR-539 targeting MMP-8 to reduce invasion and migration in osteosarcoma, mirroring its function in WT. Furthermore, miR-539 has been identified as downregulated in breast cancer, acting as a tumor suppressor. It also suppresses proliferation and promotes apoptosis in renal cell carcinoma by targeting HMGA2. These prior studies lend strong support to the current findings. Crucially, JAG1 was identified as a direct target of miR-539, with a negative correlation observed between their expression in WT tissues. Ultimately, miR-539 inhibits WT development by targeting JAG1.
Identifying downstream target genes of miR-539 was a key objective, building upon the known mechanism of miRNAs inhibiting gene expression by binding to their 3'-UTR. JAG1 was previously identified as a target gene in other cancers, with miR-186, for example, inhibiting JAG1 to reduce proliferation in multiple myeloma, a finding that aligns with our observations in Wilms' Tumor (WT). In WT, miR-539 was found to suppress epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and the expression of Notch1 and Notch3. JAG1, a cell surface ligand, is crucial in the Notch signaling pathway, activating it by interacting with Notch receptors like Notch1, Notch2, and Notch3. The JAG1/Notch pathway's role has been established in various cancers, including cervical carcinoma, choriocarcinoma, and colorectal cancer. Notably, Notch2 has been linked to promoting metastasis through EMT in bladder cancer. Similarly, miR-598 was shown to inhibit metastasis in colorectal cancer by suppressing the JAG1/Notch pathway, which drives EMT. Our findings are consistent with this, demonstrating that miR-539 inhibits WT cell proliferation and metastasis by suppressing JAG1-Notch1/3 expression.
MiR-539 is downregulated in Wilms' Tumor tissues. It inhibits tumor progression by negatively regulating JAG1-Notch1/3 expression, suggesting its potential as a diagnostic and prognostic indicator for WT.
