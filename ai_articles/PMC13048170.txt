The growing elderly population has made hip fractures a significant public health concern globally. Even with surgery, patients often experience slow recovery and lasting disabilities. Anemia is frequently observed in older individuals undergoing hip fracture surgery, and it has been linked to poorer outcomes, including increased morbidity and mortality. While blood transfusions are a common treatment for perioperative anemia in these patients, some research indicates they might actually worsen outcomes. The exact link between blood transfusions and adverse surgical results is still debated. Some studies suggest factors like erythrocyte storage duration or immune activation might play a role, but findings are inconsistent. Consequently, understanding the impact of transfusions on long-term survival after hip fracture surgery is crucial for both clinical practice and ongoing research.
This retrospective cohort study aimed to examine the link between perioperative blood transfusions and long-term survival in elderly hip fracture surgery patients. Employing diverse statistical methods, including regression and propensity score analyses, we investigated this association. Our hypothesis, informed by prior research, suggested that blood transfusions might elevate the risk of mortality post-surgery. Ultimately, this analysis sought to equip healthcare providers with crucial data on transfusion risks, thereby aiding clinical decisions during the perioperative phase of hip fracture surgery.
Following Institutional Review Board approval (IRB-TPEVGH No. 2016-10-004CC), electronic medical records of patients aged 70 years and older who underwent hip fracture surgery with general or spinal anesthesia at Taipei Veterans General Hospital from January 2013 to December 2015 were examined. Patients with pathologic hip fractures, high-energy injuries, or incomplete data were excluded. Participants were then divided into two groups: those who received perioperative blood transfusions and those who did not. Research assistants, independent of data analysis, extracted patient records, and the authors verified data quality through random sampling.
Data collected from electronic medical records included patient age, sex, ASA physical status, hemoglobin, platelet count, INR, chronic renal insufficiency, perioperative blood transfusion, anesthesia time, and intraoperative blood loss. Anemia was categorized by WHO criteria: mild (women 11-11.9 g/dL, men 11-12.9 g/dL), moderate (8-10.9 g/dL), and severe (< 8 g/dL).
Overall survival, defined as the time from surgery to death, was the primary outcome. Survivors' follow-up times were censored. Patients were tracked until the endpoint, censoring date, or January 2018, whichever came first.
To analyze the data, patients were divided into two groups: those who received blood transfusions and those who did not. Differences in patient characteristics between these groups were assessed. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-squared test, while continuous variables were compared using either the t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, depending on their distribution. Non-normally distributed continuous variables underwent log transformation to mitigate skewness. The impact of transfusion status and other collected variables on overall survival was initially evaluated using univariate Cox regression analysis. Subsequently, a multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed. This multivariate analysis employed a stepwise model selection process, with entry and removal significance criteria set at 0.1 and 0.05, respectively, to identify factors associated with overall survival from those identified as significant in the univariate analysis. Recognizing the potential for imbalances in patient characteristics, logistic regression analysis was utilized to generate propensity scores. This involved incorporating the collected variables into the model, with the detailed analytical results available in the Appendix. To further address potential confounding, propensity score matching was conducted. This process used a caliper width of 0.2 times the standard deviation of the logit-transformed propensity scores to achieve sufficient balance in the collected variables between matched pairs. Comparisons of patient variables between these matched pairs were performed as described previously. To assess the association between blood transfusions and overall survival in the matched pairs, a stratified Cox regression model was employed. For additional validation, a sensitivity analysis was performed. This involved dividing the study subjects into five equal-sized groups based on quintiles of their propensity scores. A pooled hazard ratio (HR) for the transfusion effect on overall survival was then estimated across these strata using stratified Cox regression analysis. A p-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 23.0.
A total of 718 hip fracture patients were analyzed, with 68.9% (495 patients) receiving blood transfusions. The median follow-up period post-surgery was 25.9 months. Initial comparisons of patient characteristics between the transfusion and no-transfusion groups revealed significant imbalances in age, ASA classification, anemia status, anesthesia time, and blood loss. However, after employing propensity score matching, these imbalances were effectively addressed, resulting in no significant differences in patient characteristics between the 149 matched pairs.
Univariate analysis, presented in Table 2, identified seven significant predictors of overall survival following hip fracture surgery. These included blood transfusion (crude odds ratio, 2.14; 95% CI, 1.35-3.39), aging, sex, anemia status, chronic renal insufficiency, general anesthesia, and anesthesia time. ASA classification approached statistical significance (p = 0.05). However, international normalized ratio, platelet count, and intraoperative blood loss did not demonstrate an association with overall survival in this univariate analysis.
The multivariate model identified four independent predictors of overall survival following hip fracture surgery. Female sex was linked to a reduced mortality risk, while general anesthesia and aging were associated with an increased risk. Furthermore, moderate anemia presented a higher mortality risk compared to mild anemia or no anemia. Crucially, after adjusting for these four factors, blood transfusions no longer significantly impacted overall survival (HR, 1.44; 95% CI, 0.87-2.36). This lack of significant association persisted after propensity score matching (HR, 1.7; 95% CI, 0.78-3.71) and in sensitivity analyses using the quintile-stratified method (HR, 1.41; 95% CI, 0.82-2.44).
Our research indicates that perioperative blood transfusions did not correlate with an increased risk of long-term mortality in elderly patients (≥ 70 years) undergoing hip fracture surgery. Conversely, anemia was identified as an independent predictor of mortality post-surgery. Despite the common practice of using transfusions to manage acute anemia during hip fracture procedures, our analysis, employing both multivariate regression and propensity score methodologies, did not reveal a link between transfusions and overall survival. This observation aligns with findings from Smeets et al.5 but contradicts those of Engoren et al.7. A key strength of our study lies in its robust statistical approach, which effectively accounted for confounding factors within a sufficiently large sample. These results offer valuable insights for guiding transfusion strategies in the management of perioperative anemia in this patient population.
Previous research has highlighted the link between low hemoglobin and adverse outcomes. Carson et al.14 observed that reduced hemoglobin levels correlated with increased cardiac complications in patients with existing heart conditions. Similarly, Potter et al.15, through a meta-analysis of hip fracture patients, suggested that anemia upon hospital admission was linked to higher mortality. However, this meta-analysis did not find a connection between postoperative transfusions and mortality when covariates were accounted for. These findings align with our study's results, reinforcing the idea that anemia is a significant factor influencing long-term survival after hip fracture surgery. The exact reasons for anemia's impact on post-surgical outcomes are still not fully understood. Some researchers propose that it could be due to underlying systemic illnesses, inflammatory responses, inadequate oxygen supply leading to organ dysfunction under stress, or a combination of these elements.
The impact of perioperative transfusions on postoperative outcomes following hip fracture surgery remains a subject of considerable debate, with uncertainty surrounding the causal link between these transfusions and long-term mortality. To date, only one substantial randomized trial has possessed the statistical power to definitively evaluate optimal transfusion strategies in this patient population. This trial, known as the Functional Outcomes in Cardiovascular Patients Undergoing Surgical Hip Fracture Repair (FOCUS) trial, involved over 2000 elderly individuals with moderate to severe anemia and coexisting cardiovascular conditions or other risk factors. Participants were assigned to either a liberal or restrictive transfusion strategy. The FOCUS trial found that while the liberal transfusion group was more likely to receive blood, there was no significant difference in the incidence of acute coronary syndrome or short-term mortality between the two groups. These findings align with results from other randomized trials conducted in non-surgical critical care settings. In contrast to the findings from randomized controlled trials, observational studies have presented a more varied picture regarding the association between blood transfusions and morbidity and mortality after hip fracture surgery. For instance, Vochteloo et al. identified blood transfusions as a risk factor for mortality, particularly within the initial three months post-surgery. Johnston et al., in a study of over 3000 patients, observed a negative correlation between transfusions and outcomes at 120 and 365 days, but this association was only evident in univariate, not multivariate, analyses. Similarly, Carson et al., utilizing a large cohort, found no impact of transfusions on 30- and 90-day mortality. Conversely, Engoren et al. reported that blood transfusions were linked to increased long-term mortality after hip fracture surgery. These discrepancies in findings are likely attributable to variations in transfusion thresholds, patient demographics, and the analytical methodologies employed. Consequently, further extensive research is necessary to clarify the complex relationships between anemia, blood transfusions, and long-term outcomes in elderly patients undergoing hip fracture repair.
Logistic regression analysis, used to generate propensity scores, revealed five key predictors of perioperative blood transfusions in elderly hip fracture patients. These included increasing age (HR, 1.08), being female (HR, 2.18), an American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification of 3 or higher (HR, 2.16), the presence and severity of anemia (mild vs. none: HR, 3.5; moderate vs. none: HR, 14.6), and intraoperative blood loss (HR, 3.04 on a logarithmic scale). These identified risk factors can serve as a clinical guide for transfusion decisions. Patients presenting with these risk factors, especially multiple combined, face a significantly elevated likelihood of requiring perioperative blood transfusions, necessitating advance preparation for transfusion therapy.
This study has several limitations. The decision to transfuse was based on individual clinical judgment, not a standardized protocol. Its retrospective design may introduce selection bias, and unmeasured confounders could not be accounted for. Furthermore, propensity score matching has inherent limitations, including the inability to balance unmeasured characteristics.
This study found no link between blood transfusions and overall survival in elderly hip fracture patients. Anemia, however, was identified as an independent risk factor for long-term mortality. While this research offers valuable insights into transfusion strategies, prospective studies are needed to further clarify the relationship between transfusions and long-term outcomes in this patient population.
