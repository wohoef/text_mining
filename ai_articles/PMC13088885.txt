The EFSA Panel on Genetically Modified Organisms (GMO Panel) has previously evaluated genetically modified (GM) maize event DAS‐59122‐7, both as a standalone event and within stacked events. These assessments concluded that the maize events posed no greater risk to human and animal health or the environment than conventional varieties or other relevant comparators. On July 26, 2016, Pioneer submitted new nucleic acid sequencing data for maize event DAS‐59122‐7 to the European Commission, along with updated bioinformatic analyses based on this new data. The European Commission subsequently requested EFSA to review these submissions and determine if the GMO Panel's prior conclusions regarding maize event DAS‐59122‐7 remained valid. In its analysis, the GMO Panel applied the principles outlined in its guidelines for GM plant risk assessment. The new sequencing data revealed three base pair (bp) differences when compared to the original sequencing information. Two of these differences were located within a non-coding region of the insert, and one was found in the 5′ genomic flanking region. Crucially, these identified base pair differences were already present in the original plant material that formed the basis of the initial risk assessment. Consequently, with the exception of the bioinformatics analyses, the studies conducted for the original risk assessment remain valid. The updated sequencing data and the subsequent bioinformatic analyses did not reveal any new safety concerns. Therefore, the GMO Panel has concluded that its original risk assessment of maize event DAS‐59122‐7, whether as a single event or as part of stacked events, continues to be valid.

Maize event DAS-59122-7 is a genetically modified organism designed for coleopteran pest resistance and glufosinate ammonium herbicide tolerance. This is accomplished through the expression of Bacillus thuringiensis-derived cry34Ab1 and cry35Ab1 genes, which produce insecticidal proteins, and the pat gene, encoding the herbicide-tolerant phosphinothricin acetyltransferase (PAT) enzyme.

Following receipt of new sequencing data for maize event DAS-59122-7 on July 26, 2016, the European Commission tasked EFSA with its evaluation. This request, based on EU regulations, aimed to determine if previous safety conclusions for the GM maize, both as a single event and within stacked events, needed revision. The EFSA GMO Panel subsequently reviewed the provided data and methodologies, comparing them against existing assessments. This evaluation considered whether the new information necessitated any adaptations to prior opinions on the safety of maize event DAS-59122-7.
The GMO Panel adhered to its GM plant risk assessment guidelines to examine the insert sequence and conduct bioinformatics analyses. These analyses were performed in accordance with the principles outlined in the Panel's guidelines for GM plant risk assessment.
The GMO Panel considered applicant data and scientific publications for this statement.
Maize event DAS-59122-7's genetic sequence was previously provided by the applicant as part of application EFSA-GMO-NL-2005-12.1. This event contains a single insert with three distinct expression cassettes. The cry34Ab1 cassette includes the ubi1ZM promoter from Zea mays, a synthetic maize-optimized cry34Ab1 gene derived from Bacillus thuringiensis strain PS149B1, and the pinII terminator sequence from Solanum tuberosum. Similarly, the cry35Ab1 cassette features the TA peroxidase promoter from Triticum aestivum, a synthetic maize-optimized cry35Ab1 gene based on the B. thuringiensis strain PS149B1 gene, and the pinII terminator. Finally, the pat cassette is composed of the CaMV 35S promoter from Cauliflower Mosaic Virus, a synthetic maize-optimized pat gene from Streptomyces viridochromogenes, and the CaMV 35S terminator. Additionally, 2,593 base pairs of the 5' flanking region and 1,986 base pairs of the 3' flanking regions for event DAS-59122-7 were identified.
Recent resequencing of maize event DAS-59122-7 in a stacked event revealed three base pair differences compared to its 2005 sequence. These differences, two in the cry35Ab1 expression cassette's TA peroxidase promoter and one in the 5' flanking genomic region, were identified at specific base pair positions.
Sequencing of specific regions in the original maize event DAS-59122-7 material, used in 2005, confirmed that the three base pair differences identified in the stack were already present.
To assess potential risks, the applicant conducted bioinformatic analyses on the updated nucleotide sequence. These analyses aimed to determine if the DAS-59122-7 insert disrupted maize genes, if expressed proteins or other open reading frames within the insert showed similarity to known allergens or toxins, and if the insert contained sequences promoting horizontal gene transfer to microorganisms.
The sequence variations identified in maize event DAS-59122-7 in 2016 were, in fact, present in the original plant material utilized for the prior risk assessments. These three base pair differences are situated outside the coding regions of the introduced Cry34Ab1, Cry35Ab1, and PAT proteins. Specifically, two of these alterations are located within the TA peroxidase promoter of the cry35Ab1 expression cassette, and the third is found in the 5' genomic flanking region. Consequently, only a subset of the bioinformatics analyses required updating. The other studies conducted as part of the original risk assessment remain unaffected by this new sequencing information. Given the location of these base pair differences, the assessment focused on their potential impact on open reading frames (ORFs) concerning similarity to known allergens or toxins, and their implications for horizontal gene transfer (HGT). Bioinformatic screenings for allergen similarity, conducted according to EFSA guidelines, revealed no such resemblances in the affected ORFs. Furthermore, sequence analysis did not detect any similarity between these regions and microbial sequences, thus indicating no increased likelihood of HGT.
The analysis of the provided data indicates that the nucleotide differences reported in 2016 were already present in the original DAS-59122-7 maize event material used for risk assessment, both as a single event and within stacked events. These identified differences are situated outside the coding regions of the insert. Consequently, the new sequence information does not impact studies beyond bioinformatics. Updated bioinformatic analyses of the corrected sequence revealed no safety concerns. The GMO Panel therefore concludes that the prior risk assessment for maize event DAS-59122-7, encompassing both single and stacked configurations, remains valid.
