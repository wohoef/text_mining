The safety of l-lysine monohydrochloride, a feed additive produced via fermentation using a genetically modified *Escherichia coli* strain (CGMCC 7.57), was initially assessed by the EFSA FEEDAP Panel. Their initial opinion could not definitively conclude on the product's safety for target animals, consumers, users, or the environment. Consequently, the European Commission requested a new opinion from EFSA, based on further data provided by the applicant concerning the additive's characteristics. This subsequent evaluation found that the production strain and, by extension, the final product, are free of recombinant antibiotic resistance genes. Therefore, the l-lysine monohydrochloride produced using *E. coli* CGMCC 7.57 is deemed to pose no safety concerns for target species, consumers, users, or the environment, particularly regarding the genetic modification of the production strain. Furthermore, the product's endotoxin levels and dusting potential do not suggest any health risks for users.

This application seeks approval for l-Lysine monohydrochloride, a nutritional feed additive for all animal species. It is derived from *E. coli* K-12, specifically a genetically modified *E. coli* strain (CGMCC 7.57), and belongs to the amino acids, their salts, and analogues functional group.
A prior scientific opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2015) characterized this product. However, it lacked sufficient proof regarding the absence of antibiotic resistance genes in the production strain and, consequently, in the final product itself, specifically concerning the genetic modification.
New data were provided detailing the production strain's genetic modification, the additive's physical and chemical characteristics, and the manufacturing process.
To reduce bacterial endotoxins in the final product, the manufacturing process underwent improvements. Previously, three batches showed endotoxin activity between 6.3 and 6.6 IU/mg. Modifications included changing the ceramic membrane filtration from a 300 kDa pore size to a smaller 20 kDa, and implementing reverse osmosis to decrease endotoxins in the process water. Subsequent measurements of three new batches, using European Pharmacopoeia 2.6.14 methods, revealed a significantly lower endotoxin activity, ranging from 0.12 to 0.28 IU/mg.
To reduce dustiness and enlarge crystal size in the final product, the applicant modified the production process. These changes included decreasing the evaporation temperature (75–72°C) and rate (2.5–2.0 m3/h), while extending both evaporation (12–15 h) and cooling times (3–5 h).
Analysis of the new l-lysine monohydrochloride product included particle size and dustiness measurements. The mean particle diameter was approximately 600 µm, with only 0.2% to 1.2% (v/v) of particles smaller than 50 µm, based on laser diffraction analysis of three batches. This represents an improvement over the previous product, which had about 2.3% (w/w) of particles under 50 µm (based on five batches, method unspecified). The new product's dusting potential, determined by the Stauber–Heubach method across three batches, was 1.3 to 1.6 g/m3, a reduction from the 1.8 to 2.1 g/m3 observed in the 2015 assessment.

A prior EFSA opinion (2015) detailed the impact of inhaled endotoxins and their exposure limits. This information is used to calculate maximum acceptable exposure levels from feed additives.
To determine the maximum acceptable levels of endotoxin exposure from feed additives, the EFSA guidance (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012b) outlines a specific calculation. For additives in premixtures, the likely exposure duration is estimated at 800 seconds daily, based on 40 periods of 20 seconds each. Applying an uncertainty factor of two, the peak inhalation exposure extends to 1,600 seconds (0.444 hours) per day. Given a respiration volume of 1.25 m3/hour, the inhaled volume potentially containing endotoxin dust is calculated as 0.556 m3/day. This inhaled volume must not exceed 900 IU of endotoxin, meaning the dust generated from the product should contain a maximum of 1,619 IU/m3.
Assessing the potential respiratory risk from endotoxins in a product necessitates two primary measurements. These include the product's dusting potential, quantified in grams per cubic meter (g/m³), and the endotoxin activity within the dust, measured in international units per gram (IU/g) using the Limulus amoebocyte lysate assay. Should specific dust data be unavailable, the product's overall endotoxin content can serve as an alternative. To determine the endotoxin content of airborne dust (c IU/m³), a straightforward multiplication is performed: the additive's endotoxin content (a IU/g) is multiplied by its dusting potential (b g/m³). This calculated value then informs the assessment of potential inhalatory endotoxin exposure for users.
