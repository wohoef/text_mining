This research aimed to investigate the role of lncRNA ANRIL in type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) complicated by acute myocardial infarction (MI) and to elucidate its underlying mechanisms. The study employed several methods. ANRIL levels in patient peripheral blood were quantified using qRT-PCR. To model the disease, T2DM was induced in mice via streptozocin (STZ) injection, and MI was subsequently caused by ligating the left anterior descending coronary artery. Echocardiography was used to assess cardiac function. Infarct size was determined through Triphenyltetrazolium chloride (TTC) staining, while Masson staining delineated myocardial fibrosis. Myocardial cell apoptosis was detected using TUNEL staining. Western blot analysis was performed to measure the expression of myocardial fibrosis-related proteins, specifically TGF-β1, collagen I, and collagen III. The findings revealed that ANRIL was elevated in the peripheral venous blood of T2DM-MI patients and in the myocardial tissues of the T2DM-MI mouse model. Overexpression of ANRIL in these mice led to cardiac dysfunction, an increased heart-to-body weight ratio, and larger infarct sizes. Furthermore, ANRIL overexpression promoted myocardial fibrosis, increased myocardial cell apoptosis, and enhanced the expression of TGF-β1, collagen I, and collagen III. Conversely, ANRIL knockdown produced the opposite effects. In conclusion, the study suggests that ANRIL contributes to the progression and development of T2DM-MI, potentially offering new avenues for cardiovascular disease prevention and treatment.

Acute myocardial infarction (MI) outcomes are significantly worse in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) compared to non-diabetic individuals. Diabetic patients experience higher rates of mortality, morbidity, and re-infarction after MI. They also tend to have larger infarct sizes and atypical ischemic symptoms, alongside more post-infarct complications. Diabetes mellitus itself is recognized as an independent risk factor for MI in both European and Asian populations. This elevated risk is largely linked to factors like hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, and a prothrombotic state inherent in T2DM and its associated complications. Understanding the molecular mechanisms behind T2DM-complicated MI is crucial for improving clinical treatment strategies.
LncRNAs, or long non-coding RNAs, are mRNA-like molecules exceeding 200 base pairs in length that do not code for proteins. These lncRNAs are significant regulators in various cellular processes, including cancer cell proliferation, differentiation, metastasis, and cell fate determination. One such lncRNA, ANRIL (Antisense non-coding RNA in the INK4 locus), is a 3.8 kb transcript found at the 9p21.3 locus, antisense to the INK4B-ARF-INK4A gene cluster. ANRIL has been implicated in the development of several cancers, including gliomas and breast cancer. The 9p21 risk locus on chromosome 9 is linked to the severity and progression of coronary artery disease, suggesting its involvement in atherosclerosis. ANRIL is crucial for mediating these Chr9p21 associations and has been connected to numerous diseases, such as Alzheimer's, glaucoma, endometriosis, periodontitis, and notably, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Its expression is also associated with an increased risk of atherosclerosis at Chr9p21, potentially playing a role in thrombogenesis, vascular remodeling, and plaque stability. ANRIL is present in smooth muscle cells, endothelial cells, and inflammatory cells, all of which are stimulated by atherosclerosis. Furthermore, variations within ANRIL exons have been linked to MI risk in a Chinese Han population. Despite these findings, ANRIL's precise expression and role in patients with T2DM complicated by acute MI (T2DM-MI) are not yet fully understood.
This research sought to determine ANRIL's impact on T2DM-MI patients and its mechanism. Initially, ANRIL levels were measured in peripheral venous blood from T2DM-MI patients. Subsequently, a T2DM-MI mouse model was developed to assess ANRIL's effects on cardiac function, pathological alterations, myocardial fibrosis, and myocardial cell apoptosis.
This study recruited participants from Daqing Oilfield General Hospital, categorizing them into four groups. The control group consisted of 26 healthy individuals. Twenty-four patients had Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) without acute myocardial infarction (MI), forming the T2DM group. The MI group included 25 patients with acute MI but no T2DM. Finally, 34 patients experienced both acute MI and T2DM, making up the T2DM-MI group. MI diagnoses adhered to current guidelines, and specific inclusion criteria were based on a prior study. Exclusion criteria encompassed impaired glucose tolerance, secondary diabetes, and a history of ischemic heart disease. All research procedures were conducted with prior written informed consent and ethical approval from the local committee, aligning with the Declaration of Helsinki.
Male C57BL/6 mice, aged 6–7 weeks and weighing 20 ± 2 g, were acquired from the Academy of Medical Sciences, Beijing, China. The Daqing Oilfield General Hospital's Ethic Committee approved all animal procedures. Mice were housed individually, with unrestricted access to food and water, under controlled conditions of 25 ± 1 °C, 50% humidity, and a 12-hour light-dark cycle.
To establish a T2DM mouse model, 6–7 week old mice, after an overnight fast, received daily intraperitoneal injections of streptozocin (STZ, 50 mg/kg; Sigma–Aldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) for five consecutive days. This STZ solution, a common method for inducing T2DM models, was freshly prepared by dissolving it in 0.1 M citrate buffer (pH 5.5) and sterile-filtered. Control mice were administered an equivalent volume of citrate buffer. Following STZ administration, fasting blood glucose levels were monitored for three days. Mice exhibiting fasting blood glucose exceeding 11.1 mmol/L were identified as T2DM and included in subsequent experiments.
To induce myocardial infarction (MI) in T2DM mice, the left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD) was ligated. This procedure involved exposing the LAD via a left thoracotomy and securing a 7-0 silk suture around the artery. Prior to surgery, mice were anesthetized using intraperitoneal ketamine (100 mg/kg) and xylazine (5 mg/kg), and ventilated with 1.0 FiO2. Successful LAD ligation was verified by observing a pale left ventricle, S-T elevation, and a broadened QRS complex. Following 30 minutes of ischemia, the suture was released, the polyethylene tube removed, and reperfusion confirmed. The chest was then closed in layers, and the animals were allowed to recover.
For this study, mice were allocated into five distinct groups, each containing 10 animals. The control group consisted of mice that received a citrate buffer injection, serving as a control for T2DM induction, followed by sham surgery for MI. The T2DM-MI group comprised mice induced with both T2DM and MI. To investigate ANRIL's role, three additional T2DM-MI groups were established: one received an empty plasmid (T2DM-MI + vector group), another was injected with an ANRIL overexpression plasmid (T2DM-MI + pcDNA-ANRIL group), and the third received an ANRIL knockdown plasmid (T2DM-MI + shRNA-ANRIL group). All plasmid injections, at a dosage of 800 ng/kg, were administered directly into the infarcted region of the left ventricular wall. The high and low expression ANRIL plasmids (pcDNA3.1-lncRNA ANRIL and pcDNA3.1-shLncRNA ANRIL, respectively) were sourced from Shanghai Genechem Co., Ltd. A key physiological parameter, the heart weight to body weight ratio, was subsequently determined for all mice across these groups.
Four weeks post-surgery, the left ventricle's structure and function in each mouse were assessed using non-invasive transthoracic echocardiography, as detailed in prior research. This procedure involved anesthetizing the mice, after which a technician, unaware of the experimental groups, utilized a Sino-Japanese joint AloCa5000 colour ultrasound diagnostic apparatus. Heart structure and function parameters, specifically the left ventricular end-diastolic inner diameter (LViDd), left ventricular end-systolic inner diameter (LViD), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV), left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), ejection fraction (EF), fractional shortening (FS), and left ventricular mass (LVM), were automatically recorded via a two-dimensional ultrasound-guided M-curve.
To determine infarct size, heart tissues were first harvested from anesthetized mice and immediately frozen at -20 °C for 5 minutes. The hearts were then sectioned into five slices, from apex to base. From these, one 3 mm-thick tissue slice was selected and incubated in a 2% TTC solution at 37 °C for 20 minutes. Following staining, the slices were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 hours. Infarcted areas appeared white, while healthy myocardium stained red. Photographs of the slices were taken and analyzed with Image-Pro Plus software, with infarct size quantified as a percentage of the total myocardial mass.
Serial sections of paraffin-embedded tissues underwent routine HE staining. Pathological alterations were then examined via light microscopy. Images of these stained sections were captured and subsequently analyzed using Image-Pro Plus software.
To identify fibrotic regions within the myocardium, serial sections of paraffin-embedded tissues underwent Masson's trichrome staining. Under an 8x stereomicroscope, photographs were taken of the slides, revealing fibrotic areas as blue and viable tissue as red.
To detect cell death, 4-μm-thick paraffin-embedded heart tissue sections underwent TUNEL staining. This was performed using an In Situ Cell Death Detection Kit (Roche Diagnostics, Cat. 11684817910) following the manufacturer's protocol.
Myocardial tissues were processed to extract protein, initially by lysing them in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors for 30 minutes at 4 °C. A RIPA lysis buffer kit from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc. (Dallas, TX, USA) was utilized for this preparation. The extracted proteins were then resolved using 8% SDS-PAGE gels before being transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Amersham; GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA). To prevent non-specific binding, these membranes were blocked overnight at 4 °C with 5% fat-free milk (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany). Following blocking, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with specific primary antibodies: TGF-β1 (1:160), collagen I (1:500), collagen III (1:100), and GAPDH (1:1000). After three washes with TBST, a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (1:1000, Goldenbridge Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China; cat. A50-106P) was applied for 1 hour at room temperature. Protein detection was performed using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit (ECL; Wuhan Booute Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China; Cat. orb90504), and band intensities were quantified with Quantity One software, with GAPDH serving as the loading control.
Gene expression levels were quantified using the comparative 2−ΔΔCq method. For this, total RNA was extracted from either peripheral blood or myocardial tissues following the manufacturer's protocol for TRIzol (Invitrogen). This isolated RNA was then converted into cDNA using a High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.). Subsequent RT-qPCR was performed with a SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ Kit (TaKaRa Bio, Inc., Otsu, Japan). ANRIL expression was normalized against the internal control GAPDH, utilizing specific primer pairs for both lncRNA ANRIL and GAPDH.
Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 19.0 software. Results are reported as means ± standard deviation. To compare two independent groups, Student's t-test was employed, while one-way ANOVA was used for comparisons involving multiple groups. A p-value below 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.
Figure 1 illustrates the ANRIL expression levels in peripheral venous blood. Patients with T2DM exhibited a significant elevation in ANRIL compared to control subjects (p < 0.05). Similarly, MI patients also showed a notable increase in ANRIL expression when contrasted with controls (p < 0.05). Critically, ANRIL expression was further and significantly elevated in T2DM-MI patients, surpassing levels observed in both T2DM-only (p < 0.05) and MI-only (p < 0.001) patient groups. These findings suggest a link between heightened ANRIL expression and the progression and development of T2DM complicated by MI.
To create the T2DM-MI mouse model, C57BL/6 mice first underwent a high-fat diet and received daily intraperitoneal injections of STZ for five days to induce T2DM. A control group was given an equivalent volume of citrate buffer. Following STZ administration, fasting blood glucose levels were monitored for three days, with mice exceeding 11.1 mmol/L confirmed as diabetic (Table 1). Prior to MI surgery, insulin resistance was also verified in these T2DM mice through measurements of relevant parameters (Supplementary Table). After the T2DM model was established, the MI model was then induced. Subsequent echocardiography revealed significant cardiac dysfunction in the T2DM-MI mice compared to controls (Table 2), characterized by elevated LViDd, LViD, LVESV, LVEDV, and LVM, alongside reduced EF and FS (p < 0.05). These findings collectively confirmed the successful development of the T2DM-MI mouse model.
In T2DM-MI mice, ANRIL expression was found to be significantly elevated in myocardial tissues when compared to the control group (p < 0.001). To investigate its role, ANRIL levels were then experimentally manipulated, either increased (overexpressed) or reduced (knocked down), with these changes verified by qRT-PCR. Subsequent analysis of cardiac function revealed that ANRIL overexpression in T2DM-MI mice led to a significant increase in parameters such as LViDd, LViD, LVESV, LVEDV, and LVM, while simultaneously decreasing EF and FS (p < 0.05). Conversely, ANRIL knockdown produced the opposite effects, significantly reducing LViDd, LViD, LVESV, LVEDV, and LVM, and increasing EF and FS (p < 0.05). These findings collectively suggest that elevated ANRIL expression contributes to cardiac dysfunction in the T2DM-MI mouse model.
Pathological alterations were assessed across the different groups (Fig. 3A). Microscopic examination revealed a notable increase in the heart/body weight ratio in T2DM-MI mice compared to controls (p < 0.05). This ratio was further elevated by ANRIL overexpression in T2DM-MI mice (p < 0.05), while ANRIL knockdown led to its significant reduction in these mice (p < 0.05) (Fig. 3B). Additionally, the infarct size in T2DM-MI mice was substantially larger than in the control group (p < 0.001). ANRIL overexpression exacerbated this, significantly increasing infarct size compared to the T2DM-MI + vector group (p < 0.01). Conversely, ANRIL knockdown resulted in a significant decrease in infarct size in the T2DM mice (p < 0.01) (Fig. 3C).
Myocardial pathology was assessed through HE staining, while Masson's trichrome staining was utilized to identify fibrotic regions within the myocardium (Fig. 4A and 4B, respectively). The control group exhibited a complete myocardial tissue structure, devoid of any fibrosis. Conversely, in the T2DM-MI and T2DM-MI + vector groups, myocardial cells displayed uneven staining, disorganized myocardial fibers, narrowed gaps, and clear inflammation in necrotic areas. The T2DM-MI + pcDNA-ANRIL group showed similar disorganization of myocardial fibers, alongside a significant presence of fibroblasts in the myocardial vasculature and mesenchyme, indicating severe fibrous connective tissue hyperplasia. In contrast, the T2DM-MI + sh-ANRIL group presented with largely uniform cardiomyocyte staining and a near-normal appearance, with only slight hypertrophy, minimal myocardial necrosis, and mild inflammatory infiltration in the infarct area. Furthermore, the blue-stained collagen fibers were more pronounced in the T2DM-MI and T2DM-MI + vector groups compared to the control group. Notably, ANRIL overexpression exacerbated the presence of collagen fibers, whereas ANRIL knockdown mitigated them when compared to the T2DM-MI + vector group.
Myocardial cell apoptosis was assessed in T2DM-MI mice using TUNEL staining. Compared to the control group, the T2DM-MI group exhibited a significant increase in the percentage of apoptotic myocardial cells (Fig. 5, p < 0.001). Furthermore, ANRIL overexpression notably elevated the apoptotic index (p < 0.01), while ANRIL knockdown produced the inverse effect (p < 0.01).
The study investigated ANRIL's impact on key proteins associated with myocardial fibrosis in T2DM-MI mice. It is known that TGF-β1 stimulates cardiac fibroblast proliferation and extracellular matrix production, while fibrillar collagen types I and III are primary constituents of the myocardial collagen matrix. In the T2DM-MI group, the protein levels of TGF-β1, collagen I, and collagen III were notably elevated compared to the control group (p < 0.01, p < 0.05, and p < 0.01, respectively). Furthermore, ANRIL overexpression led to a significant increase in the expression of these same proteins (TGF-β1, collagen I, and collagen III) when compared to the T2DM-MI + vector group (p < 0.01, p < 0.05, and p < 0.05, respectively). Conversely, reducing ANRIL levels (ANRIL knockdown) resulted in the opposite outcome for these proteins (p < 0.05, p < 0.05, and p < 0.05, respectively).
Our investigation initially revealed elevated ANRIL levels in the peripheral venous blood of T2DM-MI patients. Similarly, ANRIL was found to be upregulated in the myocardial tissues of our T2DM-MI mouse model. These findings align with previous research showing increased ANRIL expression in mice experiencing both T2DM and cerebral infarction. The ANRIL locus on chromosome 9p contains distinct, closely linked single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) that Broadbent et al. suggested influence susceptibility to coronary artery disease and diabetes. ANRIL is crucial for mediating Chr9p21 associations and has been implicated in various diseases, including T2DM. Furthermore, a meta-analysis has confirmed the association of specific genetic variants at Chr9p21 with T2DM. Notably, ANRIL variants have also been linked to MI risk within a Chinese Han population.
Our investigation then focused on how ANRIL influenced cardiac function and pathological alterations in T2DM-MI mice. Overexpression of ANRIL led to cardiac dysfunction, an elevated heart/body weight ratio, and a larger infarct size in these mice. Conversely, reducing ANRIL levels produced the opposite outcomes. Previous research by Pfeffer et al. (29) established that rats with infarcts exceeding 46% developed congestive heart failure, characterized by diminished cardiac output, increased filling pressures, and limited response to stress. The heart/body weight ratio is a recognized indicator for myocardial hypertrophy and can also evaluate myocardial hypotrophy (22). Therefore, our findings imply that ANRIL plays a role in the development of heart failure and myocardial hypotrophy in T2DM-MI mice.
Cardiac myocytes in adults are terminally differentiated cells, meaning they are unable to divide. Research indicates that apoptosis in these cells contributes to the progression from compensated to decompensated heart failure. The structural foundation for diastolic dysfunction is laid by myocardial fibrosis. Furthermore, the quality, not merely the quantity, of tissue is responsible for pathological left ventricular hypertrophy, which is linked to adverse cardiovascular events and sudden cardiac death. The primary constituents of the myocardial collagen matrix are fibrillar collagen types I and III. Collagen type I makes up approximately 80% of the total collagen protein, while collagen III accounts for a smaller proportion, around 11%. These fibrillar collagens act as connectors between muscle cells, muscle fibers, and blood vessels, forming a supportive scaffold for both the muscular and vascular systems. In the current study, ANRIL overexpression in T2DM-MI mice led to several observations: myocardial fibers were disorganized, numerous fibroblasts were present in the myocardial vasculature and mesenchyme, and there was severe hyperplasia of fibrous connective tissue. Additionally, ANRIL overexpression significantly elevated the apoptotic index in these mice. This overexpression also resulted in myocardial cell apoptosis and an increased expression of myocardial fibrosis-related proteins, specifically TGF-β1, collagen I, and collagen III. These findings suggest that ANRIL overexpression promotes myocardial fiber cell apoptosis and myocardial fibrosis by enhancing the components of the myocardial collagen matrix in T2DM-MI mice.
Our research suggests ANRIL plays a role in the progression of T2DM-MI. We observed elevated ANRIL levels in both the peripheral blood of T2DM-MI patients and the heart tissue of T2DM-MI mice. Overexpression of ANRIL in these mice resulted in cardiac dysfunction, including heart failure, myocardial hypotrophy, and increased myocardial fiber cell apoptosis. These findings offer new avenues for understanding and potentially treating cardiovascular diseases.
