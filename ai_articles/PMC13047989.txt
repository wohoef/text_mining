This study aimed to uncover the molecular mechanisms by which exosomes derived from human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUCMSC-Exos) mitigate acute lung injury (ALI) caused by burns. Our methodology involved several steps. First, hUCMSC-Exos were isolated and characterized using transmission electron microscopy, and their protein markers, CD9 and CD63, were identified via western blot. We quantified miR-451 expression within these exosomes using qRT-PCR. To assess the impact on ALI, we measured inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) in both lung tissues and serum, and oxidative stress indicators (malondialdehyde, myeloperoxidase, superoxide dismutase) in lung tissues, all through ELISA. Lung tissue morphology was examined with hematoxylin–eosin staining, and apoptosis was detected using TUNEL assays. Finally, western blotting was employed to analyze the expression of proteins associated with the Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/NF-κB signaling pathway in burned lung tissues. The findings revealed that hUCMSC-Exos effectively lowered TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels in burned rats. This beneficial effect was negated when miR-451 expression within the hUCMSC-Exo group was inhibited. Ultimately, our research indicates that hUCMSC-Exo-derived miR-451 ameliorates ALI by modulating the TLR4/NF-κB pathway. Therefore, we conclude that hUCMSC-derived exosomes, through miR-451, can lessen burn-induced ALI.

Severe burns necessitating emergency surgery frequently lead to multi-organ damage. Among these complications, acute lung injury (ALI) is a prevalent issue, typically manifesting early in the post-burn period. The progression of ALI often results in acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), a condition linked to high mortality rates in burn patients. Currently, clinical management for burn-induced ALI primarily relies on symptomatic approaches, as effective strategies to prevent or mitigate its onset are lacking. Thermal scalds, a common burn type, trigger widespread inflammatory responses. The development of ALI involves several inflammation-related pathways, including the Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/NF-κB, Nrf2/HO-1, and MAPK/ERK pathways.
MSCs are a promising cell source for therapies. Specifically, hUCMSCs have shown potential in mitigating inflammatory conditions like spinal cord injury, Alzheimer's disease, and hyperglycemia-induced retinal inflammation. Furthermore, exosomes, which are extracellular vesicles released by MSCs, are gaining recognition for their therapeutic advantages. These vesicles, typically 30-120 nm in diameter, are particularly attractive for treating lung diseases, including ALI. For instance, MSC exosomes have been shown to reduce endotoxin-induced ALI by inhibiting inflammatory cell infiltration and response, and MSC-derived extracellular vesicles lessened lung injury in a rat model of bronchopulmonary dysplasia.
Exosomes originating from MSCs transport various molecules, including proteins, mRNA, and miRNA, delivering them to other cells to influence cellular functions. Research indicates that exosome-delivered miRNAs can modulate inflammatory responses by regulating proteins within inflammatory signaling pathways. Specifically, miR-451 is notably abundant in MSC exosomes and has been shown to suppress the TLR4/NF-κB pathway by targeting TLR4. Therefore, we hypothesized that hUCMSC-Exos mitigate burn-induced ALI by utilizing miR-451 to regulate inflammation through the TLR4/NF-κB pathway, thereby promoting tissue recovery.
Human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUCMSCs) were acquired from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. They were cultured in DMEM/high glucose medium (HyClone Laboratories), enriched with 10% defined fetal bovine serum (HyClone Laboratories), and incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2.
HUCMSCs were transfected with either a miR-451 inhibitor or a control using Lipofectamine 2000, following the manufacturer's instructions.
Exosomes were isolated from the supernatant of hUCMSCs using a multi-step centrifugation and precipitation method. First, hUCMSCs, grown to 80-90% confluence, had their growth medium (10% FBS DMEM) replaced with exosome-free 10% FBS DMEM. This exosome-free FBS was prepared by ultracentrifugation at 100,000g for 16 hours. The hUCMSC conditioned medium then underwent sequential centrifugation steps: 300g for 20 minutes, 2000g for 20 minutes, and 10,000g for 30 minutes, to eliminate cellular debris. The resulting clear supernatant was chilled on ice before being combined with ExoQuick-TC exosome precipitation solution (5:1 ratio) and incubated overnight at 4°C. Following this, the mixture was centrifuged at 1500g for 30 minutes, and the supernatant was discarded. The pelleted hUCMSC-exosomes were then resuspended in 100-200 µL of PBS and stored at –80°C. Total exosome protein content was measured. To ensure the ExoQuick-TC itself didn't interfere with subsequent analyses, a control was run using fresh conditioned medium treated identically with ExoQuick-TC, PBS, and stored at –80°C.
Exosome morphology and size distribution were assessed using a transmission electron microscope and Nanosizer technology, respectively, after resuspending purified hUCMSC-Exos in PBS. Western blotting confirmed exosome presence by detecting specific markers, CD9 and CD63.
To quantify miR-451, 10 ng of total RNA underwent reverse transcription and amplification using a specific miRNA kit from Applied Biosystems. Normalization of all results was performed against U6 levels, measured with the ABI miRNA U6 assay kit. miR-451 itself was extracted using an Ambion miRVana kit.
For protein analysis, samples were first prepared by extracting proteins using RIPA buffer. A Bio-Rad kit was then used to quantify the protein concentration. Following this, about 30 µg of protein per sample underwent separation on a 10% SDS-polyacrylamide gel before being transferred to polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. These membranes were then blocked with 5% skim milk. Primary antibodies, including anti-CD9, anti-CD63, anti-p65, anti-TLR4, and anti-p-p65 (from Abcam and Santa Cruz Biotechnology at specified dilutions), were applied for overnight incubation at 4°C. After washing, secondary antibodies were added for a 1-hour incubation at room temperature. Finally, protein detection on the membranes was performed using Super ECL Plus detection reagent after a TBST wash.
Commercial ELISA kits from Abcam were utilized to measure TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels in both rat lung tissues and serum. Additionally, lung tissue concentrations of myeloperoxidase (MPO), malondialdehyde (MDA), and superoxide dismutase (SOD) were quantified using these kits. All concentrations were derived from their respective optical density values.
After fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde, lung tissues were paraffin-embedded. Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining was then used for light microscopic examination of sections.
Apoptosis in lung tissues was assessed using TUNEL staining following manufacturer instructions. Quantification of apoptotic cells, specifically TUNEL-positive cells, was achieved with Image-Pro Plus software.
The Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees of the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University granted approval for this study. Female SD rats, aged six weeks and weighing 200 ± 20 g, were procured from the Shanghai Laboratory Animals Center (SLAC) in Shanghai, China. These animals were maintained under controlled environmental conditions, including consistent temperature, humidity, and a 12-hour light/dark cycle, with unrestricted access to food and water. To induce a severe burn model, a previously established protocol was followed, resulting in a 30% total body surface area (TBSA) full-thickness burn. This involved anesthetizing the rats with an intraperitoneal injection of 300 mg/kg Avertin (20 mg/mL 2,2,2-tribromoethanol, Sigma, USA), followed by complete removal of dorsal hair. The rats' backs were then immersed in 94°C hot water for 12 seconds. Post-burn, the wound was disinfected with a 1% iodine tincture and kept dry to prevent infection, and a 5 mL balanced salt solution was immediately administered to mitigate shock.
Following burn injury, rats were randomly allocated to several experimental groups. The sham group underwent a 12-second immersion in 37°C water, mirroring the burn procedure without actual injury. The PBS group received an equivalent volume of PBS. Other groups received tail vein injections of hUCMSC-exosomes: the hUCMSC-Exo group received 800 μg of hUCMSC-exosomes in 1 mL of PBS. The NCI-Exo group was administered 800 μg of hUCMSC-exosomes, previously transfected with an inhibitor control, in 1 mL of PBS. Lastly, the miR-451I-Exo group received 800 μg of hUCMSC-exosomes, transfected with a miR-451 inhibitor, also in 1 mL of PBS. Wounds remained open, and animals were euthanized at either 24 or 48 hours post-treatment for subsequent lung tissue pathology analysis.
Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Data are presented as mean ± SD, with Student's t-test used for two-group comparisons and ANOVA for multiple groups.
Exosomes were successfully isolated from hUCMSC culture supernatants. Characterization of these hUCMSC-Exos confirmed their purity and suitability for further experiments. Transmission electron microscopy revealed spherical, cup-shaped vesicles measuring 40-160 nm in diameter (Fig. 1A, B). Western blot analysis further validated the isolation, showing high expression of exosome markers CD9 and CD63 in the hUCMSC-Exos (Fig. 1C). Additionally, miR-451 levels were significantly elevated in the hUCMSC-Exos compared to the hUCMSCs themselves (Fig. 1D).
To assess how hUCMSC-Exos impact inflammatory cytokines in burned rats, the animals were randomly assigned to six distinct groups: sham, burn, burn + PBS, burn + Exo, burn + NCI-Exo, and burn + miR-451I-Exo. Our ELISA analysis of rat lung tissues revealed a significant increase in TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels at 6, 12, 24, and 48 hours post-burn injury, compared to the sham group. However, treatment with hUCMSC-Exos effectively reduced these elevated inflammatory markers. This beneficial effect was negated when miR-451 expression within the hUCMSC-Exo group was inhibited, as shown by a notable decrease in miR-451 in exosomes from hUCMSCs transfected with the miR-451 inhibitor (Fig. 2A). Consistent with these lung tissue findings, a similar reduction in TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels was observed in the rat serum (Fig. 2E-G). These findings collectively indicate that miR-451 derived from hUCMSC-Exos plays a crucial role in suppressing inflammation triggered by burn injury.
Our investigation extended to understanding how miR-451, delivered by hUCMSC-Exos, impacts acute lung injury (ALI) in burned rats. We began by assessing lung function parameters, including inspiratory capacity (IC), central airway resistance (Rn), and lung compliance (Crs), after 6 hours of ALI and exosome treatment, as detailed in the provided table. ELISA results, obtained at both 24 and 48 hours post-burn, showed that levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), myeloperoxidase (MPO), and superoxide dismutase (SOD) were notably lower in the burn models compared to the sham group. However, hUCMSC-Exo administration significantly increased these levels, an effect that was subsequently reversed when miR-451 expression was inhibited (Fig. 3A–C). Histological examination of cutaneous wounds 48 hours after injury further supported these findings, demonstrating that hUCMSC-Exo treatment significantly reduced lung injury. This protective effect was diminished upon miR-451 inhibition (Fig. 3D). Additionally, TUNEL assays performed on lung tissues 48 hours post-burn revealed that the ability of hUCMSC-Exos to suppress apoptosis was substantially impaired when exosomal miR-451 expression was inhibited (Fig. 3E). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis confirmed a significant upregulation of miR-451 in the hUCMSC-Exo treated group compared to the PBS-treated group (Fig. 3F). To delve deeper into the mechanism by which hUCMSC-Exo-derived miR-451 ameliorates ALI, we investigated the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway, a key regulator of inflammatory responses. We specifically analyzed the expression of TLR4 and its downstream targets, NF-κB/P65 and p-P65. Burned rats exhibited significantly elevated levels of TLR4 and p-P65 proteins compared to the sham group. Importantly, hUCMSC-Exo administration led to a significant downregulation of both TLR4 and p-P65 protein expression at 48 hours. This downregulation was reversed when miR-451 expression in the hUCMSC-Exo group was inhibited. No statistically significant differences were observed in P65 expression across the groups following burn injury (Fig. 3G). These collective results strongly indicate that hUCMSC-Exo-derived miR-451 improves ALI by modulating the TLR4/NF-κB pathway.
This study successfully isolated and characterized exosomes from human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cells (hUCMSCs). Consistent with prior research, which has demonstrated the efficacy of MSC-derived exosomes in various conditions, our findings show that hUCMSC-Exos reduce inflammation following burn injury. Previous studies have reported that MSC exosomes decrease pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β and TNF-α in cartilage repair, and hUCMSC exosomes lower TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 in inflammatory bowel disease. Additionally, hUCMSC exosomes were shown to mitigate LPS-induced macrophage inflammation and acute lung injury in rats. Our results align with these observations, showing a reduction in TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 levels in lung tissues after burn.
The transfer of microRNAs (miRNAs) through exosomes is crucial for controlling inflammation. For instance, exosome-delivered miRNA-122 in monocytes has been shown to curb the HO-1 pathway, leading to increased pro-inflammatory cytokines in alcoholic hepatitis in mice. Macrophages, when activated, release exosomes rich in miR-155, which acts as a paracrine mediator of inflammation. Similarly, after traumatic brain injury, elevated miR-124-3p in microglial exosomes suppresses neuronal inflammation. Furthermore, miR-181c directly binds to TLR4's 3′-UTR, thereby inhibiting its expression and limiting inflammatory responses. Our research specifically revealed that hUCMSC-Exos significantly reduce inflammation in severe burn-induced ALI, an effect that was negated when miR-451 within these exosomes was inhibited.
The TLR4/NF-κB pathway is crucial in inflammatory responses, implicated in conditions like atherosclerosis, traumatic brain injury, and optic nerve crush. Our in vivo investigation into miR-451's role in inflammation focused on this pathway. We observed that hUCMSC-Exo administration led to a significant decrease in TLR4 and p-P65 protein expression. This effect was reversed when miR-451 was inhibited, supporting previous findings that miR-451 directly targets and inhibits TLR4 expression, thereby reducing LPS-induced inflammation. These results collectively indicate that miR-451, delivered by hUCMSC-Exos, mitigates ALI-related inflammation by modulating the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway.
Our study concludes that hUCMSC-Exos effectively mitigate burn-induced inflammation. We further revealed that miR-451 is crucial to this exosome-mediated anti-inflammatory effect. Specifically, inhibiting miR-451 led to increased TLR4 expression and subsequent p65 activation in burn-induced ALI rats. These insights offer a deeper understanding of ALI mechanisms and suggest a promising therapeutic target.
