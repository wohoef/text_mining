Osteosarcoma is a highly aggressive cancer known for its rapid metastasis to organs like the lungs, liver, and brain. Despite therapeutic advancements, its prognosis remains unfavorable. This study observed that microRNA-511 (miR-511) is downregulated in osteosarcoma cell lines (MG63, U-2 OS, Saos-2), while mitogen activated protein kinase 1 (MAPK1) is upregulated. Evidence suggests MAPK1 is a target of miR-511, as miR-511 mimic transfection led to decreased MAPK1 expression. Furthermore, this transfection inhibited osteosarcoma MG63 cell proliferation and invasion. In vivo studies using nude mice also demonstrated that miR-511 mimic transfection reduced metastatic tumor burden. These anti-cancer effects are attributed to MAPK1 targeting, offering a novel perspective on osteosarcoma's molecular pathogenesis.

Osteosarcoma, a prevalent solid bone cancer in children and adolescents, frequently affects the proximal humerus, tibia, and distal femur, leading to significant morbidity and mortality. Symptoms such as joint pain, swelling, limited movement, and fractures are common. While surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy form the standard treatment, these therapies carry severe side effects like nephrotoxicity and cardiotoxicity. Consequently, understanding the molecular mechanisms driving osteosarcoma development and identifying novel therapeutic strategies are crucial for improving patient outcomes and preventing disease progression.
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are increasingly recognized as crucial indicators of cancer severity and prognosis. Over the past decades, research has revealed the presence of numerous dysregulated miRNAs in the pathogenesis of osteosarcoma. While microRNA-511 (miR-511) has been found to be altered in various cancers, including breast, colorectal, and lung adenocarcinoma, its specific role in osteosarcoma progression remained unclear. Concurrently, the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascade, a critical pathway in cellular processes, involves MAPK1 as a downstream oncogene. Previous studies have shown elevated MAPK1 levels in several human cancers, suggesting its potential involvement in osteosarcoma development.
Based on Targetscan predictions, the 3'UTR of MAPK1 exhibits perfect complementarity with miR-511-3p. This suggests MAPK1 is a potential target of miR-511-3p, and restoring miR-511-3p expression could suppress osteosarcoma by affecting the MAPK signaling pathway. Our research thus explored the effects of miR-511 mimic on osteosarcoma proliferation and invasion, investigating the underlying molecular mechanisms both in vitro and in vivo.
Osteosarcoma cell lines, specifically MG63, U-2 OS, and Saos-2, along with the non-neoplastic osteoblast cell line hFOB1.19, were obtained from ATCC. Each cell line was maintained under specific culture conditions. MG63 cells were cultured in EMEM with 10% FBS. U-2 OS and Saos-2 cells were cultured in McCoy's 5a Medium Modified, with 10% FBS for U-2 OS and 15% FBS for Saos-2. The hFOB1.19 cells were cultured in a mixed medium of Ham's F12 and DMEM, supplemented with L-glutamine, G418, and 10% FBS. All cell cultures, with the exception of hFOB1.19 which were maintained at 34°C, were incubated at 37°C in a CO2 incubator.
Nude mice, aged 6–8 weeks, were acquired from North Sichuan Medical College Affiliated Hospital. Ethical approval for the experimental protocol was granted by the Committee on the Ethics of Animal Experiments of the same institution.
TargetScan predicted an interaction between miR-511 and MAPK1. For experiments, MG63 cells were transfected using Lipofectamine 2000 with various constructs including miR-511 mimics (wild-type and mutant), mimic control, pcDNA-MAPK1, and pcDNA vector. These reagents were sourced from GenePharma Co., Ltd. Transfection protocols were followed precisely as per manufacturer instructions. Following 48 hours post-transfection, the cells were utilized for further experimental analysis.
To analyze gene expression, total RNA was isolated from MG63, U2OS, Saos-2, and hFOB1.19 cell lines using TRIzol reagent. Following the manufacturer's protocol, RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA. The mRNA levels of miR-511 and MAPK1 were then quantified using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix on a Real-time PCR system. Specific primers for MAPK1, miR-511-3p, and U6 were synthesized. Data analysis was performed using the 2-ΔΔCt method, with miR-511 normalized to U6 and MAPK1 normalized to β-actin.
To assess cell migration, MG63 cells were seeded in serum-free EMEM within the upper transwell chambers. The lower chambers contained EMEM with 10% FBS. Following a 24-hour incubation, cells that migrated to the lower chambers were fixed, stained with hematoxylin, counted, and imaged using an inverted microscope.
To assess the impact on neovascularization, a Matrigel tube formation assay was performed. MG63 cells were cultured to 90% confluency, then incubated in serum-free medium for 24 hours at 37°C. The resulting conditional medium was collected and stored. Subsequently, HUVECs (2 × 10⁴) were seeded onto a Matrigel matrix using this conditional medium. Following incubation at 37°C with 5% CO₂ for 12 hours, photomicrographs were captured. The lengths of the formed tubular structures were then quantified using Image-Pro Plus 6.0 software.
To assess cell invasion, transfected cells were placed in matrigel-coated upper chambers with serum-free medium. After 24 hours, invasive cells were stained with crystal violet and photographed.
Forty-eight hours post-transfection, Western blot analysis was employed to assess the expression levels of Ki67, cleaved caspase-3, VEGF, β-catenin, TCF4, and c-MYC. GAPDH served as the internal control. Visualization of immunoreactive bands was achieved using a Chemiluminescent ECL Reagent Kit.
To assess the impact on the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, a TOP/FOP-FLASH assay was conducted. Transfected MG63 cells were incubated with a mixture containing serum-free DMEM, β-catenin and TCF4 expression vectors, and a Renilla luciferase vector. Luciferase activity was subsequently measured.
To assess the in vivo effects, nude mice were divided into two groups (n=18). One group received subcutaneous injections of MG63 cells transfected with a vector (control), while the other received cells transfected with pcDNA-MAPK1. Tumor volume was measured every five days for 30 days using the formula: volume = length × width² × 0.5. Survival rates were also recorded at various intervals up to 30 days post-injection.
Tissue sections underwent formalin fixation and paraffin embedding. Immunohistochemistry for Ki67 and VEGF was performed on these 3 μm sections. Images were captured at 400x magnification.
To assess tumor spread to the lungs and liver, HE staining was utilized. Lung and liver tissues from mice were fixed, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned. These sections underwent deparaffinization and hydration before examination via H&E staining to observe tumor focal lesions.
Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 17.0). Data, presented as means ± SD, were analyzed via one-way ANOVA. P values less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.
TargetScan predicted binding sites between miR-511 and MAPK1 (Fig. 1A). In osteosarcoma cells (MG63, U2OS, Saos-2), miR-511 expression was significantly lower than in non-neoplastic hFOB1.19 cells (Fig. 1B). Conversely, MAPK1 expression was markedly elevated in these osteosarcoma cell lines compared to hFOB1.19 cells (Fig. 1C), with the most pronounced changes observed in MG63 cells.
Osteosarcoma cells exhibit altered expression of miR-511 and MAPK1. Targetscan predicted binding sites between miR-511 and MAPK1. RT-PCR analysis revealed significantly lower miR-511 levels and higher MAPK1 levels in osteosarcoma cell lines (MG63, U2OS, Saos-2) compared to normal cells (hFOB1.19). These findings indicate a dysregulation of both molecules in osteosarcoma.
Transfection with miR-511 mimic successfully increased miR-511 expression, demonstrating effective transfection. This intervention significantly lowered both the mRNA and protein levels of MAPK1, suggesting an inverse correlation between miR-511 and MAPK1. Furthermore, a luciferase reporter assay confirmed that miR-511 directly targets MAPK1, leading to its negative regulation.
MAPK1 was identified as a target of miR-511. MG63 cells were divided into three groups: control, scramble mimic transfection (mimic NC), and miR-511 mimic transfection. RT-PCR analysis confirmed successful miR-511 mimic transfection, leading to a significant increase in miR-511 expression. Concurrently, the expression of MAPK1 was significantly reduced in the miR-511 mimic group compared to the control. Further validation through a luciferase reporter assay demonstrated that miR-511 directly targets MAPK1, as evidenced by the reduced luciferase activity when co-transfected with wild-type miR-511 compared to a mutant version. These findings collectively indicate that miR-511 negatively regulates MAPK1 expression.
The proliferation of MG63 osteosarcoma cells was investigated in relation to miR-511 expression. Cells were divided into experimental groups: a control group of normal MG63 cells, a group transfected with pcDNA-MAPK1, and a vector control group. The expression of MAPK1 at both mRNA (Fig. 3A) and protein (Fig. 3B) levels was confirmed to be elevated following pcDNA-MAPK1 transfection. Further experiments involved four groups: control, miR-511 mimic transfection, pcDNA-MAPK1 transfection (MAPK1 group), and co-transfection with both pcDNA-MAPK1 and miR-511 mimic (MAPK1 + mimic group). Cell proliferation was assessed using BrdU staining (Fig. 3C), revealing that miR-511 mimic transfection significantly reduced proliferation compared to the control. Conversely, MAPK1 overexpression led to increased proliferation. Importantly, the co-transfection group showed a diminished effect, indicating that miR-511 counteracted MAPK1's proliferative influence. Western blot analysis of proliferation markers PCNA and Ki67 (Fig. 3D) corroborated these findings, showing reduced expression in the mimic group and increased expression in the MAPK1 group, with miR-511 mimic reversing the MAPK1-induced upregulation. These results collectively demonstrate that increased miR-511 suppresses MG63 cell proliferation by targeting MAPK1.
The invasive capacity of MG63 cells was investigated following transfection with miR-511 mimic, pcDNA-MAPK1, or a combination of both. Transwell assays revealed that miR-511 mimic transfection significantly reduced cell invasion compared to the control group. Conversely, pcDNA-MAPK1 transfection led to increased invasion. When both were present, the inhibitory effect of miR-511 was partially counteracted. Furthermore, miR-511 mimic suppressed neovascularization, as indicated by tube formation assays, while MAPK1 promoted it. Western blot analysis showed that miR-511 mimic decreased the expression of VEGF and N-cadherin while increasing E-cadherin. MAPK1 exhibited the opposite effect. These changes were reversed when miR-511 mimic was co-transfected with pcDNA-MAPK1.
Transfecting MG63 cells with pcDNA-MAPK1 significantly elevated both MAPK1 mRNA and protein levels compared to vector transfection.
The effect of miR-511 on MG63 cell proliferation was assessed using a BrdU assay. Results indicated that transfection with miR-511 mimic led to a significant decrease in cell proliferation compared to the control group. Conversely, cells overexpressing MAPK1 showed increased proliferation. When both miR-511 mimic and pcDNA-MAPK1 were introduced, the proliferative effect was diminished. Western blot analysis revealed that miR-511 mimic suppressed the expression of proliferation markers PCNA and Ki67, while MAPK1 overexpression upregulated them. Crucially, miR-511 was able to counteract the MAPK1-induced increase in PCNA and Ki67. These observations collectively demonstrate that elevated miR-511 inhibits MG63 cell proliferation by targeting MAPK1.
To assess the in vivo anti-cancer effects of miR-511, nude mice were divided into two groups: a control group receiving MG63 cells transfected with miR-NC, and a mimic group receiving MG63 cells transfected with miR-511 mimic. Tumor weight was significantly reduced in the mimic group compared to the control group, as evidenced by gross tumor weight measurements and representative tumor images. RT-PCR analysis confirmed a substantial increase in miR-511 levels and a corresponding decrease in MAPK1 expression in the mimic group. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed reduced expression of Ki67 and VEGF in tumors from the mimic group. Furthermore, HE staining of liver and lung tissues showed a decrease in tumor focal lesions in the mimic group, indicating that elevated miR-511 expression inhibited osteosarcoma metastasis in vivo.
The invasive capabilities of MG63 cells were assessed using a Transwell assay to understand the role of miR-511. Results indicated that cells transfected with miR-511 mimic exhibited reduced invasion compared to the control group. Conversely, cells overexpressing MAPK1 showed increased invasiveness. When both miR-511 mimic and pcDNA-MAPK1 were introduced, the effect of MAPK1 overexpression was attenuated. Further investigation using Matrigel tube formation assay revealed that miR-511 mimic inhibited neovascularization, whereas MAPK1 promoted it. Protein expression analysis showed that VEGF and N-cadherin levels were reduced in the mimic group but elevated in the MAPK1 group. In contrast, E-cadherin expression increased with miR-511 mimic and decreased with MAPK1. Crucially, miR-511 was able to reverse the MAPK1-induced changes in these protein expressions. These observations collectively demonstrate that increased miR-511 suppresses MG63 cell invasion by targeting MAPK1.
To confirm the anti-cancer effects of miR-511 in vivo, tumor weight was assessed. As illustrated in Figure 5A, tumor weight was significantly reduced in the mimic group compared to the control group (P<0.05), with corresponding tumor images shown in Figure 5B. The mimic group also exhibited a substantial increase in miR-511 levels and a marked decrease in MAPK1 expression compared to the control (Figure 5C, P<0.05). Furthermore, the expression of Ki67 and VEGF was notably lower in the mimic group (Figure 5D). Histological examination of liver and lung tissues via HE staining revealed that the mimic treatment decreased focal lesions, indicating that elevated miR-511 expression effectively inhibited osteosarcoma metastasis in vivo.
Osteosarcoma, an aggressive cancer originating from bone metaphyses, exhibits rapid metastasis. Despite treatment advancements, its prognosis remains poor. Understanding the molecular mechanisms driving osteosarcoma is crucial for developing effective therapies.
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) play a crucial role in regulating gene expression post-transcriptionally, and their significance in various human cancers is well-established. While miR-511 has been identified as a tumor suppressor in numerous cancers, including lung, prostate, gastric, gallbladder, hepatocellular carcinoma, and colorectal cancer, a few studies have indicated its oncogenic role in specific conditions like HCV-associated diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. Our research, however, revealed a notable downregulation of miR-511 in osteosarcoma cell lines (MG63, SaOS2, and U2OS). Crucially, restoring miR-511 levels through mimic transfection effectively inhibited osteosarcoma cell proliferation, invasion, and in vivo tumor growth. These findings strongly suggest that miR-511 functions as a tumor suppressor in the context of osteosarcoma.
While miR-511 has been shown to target various genes including AKT1 and TRIM24, our study focused on its interaction with MAPK1. Using TargetScan, we predicted a potential binding site between miR-511 and MAPK1, which was subsequently validated through luciferase reporter assays. We observed that MAPK1 expression is elevated in osteosarcoma cell lines (MG63, Saos2, U2OS), inversely correlating with miR-511 levels. Crucially, overexpressing MAPK1 diminished the inhibitory effects of miR-511, reinforcing their functional relationship. This finding aligns with previous research indicating MAPK1 upregulation in other cancers like breast, lung, and ovarian cancer. In the context of osteosarcoma, our study demonstrates that MAPK1 is highly expressed and regulated by miR-511, suggesting its role as an oncogene in this disease, a conclusion supported by existing literature.
To assess miR-511's in vivo effects, we examined tumor growth, MAPK1, Ki67, and VEGF expression, and metastasis in mice. Restoring miR-511 levels significantly inhibited tumor growth and reduced MAPK1, Ki67, and VEGF expression. Given osteosarcoma's propensity to metastasize to the liver and lungs, we investigated tumor spread. Our findings indicate that miR-511 mimic transfection effectively reduced xenograft tumor metastasis to these organs.
This study shows that increased miR-511 inhibits osteosarcoma development by targeting MAPK1, both in cell cultures and in living organisms. MAPK1's oncogenic role suggests it could be a therapeutic target, and miR-511 may serve as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for osteosarcoma.
