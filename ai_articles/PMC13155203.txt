The mammary epithelium undergoes significant growth and remodeling during pregnancy, pointing to the involvement of stem cells. However, their origin, identity, and behavior within the intact tissue have remained unclear. To address this, we employed an Axin2CreERT2 allele to label and trace Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells throughout mammary gland development. Our findings reveal a shift in Wnt/β-catenin signaling around the time of birth. We observed that Axin2+ cells contribute to different epithelial lineages—basal and luminal—depending on the developmental stage. Furthermore, a notable discrepancy emerged between the developmental potential observed in transplantation assays and the behavior of the same cell population in its native environment. Crucially, Axin2+ cells in adult mammary glands were shown to construct alveolar structures across multiple pregnancies, confirming the presence of a Wnt/β-catenin-responsive adult stem cell. This research illuminates dynamic alterations in Wnt/β-catenin signaling within the mammary epithelium and provides valuable insights into the developmental trajectories of mammary gland stem and progenitor cells.

The mammary gland exhibits remarkable proliferative and differentiation capabilities. During puberty, its rudimentary ductal tree undergoes rapid growth and extensive branching morphogenesis, invading surrounding stromal tissue to establish the complex epithelial network of the adult parenchyma. The adult mammary epithelium retains significant proliferative capacity, enabling dynamic tissue remodeling throughout pregnancy and lactation. This involves a substantial increase in cell number and the development of milk-producing alveoli. Following lactation, these alveolar structures regress through involution, returning the gland to a pre-pregnancy state. The ability of this cycle to repeat multiple times throughout an animal's reproductive life strongly suggests the presence of stem cells responsible for the long-term maintenance of mammary tissue structure and function.
The presence of stem cells within the adult mammary epithelium is widely acknowledged. For decades, the ability of cells to regenerate a complete ductal tree after transplantation into a cleared fat pad has been the benchmark for assessing stem cell potential. A significant breakthrough came with the identification of cell populations exhibiting superior regenerative capacity, which could be prospectively isolated using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) based on specific surface marker profiles. However, the regenerative potential demonstrated in transplantation assays may not accurately represent how these cells behave and differentiate within their native tissue. Therefore, to truly understand the origin and function of mammary stem and progenitor cells in a normal developmental setting, lineage tracing analyses are essential. This approach has been notably absent in mammary gland research, leaving fundamental questions about the origin, identity, and behavior of these critical cell populations unanswered.
Crucial for mammary gland development and function, Wnt/β-catenin signaling also maintains stem cells across various tissues. Its role begins during embryogenesis, where it's essential for mammary placode formation. Postnatally, this signaling pathway governs branching morphogenesis, alveolar bud development, and early lobuloalveolar growth during pregnancy.
Axin2 is a well-established direct target of Wnt/β-catenin signaling, and prior research showed that Axin2+ cells possess regenerative capabilities in mammary gland transplantation. Leveraging this, we employed Axin2 as a functional stem cell marker to investigate the role of Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells in mammary gland development and differentiation through lineage tracing. To achieve this, we developed the Axin2CreERT2 mouse model, enabling us to label these cells in situ at various developmental times and monitor their behavior across different stages.
To trace Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells, we engineered an Axin2CreERT2 mouse model. This involved inserting a tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase (CreERT2) into the endogenous Axin2 gene. Mice carrying this Axin2CreERT2 allele were then bred with reporter strains, specifically Rosa26-lacZ (R26RlacZ) and Rosa26-mT/mG (R26RmTmG). Following tamoxifen (TM) administration, Cre activity within these mice triggers recombination at the reporter loci. This recombination creates a permanent genetic label. In R26RlacZ mice, this label manifests as sustained lacZ expression, while in R26RmTmG mice, it results in a switch from dTomato to green fluorescent protein (GFP) expression. Crucially, this genetic mark is inherited by all daughter cells, enabling the long-term tracking of the Wnt/β-catenin-responsive lineage's developmental trajectory.
To validate the efficacy of Axin2CreERT2 in marking stem cells, we first examined its function in the intestinal epithelium. This tissue undergoes rapid turnover, with Wnt/β-catenin-responsive stem cells located at the base of intestinal crypts being essential for its continuous renewal. Previous studies have shown that Cre-mediated recombination in these stem cells leads to the long-term labeling of entire crypt/villus units. Our findings align with these observations, as Axin2CreERT2 initially labels cells at the crypt base. Within a week, progeny from these labeled cells migrate along the crypt/villus axis, forming continuous ribbons of labeled cells extending from the crypt base to the villus tip. The persistence of these recombined cells for up to 350 days, significantly exceeding the lifespan of the intestinal epithelium, confirms that Axin2CreERT2 effectively marks intestinal stem cells.
Mammary placode formation is initially indicated by localized Wnt10b expression and Wnt/β-catenin reporter gene activity. Consistent with this, we observed strong Axin2lacZ expression in mammary placodes of E12.5 embryos. To investigate the role of these Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells in mammary gland development, tamoxifen was administered to pregnant females at E12.5, E14.5, or E17.5. The mammary glands of the resulting female offspring were then analyzed in adulthood.
In Axin2CreERT2/+;R26RmTmG/+ mice, whole-mount confocal microscopy revealed large, GFP+ clones within the dTomato+ mammary epithelium. These clones, numbering 0-2 per gland, reached the furthest points of the ductal network. Within these clones, the GFP+ cells exclusively adopted a luminal cell fate, a finding confirmed by costaining for basal (K14) and luminal (K8) markers, and by FACS analysis. This observation held true irrespective of whether recombination was induced at embryonic days E12.5, E14.5, or E17.5. Therefore, Wnt/β-catenin signaling in the embryonic mammary bud designates the future luminal cell lineage, with some Axin2-expressing cells in the mammary placode already committed to this fate.
The function of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in early postnatal mammary gland development is not well understood. However, in 2-week-old mice, Axin2lacZ reporter activity is observed in a few cells linked to the primary and secondary ducts of the developing mammary epithelium. To investigate how these Axin2+ cells contribute to the growing epithelial network, we administered tamoxifen (TM) to prepubescent mice (postnatal days 14–16). Following puberty and into adulthood, we examined the mammary glands for labeled cells. A single TM dose led to only a small number of lacZ+ or GFP+ cell clusters in the treated mice. This indicates that the progeny of Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells present before puberty undergo significant proliferation during puberty and integrate into the basal layer of the adult mammary epithelium. Notably, these labeled cells were found throughout the ductal network, even at the furthest tips. This observation suggests that the labeled cells maintain their basal cell identity as they migrate along the developing epithelial ducts during puberty.
Flow cytometry analysis corroborated the basal cell identity of the Wnt/β-catenin-responsive lineage identified in prepubescent mice. GFP-positive cells were significantly more prevalent in the basal Lin−; CD24+;CD29hi population (2.9% ± 1.8%) compared to the luminal Lin−;CD24hi;CD29+ population (0.2% ± 0.3%). In a substantial number of animals (7 out of 14), no GFP+ cells were observed within the luminal compartment. A considerable portion of the GFP+ cells (67.2%) were likely stromal cells, as they did not fall into either the defined basal (30.8%) or luminal (2.1%) epithelial cell gates.
Experiments tracing cell lineages from embryonic and prepubescent stages indicate a significant shift in Wnt/β-catenin signaling around birth. This change means that Axin2 marks prospective luminal cells in embryos (E12.5-E17.5) but switches to marking basal cells in prepubescent mice (P14-P16).
To investigate the behavior of prepubescent Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells during pregnancy, we induced recombination in P14 mice and examined midpregnant mammary glands. Labeled cells were observed as basal, myoepithelial cells surrounding developing alveoli. The sparse distribution of these labeled cell clusters, with the majority of the gland remaining unlabeled, suggests they originated from single recombination events. These findings indicate that the Wnt/β-catenin-responsive lineage, marked in the prepubescent mammary gland, proliferates during puberty and pregnancy as the epithelial network expands. However, throughout this period, these cells remain committed to the basal cell fate.
Following lactation and weaning, the mammary gland undergoes significant remodeling, characterized by the regression of alveolar structures through apoptosis and the removal of milk-secreting cells. To ascertain if Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells identified in prepubescent mammary glands function as stem cells, their survival through repeated tissue turnover was assessed. Lineage tracing across several pregnancies revealed that these cells are long-lived, exhibiting sustained proliferation and an increase in number with each gestation. Consequently, substantial portions of the mammary epithelium, encompassing numerous alveolar clusters, became populated by the clonal progeny of a single recombined cell, while the surrounding tissue remained unlabeled. Importantly, within these clonal expansions, the labeled cells consistently maintained their basal layer localization. These findings lead to the conclusion that Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells residing in the basal layer of the prepubescent mammary gland are unipotent stem cells possessing enduring proliferative capacity.
Based on lineage tracing showing prepubescent Axin2+ cells did not become luminal cells, we predicted they wouldn't regenerate a ductal tree in transplantation assays. To investigate, we isolated GFP+ basal cells from 9-13 week-old mice treated with tamoxifen prepubescently. These sorted cells were then used in transplantation experiments to assess their regenerative capacity.
Remarkably, these sorted cells demonstrated a potent ability to regenerate a complete mammary ductal tree. Even a small number, as few as 50 cells, could generate GFP-expressing outgrowths featuring normal basal and luminal layers. These regenerated glands were also capable of developing alveoli during pregnancy. Furthermore, when these GFP-labeled basal cells were reisolated from primary outgrowths, they retained their regenerative capacity, successfully forming mammary glands in secondary transplantation experiments. This indicates that the cleared fat pad environment stimulates a regenerative potential not typically employed under normal developmental circumstances.
During puberty, the mammary epithelium undergoes significant outgrowth, characterized by branching morphogenesis and ductal tree elongation. This process is powered by rapidly dividing terminal end bud (TEB) structures. By adulthood, the epithelial network has fully colonized the stromal fat pad, and TEBs regress. Given the expression of multiple Wnt-pathway genes by TEBs and surrounding stroma, Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells likely play a role in this pubertal epithelial expansion.
During puberty, when terminal end buds (TEBs) form, we examined the expression pattern of Axin2lacZ in reporter mice at postnatal day 28. Whole-mount X-gal staining of mammary glands revealed that Axin2lacZ was primarily present in the stroma surrounding the TEB's neck region. Histological analysis of tissue sections showed Axin2lacZ expression within the epithelium as well, appearing in a few TEB body cells and in basal cells closer to the TEB. Similar findings were observed in pubescent Axin2CreERT2 mice, analyzed shortly after tamoxifen administration with either the R26RlacZ or R26RmTmG reporter.
To understand the fate of Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells during puberty, we administered tamoxifen (TM) to pubescent mice expressing Axin2CreERT2 and the R26RmTmG reporter. We then examined the contribution of labeled cells to the adult mammary gland (Figure 5D). In adult virgin mice, offspring of these cells appeared as GFP+ tracts integrated into both the basal (Figure 5E) and luminal (Figure 5F) layers of the ductal epithelium. During pregnancy, cells labeled during puberty underwent significant clonal expansion, forming distinct clusters of either basal or luminal alveolar cells (Figures 5H–5M). These findings indicate that during puberty, Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells contribute to both basal and luminal lineages of the developing epithelial network. The observation of independent basal and luminal clones during pregnancy suggests that the bilayered ductal epithelium originates from separate basal and luminal precursors, both of which are Axin2+ during puberty.
The developmental stage of the mammary gland dictates the contribution of Axin2+ cells to basal and luminal lineages, as revealed by lineage tracing in embryos, prepubescent, and pubescent mice. The study then investigated the function of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in the adult mammary gland, aiming to clarify the origin and hierarchy of stem cells responsible for alveolar development during pregnancy.
In adult mammary epithelium, Axin2lacZ expression is limited to a subset of basal cells. To investigate this, we examined glands from 9-week-old virgin mice (Axin2CreERT2/+;R26RmTmG/+) treated with tamoxifen 48 hours prior. Confocal microscopy, combined with basal and luminal markers, revealed that recombined cells were exclusively located in the basal layer.
Two weeks after tamoxifen administration in adult virgin mice, whole-mount confocal microscopy revealed labeled cells. These cells appeared as elongated myoepithelial cells along primary and secondary ducts. Additionally, GFP+ cells were observed surrounding lateral alveolar buds, structures that develop into secretory alveoli during pregnancy. Labeled cells were also found at the distal tips of the epithelium, cradling the end buds.
Flow cytometry analysis was performed on mammary glands from 13 adult virgin mice, aged 8–10 weeks, which had received tamoxifen (TM) and were traced for varying durations (48 hours to 66 days) (Figures 6D–6G). Across all trace lengths, a significantly higher proportion of GFP+ cells were consistently found within the Lin−;CD24+;CD29hi basal cell population (5.2% ± 2.9%, n=13) compared to the bulk Lin− cells (2.0% ± 1.2%, n=13). In contrast, GFP+ cells were infrequent in the Lin−;CD24hi;CD29+ luminal cell population (0.2% ± 0.2%, n=13), with 5 out of 13 animals showing no GFP+ cells in this compartment. Basal cells constituted approximately half of all labeled cells (53.9% ± 16.7%, 1,176 out of 1,969 GFP+ events), while only a small fraction (1.5% ± 1.9%, 23 out of 1,969 GFP+ events) were in the luminal population. The remaining labeled cells (44.6% ± 17.3%) primarily expressed low levels of CD24 (Figure 6E) and were identified as stromal fibroblasts, which also exhibit Axin2 expression based on histological data. These findings collectively indicate that in adult mammary glands, Axin2CreERT2 predominantly labels cells within the basal Lin−;CD24+;CD29hi epithelial population, aligning with prior observations using Axin2lacZ reporter mice. Furthermore, Axin2CreERT2-marked cells maintain their localization within the basal layer of the quiescent adult virgin mammary epithelium.
Adult virgin mammary cells, marked by Axin2CreERT2 and identified as GFP+ Lin−;CD24+;CD29hi, exhibited high efficiency in regenerating complete epithelial networks upon transplantation. However, this regenerative capacity, as observed in transplantation assays, does not accurately represent the normal in vivo developmental potential of mammary gland stem cells. This discrepancy was highlighted by our findings and those of Van Keymeulen et al. (2011). Consequently, we investigated the behavior of these cells during pregnancy. The precise in vivo function of adult mammary stem cells in forming alveolar structures is not well understood, and the existence of a bipotent adult stem cell capable of generating both basal and luminal alveolar cells remains a subject of debate.
To examine the behavior of Axin2+ cells in adult mammary glands, we administered tamoxifen (TM) to 8-week-old virgin mice. One week later, timed matings were initiated to induce pregnancy (Figure 6H). By day 14.5 of gestation, most of the mammary epithelium showed no labeling (lacZ- or GFP-), indicating that TM had caused sporadic recombination of the reporter alleles. However, certain areas displayed dense clusters of labeled cells (Figures 6I, 6J, and S5). Along the main ducts, these GFP+ cells were confined to the basal layer. In contrast, alveoli contained GFP+ luminal alveolar cells, which are involved in milk production and secretion towards the end of pregnancy (Figures 6K, 6L). Notably, within these GFP+ clusters, adjacent GFP+ luminal (K8-positive) and basal (K14-positive) cells were observed (Figures S5, S6).
In adult virgin mammary glands, lineage tracing revealed that Axin2+ cells contribute to both basal and luminal alveolar cells during the first pregnancy. The presence of distinct, unlabeled epithelial regions between labeled cell clusters indicates that these clusters likely originate from a single recombination event.
Adult virgin mice received tamoxifen, then underwent up to three pregnancy, lactation, and involution cycles. Mammary glands were subsequently analyzed to determine the presence and fate of labeled cells originating from adult Axin2+ cells.
Mammary glands from multiparous mice, after two full pregnancy cycles (~15–16 weeks post-tamoxifen), showed distinct differences compared to nulliparous littermates. Even 21 days after involution, which typically completes within two weeks, the multiparous glands presented a more disorganized structure. This included a denser epithelial network with increased tertiary branches and residual alveoli. Labeled cells persisted in both nulliparous and multiparous animals. In nulliparous mice, these labeled cells often appeared as tracts along the epithelial ducts. In both groups, dense clusters of labeled cells were observed in smaller branches, possibly indicating the formation of new side branches that emerge from existing ducts during recurrent estrous cycles. Furthermore, multiparous mice exhibited labeled regions within regressing alveoli.
Following three cycles of pregnancy, lactation, and involution, labeled cells were still present in the mammary glands of Axin2CreERT2/+;R26RmTmG/+ mice, contributing to alveolar structures during the third pregnancy. Within these developing clusters, adjacent GFP-positive basal (K14+) and luminal (K8+) cells were observed. Even 21 days after the third litter was weaned, approximately 22 weeks after tamoxifen administration, labeled cells persisted as alveoli remnants were cleared. Analysis of GFP+ cell distribution across various mammary cell populations revealed significant variability in GFP+ luminal cells, regardless of parity. However, the proportion of GFP+ basal cells remained consistent between nulliparous mice and those that completed three reproductive cycles, indicating that Axin2+ cells in the adult basal layer possess self-renewal capabilities.
Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells in adult virgin mammary glands persist through the post-pregnancy remodeling of the lobuloalveolar compartment. This indicates that Axin2CreERT2 identifies long-lived adult mammary stem cells. These cells are essential for building alveolar structures across successive pregnancies. The presence of adjacent labeled basal and luminal cells within the same cluster during multiple pregnancies suggests a clonal origin, implying a single bipotent Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cell can form a complete alveolar structure.
Understanding the relationships between mammary gland epithelial cell populations has led to two primary models for stem and progenitor cell hierarchies. One model posits independent ductal and lobular progenitors capable of generating both basal and luminal cells. This view is largely informed by transplantation experiments, which sometimes yield limited outgrowths. In contrast, a second model suggests an earlier divergence of basal and luminal cell lineages. This model, supported by in vitro colony formation assays and recent lineage tracing studies, predicts the existence of a myoepithelial progenitor and indicates that both myoepithelial and luminal lineages harbor long-lived, unipotent stem cells during puberty.
Wnt/β-catenin signaling plays a crucial role in mammary gland development and differentiation from embryogenesis through postnatal life, and is essential for stem cell maintenance across various tissues. Axin2, a direct target of this pathway, serves as a validated stem cell marker. Our research demonstrates that Axin2 is expressed in a specific subset of postnatal mammary epithelial cells. By tracing the lineage of these Axin2-expressing cells at different developmental stages, we provide significant insights into the origin and behavior of mammary gland stem cells.
Dynamic changes in Axin2 expression and the resulting fate of Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells are observed based on the timing of tamoxifen administration. A significant switch in Wnt/β-catenin signaling occurs around birth. In embryos, Axin2+ cells designate the future luminal lineage, but by two weeks of age, these responsive cells are solely committed to the basal cell fate.
Despite the mammary gland's perceived quiescence in early postnatal life, crucial cell fate decisions are made during this time. Specifically, unipotent myoepithelial stem cells, responsive to Wnt/β-catenin signaling, are established. The progeny of these prepubescent Axin2+ cells maintain their basal cell fate throughout pregnancy. During this period, they proliferate to form extensive clusters of contractile myoepithelial cells surrounding the alveoli. Furthermore, these cells exhibit long-term self-renewal capabilities, persisting through multiple cycles of pregnancy and involution.
Prior to puberty, Wnt/β-catenin responsiveness is limited to the basal lineage. However, at the onset of puberty, Axin2+ cells emerge in terminal end buds (TEBs), indicating their role in specifying the luminal lineage. These observations suggest a sequential development of two Wnt/β-catenin-responsive lineages that contribute to distinct basal and luminal cell lineages during puberty. This aligns with findings from Van Keymeulen and colleagues. Nevertheless, the extent to which the Axin2+ prepubescent and pubescent cell lineages identified in this study overlap with the K14+ and K8+ lineages reported in their work, marked using Cre- and rtTA-transgenic lines during puberty, remains to be determined.
Our study revealed a significant divergence between the regenerative potential of mammary cells in transplantation assays and their actual developmental behavior in situ. Specifically, cells responsive to Wnt/β-catenin signaling in the prepubescent mammary gland, which lineage tracing shows remain confined to the basal cell fate through puberty and multiple pregnancies, exhibit a remarkable capacity for regenerating both basal and luminal layers when transplanted. This multipotent potential in transplantation assays is observed for basal Lin−;CD24+;CD29hi cells, particularly when luminal cells are absent or limited, as previously reported.
The observed difference between the developmental and regenerative potential of these cells could be explained by their function as facultative stem cells in normal development. In this capacity, their multipotent potential might only be activated when necessary, such as following tissue damage. Alternatively, considering research showing that progenitor cells from diverse origins can be reprogrammed by the fat pad environment to adopt mammary cell fates, transplantation might simply unlock a regenerative capacity that is not typically employed in vivo. Regardless of the precise mechanism, these findings suggest that lineage tracing should be the preferred method for assessing normal developmental stem cell potential.
The location of adult mammary stem cells has long been hypothesized to be in the suprabasal layer, and these cells are thought to form alveolar structures during pregnancy. However, definitive proof of their existence has been lacking. Recent lineage tracing studies propose that basal and luminal alveolar cells originate from separate precursors established before or during puberty. Our findings also indicate the development of a unipotent basal stem cell responsive to Wnt/β-catenin signaling in prepubescent mice. Consequently, the presence of a bipotent adult stem cell remains a subject of debate.
To directly investigate the existence of a bipotent adult stem cell, we induced recombination in adult virgin mammary glands and traced the lineage of labeled Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells through multiple reproductive cycles. Following tamoxifen administration to adult virgin mice, we analyzed their contribution to alveolar development. We observed clonal GFP+ clusters containing adjacent basal and luminal alveolar cells. These labeled cells proved to be long-lived, consistently generating both basal and luminal progeny across subsequent pregnancies. This indicates that Axin2 serves as a stem cell marker for both basal and luminal lineages in the adult mammary epithelium. While it's possible these cells originated from independent basal and luminal precursors, marking a long-sought adult luminal stem cell, we find this less likely for several reasons.
While FACS analysis of adult virgin mice treated with tamoxifen (TM) reveals a small percentage of GFP+ cells in the luminal population (Figure 6E), these gated populations are not pure. Consequently, these rare GFP+ cells may originate from basal or stromal cell contamination. This is further supported by observations in prepubescent mice, where similar percentages of GFP+ cells appear in the luminal population after recombination (Figure 3M). However, lineage tracing experiments consistently show that prepubescent Axin2+ cells do not contribute to luminal alveolar cells, even after multiple pregnancies.
Microscopic analysis of adult virgin mice, following tamoxifen administration, revealed labeled cells predominantly in the basal layer. This finding aligns with prior research indicating that Wnt/β-catenin pathway receptors are enriched in basal cells. Furthermore, it corroborates the study's earlier observation of Axin2lacZ expression within the basal layer of adult virgin mammary glands.
The observation of adjacent GFP+ basal and luminal alveolar cells within the same cluster during pregnancy, coupled with the low frequency of these labeled clusters, strongly suggests a common precursor origin. This finding supports a model where Axin2CreERT2 identifies a bipotent adult stem cell, implying the potential coexistence of both unipotent and multipotent stem cells within the mammary epithelium. Further research employing more specific stem cell and lineage markers is necessary to definitively exclude Axin2 positivity in independent basal and luminal adult stem cells. Nevertheless, it is clear that Wnt/β-catenin-responsive cells in the adult virgin mammary epithelium are long-lived stem cells capable of surviving multiple cycles of lobuloalveolar tissue remodeling. Future investigations should aim to elucidate the relationship between these Axin2+ adult mammary stem cells and previously identified cell populations labeled during puberty, as well as with self-renewing cells identified after parity.
To create mice with CreERT2 expression driven by the endogenous Axin2 promoter and enhancer sequences, the original targeting construct for Axin2lacZ mice was modified. Further details are available in the Supplemental Experimental Procedures.
Reporter mouse strains R26RlacZ and R26RmTmG were acquired from Jackson Laboratories. Dr. W. Birchmeier provided Axin2lacZ mice. Nude mice were obtained from Charles River. All experimental procedures adhered to NIH guidelines and received approval from the Stanford University Animal Care and Use Committee.
Tamoxifen (TM) was administered via a single intraperitoneal injection to induce recombination. The dosage varied based on the developmental stage of the mice. For prepubescent mice (postnatal days 14-16), the total dose was 1 mg. Pubescent mice (postnatal days 28-35) received 2 mg, while adult virgins (postnatal days 56-63) were given 4 mg. Pregnant mothers received a lower dose of 0.5 mg/25 g body weight for embryonic recombination between E12.5 and E17.5. The TM stock solution was prepared at 10–20 mg/ml in corn oil with 10% ethanol.
Detailed methods for whole-mount analysis of fluorescent and X-gal-stained mammary glands, including X-gal staining procedures, are provided in the Supplemental Experimental Procedures.
Mammary tissue samples, fixed with either PFA or ethanol, were processed using standard paraffin embedding techniques. These embedded tissues were then sectioned using a Leica Microtome. For X-gal-stained samples, sections measured 2–5 μm, while R26RmTmG samples were 10 μm thick. All sections were mounted onto superfrost slides and dried overnight at 37°C. Antigen retrieval was performed on paraffin-fixed samples using Tris/EDTA (pH 9.0). Subsequently, sections were stained with antibodies targeting keratin 14 (K14), smooth muscle actin (SMA), keratin 8 (K8), laminin, and GFP. Immunofluorescence was carried out using Alexa 488-, Cy3-, or Cy5-conjugated secondary antibodies, or immunohistochemistry was performed with the Vectastain ABC system and DAB substrate, following manufacturer instructions. Notably, the dTomato signal was undetectable after routine paraffin embedding and histology, allowing for the simultaneous use of Cy3 and Cy5 channels for detecting fluorescently labeled antibodies against various markers.
To assess mammary repopulating capacity, sorted GFP+ Lin−;CD24+;CD29hi cells were injected into the cleared fat pads of 21-day-old nude recipient mice. Mammary epithelial cells were first stained with antibodies targeting Ter119, CD31, CD45, CD24, CD29, and CD49f, then analyzed and sorted using a BD Facs Aria. Outgrowths were examined 4–8 weeks post-transplantation, and mammary repopulating unit (MRU) frequencies were determined with L-calc software. Detailed protocols for cell isolation, staining, and transplantation are available in the Supplemental Experimental Procedures.
