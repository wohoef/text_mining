Previous research has established a significant link between specific single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in the ARID5B gene and the susceptibility to childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) in Caucasian populations. However, investigations into these associations within Asian populations have been less extensive. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between ARID5B SNPs, specifically rs7089424 and rs10994982, and the risk of ALL in Chinese children. To achieve this, the study enrolled 190 pediatric ALL patients and 270 healthy controls. Genotyping for ARID5B rs7089424 and rs10994982 was performed using a combination of PCR amplification and mass spectrometry. Statistical analysis, including the chi-squared test, was employed to compare allele frequencies and genotype distributions between the patient and control groups. The results revealed statistically significant differences in the risk allele frequencies of both ARID5B rs7089424 (G allele, p = 0.001) and rs10994982 (A allele, p = 0.000) when comparing B-lineage ALL (B-ALL) patients with controls. Furthermore, the genotype distributions for these two SNPs also showed significant differences in B-ALL patients compared to controls (rs7089424, p = 0.004; rs10994982, p = 0.001). An in-depth analysis of the correlation between ARID5B genotypes and clinical risk classification indicated a strong association between the GG genotype of rs7089424 and the AA genotype of rs10994982 with the medium-risk and low-risk groups of B-ALL. Additionally, the GG and GT genotypes of rs7089424, along with the AA genotype of rs10994982, appeared to be linked to the susceptibility of the hyperdiploid subtype of childhood B-ALL. In conclusion, ARID5B rs7089424 and rs10994982 may function as genetic markers for B-ALL susceptibility in the Chinese pediatric population. Moreover, these two ARID5B SNPs are associated with the risk of B-hyperdiploid ALL, a subtype that demonstrates a more favorable therapeutic response compared to other ALL subtypes.

Childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), the most prevalent childhood malignancy, is a genetically intricate disease contributing significantly to pediatric cancer mortality. Its development is thought to stem from a combination of environmental and genetic factors. Genome-wide association studies (GWASs) have greatly enhanced our understanding of the genetic predispositions to childhood ALL. Notably, in 2009, two separate studies identified several single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) on chromosomes 10q21.2 (ARID5B), 7p12.2 (IKZF1), and 14q11.2 (CEBPE) as being strongly linked to the risk of developing ALL in children.
ARID5B, a transcription factor belonging to the AT-rich interaction domain family, is crucial for regulating embryonic development, cell growth, and differentiation by repressing gene expression in a tissue-specific manner. While numerous ARID5B single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), including rs10824936, rs10994982, rs7089424, rs7073837, and rs10740055, have been linked to childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) susceptibility in Caucasian populations, research in Asian populations has been more limited. Previous studies have shown associations between ARID5B SNPs and ALL in Asian children, with findings varying across different ethnic groups. For instance, ARID5B rs10821938 was associated with B-cell precursor ALL in Thailand, and ARID5B rs10821936 was identified as a potential risk biomarker in Chinese children. Another Chinese study reported a significant difference in the distribution of ARID5B rs7073837 genotypes between ALL patients and controls. In contrast, a study in Indian children found that ARID5B rs10821936 was unexpectedly associated with a decreased risk of B-lineage ALL.
Previous meta-analyses highlighted significant associations between ARID5B SNPs rs7089424 and rs10994982 and childhood ALL risk, with rs10994982 also strongly linked to B-hyperdiploid ALL. However, these specific ARID5B SNPs had not been previously confirmed in Chinese populations regarding their relevance to childhood ALL. Therefore, this case-control study, involving 190 ALL cases and 270 controls, aimed to validate the relationship between ARID5B SNPs rs7089424, rs10994982, and ALL risk in Chinese children.
Between May and December 2015, 190 pediatric ALL patients from Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, and 270 healthy controls were recruited. Controls were selected to exclude any history of malignant tumors or hematological diseases. All patient diagnoses were confirmed using morphology, immunology, cytogenetics, and molecular biology, adhering to the French–American–British classification. Furthermore, clinical risk stratification and immunophenotyping of ALL patients were conducted according to the 2006 Chinese Medical Association guidelines for childhood ALL diagnosis and treatment.
The study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Children’s Hospital, Zhejiang University School of Medicine, adhering to the Declaration of Helsinki principles.
Genomic DNA was extracted from 200 µL of EDTA-anticoagulated blood samples collected from 190 ALL patients and 270 controls. The extraction followed the protocol of the Whole Blood Genomic DNA Extraction Kit (Tissuebank Biotechnology Co, Ltd, Shanghai, China). The isolated DNA was then stored at -80°C for subsequent analysis.
Primers for ARID5B SNPs rs7089424 and rs10994982 were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai). The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) involved a 5 µL reaction volume. This mixture contained deionized water, 10× buffer with 15 mM MgCl2, 25 mM MgCl2, dNTPs, PCR primers, HotStar Taq polymerase, and the DNA sample. The PCR protocol began with a 15-minute incubation at 94°C. This was followed by 45 cycles, each consisting of 20 seconds at 94°C, 30 seconds at 56°C, and 1 minute at 72°C. A final 3-minute extension at 72°C concluded the PCR process.
Following PCR amplification, the reaction mixture was treated to remove residual dNTPs. This involved adding deionized water, SAP buffer, and shrimp alkaline phosphatase. The mixture was then incubated at 37°C for 40 minutes, followed by a 5-minute incubation at 85°C to deactivate the enzyme.
For the single base extension reaction, a 2 µL system was prepared. This included deionized water, 10× iPLEX buffer, iPLEX termination mix, iPLEX enzyme, and a specific single base extension primer. The reaction commenced at 94°C for 30 seconds. Subsequently, it underwent 40 cycles, each involving a 5-second denaturation at 94°C, followed by five cycles of 5-second incubations at 52°C and 5-second incubations at 80°C. A final extension at 72°C for 3 minutes concluded the process.
Following centrifugation at 1000 rpm for one minute, the single base extension reactant was resuspended in 25 µL of deionized water. This solution underwent desalination with cation exchange resin (Sequenom) before being prepared for genotyping. Samples were then applied to a 384-well spectroCHIP (Sequenom) using the MassARRAY Nanodispenser. Genotyping was conducted on a matrix-assisted laser desorption ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometer, with results subsequently analyzed using MassARRAY Typer version 3.4 software.
To assess statistical significance, a chi-squared test was employed to examine allele frequencies and genotype distributions between patients and controls. Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium for each SNP was also determined using a goodness-of-fit chi-squared test. Logistic regression analysis provided odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals, with a p-value less than 0.05 considered significant.
The pediatric ALL patients in this study ranged from 5 months to 15 years old, with an average age of 5.86 ± 3.59 years at diagnosis. The most common age group for ALL diagnosis in children was between 3 and 6 years old, accounting for 36.8% of cases. Other age group proportions were: 0-1 year (2.6%), 1-3 years (22.1%), 6-9 years (17.9%), 9-12 years (13.7%), and over 12 years (6.9%). The patient cohort consisted of 119 boys and 71 girls, yielding a gender ratio of 1.7:1. Importantly, no significant differences in age or gender were observed between the ALL patients and the control group. Genotyping of ARID5B single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) rs7089424 and rs10994982 was performed, with results illustrated in Figure 1. Both SNPs adhered to Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium in both acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) patients and control groups, showing no significant deviations (p = 0.29 for rs7089424 and p = 0.32 for rs10994982). Analysis revealed significant differences in allele frequencies for both ARID5B SNPs between B-lineage ALL (B-ALL) patients and controls (Table 3). Specifically, the G allele of rs7089424 (p = 0.001, OR = 1.59) and the A allele of rs10994982 (p = 0.000, OR = 1.67) were associated with an increased risk of B-ALL. Furthermore, the genotype distributions of both rs7089424 (p = 0.004) and rs10994982 (p = 0.001) also differed significantly between B-ALL patients and controls. In contrast, no such differences were observed for either SNP's allele frequencies or genotype distributions when comparing T-lineage ALL (T-ALL) patients to controls.
Investigating the connection between ARID5B rs7089424 and rs10994982 genotypes and ALL clinical risk classification revealed significant associations. Specifically, the GG genotype of ARID5B rs7089424 showed a strong correlation with both medium-risk and low-risk B-ALL patient groups, with odds ratios of 3.10 and 2.98 respectively. The GT genotype of this SNP, however, did not demonstrate any relationship with B-ALL clinical risk classification. In parallel, the AA genotype of ARID5B rs10994982 was also significantly linked to the medium-risk and low-risk B-ALL groups, exhibiting odds ratios of 2.64 and 3.55. Conversely, the AG genotype of rs10994982 showed no correlation with B-ALL clinical risk classification. These associations were not observed in T-ALL patients, where neither ARID5B rs7089424 nor rs10994982 genotypes correlated with clinical risk groups.
In our analysis of dominant cytogenetic and molecular subtypes within B-ALL patients, we observed that specific genotypes of ARID5B SNPs were linked to hyperdiploid subtype susceptibility. Specifically, the GG and GT genotypes of rs7089424, along with the AA genotype of rs10994982, were associated with an increased risk of developing this subtype of childhood B-ALL, as indicated by significant odds ratios and confidence intervals.
This case-control study confirmed that ARID5B SNPs rs7089424 and rs10994982 are associated with B-ALL susceptibility in Chinese children. Individuals carrying the G allele of rs7089424 or the A allele of rs10994982 exhibited a higher incidence of B-ALL compared to those with the T allele of rs7089424 or the G allele of rs10994982, respectively. Consequently, these alleles may serve as potential predictive markers for childhood B-ALL in this population.
Confirming findings from previous research, this study's analysis of ARID5B single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and ALL immunophenotypes indicated that rs7089424 and rs10994982 are linked to the risk of developing B-ALL. A potential mechanism involves recombination activating gene 1 (RAG1), crucial for early B-cell differentiation. Precursor B-cell development in the bone marrow requires RAG-1 and RAG-2 endonuclease activation for two DNA recombination rounds. Previous reports suggest ARID5B expression correlates with RAG1 expression in the bone marrow, offering insight into ARID5B's role in B-ALL pathogenesis.
Previous research on the association between ARID5B SNPs rs7089424 and rs10994982 and B-ALL has yielded inconsistent findings in Asian populations. While a case-control study in Yemeni children supported our results, identifying these SNPs as risk factors for ALL, a Korean GWAS only noted a link between ARID5B rs7089424's close relative, rs7089426, and pediatric ALL risk. Furthermore, studies in Thai and Chinese populations found no statistically significant association between ARID5B rs7089424 and B-cell precursor ALL or between rs7089424 genotype distribution and ALL cases, respectively. These discrepancies may stem from variations in sample size, participant selection, genotyping techniques, and statistical analysis methods.
Subtype analysis revealed a significant correlation between the GG genotype of ARID5B rs7089424 and the AA genotype of ARID5B rs10994982 with the medium- and low-risk subgroups of B-ALL. This suggests that homozygous mutations in these SNPs may increase the likelihood of developing medium or low-risk B-ALL.
Further investigation into B-ALL subtypes revealed that specific genotypes of ARID5B SNPs rs7089424 (GG and GT) and rs10994982 (AA) were associated with B-hyperdiploid ALL, differentiating it from other subtypes. These findings partially align with prior research confirming the link between rs10994982 and B-hyperdiploid ALL. Notably, this study is the first to explore the role of rs7089424 in the risk of developing this specific subtype. Given that B-hyperdiploid ALL generally responds better to methotrexate chemotherapy, these ARID5B SNPs could potentially aid in guiding treatment decisions and predicting prognosis, in addition to their role in risk prediction.
This study indicates that ARID5B SNPs rs7089424 and rs10994982 may function as genetic markers for B-ALL susceptibility in Chinese children. Additionally, these SNPs are linked to the risk of B-hyperdiploid ALL, a subtype with a more favorable treatment outcome. Supplementary data is available online.
