Existing models for predicting pathological outcomes in prostate cancer (PCa), relying on established clinical and prostate-specific antigen (PSA)-derived parameters, are insufficient. Promising results have emerged with p2PSA and its related biomarkers for PCa diagnosis and prognosis. This study aimed to determine if p2PSA-derived biomarkers could enhance the prediction of aggressive pathological outcomes following radical prostatectomy (RP). Between February 2017 and December 2018, patients diagnosed with PCa and treated with RP were prospectively enrolled. Preoperative blood samples were analyzed for total PSA (tPSA), free PSA (fPSA), the percentage of fPSA (%fPSA), [-2]proPSA (p2PSA), and the percentage of p2PSA (%p2PSA). The Prostate Health Index (PHI) was calculated using the formula (p2PSA/fPSA) × √tPSA. Prostate volume was measured via transrectal ultrasound using the ellipsoid formula, and PHI density was derived by dividing PHI by prostate volume. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of various PSA/p2PSA derivatives. Aggressive pathological outcomes post-RP were defined as a pathological T3 stage or a Gleason score (GS) greater than 6, as determined from RP specimens. A total of 144 patients were included in the analysis. Postoperative GS was greater than 6 in 86.1% of patients, and the pathological T stage was T3a or higher in 54.2%. Among all assessed PSA and p2PSA-derived biomarkers, PHI density demonstrated the strongest ability to predict aggressive pathological outcomes after RP, with an odds ratio of 8.796 (p = 0.001) for having an aggressive outcome. In multivariate analysis, incorporating %fPSA into the base model did not improve predictive accuracy (area under the curve). However, the addition of PHI and PHI density to the base model improved accuracy by 2% and 16%, respectively, in predicting a pT3 stage or GS ≥ 7. For patients with a PHI density below 1.125, the risk of a pT3 stage or GS ≥ 7 was 20.8%, compared to 64.6% for those with a PHI density above 1.125, yielding a sensitivity of 74.6% and a specificity of 88.9%. In conclusion, PHI density shows potential to further improve the prediction of aggressive pathological outcomes after RP. This biomarker could be valuable for preoperative patient counseling and may assist in decision-making regarding definitive treatment versus active surveillance for newly diagnosed PCa.

Prostate cancer (PCa) is a growing global health concern, with increasing incidence and mortality rates posing a significant burden on healthcare systems, particularly in Taiwan. While the widespread use of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) has led to earlier diagnosis of localized PCa, standard treatment choices for this stage include active surveillance (AS), radical prostatectomy (RP), and radiotherapy (RT).
Current management decisions for localized prostate cancer, choosing between active surveillance (AS), radical prostatectomy (RP), or radiotherapy (RT), depend on risk stratification systems like D’Amico/NCCN and EAU. However, reported instances of significant upgrading or staging changes after RP can diminish the utility of these existing models.
Radical prostatectomy (RP) is the standard treatment for localized prostate cancer (PCa), but it carries risks of functional impairment like incontinence and erectile dysfunction. Achieving optimal oncological outcomes while preserving nerve function necessitates preoperative assessment of the capsule's status to enable nerve-sparing techniques. While existing nomograms and look-up tables, developed over a decade ago using older biomarkers and biopsy methods, offer guidance, their applicability to current clinical practice may be limited due to these outdated methodologies.
Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) originates from preproPSA, a protein with a 17-amino acid leader sequence. This precursor can be cleaved into proPSA, which has seven additional amino acids. Truncated forms of proPSA arise from partial removal of this leader sequence, with p2PSA being a stable variant.
Research indicates that p2PSA and its related biomarkers, including the percentage of p2PSA to free PSA (%p2PSA) and the Prostate Health Index (PHI), demonstrate superior performance compared to standard PSA in predicting prostate cancer (PCa) after transrectal ultrasound-guided prostate biopsy (TRUSP Bx). Higher PHI values have also been linked to elevated Gleason scores (GS) in TRUSP Bx specimens. Moreover, PHI and %p2PSA have shown a stronger correlation with post-prostatectomy pathological outcomes than PSA in various populations. PSA density has also emerged as a more effective predictor of tumor upgrade and pathological characteristics following prostatectomy. Consequently, this prospective study aimed to examine the relationship between PHI, %p2PSA, and PHI density (PHI divided by prostate volume) and pathological features observed after prostatectomy.
Between February 2017 and December 2018, patients diagnosed with prostate cancer (PCa) and treated with radical prostatectomy (RP) at a single medical center were prospectively enrolled. This study aimed to evaluate how accurately p2PSA-derived biomarkers could predict aggressive pathological outcomes following RP. Ethical approval was obtained from the Taipei Veterans General Hospital Institutional Review Board/Ethics Committee (approval code: 201708017A). All participants received detailed information about the procedures and potential complications, and provided signed informed consent.
Patients diagnosed with PCa and treated with RP were included, with all cancers confirmed by at least a 12-core TRUSP Bx. Those who received androgen deprivation therapy or 5-α-reductase inhibitors prior to blood collection were excluded.
Before radical prostatectomy, clinical information such as age, body mass index, and digital rectal examination (DRE) findings were gathered. Preoperative blood samples were analyzed for total PSA (tPSA), free PSA (fPSA), the percentage of fPSA (%fPSA), and [-2]proPSA (p2PSA), as well as the percentage of p2PSA (%p2PSA). The Prostate Health Index (PHI) was then computed using the formula (p2PSA/fPSA) × √tPSA. Prostate volume was determined via transrectal ultrasound, employing the ellipsoid formula (width × height × length × 0.523), and PHI density was derived by dividing PHI by prostate volume. These blood samples were processed using a Beckman Coulter DxI800 Unicel Immunoassay system. Following surgery, experienced genitourinary pathologists, unaware of the blood test results, assessed pathological outcomes, including tumor stage, Gleason score (pGS), and nodal status. Aggressive pathological outcomes were specifically defined as a pathological T3 stage or a Gleason score of 7 or higher. Prostate cancer staging and grading adhered to the 2010 American Joint Committee on Cancer guidelines.
Continuous variables were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test, while categorical variables were analyzed with the χ2 test. Descriptive statistics, including mean, median, and interquartile range (IQR), were provided for categorical variables. The predictive accuracy of each variable for aggressive pathological outcomes was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Logistic regression models, both univariate and multivariable, were employed for outcome prediction. The base model for multivariate analysis incorporated age, total PSA (tPSA), biopsy Gleason score (GS), and abnormal digital rectal examination (DRE) findings.
Statistical analyses utilized SPSS version 22. A two-sided p-value less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

The predictive capabilities of p2PSA-derived biomarkers for pathological T3 stage or Gleason score (GS) ≥ 7 were assessed. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, illustrated in Figure 1, depicted the performance of preoperative PSA and p2PSA derivatives, including %p2PSA, PHI, and PHI density. Table 3 indicates that PHI density yielded the largest area under the curve. Subsequent univariate and multivariate analyses, also presented in Table 3, revealed that among established parameters, the biopsy-determined GS was the most potent predictor of adverse pathological outcomes (pT3 or GS ≥ 7) post-prostatectomy, followed by preoperative PSA, age, and abnormal digital rectal examination findings. When p2PSA-derived parameters (%p2PSA, PHI, and PHI density) were added to the base model, PHI density demonstrated the highest odds ratio (OR = 8.796, p = 0.001). In multivariate analysis, incorporating PHI and PHI density into the base model significantly enhanced predictive accuracy (area under the curve) for pT3 stage or GS ≥ 7, increasing it from 73.1% to 75.1% and 89.1%, respectively. Conversely, adding %fPSA did not improve accuracy. For PHI density, a value below 1.125 was associated with a 20.8% risk of pT3 stage or GS ≥ 7, while a value above 1.125 corresponded to a 64.6% risk, with a sensitivity of 74.6% and a specificity of 88.9%.
Patients classified as very low or low-risk according to National Comprehensive Cancer Network criteria were examined for upgrading and upstaging. Among the 23 low-risk patients, 19 experienced a Gleason score (GS) upgrade, exhibiting significantly higher PHI (56.9 vs. 38.3; p < 0.001) and PHI density (1.91 vs. 0.89; p < 0.001) compared to those not upgraded. In the very low-risk group, 9 out of 17 patients also had their GS upgraded, though PHI and PHI density differences were not statistically significant. Furthermore, 19 (82.6%) of the low-risk patients were upstaged to pT2b or higher, as were 13 (76.4%) of the very low-risk patients. Again, no statistically significant differences in PHI or PHI density were observed between these upstaged groups.
This prospective study evaluated the predictive power of various biomarkers, including PSA, fPSA, PHI, %p2PSA, PSA density, and PHI density, for adverse pathological outcomes, specifically pT3 stage or Gleason score (GS) ≥ 7, following radical prostatectomy (RP). Our findings indicate that PHI density demonstrated the strongest ability to predict high stage or high grade disease among the assessed markers. To our knowledge, this research is the first to demonstrate PHI density's superior performance compared to existing biomarkers, suggesting its potential to enhance current predictive models.
The widespread adoption of PSA screening by the FDA in 1994 led to a significant shift towards earlier prostate cancer diagnoses in many countries. However, this has sparked debate regarding its impact on mortality, with concerns about overdiagnosis and overtreatment. In Taiwan, where PSA screening is not officially endorsed, stage IV prostate cancer diagnoses are twice as common compared to the U.S. Consequently, prostate cancer in Taiwan is generally detected at a later stage. This is evidenced by higher average PSA levels (11 ng/mL) and a greater proportion of patients presenting with pT3 disease after radical prostatectomy (54.2%), figures considerably higher than those observed in European and Hong Kong studies. These distinct pathological characteristics within the Taiwanese cohort may influence how effectively biomarkers and existing models can predict outcomes following radical prostatectomy.
To address the overdiagnosis and overtreatment of early or indolent prostate cancer (PCa) identified by PSA, active surveillance with delayed treatment is an option. However, accurately identifying truly indolent PCa remains difficult. Current established methods for stratifying the risk of PCa's biological behavior, such as the D’Amico/NCCN and EAU risk stratification systems or Partin's table, are considered the gold standard. While these models are based on extensive data and possess strong statistical power, they were developed over a decade ago, predating the availability of newer biomarkers like PHI and PCA3. Therefore, integrating these more recent biomarkers, which have demonstrated superior performance compared to PSA, appears promising for improving the accuracy of identifying indolent PCa.
In this study, PHI and %p2PSA did not identify high-stage or high-risk prostate cancer, contrary to previous findings. Several factors could explain this discrepancy. We are confident that assay results were not the cause, as our cohort analysis using the same system for diagnosing PCa via TRUSP Bx showed consistent performance for PHI and %p2PSA compared to earlier studies. A more likely reason lies in the specific tumor characteristics of our patient group. With a mean PSA level of 11.0 ng/mL at treatment, higher than the typical 5-7 ng/mL in other series, and 54.2% of our cohort presenting with pT3 disease, our patients generally had more advanced cancer. Consequently, established models relying on Gleason score (GS) and PSA may have been more effective in predicting outcomes for these higher-stage patients than for those with lower-stage disease. This could also explain why PHI or %p2PSA, when used individually, did not demonstrate a significant impact in our cohort.
Similar to PSA density, incorporating prostate volume into predictive models can help distinguish between benign enlargement and cancer. Consequently, we examined PHI density (PHI divided by prostate volume) as a predictor of adverse pathological outcomes, even in patients with high-stage cancer. The odds ratio for adding PHI density to existing popular markers was 8.796. This finding aligns with a recent study by Tosoian et al., which demonstrated PHI density's superior performance over PHI and %p2PSA in identifying clinically significant cancer based on clinicopathological features after TRUSP biopsy.
The study's strengths include prospective plasma sample collection before radical prostatectomy, adherence to product recommendations, and pathological review by experienced genitourinary pathologists. Furthermore, the utilization of PHI derivatives, specifically PHI density, significantly improved the prediction of adverse pathological outcomes following RP.
This study has several limitations. The relatively small sample size may have diminished the ability to predict high-stage prostate cancer postoperatively, especially when compared to existing models incorporating Gleason score, PSA, or Partin's table. Furthermore, the absence of oncological follow-up data could impact the reliability of the biomarker derivatives. However, the established link between higher stage/grade prostate cancer and poorer prognosis with conservative treatments like active surveillance partially addresses this. Additionally, variations in prostate volume measurements due to operator differences during transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) could introduce bias. Finally, the inability to include tumor volume parameters prevented comparison with the Epstein criteria.
PHI density and other p2PSA derivatives significantly improved the prediction of adverse pathological outcomes. These factors could aid in preoperative patient counseling and assist in decisions regarding definitive treatment versus active surveillance.
