The European Commission requested a scientific opinion from the Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) regarding the safety and efficacy of dry grape extract. This assessment specifically concerns its use as an additive in drinking water for all animal species and categories. The dry grape extract, which is a blend of two extracts derived from the seeds and skin of Vitis vinifera subsp. vinifera, had been previously evaluated by the FEEDAP Panel for its application in animal feed. In its prior assessment, the FEEDAP Panel determined that the additive was safe for all animal species, excluding dogs, when incorporated into complete feed at a maximum level of 100 mg/kg. A safe dietary concentration for dogs, however, could not be established. Furthermore, the previous opinion concluded that there were no safety concerns for consumers or environmental risks. However, the safety for users remained inconclusive. These prior conclusions regarding consumer, user, and environmental safety are also applicable to the use of the additive in drinking water, as these aspects are not dependent on the method of delivery. The current evaluation noted that if the maximum proposed dose of 100 mg/L in water were applied, the established safe concentration in feed could be surpassed for several target species. Taking into account the safety margin identified in the earlier assessment, a revised maximum concentration of 50 mg/L in drinking water is now deemed safe for all animal species, with the exception of dogs. Finally, due to the primary component of the additive, the proanthocyanidin-rich seed extract, being a recognized flavoring agent in food, its function in animal drinking water is considered to be essentially the same. Therefore, a separate demonstration of efficacy for this application is not deemed necessary.

Community authorization for animal feed additives is governed by Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003. Specifically, Article 4(1) of this regulation mandates that applications for new feed additives or new uses of existing ones must adhere to the submission procedures outlined in Article 7.
NOR-FEED SUD requested the European Commission authorize dry grape extract as a sensory additive (flavouring) for all animal species in feed.
The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) deemed the supporting documents for the dry grape extract application valid on June 17, 2016. EFSA received this technical dossier directly from the applicant. The Commission had previously sent the application to EFSA, as mandated by Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, for authorization of a feed additive or a new use of an existing one under Article 4.
After confirming the applicant's submitted details and documents, EFSA is mandated by Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 to evaluate if the feed additive meets Article 5's requirements. This assessment involves EFSA issuing an opinion regarding the dry grape extract's safety for animals, consumers, users, and the environment, as well as its efficacy under the specified usage conditions.
The dry grape extract, a feed additive, is a polyphenol-rich blend. It combines extracts from the seeds and skin of Vitis vinifera subsp. vinifera. This additive functions as a flavoring, categorized as a sensory additive.
In 2016, the FEEDAP Panel of EFSA previously assessed the safety and efficacy of dry grape extract as a feed flavouring for all animal species. Their conclusion stated that the additive was safe for all species, excluding dogs, at a maximum use level of 100 mg/kg in complete feed. For dogs, a safe dietary concentration could not be determined. Additionally, the Panel found no concerns regarding consumer safety or environmental risks, but they were unable to reach a conclusion on user safety.
Authorization is now sought to use this additive in animal drinking water.
This evaluation relies on a technical dossier provided by the applicant, supporting the authorization of dry grape extract for use in drinking water. The dossier adhered to the requirements of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, Regulation (EC) No 429/2008, and relevant EFSA guidelines.
To produce this report, the FEEDAP Panel utilized information from the applicant, prior EFSA risk assessments, and expert knowledge.
The EURL affirmed the prior assessment's conclusions and recommendations remain valid for this application.
To evaluate the dry grape extract's safety and effectiveness, the FEEDAP Panel adhered to the guidelines outlined in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008. This included following specific guidance documents: one for preparing dossiers on sensory additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2012) and another statement concerning feed additives delivered through water (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2010).
This additive is designed for use in animal drinking water across all species, with no withdrawal period required. The suggested dosage ranges from 2.5 to 100 mg per liter of water.
Dry grape extract, a feed additive, is a polyphenol-rich blend sourced from the seeds and skin of *Vitis vinifera* subsp. *vinifera*.
This application concerns an additive identical in specifications and manufacturing to one previously evaluated (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2016). Therefore, existing data on its composition, impurities, physical properties, and stability remain valid. Only characteristics relevant to its use in drinking water are detailed subsequently.
Three batches of the additive were tested for water solubility at ambient temperature. The dry grape extract showed a mean solubility of 91% (ranging from 88–95%) at 50 g/L. Given its solubility and easy dispersion, proving homogeneity in drinking water is deemed unnecessary.
No loss of dry grape extract was observed when its stability in drinking water was monitored.
The FEEDAP Panel's 2016 opinion determined that the additive was safe for most animal species at a maximum of 100 mg/kg in complete feed, but a safe dietary concentration for dogs could not be established. Additionally, no concerns were found regarding consumer or environmental safety, though user safety remained inconclusive.
The previous opinion's conclusions regarding consumer, user, and environmental safety remain valid for this additive when delivered via drinking water. These safety aspects, being independent of delivery method, are therefore not re-evaluated in the current assessment.
The proposed maximum concentration of 100 mg of the additive per liter of drinking water could result in a higher dosage than the established maximum for feed, which is 100 mg per kilogram of complete feed. Specifically, for poultry, pigs, and rabbits, consuming the additive at this maximum proposed water level would lead to an intake 2–3 times greater than through feed. This means that for several animal species, the safe concentration previously determined for feed could be surpassed if the maximum dose in water were applied. However, taking into account the safety margin established in an earlier assessment, a concentration of 50 mg/L in drinking water is deemed safe for all animal species, with the exception of dogs.
The FEEDAP Panel previously determined that dry grape extract is effective as a feed additive for all animal species. This decision stemmed from acknowledging that the primary ingredient, proanthocyanidin-rich seed extract, is known for its flavoring properties in food and beverages like wine. Consequently, given its similar role in feed, no further efficacy demonstration was deemed essential.
The FEEDAP Panel determined that this finding is also valid for dry grape extract administered via drinking water.
If the maximum proposed dose in water were applied, the safe concentration in feed could be surpassed for several animal species. However, based on the previous assessment's safety margin, 50 mg/L in drinking water is deemed safe for all animal species, excluding dogs.
The Panel determined that using dry grape extract in water at safe feed levels poses no environmental or consumer risks. However, the safety for users could not be definitively established.
Given that the primary ingredient, proanthocyanidin-rich seed extract, is known for flavoring food and wine, and its role in animal drinking water is similar, efficacy demonstration is deemed unnecessary.
NOR-FEED SUD submitted dry grape extract for all animal species in May 2016, prompting Member State comments.
