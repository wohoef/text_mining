This study explored the diagnostic and prognostic potential of circulating microRNA-101 (miR-101) in osteosarcoma, a role previously underexplored despite miR-101's known tumor-suppressive functions in other cancers. Researchers analyzed serum miR-101 levels in 152 osteosarcoma patients and 70 healthy individuals using quantitative reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). The findings revealed significantly lower miR-101 expression in osteosarcoma patients compared to healthy controls. Furthermore, post-treatment serum miR-101 levels were markedly elevated compared to pre-treatment levels within the patient group. A strong correlation was observed between reduced serum miR-101 and advanced clinical stages, as well as the presence of distant metastasis. Diagnostic utility was demonstrated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, indicating that serum miR-101 could effectively differentiate osteosarcoma patients from healthy individuals with high sensitivity and specificity. Prognostically, patients with higher miR-101 expression exhibited extended overall survival and recurrence-free survival. Both univariate and multivariate statistical analyses further confirmed that decreased serum miR-101 expression independently correlated with poorer overall survival and recurrence-free survival outcomes. These results collectively suggest that serum miR-101 holds promise as a valuable biomarker for both the clinical diagnosis and prognosis of osteosarcoma.

Osteosarcoma, the most common bone malignancy, causes significant cancer-related mortality in children and young adults. Despite diagnostic and therapeutic progress, the five-year survival rate remains low, particularly with recurrence or metastasis. This highlights a critical need for effective biomarkers to enhance osteosarcoma diagnosis and prognostic prediction.
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression. Research indicates their crucial involvement in cancer development, acting as either oncogenes or tumor suppressors. These molecules influence various biological processes during tumor initiation and progression, including cell proliferation, invasion, metastasis, and angiogenesis. Notably, specific miRNAs, such as miR-95-3p, miR-300, and miR-101, exhibit abnormal expression patterns in osteosarcoma.
Previous research indicates miR-101, encoded on chromosomes 1p31.3 and 9p24.1, functions as a tumor suppressor in osteosarcoma. Studies have consistently shown reduced miR-101 levels in osteosarcoma bone tissues and cell lines. Restoring miR-101 expression has been demonstrated to inhibit cancer cell viability, migration, and invasion, while also promoting apoptosis. This tumor-suppressive effect can be counteracted by the overexpression of ROCK1 or c-FOS. Furthermore, in vitro evidence suggests that miR-101 significantly reduces chemotherapy-induced autophagy and related protein expression in osteosarcoma cells. By blocking autophagy, miR-101 enhances the sensitivity of these cancer cells to chemotherapy. Despite these findings, the diagnostic and prognostic utility of serum miR-101 in osteosarcoma remains largely unexplored.
This study investigated miR-101's clinical role in osteosarcoma. We measured serum miR-101 levels in participants to assess its correlation with patient clinical features. Prognostic value was determined using Kaplan-Meier, univariate, and multivariate analyses. This explored miR-101's potential as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for osteosarcoma.
Before study commencement, all participants provided written informed consent. The Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou University of Traditional Chinese Medicine approved the study's protocols.
This study enrolled 152 osteosarcoma patients and 70 healthy controls, matched for age and gender. All patients were treatment-naïve prior to surgery. Osteosarcoma diagnoses were confirmed through imaging (X-ray, CT, MRI) and biopsy, with classification following the World Health Organization system. Patients underwent regular follow-up until death or the study's conclusion. Serum samples, collected from the Clinical Laboratory at The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, were stored at -80°C for subsequent RNA extraction.

RNA was extracted and assessed for purity and concentration. Reverse transcription then prepared the RNA for quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). This PCR utilized a Taqman MicroRNA Assay Kit and a MyiQ Real-Time PCR Detection System. miR-101 PCR primers were used, with U6 serving as an internal control. Relative miR-101 expression levels in serum were calculated via the 2-ΔΔCt method. All procedures were performed in triplicate.

Statistical analysis was conducted using MedCalc 13.0 and GraphPad Prism 6.04. To compare serum miR-101 levels between patients and controls, the Mann Whitney U test was employed. The relationship between miR-101 expression and clinicopathological features was examined using the Chi-square test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to evaluate miR-101's effectiveness in distinguishing osteosarcoma patients from healthy individuals. Survival curves, constructed via the Kaplan-Meier method, were compared using log-rank tests. Cox proportional hazard models assessed risk ratios for survival-associated factors. A P-value below 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Among 152 osteosarcoma patients, miR-101 expression was categorized as either high (70 cases) or low (82 cases) based on the median level. Low serum miR-101 significantly correlated with advanced clinical stage (P=0.013) and distant metastasis (P=0.028). Conversely, no significant association was found between miR-101 expression and patient age, sex, tumor diameter, or anatomical location (all P>0.05).
To assess osteosarcoma prognosis, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank tests were conducted. Patients with lower miR-101 expression exhibited significantly poorer overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) compared to those with higher expression (P=0.011 and P=0.005, respectively). Univariate analysis further identified low miR-101 expression (P=0.012 for OS, P=0.006 for RFS), distant metastasis (P=0.023 for OS, P=0.017 for RFS), and advanced clinical stage (P=0.019 for OS, P=0.025 for RFS) as indicators of unfavorable prognosis. These findings were corroborated by multivariate analysis, which confirmed low miR-101 expression (P=0.014 for OS, P=0.008 for RFS), distant metastasis (P=0.027 for OS, P=0.020 for RFS), and clinical stage (P=0.025 for OS, P=0.018 for RFS) as independent prognostic factors for both OS and RFS.

Circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) are stable in blood and serum, making them promising non-invasive biomarkers for cancer diagnosis and prognosis. In osteosarcoma, various circulating miRNAs exhibit altered expression. For instance, miR-95-3p downregulation has been observed in osteosarcoma patients compared to healthy individuals. This reduced miR-95-3p expression correlated with advanced clinical stage, metastasis, and diminished survival rates, suggesting its role as a tumor suppressor. Conversely, elevated levels of miR-300 were found in osteosarcoma patients relative to controls. This aberrant increase in miR-300 expression was linked to a poorer prognosis, implying an oncogenic function for miR-300 in the disease. These examples highlight the diverse roles of miRNAs in osteosarcoma.
MiR-101 dysregulation has been widely implicated in the development of various human cancers. Studies have consistently shown that altered miR-101 expression contributes to tumorigenesis across multiple cancer types. In lung cancer, for instance, an inverse correlation exists between miR-101 and DNMT3A levels in both cancer cell lines and clinical tissues. Overexpression of DNMT3A or a reduction in miR-101 expression promotes cell proliferation and migration in vitro, and enhances tumorigenicity in vivo. Furthermore, while EZH2 loss suppresses lung cancer progression, miR-101 downregulation significantly diminishes this inhibitory effect. Similarly, in breast cancer, miR-101 levels are inversely related to aggressive disease progression. Restoring miR-101 expression effectively inhibits carcinogenesis both in vitro and in vivo by repressing SOX-2 or CXCR7. Papillary thyroid carcinoma tissues exhibit significantly reduced miR-101 expression. This downregulation has been shown to impede tumor growth in vitro by targeting Rac1 or USP22. Hepatocellular carcinoma tissues also display a dramatic underexpression of miR-101. Increasing miR-101 expression in these cells reduces their oncogenic activities, with VEGF-C and Girdin identified as downstream targets. In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, miR-101 downregulation is observed in both cancerous tissues and cell lines. Overexpressing miR-101 substantially inhibits cancer cell proliferation and motility through direct silencing of COX-2 or EZH2. Ovarian cancer cells show a marked decrease in proliferation, migration, and invasion when miR-101 is upregulated. MiR-101 expression in this context is inversely associated with brain-derived neurotrophic factor, SOCS-2, ZEB1, and ZEB2. Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) samples demonstrate decreased miR-101 expression compared to normal tissues. In vitro and in vivo evidence indicates that elevated miR-101 expression is strongly linked to reduced cell growth and motility in GBM. In cervical cancer, increased miR-101 expression correlates with a significant reduction in cellular motility. Conversely, miR-101 downregulation is observed in patients with cervical lymph node metastasis and is associated with shorter survival times. For bladder transitional cell carcinoma, low miR-101 expression predicts a worse patient prognosis and is strongly associated with advanced tumor stage and lymph node metastasis. Gastric cancer tissues exhibit lower miR-101 levels compared to normal paracancerous tissues. This reduced miR-101 expression leads to increased cancer cell proliferation and decreased apoptosis by inducing COX-2 expression. Finally, in prostate cancer, in vitro and in vivo analyses have demonstrated that ectopic miR-101 expression significantly inhibits the tumorigenic properties of cancer cells, with COX-2 identified as a direct target.
This initial study established a significant decrease in serum miR-101 expression among osteosarcoma patients. This reduction suggests miR-101's potential as a diagnostic marker, exhibiting a moderate diagnostic value for osteosarcoma, with an AUC of 0.850, and sensitivity and specificity rates of 78.95% and 82.86%, respectively. Furthermore, lower miR-101 expression was strongly correlated with more advanced clinical stages and the presence of distant metastasis. Interestingly, in osteosarcoma patients without distant metastasis, post-treatment blood samples showed elevated miR-101 levels compared to pre-treatment samples. This observation indicates that serum miR-101 levels could serve as a valuable tool for monitoring treatment efficacy. Survival analysis using the Kaplan-Meier method revealed that patients with higher miR-101 expression experienced significantly longer overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS). Importantly, serum miR-101, along with clinical stage and distant metastasis, emerged as independent prognostic indicators for both OS and RFS in osteosarcoma patients. These findings align with prior research. The observed decrease in serum miR-101 in osteosarcoma patients could stem from two primary mechanisms: either a reduction in miR-101 levels within tumor cells, leading to diminished secretion into the bloodstream, or decreased packaging of miR-101 into exosomes—circulating extracellular vesicles that transport various biological components—by cancer cells, thereby indirectly lowering circulating miR-101 levels. While these results are promising, the study's limited sample size necessitates further investigation with larger clinical cohorts to validate these findings.
This study demonstrates that miR-101 is frequently downregulated in osteosarcoma patients. Therefore, miR-101 could serve as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for osteosarcoma.
