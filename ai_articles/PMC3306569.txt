Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) carries a grim prognosis, with an average survival of merely six months and a five-year survival rate below 5%. Despite progress in understanding PDAC's development over recent decades, patient survival has remained unchanged. While numerous clinicopathological and immunohistological factors have been linked to survival duration, their precise influence remains undefined. To clarify the independent impact of these variables on survival, a multivariate analysis was performed on 78 PDAC cases. This analysis incorporated clinicopathological parameters and the immunohistochemical expression of p53, Smad4, Axl, ALDH, MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, and PMS2. The only independent prognostic factors identified were a lymph node ratio below 0.2 (p = 0.004), tumor-free resection margins (p = 0.044), and Smad4 expression (p = 0.004). The expression of other proteins investigated did not show a significant association with survival. Discrepancies observed when comparing these findings with other studies are likely attributable to variations in immunohistochemical staining quantification and the absence of multivariate analysis in those studies, highlighting the critical need for standardized immunohistochemistry methods in prognostic research.

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) carries a grim outlook, with its annual mortality rate nearly matching its incidence. In the USA, around 36,800 individuals succumb to PDAC each year, positioning it as the fourth leading cause of cancer-related fatalities. Despite progress in understanding PDAC's development, this knowledge hasn't led to better patient outcomes; 5-year survival rates have remained below 5% for decades. The majority of patients are diagnosed with advanced or metastatic disease, making curative surgery difficult. Even among the 5-20% of patients eligible for resection, only 10-18% achieve 5-year survival, and even then, recurrence after five years means 5-year survival doesn't guarantee a cure.
The precise impact of certain clinicopathological factors on patient survival in pancreatic cancer remains unclear, despite their established association. These factors include a low disease stage, the status of resection margins, and the presence of lymph node metastasis. Beyond these, numerous tumor-specific protein expression patterns have also been linked to overall survival in pancreatic cancer patients. For instance, sustained expression of Smad4, a tumor suppressor gene implicated in roughly 55% of PDAC cases, has been identified as a significant predictor for improved disease-free and overall survival. Similarly, microsatellite instability, stemming from defects in mismatch repair (MMR) genes, has been suggested to positively influence prognosis, though this finding lacks universal confirmation across all studies. Axl, a receptor tyrosine kinase frequently involved in cancer progression, is another protein whose expression has been correlated with PDAC prognosis. More recently, research by Rasheed et al. connected aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH) expression to a poorer prognosis, proposing that ALDH-positive cells possess tumor-initiating capabilities and that their proportion negatively impacts overall survival. Critically, most of these studies evaluated clinical and histological characteristics without accounting for other prognostic variables through multivariate analysis.
A multivariate analysis was performed on 78 PDAC cases to determine the independent impact of various clinical and histological factors on survival. This analysis incorporated clinicopathological parameters alongside the expression patterns of several proteins previously linked to PDAC survival. Specifically, p53, Smad4, Axl, ALDH, and the mismatch repair genes MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, and PMS2 were investigated.
This study utilized paraffin-embedded tissue from 78 primary infiltrating pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas (PDACs). These samples were sourced from the Surgical Pathology archives of three cancer treatment centers: the University Medical Center Utrecht, the Academic Medical Center Amsterdam, and the Erasmus Medical Center Rotterdam. For each patient, clinical information such as age, sex, tumor size, TNM-stage, histological grade, lymph-node status, and precise survival time in months was collected. However, data on patient treatment or tumor recurrence times were unavailable, preventing their correlation with the study's investigated parameters.
Tissue microarrays (TMAs) were constructed from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue. First, neoplastic areas were identified on hematoxylin-eosin stained sections. Then, three 0.6 mm cores were extracted from the donor block for each case and inserted into a receiver block. Additionally, a 0.6 mm non-neoplastic lymph node core served as a control for each patient.
Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was conducted on 4 μm-thick tissue microarray (TMA) sections to assess the expression of several proteins: p53, Smad4, Axl, ALDH, and the four mismatch repair (MMR) proteins (MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, and PMS2). Standard deparaffinization techniques were initially applied to the sections. To prevent non-specific staining, endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using 3% H2O2 in methanol for 10 minutes. If required, sections underwent pretreatment with ARS pH 9 in an autoclave for 10 minutes, followed by a 10-minute cooling period. Before the primary antibodies were introduced, sections were incubated with Protein Block Serum-Free (Dako Cytomation). Following primary antibody application, antibody binding was made visible using the PowerVision + Poly-HRP kit (Immunologic). The chromogen used for visualization was either 3,3-diaminobenzidin (DAB; Sigma-Aldrich) or DAB+ (Dako Cytomation). Finally, sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, and coverslips were affixed. Detailed information regarding the primary antibodies, including their manufacturers, pretreatment requirements, dilutions, and incubation times, is provided in Table 1.
Immunohistochemistry (IHC) labeling was evaluated by a single researcher, with cutoff levels established through agreement with an experienced pathologist using a multiheaded microscope. Any uncertain cases were re-examined with the pathologist, and final scores were determined by consensus. The scoring criteria varied depending on the specific antibody used. For p53, nuclear protein accumulation exceeding 80% in cancer cells was considered positive, while absent or faint diffuse staining indicated normal p53 expression and was scored as negative. Smad4 and the mismatch repair (MMR) proteins were deemed negative if no labeling was present in neoplastic cells; otherwise, weak or strong labeling was scored as positive. Axl staining was categorized as negative if labeling appeared in 0-10% of neoplastic cells, and positive if it was present in over 10% of these cells. These scoring methodologies align with established practices in prior publications [15, 19, 20]. ALDH staining was assessed using two distinct approaches. Initially, sections were scored similarly to Axl. Subsequently, ALDH staining was also evaluated following the method described by Rasheed et al. [17]. Under this stricter criterion, only tumors displaying intense nuclear and cytoplasmic staining in neoplastic cells, at least twice as strong as in adjacent normal pancreatic acinar cells, were classified as positive (Figure 1). Although individual tissue cores sometimes showed varying staining intensities, the overall IHC score for each tumor was derived from a comprehensive assessment of the entire cancer cell population across all three cores, rather than on a core-by-core basis.
For statistical analysis, SPSS version 15.0 was utilized. The Kaplan-Meier method was employed to estimate survival, with statistical significance assessed via the log-rank test. GraphPad Prism generated the Kaplan-Meier graphs. Pearson's Chi-Square test determined associations between various factors. To identify correlations between clinical characteristics and protein expression, Pearson's correlation coefficient was computed. Variables exhibiting prognostic potential in univariate analyses (p ≤ 0.10) or significant correlation with other parameters were subsequently included in the multivariate analysis. Non-significant variables were progressively eliminated. The Cox proportional hazards model was applied, with a p-value below 0.05 indicating statistical significance.
The study involved 78 pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) patients, all of whom had their diagnosis confirmed. Their average age was 63, with a range from 40 to 77 years. The median overall survival for this cohort was 27 months. A small fraction of patients achieved long-term survival, with 12 individuals (15%) surviving for 5 years and only 2 (3%) reaching 10-year survival. Regarding tumor location, the majority of patients (75) presented with tumors in the head of the pancreas, while only three had tumors in the tail. Surgical interventions varied: 69 patients underwent a Whipple’s pancreatoduodenectomy, 7 had either a pylorus-preserving pancreatoduodenectomy (6 cases) or a complete pancreatoduodenectomy (1 case), and 2 patients received a corpus/tail resection. The type of surgical procedure performed did not significantly impact patient survival (p = 0.48). Univariate analysis was conducted to assess the influence of various factors on survival. Age, gender, tumor size, and histological grade were not found to be associated with differences in survival time. However, a borderline significant correlation with improved survival was observed for the absence of lymph node metastasis (p = 0.07) and a tumor-free resection margin (p = 0.06). More definitively, two variables significantly improved survival in the univariate analysis: lymph node ratio (LNR) and tumor stage. Patients with an LNR less than 0.2 had a median overall survival of 34.5 months, compared to 15.6 months for those with an LNR greater than 0.2 (p = 0.002). Similarly, patients with stage I or IIA tumors demonstrated a median overall survival of 43.1 months, substantially longer than the 21.0 months for those with stage IIB or III tumors (p = 0.02). These demographic and tumor characteristics, along with their univariate survival analysis, are detailed in Table 2.
Immunohistochemistry was used to assess the expression of eight distinct proteins. Axl expression was detected in 22% of tumors, with the remaining tumors being Axl negative. Smad4 expression was entirely absent in 43% of cases. Strong nuclear p53 staining, indicative of a protein defect, was present in 54% of cases. While 75% of tumors showed positive ALDH protein staining, only 11 tumors (16%) displayed the high-intensity ALDH staining described by Rasheed et al. MSH-2, MSH-6, PMS-2, and MLH-1, all mismatch repair proteins, showed unaffected staining in 95%, 97%, 93%, and 99% of cases, respectively.
In the univariate analysis, Smad4 expression emerged as a significant prognostic factor for survival (p = 0.008). Conversely, none of the other examined variables demonstrated a statistically significant prognostic value. This analysis, detailed in Table 3, evaluated various histological factors in 78 PDAC patients, including protein expression patterns for P53, Axl, ALDH (both diffuse and high-expression), MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, and PMS2, none of which showed a notable correlation with survival outcomes.
Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used to assess relationships among clinical characteristics and protein expression. A strong correlation was observed between tumor stage and both lymph node ratio (p < 0.001) and resection margin (p = 0.04). Additionally, MMR protein expression showed significant correlations, specifically between MSH2 and MSH6 (p = 0.005), and between PMS2 and MLH1 (p < 0.001).
Multivariate analysis was performed on variables showing prognostic potential (p ≤ 0.10) in univariate analysis or significant correlations with other factors. This comprehensive analysis incorporated lymph node status, lymph node ratio (LNR), resection margin, tumor stage, and Smad4 expression. The results, detailed in Table 4, revealed that an LNR below 0.2, a tumor-free resection margin, and sustained Smad4 expression were independently associated with significantly improved survival outcomes.
Despite significant advancements in understanding pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) pathogenesis over recent decades, patient survival remains poor, typically only a few months post-diagnosis. A crucial approach to improving this understanding involves a thorough examination of factors influencing survival and prognosis. Identifying specific patient or tumor traits linked to longer survival is therefore a key strategy. While numerous studies have explored the prognostic value of various factors, a complete and clear picture is still lacking. Tumor size, lymph node involvement, resection margins, and histological grade have all been implicated in affecting survival, yet findings across studies are inconsistent. Notably, lymph node ratio (LNR) stands out as the sole prognostic factor consistently significant across all studies where it has been assessed.
Recent studies have identified numerous potential prognostic indicators in PDAC through immunohistochemical protein expression analysis, beyond demographic variations. However, these studies often lacked multivariate analysis to confirm the independent prognostic value of these proteins. To address this, our study conducted a multivariate analysis, incorporating a wide range of implicated characteristics. Our primary goal was to pinpoint which proteins maintained prognostic significance within this multivariate framework, building upon previous findings regarding clinical characteristics and protein roles in survival.
This study identified a lymph node ratio (LNR) below 0.2 as the most significant prognostic indicator, aligning with previous research. Additionally, sustained Smad4 expression, a factor previously noted for its prognostic value, was confirmed as a favorable predictor of survival in this cohort, even after multivariate analysis.
Previous research has linked positive Axl expression in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) to diminished overall survival. However, the methodologies of these studies present challenges for direct comparison. For instance, Koorstra et al. did not conduct a multivariate analysis. While Song et al. did perform a multivariate analysis incorporating Axl expression and lymph node involvement, finding a marginally significant impact of Axl on survival, their study was limited to stage II PDACs. In contrast, our study found no correlation between Axl expression and either lymph node status or survival time. Additionally, only 23% of the tumors in our cohort exhibited Axl expression, a significantly lower percentage than reported elsewhere. This discrepancy might stem from differences in Axl expression scoring methods. Given these conflicting findings, definitive conclusions regarding Axl's prognostic value in PDAC remain elusive, underscoring the need for further investigation into its role in disease progression.
The study investigated the impact of mismatch repair (MMR) proteins (MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, and PMS2) on survival, a group previously linked to patient outcomes. Defects in these genes, whether mutations or epigenetic alterations, can cause microsatellite instability, which some research suggests leads to a better prognosis compared to microsatellite stable tumors. Although direct assessment of microsatellite instability was not performed, MMR protein activity was evaluated using immunohistochemistry (IHC). Consistent with prior findings, about 13% of the tumors exhibited a lack of expression in at least one MMR protein. A strong correlation was observed among the expression levels of these proteins, particularly between MSH2 and MSH6, and between MLH1 and PMS2. This correlation is anticipated given their roles as heterodimers in DNA mismatch repair. Nevertheless, no significant association was found between MMR protein expression and patient survival in this study.
Research into aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH) expression in cancers has recently concentrated on a small subset of cancer cells, roughly 1% of the tumor volume, known for their elevated tumorigenic capacity. These "tumor-initiating cells" have been a subject of intense study over the past decade, including a recent publication by Rasheed et al. [17] that explored their prognostic importance in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). While immunohistochemistry (IHC) in our study showed a substantial number of neoplastic cells expressing ALDH, Rasheed et al. [17] specifically highlighted cells with strong nuclear ALDH staining, at least twice as intense as normal pancreatic acinar cells. This selective focus identified a small percentage of ALDH-high tumors with notable prognostic implications. In our cohort, 75% of tumors displayed positive ALDH staining, though with varying intensities, and this general ALDH expression showed no link to survival time. Even when applying the stricter scoring criteria proposed by Rasheed et al. [17], which reduced the percentage of ALDH-high tumors to 16%, we still observed no correlation between these highly expressing tumors and patient survival. Given the current emphasis in cancer research on "tumor-initiating cells," further investigation into ALDH protein staining via IHC is warranted, especially considering the inconsistencies observed across different studies.
Immunohistochemistry (IHC) offers a valuable, cost-effective, and rapid approach for assessing protein expression in a laboratory setting. For instance, IHC reliably reflects Smad4 expression and identifies p53 defects through nuclear protein accumulation. Nevertheless, a standardized scoring methodology for IHC-stained tumors is often lacking for many proteins, complicating both interpretation and inter-study comparisons. This challenge was evident in our ALDH expression analysis, where employing two distinct scoring methods yielded varying proportions of ALDH-positive tumors. Therefore, standardizing IHC scoring is crucial to enhance the precision of protein expression evaluation and generate robust quantitative data.
This study's multivariate analysis confirmed that lymph node ratio (LNR) and resection margin are significant prognostic indicators for PDAC. Alongside Smad4 expression, these three factors demonstrably influenced survival time and are crucial for personalized prognostication. While other proteins examined in this study lacked a significant association with overall survival in the multivariate analysis, their prognostic value has been suggested in other research. Further investigation is necessary to definitively establish the role of these additional proteins in predicting prognosis.
