We report the synthesis and characterization of four fully-conjugated indacenedithiophenes (IDTs). Unlike anthradithiophenes, both syn and anti isomers are regioselectively synthesized. Thiophene fusion increases the central indacene core's paratropic character, as DFT calculations and 1H NMR spectroscopy confirm. IDTs show red-shifted absorbance maxima compared to their all-carbon analogues and undergo two-electron reduction and one-electron oxidation.

Highly conjugated polycyclic hydrocarbons, including higher acenes, have garnered significant attention due to their intriguing optical and electronic characteristics. Pentacene and its derivatives, for instance, find application in devices like field-effect transistors, photovoltaics, and light-emitting diodes. However, their vulnerability to oxidative and photolytic degradation necessitates the exploration of alternative, acene-like structures. Anthradithiophene (ADT), a structural analogue of pentacene, emerged as an early substitute. Incorporating heterocycles into the acene skeleton, a strategy known as thieno-fusion, offers several advantages. This approach allows for the fine-tuning of physical and electronic properties, leading to enhanced stability, high electron mobilities, and simplified functionalization. Notably, ADT and its derivatives exhibit hole mobilities comparable to pentacene while demonstrating superior oxidative stability. Generally, these acene molecules and their numerous derivatives function as organic p-type compounds. In contrast, the literature presents a considerably smaller number of organic n-type structures. This disparity highlights an urgent need for novel molecular frameworks capable of efficient electron transport.
Recent investigations by our group and others have focused on indenofluorene (IF) isomers as promising n-type materials, owing to their capacity for reversible two-electron acceptance. The electronic characteristics and stability of IFs are amenable to modification through strategic functionalization. Early research indicated that incorporating diverse electron-rich and electron-poor groups at the 2 and 8 positions of the indeno[1,2-b]fluorene skeleton (e.g., 3a) yielded only minor alterations in the compounds' electronic properties. However, subsequent studies revealed that attaching aryl groups at the 6 and 12 positions of [1,2-b]IFs (3b) significantly enhanced the variability of their redox behavior, with some exhibiting amphoteric properties. Both Tobe's group and ours independently reported that the [2,1-a] isomer 4 and the previously unidentified [2,1-c] isomer 5 also displayed superior electrochemical properties, characterized by smaller HOMO-LUMO gaps compared to the [1,2-b]IFs. More recently, Tobe et al. described the novel [2,1-b] isomer 6; however, its inherent instability may limit its practical application in organic electronics.
Building on prior success, we explored the structural analogy between indeno[1,2-b]fluorene and indacenodithiophene skeletons, mirroring the pentacene-anthradithiophene relationship. This paper details the synthesis of two indacenedithiophenes (IDTs, 7a,c) and two indacenedibenzothiophenes (IDBTs, 7b,d) from indacenedione precursors (8a–d). We present comprehensive optical, electrochemical, computational, and structural data for these novel electron-accepting molecules.
Computational studies of simplified indacenedithiophene (IDT) structures (7a′–d′) predicted intriguing optical and electronic properties for these 20 π-electron, antiaromatic compounds. Specifically, NICS(1) calculations revealed that the weaker aromaticity of the fused thiophene rings (ring C in 7a′–d′), when compared to model [1,2-b]IF 3′, would allow the antiaromatic character of the central indacene core (rings A and B) to become more pronounced, reaching 60–90% of that observed in s-indacene (9). This enhanced paratropicity was further supported by predictions of lower low-energy transitions in the absorption spectrum, a characteristic shared with other highly paratropic molecules. Furthermore, Density Functional Theory (DFT) calculations indicated that IDTs would exhibit lower HOMO/LUMO energy levels and smaller energy gaps compared to structurally analogous indenofluorenes (IFs). These findings suggest a significant departure from anthradithiophenes (ADTs), which, unlike IDTs, show an increase in energy gaps when compared to their pentacene derivatives.
Indacenedithiophenes (IDTs) 7a,c and indacene-dibenzothiophenes (IDBTs) 7b,d were synthesized via a method analogous to indenofluorene production. This involved nucleophilic addition to indacenediones 8a–d, followed by SnCl2-mediated dearomatization. Mesityl lithium was chosen as the nucleophile, as calculations indicated this bulky group would kinetically stabilize the indacene core. While dione 8a was previously known, diones 8b–d were synthesized using a modified McCulloch et al. procedure. For example, the synthesis of 7d/8d began with a Stille cross-coupling of dibromide 10 and stannane 11, yielding diester 12. Saponification of 12 produced diacid 13, which was then converted to an acid chloride. Subsequent intramolecular Friedel–Crafts acylation afforded dione 8d. Treatment of 8d with MesLi generated a crude diol, which was then reductively dearomatized with SnCl2 to yield the fully conjugated 7d. This synthetic approach offers a key advantage: it produces IDTs with a defined structure. In contrast, anthradithiophene (ADT) and its derivatives are typically synthesized and studied as inseparable mixtures of syn- and anti-isomers, due to the regiorandom Aldol condensations used in their precursor dione formation.
Stable, dark blue-green indacenedithiophenes (IDTs 7a,c) and indacenedibenzothiophenes (IDBTs 7b,d) were isolated in modest yields. As predicted by calculations, their stronger antiaromatic character was confirmed by 1H NMR spectroscopy. Specifically, the ring A proton signals in IDTs appeared around 6.1 ppm, a notable shift compared to approximately 7.3 ppm in diones 8a–d and 7.1 ppm in derivatives of 3 and 4.
Electronic absorption spectra for diones 8 and indacenedithiophenes (IDTs) 7 are presented in Figures 5 and 6, respectively. Table 2 summarizes these spectroscopic data, alongside calculated and experimental HOMO and LUMO energies and their corresponding energy gaps. Diones 8 exhibit strong absorptions between 275 nm and 325 nm, with additional broad bands in the 550–600 nm range, indicative of weak π → π* transitions. IDTs display maximum absorbance peaks from 315 to 375 nm. However, significant distinctions among the thiophene-containing structures emerge from their lower energy absorptions. Firstly, the extended conjugation in indacenedibenzothiophenes (IDBTs) 7b,d leads to a lower maximum absorption wavelength (λmax) of 624/632 nm, in contrast to the analogous IDTs 7a,c, which show λmax values of 561/592 nm. Secondly, the "syn" isomers (7c,d), characterized by the sulfur atom and mesityl group of adjacent rings being on the same side, possess a lower λmax compared to their "anti" counterparts (7a,b), where these groups are on opposite sides. Thirdly, and consistent with computational predictions, IDTs demonstrate a reduction in their energy gaps by 0.2–0.3 eV when compared to the analogous dimesityl derivative of indenofluorene 3b, which has a low energy λmax of 516 nm. This observation notably diverges from the established pentacene/anthradithiophene comparison.
The electrochemical behavior of indacenedithiophenes (IDTs) 7a,c and indacenedibenzothiophenes (IDBTs) 7b,d was investigated. All compounds exhibited a single reversible reduction in solution, with a subsequent irreversible second reduction. For the IDTs, the initial oxidation of 7a was quasi-reversible, while 7c's was irreversible. In contrast, both IDBTs (7b,d) displayed fully reversible first oxidations. Comparing IDTs and IDBTs, the extended π-conjugation in IDBTs did not significantly alter their HOMO energy levels. However, IDBTs showed LUMO levels that were 0.15–0.25 eV lower than those of the corresponding IDTs. Calculated HOMO levels aligned well with experimental cyclic voltammetry data. Conversely, calculated LUMO levels were higher than experimental values, a common discrepancy for DFT-derived LUMO levels in molecules with a p-quinodimethane motif. When compared to indenofluorenes (IFs) with similar functionalization, thiophene substitution in IDTs and IDBTs led to a destabilized HOMO and a stabilized LUMO. This effect resulted in a smaller bandgap, evidenced by a red-shifted λmax. Like IFs, which undergo two reductions due to dianion stabilization and aromatization of their antiaromatic indacene core to a [4n + 2] π-electron system, IDTs 7a,c and IDBTs 7b,d demonstrated similar two-electron reduction behavior.
Electrochemical analysis of diones 8a,c by cyclic voltammetry revealed two reversible reductions and no oxidations. However, the poor solubility of dibenzodiones 8b,d in suitable solvents prevented a similar investigation of their electrochemical behavior.
X-ray diffraction analysis was performed on crystals of 7a,b,d (grown by acetonitrile diffusion into CH2Cl2) and 7c (obtained via slow CD2Cl2 evaporation). Figure 9 illustrates the C2-symmetric structures of these four molecules. A comparison of selected bond lengths in the indacenedithiophenes (IDTs) with those of the dimesityl derivative of [1,2-b]IF 3b is provided in Table 3. The central six-membered ring in 7a and 3b exhibits highly similar bond lengths, with differences ranging from 0.002 to 0.004 Å. However, the five-membered rings show greater variability, which is expected due to their fusion with thiophene rings in 7a and benzene rings in 3b. When comparing 7a,c with their benzo-fused counterparts 7b,d, the indacene core's bond lengths appear more homogenous in 7b and 7d. This homogenization suggests enhanced paratropicity within the core of the benzo-fused IDTs, consistent with observations by Hafner and co-workers for indacene derivative 9, and aligning with NICS(1) values for rings A and B in Table 1. Furthermore, the dihedral angle between the mesityl group and the IDT core is smaller for the syn isomers 7c (63.6°) and 7d (60.6°) than for the anti isomers 7a (68.2°) and 7b (74.3°). This reduced angle in syn isomers likely contributes to increased overall conjugation, potentially explaining their longer wavelength absorbances. Aside from this, no other significant structural differences were observed between the syn and anti isomers.
The crystal structures of 7a and 7b reveal a herringbone packing arrangement of the indacenedithiophene (IDT) core, where sulfur atoms mediate the shortest intermolecular distances. In 7a, each sulfur atom forms two close contacts (3.312 Å and 3.421 Å) with the five-membered ring of neighboring molecules, while the S⋯S contact is comparatively longer at 4.202 Å. Compound 7b displays a similar packing motif; however, the presence of additional benzo groups alters the interactions. Here, sulfur atoms are positioned closer to the thiophene rings of adjacent molecules, resulting in S⋯C contacts ranging from 3.406 Å to 3.520 Å, and a shorter S⋯S contact of 3.707 Å.
Crystal packing analysis revealed distinct arrangements for the synthesized compounds. While 7a, 7b, and 7d all exhibited a herringbone-like packing, the specific interactions varied. For 7d, the shortest carbon-carbon contacts between molecules measured 3.499 Å and 3.352 Å, occurring between the central ring of one molecule and the peripheral ring of an adjacent one. However, these closest contacts were not located in regions with significant LUMO density. In contrast, IDT 7c displayed a one-dimensional structure characterized by a slight overlap of thiophene units from neighboring molecules, with an average interplanar distance of 3.615 Å. This parallel columnar arrangement in 7c differed notably from the herringbone pattern observed in 7a, 7b, and 7d. Furthermore, the longest sulfur-sulfur contact in 7c, at 4.829 Å, indicated that these interactions did not play a role in dictating its crystal packing.
We successfully synthesized fully conjugated indacenedithiophenes (IDTs), achieving regioselective control over both syn and anti isomers. Optical and electrochemical data, supported by computational analysis, confirmed stabilized HOMO and LUMO energy levels for these compounds (7a–d). Notably, IDTs exhibit smaller energy gaps compared to their all-carbon indenofluorene counterparts, a characteristic attributed to the enhanced paratropicity of the indacene core resulting from thiophene fusion. This contrasts with the anthradithiophene/pentacene analogy. X-ray crystallography further revealed short intermolecular contact distances within LUMO-rich regions. These findings collectively position 7a–d as promising candidates for electronic applications. Future research will focus on derivatizing and polymerizing the IDT structure, alongside constructing devices to evaluate their performance as organic semiconductors.
