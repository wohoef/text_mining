Pancreatic cancer presents a grim prognosis, largely attributed to the limited advancements in its diagnosis and treatment over recent decades. This study aimed to explore the role of microRNA 138 (miR-138-5p) in regulating pancreatic cancer cell growth and to evaluate its potential as a therapeutic target. To achieve this, quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) was employed to measure miR-138-5p expression in eight pancreatic cancer cell lines and eighteen primary human pancreatic cancer samples. Exogenous over-expression of miR-138-5p was induced in the pancreatic cancer cell lines Capan-2 and PANC-1 using a lentiviral vector containing miR-138-5p mimics (lv-miR-138-5p). The impact of this over-expression on cell proliferation was subsequently assessed through an in vitro propidium iodide fluorescence assay. Furthermore, Capan-2 cells engineered to over-express miR-138-5p were transplanted into nude mice to investigate its in vivo effects on tumor growth. A predicted target of miR-138-5p, FOXC1, was initially validated using a luciferase assay. Following validation, FOXC1 was down-regulated using small interfering RNA (siRNA) to determine its effect on pancreatic cancer cell growth. The results revealed a significant down-regulation of miR-138-5p in both pancreatic cancer cell lines and primary human pancreatic cancer samples when compared to a human pancreas ductal epithelial (HPDE) cell line and normal pancreatic tissues, respectively (P < 0.05). In the Capan-2 and PANC-1 cell lines, lentiviral transfection successfully mimicked the up-regulation of endogenous miR-138-5p expression, which concurrently inhibited cancer cell proliferation (P < 0.05). Exogenous over-expression of miR-138-5p also demonstrated a significant inhibition of tumor formation in vivo. Through a luciferase assay, it was confirmed that miR-138-5p directly targets FOXC1. Consistent with this finding, FOXC1 expression was found to be down-regulated following miR-138-5p over-expression in pancreatic cancer cells. Finally, silencing of FOXC1 by siRNA was observed to have an inhibitory effect on pancreatic cancer cell growth. In conclusion, these findings suggest that miR-138-5p plays a crucial role in regulating pancreatic cancer cell growth, potentially by targeting FOXC1. The over-expression of miR-138-5p presents a promising novel therapeutic strategy for patients diagnosed with pancreatic cancer.

Pancreatic cancer is a leading cause of cancer mortality, ranking seventh in China and fourth in the United States. Annually, it claims over a quarter million lives in the U.S. alone. The dismal prognosis associated with pancreatic cancer stems from decades of limited progress in its diagnosis and treatment. Consequently, there is a critical need to identify and develop novel approaches for detecting and managing this disease.
MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNA molecules, typically 19-23 nucleotides in length. They exert their function by regulating endogenous gene expression through the degradation or translational inhibition of target messenger RNAs (mRNAs). Over recent years, research has demonstrated that miRNAs play a role in a variety of biological processes, such as embryonic development, neural differentiation, and programmed cell death (apoptosis). Crucially, an increasing amount of evidence suggests a direct link between altered miRNA expression levels and the development of cancer. In the context of pancreatic cancer, several miRNAs have been identified as being differentially expressed in tumor tissues when compared to adjacent normal tissues. For instance, microRNA-21 has been observed to be up-regulated in primary pancreatic cancers and to influence the apoptosis of pancreatic cancer cells, potentially serving as a biomarker. MicroRNA-34 is another example, known for its significant role in regulating the growth of pancreatic cancer stem cells.
The role of miR-138 in cancer growth and apoptosis has been established in various malignancies, such as lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and leukemia. Furthermore, a recent investigation indicated that increasing miR-138 levels in the PANC-1 pancreatic cancer cell line correlated with reduced focal adhesion kinase (FAK) expression. However, a definitive understanding of miR-138's direct involvement in pancreatic cancer development was still lacking.
FOXC1, a member of the forkhead box (FOX) transcription factor family, was initially identified in relation to Axenfeld-Rieger syndrome. Beyond its role in embryonic development, FOXC1 has been recognized as a biomarker in breast cancer. Research indicates that elevated FOXC1 levels can enhance cancer cell invasion and correlate with a poorer prognosis for cancer patients. While a high expression of FOXC1 is strongly linked to the development of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas, its precise function in pancreatic cancer progression remains largely uncharacterized.
Our study revealed that miR-138-5p, a prevalent human miR-138 isoform, is significantly downregulated in both primary pancreatic cancers and their derived cell lines. Furthermore, we observed that introducing miR-138-5p exogenously suppressed pancreatic cancer cell growth in vitro and in vivo. This inhibitory effect was mirrored by siRNA-mediated silencing of FOXC1, identified as a direct target of miR-138-5p.
Pancreatic cancer cell lines, including AsPC-1, BxPc-3, Capan-1, Capan-2, CFPAC-1, PANC-1, MIA PaCa-2, and SW1990, were acquired from the American Type Culture Collection. These cells were maintained in CS-C medium with 10% fetal bovine serum. The HPDE human pancreas ductal epithelial cell line was provided by Dr. M.S. Tsao and cultured as previously described. Additionally, primary human normal pancreatic epithelial cells were obtained from Cell System and cultured according to manufacturer instructions in CS-C medium with 10% FBS.
Between January 2013 and March 2014, primary pancreatic tissue samples were collected from 18 patients undergoing pancreatic resection at the Affiliated Hospital of Guiyang Medical College, China. This group comprised 10 males and 8 females, with an average age of 58.5 years (ranging from 34 to 67 years). All patients were diagnosed with stage IV pancreatic carcinoma, confirmed by biopsy or radiography. Informed consent was obtained from all participants. Following patient demise, autopsies were conducted promptly (<8 hours). Primary pancreatic carcinomas and adjacent normal tissues were rapidly harvested. A portion of each sample was preserved in OCT compound and stored at -80°C. Only cancer tissues definitively diagnosed as ductal adenocarcinoma were utilized. The study protocols received approval from the Ethics Committees of the Affiliated Hospital of Guiyang Medical College and the Affiliated Tongji Hospital.
To analyze RNA and miRNA fractions, Trizol reagent was used for isolation from both tissues and cell lines, following the manufacturer's protocol. RNA concentrations were then determined using a NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer, measuring absorbance at 260 and 280 nm, and further assessed with an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Quantitative real-time reverse transcription-PCR (qRT-PCR) was conducted using a TaqMan miRNA Assay. Amplification involved 40 cycles, each consisting of 15 seconds at 95 °C and 1 minute at 60 °C. Expression levels of miR-138-5p and FOXC1 were normalized against the housekeeping genes U6 and GAPDH, respectively.
To facilitate the exogenous over-expression of miR-138-5p, synthetic oligonucleotide mimics and their non-specific controls were obtained from Ribobio. These sequences were subsequently amplified and integrated into pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-coGFP constructs, resulting in the creation of a lentiviral vector for miR-138-5p mimics, designated lv-miR-138-5p, and a corresponding non-specific control vector, lv-control. The production of lentiviral particles involved a multi-step process. Initially, HEK-293 T cells were seeded in 10 cm tissue culture dishes at a density of 7 × 105 cells per dish with 5 mL of medium. Following overnight incubation at 37 °C with 5% CO2, transfection cocktails were added to the cells for 12–15 hours. After this period, the culture media were replaced with fresh media to remove the transfection reagents. On the fourth day, the culture supernatants, containing the lentiviral particles, were collected via centrifugation, transferred to storage tubes, and kept at 4 °C. The titers of these lentiviral particles were then precisely determined using a Lenti-X qPCR titration kit. Finally, pancreatic cancer cells were transfected with these lentiviruses, utilizing Lipofectamine 2000 according to the manufacturer's protocol.
To assess the impact of miR-138-5p on pancreatic cancer cell growth, cells were first transfected with either lv-miR-138-5p or a control lentivirus. Following a 24-hour incubation period, viable cells were transferred to 24-well plates at a density of 2 × 10^4 cells per well and cultured for an additional 72 hours. Cell proliferation was then quantified by measuring propidium iodide fluorescence intensity at 24, 48, 72, and 96 hours post-seeding, using a CytoFluor II multiwell plate reader. Additionally, a colony formation assay was conducted. In this assay, 1,000 cells per well were seeded in 6-well plates and incubated until visible clones formed, typically around 48 hours. These clones were then fixed in methanol, stained with hematoxylin, and counted under a light microscope. All experiments were performed in triplicate.
To assess the in vivo impact of miR-138-5p, Capan-2 cells were first transfected with either lv-miR-138-5p mimic or a control vector for 24 hours. Subsequently, these cells were collected and subcutaneously injected into 6-week-old female athymic nude mice. Tumor volumes were then calculated using the formula (length × width2)/2. All animal procedures adhered to protocols approved by the relevant Animal Committees.
To investigate the interaction between miR-138-5p and FOXC1, constructs containing the wild-type and a mutated 3'-untranslated region (UTR) of FOXC1 were generated. These constructs, named Luc-FOXC1 and Luc-FOXC1-mu respectively, were created by amplifying the respective FOXC1 3'-UTRs from Capan-2 cell cDNA and inserting them into a luciferase reporter vector. DNA sequencing confirmed the accuracy of these constructs. Subsequently, HEK-293 cells were co-transfected with either the control pmiR-REPORT vector, Luc-FOXC1, or Luc-FOXC1-mu, along with a β-galactosidase vector and the Lv-miR-138-5p vector. Following a 24-hour incubation period, luciferase activity was measured using a luciferase reporter assay system. The obtained signals were then normalized against the activity of the β-galactosidase control vector. These experiments were conducted in triplicate.
To investigate the effect of FOXC1 knockdown, Capan-2 and PANC-1 cells were transfected with either a FOXC1-specific siRNA (si-FOXC1) or a non-specific control siRNA (si-NC) using Lipofectamine 2000. After 48 hours of transfection, cell proliferation was assessed. The effectiveness of the siRNA in reducing FOXC1 expression was confirmed by qRT-PCR at the same time point.
To assess FOXC1 protein levels, cell lysates were prepared. Protein concentrations were quantified using a BCA kit. Subsequently, 20 μg of protein underwent SDS-PAGE and transfer to a nitrocellulose membrane. Incubation with an anti-FOXC1 antibody, followed by a horseradish peroxidase-ECL method, allowed for the measurement of FOXC1 expression. GAPDH served as the internal control.
Mouse xenograft sections were deparaffinized. These sections were then incubated with primary antibodies for FOXC1 and human Ki67. Protein visualization was achieved using NexES automated stainers and the I-View Detection Chemistry system.
Tumor volumes in vivo were analyzed with one-way ANOVA. Other data, presented as mean ± SD, were evaluated using a Student's t-test. Significance was determined at P < 0.05. All experiments were conducted at least in triplicate.
The miR-138 family includes miR-138-5p, which has been implicated in various cancers. Another isoform, miR-138-1-3p, shows no significant biological function in humans. Consequently, this study concentrated on miR-138-5p.
To investigate the role of miR-138-5p in pancreatic cancer, its expression levels were first examined. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was employed to analyze miR-138-5p expression in nine pancreatic cancer cell lines, alongside a normal human pancreas ductal epithelial cell line (HPDE) and primary normal pancreatic epithelial cells. This analysis revealed a significant downregulation of miR-138-5p in all tested pancreatic cancer cell lines when compared to the HPDE and normal primary cells (P < 0.05). Further investigation extended to clinical samples. qRT-PCR was used to evaluate miR-138-5p expression in 18 primary pancreatic cancer tissues and their corresponding adjacent normal pancreatic tissues. Consistent with the cell line findings, miR-138-5p expression was found to be significantly lower in the pancreatic cancer tissues compared to the non-cancerous tissues (P < 0.05). These results, presented in Figure 1, indicate a general downregulation of miR-138-5p in pancreatic cancer at both the cellular and clinical levels. The experiments were conducted in triplicate, and data are shown as mean ± standard deviation.
To investigate the functional impact of miR-138-5p in pancreatic cancer, we employed a lentiviral vector engineered to exogenously over-express miR-138-5p, designated as lv-miR-138-5p. This approach was applied to two pancreatic cancer cell lines: Capan-2 and PANC-1. Initially, we confirmed the successful transfection and subsequent up-regulation of miR-138-5p in both cell lines (Fig. 2a, P < 0.05). Following this, we conducted cell proliferation assays on these transfected cells. The data revealed a significant inhibition of proliferation in both Capan-2 and PANC-1 cells when treated with the lv-miR-138-5p lentivirus. This inhibitory effect was observed consistently from 48 hours to 96 hours post-transfection (Fig. 2b, P < 0.05). These findings demonstrate that increasing miR-138-5p levels effectively suppresses the growth of pancreatic cancer cells.
To investigate the in vivo impact of elevated miR-138-5p levels on pancreatic cancer, Capan-2 cells were first treated with either a miR-138-5p mimic lentivirus (lv-miR-138-5p) or a control lentivirus (lv-control) for 24 hours. Following this transfection, the cells were harvested and injected subcutaneously into female athymic nude mice. The subsequent analysis revealed a significant suppression of Capan-2 tumor growth in the mice that received the miR-138-5p over-expressing cells. This inhibitory effect on tumor proliferation was further substantiated by immunostaining for Ki-67 on tissue sections derived from the mouse xenografts. These findings collectively demonstrate that exogenous miR-138-5p expression effectively curtails pancreatic cancer growth within a living organism.
To elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which miR-138-5p influences pancreatic cancer cell growth, our investigation focused on identifying its downstream targets. Previous research had established that FOXC1, a transcription factor belonging to the forkhead box (FOX) family, is frequently overexpressed in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas, with a high expression level noted in 85 patients. Leveraging bioinformatic tools such as TargetScan, Pictar, and miRANDA, we predicted that FOXC1 could be a direct target of miR-138-5p. Further substantiating this prediction, our in vivo transplantation experiments revealed a significant downregulation of FOXC1 expression in tumor samples from mice that had received miR-138-5p overexpressing Capan-2 cells. This observation, coupled with the prior knowledge of FOXC1's elevated expression in pancreatic cancer, led us to hypothesize that FOXC1 plays a role in miR-138-5p-mediated regulation of pancreatic cancer cell proliferation. To rigorously test this hypothesis, we employed a luciferase assay. This assay confirmed that miR-138-5p directly interacts with and targets FOXC1. Our subsequent qRT-PCR analyses demonstrated that FOXC1 expression is indeed upregulated in both pancreatic cancer cell lines, specifically Capan-2 and PANC-1, and in primary pancreatic cancer tissues when compared to their normal counterparts (HPDE cells and nonmalignant clinical tissues, respectively). These findings collectively indicate that the downregulation of miR-138-5p in pancreatic cancer may lead to the upregulation of its target, FOXC1, thereby potentially promoting tumor growth.
To investigate the functional impact of FOXC1 in pancreatic cancer, we utilized siRNA to reduce its expression in cancer cells. Capan-2 and PANC-1 cells were treated with either 100 nM of FOXC1-specific siRNA (si-FOXC1) or a non-specific control siRNA (si-NC) for 24 hours. Western blotting confirmed the efficacy of the transfection, demonstrating a significant decrease in FOXC1 protein levels in both cell lines following si-FOXC1 treatment (Fig. 5a). Furthermore, we observed that the consequences of FOXC1 knockdown mirrored those of miR-138-5p upregulation. Specifically, cell proliferation was substantially inhibited in both Capan-2 and PANC-1 cells, as evidenced by colony formation assays (Fig. 5b-c, P < 0.05). These findings suggest a shared inhibitory role in pancreatic cancer cell growth between miR-138-5p and the downregulation of FOXC1.
Recent comprehensive analyses of pancreatic cancer genomes, transcriptomes, and methylomes have detailed its mutational landscape in over 400 patients. Our current research establishes, for the first time, the functional role of the epigenetic modifier miR-138-5p in controlling pancreatic cancer cell growth. We observed that miR-138-5p is typically underexpressed in both pancreatic cancer cell lines and patient samples. Consequently, artificially increasing miR-138-5p levels effectively suppressed pancreatic cancer cell proliferation in both laboratory and animal models. These findings align with prior research demonstrating miR-138-5p's ability to inhibit the growth of other cancer types and enhance their sensitivity to chemotherapy. The observed downregulation of miR-138-5p in pancreatic cancer tissues, coupled with the inhibitory effect of its forced overexpression on cancer cell proliferation, suggests that its tumor-suppressive mechanism in pancreatic cancer mirrors its function in other malignancies.
Our research further demonstrates that FOXC1 is a direct target of miR-138-5p within pancreatic cancer. We observed a significant decrease in FOXC1 expression, both in vitro and in vivo, following the exogenous overexpression of miR-138-5p. Importantly, silencing FOXC1 using siRNA produced comparable results to miR-138-5p overexpression, leading to a reduction in pancreatic cancer cell proliferation. While FOXC1's role in breast cancer is complex, with studies showing both inhibitory and enhancing proliferative effects upon its upregulation, its significance in pancreatic cancer is clearer. Previous research has linked high FOXC1 expression levels to poor clinical outcomes in patients with ductal adenocarcinomas. Our findings, which show elevated FOXC1 expression in pancreatic cancer cell lines and primary tissues, coupled with the observation that its downregulation inhibits cancer cell growth, strongly suggest that FOXC1 functions as a proliferative factor in pancreatic cancer.
This study identified miR-138-5p as a novel regulatory microRNA in pancreatic cancer, revealing its role in controlling cancer cell proliferation. Upregulating this tumor suppressor may offer a new therapeutic strategy for pancreatic cancer patients.
