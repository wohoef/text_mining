The TGF-β superfamily, including TGF-β and Bone Morphogenetic Proteins (BMPs), plays a crucial role in various biological functions. In breast cancer, elevated TGF-β levels correlate with unfavorable prognoses, and suppressing TGF-β signaling can decrease metastasis. Conversely, BMP-7 has been shown to impede bone metastasis in breast cancer cells. This study utilized a 3D invasion assay to examine how BMP-7 influences TGF-β-induced invasion. Our findings indicate that BMP-7 specifically suppressed TGF-β-induced invasion in the metastatic breast cancer cell line MCF10CA1a within a spheroid invasion model, but not in its premalignant counterpart, MCF10AT. This inhibitory action was unique to BMP-7, as its close relative, BMP-6, had no impact on MCF10CA1a spheroid invasion. To understand the underlying mechanism, we investigated invasion-related genes. BMP-7 was found to reduce TGF-β-induced expression of integrin αvβ3 in spheroids. Furthermore, inhibiting integrins chemically or through β3 knockdown hindered TGF-β-induced invasion. Conversely, overexpressing integrin β3 reversed BMP-7's inhibitory effect on TGF-β-induced invasion. Therefore, BMP-7 likely exerts its anti-invasive effects by blocking TGF-β-induced integrin β3 expression.

Distant metastasis, rather than the primary tumor, is the main cause of mortality and morbidity in breast cancer patients, with bone metastasis being a particularly significant contributor. For cancer cells to metastasize, they must undergo a complex process: shedding epithelial traits, invading surrounding tissues to enter the bloodstream, surviving circulation, extravasating, and then proliferating in a new metastatic site. Consequently, invasion is a crucial stage in the metastatic cascade. Adhesion receptors, particularly the integrin family, are proteins vital to this invasive process. Integrins, composed of alpha and beta subunits, mediate cell adhesion to the extracellular matrix. Specific integrin combinations found in cancer cells promote their migration and invasion. Notably, integrin αvβ3 is highly expressed in breast cancer cells, especially those in bone metastases. Its presence encourages bone metastasis, while its inhibition reduces the cells' tendency to spread to bone.
TGF-β plays a complex role in cancer development. While it can inhibit growth and induce cell death in early stages, it actively promotes invasion and metastasis in advanced cancers. This pro-cancerous function is supported by its frequent overexpression in breast cancer, which is linked to worse outcomes and increased metastasis. Research in mouse models further confirms that blocking TGF-β signaling in breast cancer cells can decrease metastatic spread.
TGF-β and BMPs utilize similar signaling pathways. The process begins when a ligand binds to a type II receptor, which then phosphorylates a type I receptor. This activated type I receptor subsequently phosphorylates a receptor-regulated Smad (R-Smad). These phosphorylated R-Smads then combine with the common mediator Smad4 to form heteromeric complexes. These complexes move into the nucleus, where they work with other transcription factors to control the expression of target genes. Specifically, TGF-β typically signals through the TGF-β receptor type II (TβRII) and activin receptor-like kinase 5 (ALK5), leading to the phosphorylation of Smad2 and Smad3. In contrast, BMPs signal through their specific BMP receptor type II (BMPRII) and BMP type I receptors (BMPRI), which activate Smad1, Smad5, and Smad8. Beyond these Smad pathways, receptor activation also triggers other non-Smad signaling cascades, such as MAPK pathways.
TGF-β promotes invasion by triggering epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), which gives carcinoma cells a more mobile, mesenchymal form. BMP-7 has been observed to counteract TGF-β-induced EMT in normal breast and kidney cells, and also inhibits EMT and bone metastasis in breast and prostate tumors. However, BMP-7's specific impact on tumor invasion remains unexplored.
Our research utilized a spheroid invasion model, previously established, to investigate TGF-β-induced invasion. This model employs a series of MCF10A cell lines, which originate from the spontaneously immortalized breast cell line, MCF10A1. Transformation of MCF10A1 with oncogenic RAS produced MCF10AT (M-II), a cell line that forms premalignant lesions in mice, progressing to carcinoma upon grafting. Subsequently, MCF10CA1a (M-IV), a lung-metastatic cell line, was isolated from these carcinomas. Our prior work with this assay demonstrated the crucial role of Smad signaling in TGF-β-induced invasion. In the present study, we focused on BMP-7, revealing its ability to inhibit TGF-β-induced invasion by suppressing TGF-β-induced integrin β3 expression.
Dr. Fred Miller (Barbara Ann Karmanos Cancer Institute, Detroit, USA) provided and maintained MCF10AT (M-II) and MCF10CA1a (M-IV) cells as previously described.
To assess invasion, a spheroid assay was conducted following established methods [25]. Initially, 103 cells were cultured to form spheroids. These spheroids were then placed into a 1:1 mixture of collagen and methocel, which was subsequently layered onto a collagen-coated plate. Various agents, including recombinant human TGF-β3, BMP-6, BMP-7, an isotype control antibody, or a human BMP-7 neutralizing antibody, were directly incorporated into this collagen:methocel mixture at 4°C. Following a 30-minute incubation at 37°C, a medium containing either no solvent, DMSO, or the integrin inhibitor GLPG0187 was added. Over the subsequent two days, invasion was observed and quantified by measuring the area using ImageJ or Adobe Photoshop software.
For protein analysis, cells were initially cultured on a six-well plate, with 2 x 10^5 cells per well. Following a 24-hour incubation, they underwent a 16-hour starvation period in a specific medium containing 2.5% horse serum, 10 ng/ml EGF, 50 ng/ml cholera toxin, 0.25 μg/ml hydrocortisone, 5 μg/ml insulin, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 50 μg/ml streptomycin. After starvation, cells were stimulated, washed, and then lysed using either RIPA buffer or sample buffer. The resulting lysates were analyzed via SDS-PAGE and Western blotting. Phosphorylated Smad1/5/8 was identified using an anti-phospho-Smad1 antibody, while phospho-Smad2 was detected with an antibody from Cell Signaling. Total Smad1 was detected using an antibody from Zymed.
To assess transcriptional activity, 1 x 10^5 cells were plated in 24-well plates. After 24 hours, cells underwent a 4-hour transfection with either CAGA12-Luc or BRE-Luc reporter constructs, utilizing Lipofectamine 2000 or LTX. A β-galactosidase expression plasmid was co-transfected to normalize for transfection efficiency. Following another 24-hour incubation, cells were serum-starved overnight in a specialized medium containing 2.5% horse serum, EGF, cholera toxin, hydrocortisone, insulin, penicillin, and streptomycin. Subsequent stimulation lasted 8 hours. Luciferase and β-galactosidase activities were then measured. All transfections were performed in triplicate.
Gene expression levels were quantified using Q-PCR, following established methods for RNA isolation and cDNA synthesis. The comparative ∆Ct method was employed, with acidic ribosomal phosphoprotein (ARP) serving as the reference gene and the non-stimulated condition normalized to 1. Each primer set, including those for matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP2), integrin αv, and integrin β3, was analyzed in duplicate. Results are presented as mean ± S.D.
To achieve integrin β3 knockdown, M-IV cells were transduced overnight with shRNA constructs (TRCN0000003234-TRCN0000003237) from the Mission library, using SHC001 as a negative control. Polybrene (4 μg/ml) facilitated transduction. Post-recovery, cells were selected and maintained in puromycin (0.5 μg/ml). For overexpression, M-IV cells received a lentiviral integrin β3 expression construct from Dr. Deborah Novack.
Statistical significance was determined using one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni's multiple comparison test, where p < 0.05 indicated significance. All results are presented as mean ± S.D.
The study aimed to determine if BMP-7 could inhibit TGF-β-induced cellular invasion. Previous research indicated that BMP-7 counteracts TGF-β-induced epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in both healthy breast cells and aggressive, bone-seeking breast and prostate cancer cells, a process linked to reduced bone metastasis. To investigate its effect on invasion, BMP-7 was tested on M-IV, a metastatic cell line, using a spheroid invasion assay. For comparison, the M-II cell line, a RAS-transformed precursor, was also included, as RAS is known to amplify TGF-β responses. In the absence of TGF-β, BMP-7 did not affect the invasion of either M-II or M-IV cells. However, when TGF-β was present, it stimulated invasion in both cell types. Crucially, BMP-7 significantly suppressed TGF-β-induced invasion in the metastatic M-IV cells, but had no such effect on the premalignant M-II cells. This finding suggests that BMP-7 specifically targets the pro-invasive mechanisms activated by TGF-β in metastatic cells. These observations are illustrated in Figure 1a-d, which includes representative spheroid images and quantified invasion data for both M-II and M-IV cells under various treatment conditions (BMP-7, TGF-β, or both). The results, representing the mean ± S.D. from at least three spheroids per condition, were consistent across independent experiments, with statistical significance noted at p < 0.001.
To confirm that BMP-7's impact on TGF-β-induced invasion wasn't related to cell growth, proliferation assays were conducted. While TGF-β showed a slight growth-inhibitory effect on M-IV cells, BMP-7 alone had no influence on cell proliferation. Furthermore, BMP-7 did not alter the growth inhibition caused by TGF-β. Therefore, BMP-7's inhibition of TGF-β-induced invasion is independent of any effects on cell growth.
The inhibitory effect of BMP-7 on TGF-β-induced invasion appears to be specific. We investigated BMP-6, the closest homolog to BMP-7 with 73% amino acid identity, to see if other BMPs could also inhibit this invasion. Unlike BMP-7, BMP-6 did not reduce TGF-β-induced invasion in M-IV cells, nor did it affect basal invasion (Fig. 1e, f).
Our prior research indicated that the basal invasion of M-IV cells relies on TGF-β signaling [25]. Interestingly, when BMP-7 was introduced, we didn't observe any inhibitory effect on this basal invasion (Fig. 1c, d). This lack of inhibition could be attributed to the possibility that BMP-7 was already present at sufficient concentrations. Supporting this, our findings revealed that M-IV cells produce BMP-7 mRNA, and the horse serum used in our experiments also contained BMP-7 protein (data not shown). To investigate this further, we employed Noggin, a known natural antagonist of BMP-2, 4, and 7. When Noggin was applied, it significantly increased basal invasion in M-IV cells by 2.0-fold (Fig. 2a, b). In contrast, Noggin had no impact on basal invasion in the M-II cell line (Supplementary Figure 2), where TGF-β-induced invasion is not influenced by BMP-7. These results suggest that blocking BMP-2, 4, and 7 promotes invasion specifically in M-IV cells, but not in M-II cells. This implies a role for endogenous BMPs, particularly BMP-7, in regulating basal invasion in metastatic breast cancer cells.
To confirm that Noggin's effect on M-IV cell invasion was primarily due to BMP-7 inhibition, we used BMP-7 neutralizing antibodies. These antibodies increased basal invasion by 1.8-fold compared to controls. This finding suggests that BMP-7 blockade is the main reason for Noggin's invasion-promoting effect. Furthermore, it implies a fundamental antagonism between TGF-β and BMP-7 even under normal conditions.
To determine if the differing effects of BMP-6 and BMP-7 on TGF-β-induced invasion in M-IV cells stemmed from variations in their potency to induce BMP-signaling, we conducted a comparative analysis of BMP-Smad signaling activation. Our investigation employed two distinct methods. Firstly, we assessed whether BMP-6 and BMP-7 exhibited different capacities for inducing Smad1 phosphorylation. Western blot analysis revealed that both BMPs stimulated Smad1 phosphorylation to an equivalent degree within M-IV cells. Importantly, the overall levels of Smad1 remained unchanged during this process (as illustrated in Fig. 3a). Secondly, we examined the transcriptional activation of BMP-Smads. This was achieved by utilizing a luciferase-reporter construct that is regulated by BMP Responsive Elements (BRE) [32]. The results showed that both BMP-6 and BMP-7 effectively activated this reporter, leading to an approximate 30-fold increase in its activity. This indicated that both BMPs efficiently induced BMP-Smad transcriptional activity in M-IV cells (Fig. 3b). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that BMP-6 and BMP-7 do not exhibit any discernible differences in their ability to induce Smad signaling within the M-IV cell line. This suggests that the observed variations in their impact on TGF-β-induced invasion are not attributable to differences in their primary Smad signaling activation capabilities. The experimental setup involved treating cells with BMP-6 and BMP-7 at 100 ng/ml, and TGF-β at 5 ng/ml, for specific durations before analysis.
To understand how BMP-7 blocks TGF-β-induced invasion, researchers examined its impact on TGF-β Smad signaling, comparing it with BMP-6. Their initial focus was on Smad2 phosphorylation. Neither BMP-7 nor BMP-6 demonstrated any inhibitory effect on TGF-β-induced Smad2 phosphorylation (Fig. 3a). The study then investigated BMP-7's influence on TGF-β-induced Smad3 transcriptional activity using a CAGA-luciferase reporter. While TGF-β significantly increased reporter activity (approximately 55-fold), neither BMP-7 nor BMP-6 had a notable effect on this induction (Fig. 3c). These findings collectively indicate that BMP-7 does not alter TGF-β-induced Smad signaling in M-IV cells.
To understand how BMP-7 influences TGF-β-induced invasion, we investigated its impact on specific target genes. Our prior research established matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP2) as crucial for TGF-β-driven invasion. Therefore, we initially assessed whether BMP-7 modulated TGF-β-induced MMP2 expression within the M-IV spheroid model. BMP-6 was included as a control. Interestingly, neither BMP-7 nor BMP-6 altered TGF-β-induced MMP2 expression in M-IV spheroids embedded in collagen (Fig. 4a). Next, we focused on integrin αvβ3, given its known role in promoting TGF-β-induced epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and metastasis inhibition when targeted. We analyzed the expression of both integrin αv and β3 subunits. Our findings revealed that TGF-β significantly upregulated the expression of both αv and β3 in collagen-embedded spheroids. Crucially, BMP-7, but not BMP-6, effectively suppressed this TGF-β-induced expression of both integrin αv and β3 (Fig. 4b and c). This inhibitory effect of BMP-7 on TGF-β-induced integrin αv and β3 expression was specific to M-IV cells, as it was not observed in M-II cells (Supplementary Figure 3a, b). These results suggest that BMP-7's anti-invasive effect on TGF-β-induced collagen invasion in M-IV spheroids likely occurs by inhibiting TGF-β-induced integrin αvβ3 expression.
To assess the role of integrin αvβ3 in TGF-β-induced invasion, we utilized GLPG0187, a non-peptide integrin inhibitor. This compound demonstrates strong binding affinity for several αv integrins, including αvβ1, αvβ3, αvβ5, αvβ6, and αvβ8, as well as α5β1, as confirmed by in vitro competitive binding assays (Supplementary Table 1). When M-IV spheroids were treated with GLPG0187, a significant reduction in TGF-β-induced invasion was observed (Fig. 5a, b). These findings suggest that integrin αvβ3 may be essential for the TGF-β-driven invasion process in M-IV cells. This experimental approach helped to establish the importance of integrin αvβ3 in the context of TGF-β-induced cellular invasion within the spheroid model system.
To confirm integrin β3's role in TGF-β-induced invasion, we used RNAi to knock down its expression in M-IV cells. Multiple shRNA constructs successfully reduced TGF-β-induced integrin β3 mRNA levels. This reduction consequently inhibited TGF-β-induced invasion, suggesting that BMP-7's anti-invasive effect is partly due to its ability to downregulate integrin β3.
To confirm the importance of BMP-7's role in suppressing TGF-β-induced invasion via integrin β3 inhibition, researchers introduced a lentiviral vector designed to overexpress integrin β3. This genetic modification resulted in elevated integrin β3 protein levels in M-IV cells compared to their wildtype counterparts, as detailed in Supplementary Figure 4b. Interestingly, while this overexpression of integrin β3 did not independently influence TGF-β-induced invasion, it entirely negated the inhibitory impact of BMP-7. These findings, summarized in Figure 6, strongly indicate that BMP-7 exerts its anti-invasive effects by specifically reducing the expression of integrin β3, which is otherwise stimulated by TGF-β. The experiment involved transducing cells with the integrin β3 overexpression construct and then observing invasion in the presence of BMP-7, TGF-β, or both.
This study aimed to clarify how BMP-7 achieves its previously documented anti-metastatic effects. Our research focused on whether BMP-7 could counteract TGF-β-induced invasion in human breast cancer cells. We discovered that BMP-7 specifically hinders TGF-β-induced invasion in the metastatic M-IV cell line by suppressing integrin β3 expression. Notably, this inhibitory action was observed only in the metastatic cells, not in their premalignant M-II precursors. Furthermore, this effect was unique to BMP-7, as its close relative, BMP-6, did not impact TGF-β-induced invasion. This marks the first demonstration of BMP-7's ability to inhibit TGF-β-induced invasion.
The antagonistic relationship between TGF-β and BMP-7 is well-established, prompting an investigation into whether the observed inhibitory mechanism is broadly applicable across various cell types. While BMP-7 effectively reduces bone metastasis in MDA-MB231 breast cancer and PC3 prostate cancer cell lines, this inhibition is linked to a reduction in TGF-β/Smad signaling in those specific contexts. However, in our current study, BMP-7 did not show an inhibitory effect on the TGF-β-Smad pathway. Interestingly, BMP-7 also suppressed tumor progression in OCM-1 uveal melanoma cells without influencing the TGF-β-Smad pathway. These findings suggest that BMP-7 can counteract the pro-tumorigenic actions of TGF-β through both Smad-dependent and Smad-independent mechanisms, with the specific pathway depending on the cellular environment.
BMP-7's suppression of breast cancer invasion is partly attributed to its inhibition of TGF-β-induced integrin β3 expression. Given that TGF-β promotes integrin β3, which in turn boosts EMT via Src, it's plausible that BMP-7's action on integrin β3 also contributes to the known antagonism between TGF-β and BMP-7 in EMT processes.
Our findings indicate that BMP-7's inhibitory action on TGF-β-induced invasion is likely not through direct interference with TGF-β Smad signaling, despite the known dependence of this invasion on Smad3 and Smad4. While TGF-β-induced integrin β3 expression is reportedly p38-dependent, not Smad-dependent, we observed no significant impact of BMP-7 on p38 phosphorylation that would account for its inhibition of integrin β3 expression. Therefore, it is probable that BMP-7's effect is primarily mediated by its transcriptional targets, such as specific transcription factors or microRNAs, which then disrupt TGF-β-induced integrin β3 expression.
A notable observation is the differential effect of BMP-7 and BMP-6 on TGF-β-induced invasion, with only BMP-7 demonstrating inhibitory action. This is surprising given their substantial 73% amino acid homology and shared receptor binding capabilities. While BMP-6 is generally considered more potent than BMP-7 in promoting bone differentiation, BMP-7's activity is susceptible to inhibition by Noggin, unlike BMP-6, which possesses a resistance conferred by an extra lysine at residue 60. Despite these differences, both BMP-6 and BMP-7 were found to induce Smad1 phosphorylation and BMP-Smad transcriptional activity to a comparable degree in MIV cells. Although it's possible they affect non-Smad signaling pathways differently, no significant variations were observed in their impact on p38 and ERK phosphorylation in MIV cells.
The inability of BMP-7 to suppress TGF-β-induced invasion in the premalignant M-II cell line, unlike its effect on metastatic M-IV cells, suggests a link to tumor progression. This differential response mirrors observations where a dominant-negative TGF-β-receptor had varied effects on these cell lines in vivo. It is plausible that as tumors advance, the cellular response to TGF-β changes, enabling BMP-7 to exert its inhibitory influence. Genetic analysis of the MCF10A cell lines supports this, revealing that M-IV possesses an extra copy of chromosome 1's long arm—a common breast cancer alteration—along with other genetic and gene expression differences compared to M-II. These accumulated genetic changes likely underpin BMP-7's specific anti-invasive action.
BMP-7 hinders TGF-β-induced invasion by suppressing integrin β3 expression. This finding supports targeting integrin β3 or utilizing BMP-7 as strategies against breast cancer metastasis.
