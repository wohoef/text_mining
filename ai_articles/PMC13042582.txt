This study aimed to determine the prognostic significance of immunohistochemically detected p53 in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) patients undergoing adjuvant chemotherapy. While p53 gene mutations are the most frequent genomic changes in TNBC, occurring in 60–88% of cases, their clinical utility in breast cancer management remains unclear. We analyzed clinicopathologic data from 1088 TNBC patients who had curative surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. P53 positivity was defined by nuclear staining exceeding 10% via immunohistochemistry. Among the 1088 patients, 709 (65.2%) presented without lymph node metastasis (N0). Within this N0 group, 408 (57.5%) were p53-positive (p53+) and 301 (42.5%) were p53-negative (p53-). P53+ tumors in N0 patients demonstrated a trend towards improved breast cancer-specific survival (BCSS, p = 0.052) and overall survival (OS, p = 0.079) compared to p53- tumors. Multivariate analysis confirmed significantly better BCSS for p53+ tumors in N0 patients (HR 2.8, 95% CI: 1.1–7.3, p = 0.034). Conversely, no such correlation between p53 status, clinicopathologic features, and survival was observed in TNBC patients with lymph node metastasis. Therefore, immunohistochemical p53 expression was linked to better BCSS specifically in N0 TNBC patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy.

Approximately 15% of all breast cancers are Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC), characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2 expression. This aggressive subtype exhibits a rapid clinical progression, early recurrence, and a poor survival outlook.
TNBC patients face poor prognoses, partly due to limited treatment options beyond conventional and adjuvant chemotherapy. Despite extensive research, no effective targeted therapies currently exist for TNBC. While early preclinical and phase I/II trials of agents like PARP or EGFR inhibitors showed promise, subsequent clinical trials ultimately failed to demonstrate significant efficacy.
TP53, a known tumor suppressor and cell cycle regulator, frequently undergoes mutations in TNBC. These genomic alterations are observed in 60-88% of TNBC or basal-like breast cancers, significantly higher than the 13-26% seen in luminal breast cancers. Despite this high incidence, TP53 mutations have not been effectively utilized as prognostic indicators in breast cancer management.
The precise correspondence between p53 levels detected by immunohistochemistry (IHC) and the presence of mutant or wild-type TP53 remains unclear. While IHC is the prevailing method for assessing p53 status in clinical settings, with extensive published data, studies in breast cancer have shown less than a 75% correlation between IHC signals for p53 and sequencing-confirmed mutant TP53. Consequently, this study aimed to explore the prognostic significance of IHC-detected p53 in TNBC patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy.
This study included 1088 TNBC patients diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma at Samsung Medical Center from January 2003 to September 2013. Patients with bilateral breast cancer, distant metastasis at initial assessment, or those who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy were excluded. All selected patients underwent curative surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy.
Patient data reviewed included age, menopausal status, surgical procedure, and TNM stage (7th Edition AJCC). We also examined molecular biomarker expression for ER, PR, HER2, Ki-67, and p53.
Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) was identified by immunohistochemistry as negative for ER, PR, and HER2. ER and PR negativity was determined by an Allred score (AS) of ≤ 2. For HER2, a score of 0 or +1 indicated negativity; however, equivocal cases (+2) necessitated further testing with SISH or FISH. Additionally, a Ki67 labeling index cutoff of 14.0% was applied, following the St. Gallen 2011 consensus.
Board-certified pathologists diagnosed p53 status, defining nuclear staining exceeding 10% as "positive." This evaluation utilized a 1:4000 dilution of a mouse monoclonal anti-human p53 antibody (clone: BP53.12, Invitrogen/MD21704USA). Staining was conducted on an autoimmunostainer (Leica Bond Polymer Refine detection kit/Leica Bond-Max system) following manufacturer guidelines.
Post-surgery, patient treatment regimens varied. A group of 588 individuals underwent six cycles of FAC, comprising 5-fluorouracil (500 mg/m2), doxorubicin (50 mg/m2), and cyclophosphamide (500 mg/m2) administered on day 1, every 21 days. Another 358 patients received four cycles of AC (doxorubicin 60 mg/m2, cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 on day 1, every 21 days), subsequently followed by four cycles of docetaxel (either 75 mg/m2 or 60 mg/m2). For 109 patients, the treatment involved four cycles of AC (doxorubicin 60 mg/m2, cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 on day 1, every 21 days). Finally, 33 patients received six cycles of CMF, which included oral cyclophosphamide (100 mg/m2) on days 1–14, and methotrexate (40 mg/m2) and fluorouracil (600 mg/m2) on days 1 and 8, repeated every 28 days.
For statistical analysis, IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0 was utilized, with a p-value of less than 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Clinicopathologic variable frequencies were compared using Fisher's exact and chi-square tests. Survival curves were generated via the Kaplan–Meier method. Definitions for survival endpoints included Disease-Free Survival (DFS), measured from surgery to relapse (locoregional or distant metastasis), and Overall Survival (OS), from surgery to death. Breast Cancer-Specific Survival (BCSS) was determined based on the underlying cause of death (ICD-10 code). Hazard ratios for DFS, OS, and BCSS were calculated through a multivariate Cox regression model.
Patients had a median follow-up of 57 months (4-140 months) and a median age of 48 years (21-78 years).
Among the 1088 TNBC patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy, 57.3% (623) exhibited p53-positive tumors, while 42.7% (465) were p53-negative. No significant differences were observed between p53+ and p53- tumors regarding median age, T stage, or N stage. However, p53+ patients demonstrated higher nuclear grade (86.5% vs. 81.6%, p=0.034) and Ki67 levels (96.9% vs. 92.8%, p=0.032) compared to p53- patients.
Using the Kaplan-Meier method, univariate analysis revealed no significant association between p53 expression and survival outcomes (DFS, OS, or BCSS) in TNBC patients. (Fig. 1).
For the 109 patients treated with four cycles of AC, p53 expression correlated with significantly improved DFS (p = 0.036), BCSS (p = 0.023), and OS (p = 0.023). Conversely, no significant link between p53 expression and survival was observed in patients receiving alternative treatment regimens.
Among the 1088 patients, 709 (65.2%) were N0, while 379 (34.8%) had lymph node metastasis. In N0 patients, p53+ tumors (408, 57.5%) exhibited higher Ki67 levels (96.6% vs. 92.8%, p = 0.004) compared to p53- tumors (301, 42.5%). However, Ki67 expression did not differ based on p53 status in TNBC patients with lymph node metastasis.
When examining p53 expression and survival based on lymph node metastasis, N0 patients with p53+ tumors exhibited a trend towards improved breast cancer-specific survival (BCSS, p = 0.052) and overall survival (OS, p = 0.079) compared to p53- tumors, as determined by Kaplan–Meier analysis. Multivariate analysis further revealed that p53 expression in N0 patients significantly correlated with better BCSS (HR 2.8, 95% CI: 1.1–7.4, p = 0.022). Conversely, in TNBC patients with lymph node metastasis, no correlation was found between p53 status, clinicopathologic features, and either BCSS or OS.
The prognostic importance of TP53 mutations, the most frequent genetic change in breast cancer, is still debated. Prior research on the link between p53 expression and patient outcomes has yielded conflicting findings. For instance, Biganzoli et al. [11] observed poorer overall and event-free survival in p53-expressing TNBC, though their study was limited by a small sample size (n=33), a high proportion of stage I cases, and a lack of chemotherapy details. Maeda et al. [12] identified a TNBC subtype characterized by negative CK5/6, negative androgen receptor (AR), and positive p53 as having the poorest prognosis, while AR-positive and p53-negative TNBC patients showed better outcomes. However, their study noted that only low AR expression correlated with advanced clinical stage, not CK5/6 or p53 expression. Conversely, Kashiwagi et al. [13] concluded that p53 status held no significant prognostic value, irrespective of chemotherapy. Such discrepancies likely stem from variations in patient populations, breast cancer subtypes, and treatment protocols.
Studies indicate p53 expression may be a positive prognostic indicator in early TNBC. For instance, Coates et al. observed a strong link between p53 expression and improved disease-free and overall survival in ER-negative, node-negative early breast cancer patients (including TNBC and HER2 subtypes). Additionally, Jin et al. reported that p53 expression correlated with reduced lymph node metastasis and enhanced patient survival.
This study investigated p53 expression's impact on TNBC patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy, controlling for treatment regimens in multivariate analysis. A significant link between p53 expression and improved BCSS was observed, but exclusively in early-stage, node-negative (N0) TNBC patients. No such association was found for DFS, BCSS, or OS in advanced TNBC cases with lymph node metastasis.
The role of p53 as a predictor of chemotherapy response has been investigated in various studies. One recent study involving 198 TNBC patients demonstrated an independent link between p53 expression and achieving a pathologic complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (odds ratio: 3.961; p = 0.003). Furthermore, a higher pCR rate (25.8%) was observed in patients with elevated p53 and Ki67 expression compared to those with lower levels (5.2%) [17].
While TP53 mutations have been linked to resistance to anthracycline-based chemotherapy in breast cancer, their impact on taxane-based treatments is less clear. A significant phase III trial indicated that TP53 status predicted overall survival but not a specific sensitivity to taxanes. Our findings align with this, showing improved breast cancer-specific survival with p53 expression in the AC treatment group, but not in those receiving taxane-based chemotherapy. Furthermore, p53 expression showed no correlation with disease-free survival, irrespective of the chemotherapy regimen used.
This study employed immunohistochemistry (IHC) to evaluate p53 expression. While our prior research indicated a strong link between TP53 mutation status and p53 protein expression [23], the precise reflection of mutant versus wild-type TP53 by IHC-quantified p53 remains unclear. Reports show a correlation of less than 75% between IHC signals for p53 and mutant TP53, verified by sequencing [8]. Despite this, most immunohistochemically detected proteins are presumed to be mutant p53, given that wild-type p53 degrades quickly, except under cellular stress, whereas mutant p53 protein is more resistant to degradation [24, 25]. Therefore, comprehensive, well-controlled studies are essential to establish the reciprocal relationship between IHC-detected p53 protein expression and TP53 mutation status.
For early-stage TNBC patients (N0) receiving adjuvant chemotherapy, p53-positive tumors demonstrated improved breast cancer-specific survival. This association was not observed in patients with lymph node metastasis. Therefore, p53 expression appears to be a favorable prognostic indicator in early TNBC, warranting further research into its predictive value for chemotherapy response.
