This study investigated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), a rare, chronic, and fibrosing interstitial lung disease, focusing on its clinical characteristics and patient outcomes in Taiwan, an area where such data are scarce. A key objective was to resolve the ongoing debate regarding survival differences between patients with IPF alone and those with combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE). Researchers retrospectively analyzed data from patients diagnosed with IPF between 2006 and 2016, adhering to the 2011 American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society guidelines for IPF definition. The study compared clinical features, comorbidities, and outcomes between patients with CPFE and those with IPF alone, also assessing the severity of emphysema and fibrosis. A total of 114 IPF patients were included, predominantly men (86.8%) with an average age of 77.8 years. The overall median survival for IPF patients was 3.33 years. Among these, 30 patients (26.3%) met the criteria for CPFE. The CPFE group exhibited distinct characteristics: a significantly higher smoking rate (90% vs. 50%), greater forced vital capacity (82% vs. 59%), and lower fibrosis scores (8.5 ± 2.9 vs. 10 ± 3.2) compared to the IPF-alone group. However, baseline room air saturation and the prevalence of pulmonary hypertension were comparable between the two groups. Crucially, the study found no significant difference in survival time between the CPFE and IPF-alone groups (median survival of 3.58 vs. 2.39 years, respectively). Multivariate analysis identified higher fibrosis scores, room air saturation below 90%, and lung cancer as significant predictors of mortality. The conclusion drawn was that emphysema did not significantly impact IPF patient survival; instead, outcomes were primarily influenced by baseline disease severity and other co-existing conditions.

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, and rare interstitial lung disease (ILD) of unknown cause, characterized by fibrosis. It represents the most prevalent and severe form of idiopathic interstitial pneumonia. Patients with IPF face an unpredictable and variable clinical progression, inevitably leading to hypoxic respiratory failure. Historically, no effective treatments existed, though two new medications have recently shown promise in slowing lung function decline and potentially extending survival. IPF is identifiable by specific radiological and histopathological patterns consistent with usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP). Median survival rates for IPF have been reported between 3.5 to 4.4 years in the United States, 3.7 years in Finland, and 2.9 to 3.8 years in Asia.
Despite Taiwan's population of 23.4 million, predominantly Chinese with cultural ties to China, data on Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF) are scarce. A single prior study by Lai et al., utilizing the Taiwan National Health Insurance database, reported an unusually brief median IPF survival of 0.7 to 0.9 years. This study, conducted between 1997 and 2007, included 789 IPF patients from a larger cohort of over 2.6 million individuals, representing 10% of the Taiwanese population.
Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) presents with unique clinical characteristics compared to isolated IPF. While emphysema is detected in 8% to 51% of IPF patients through HRCT, pulmonary fibrosis appears in 4.4% to 8% of emphysema patients. The impact of CPFE on patient survival remains a subject of debate. Earlier research, which often suggested a worse prognosis for CPFE than for IPF, suffered from inconsistent CPFE definitions, diverse patient groups, and insufficient consideration of confounding variables.
This study aimed to compare outcomes for IPF patients with and without emphysema, focusing primarily on survival time. Additionally, it sought to identify factors linked to IPF mortality.
A retrospective cohort study was conducted at a 1455-bed tertiary medical center in southern Taiwan. The study utilized existing medical records and radiological reports. Patients were identified from the hospital's electronic medical chart system by searching keywords such as "idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis," "IPF," "usual interstitial pneumonia," and "UIP." Data collection spanned from January 1, 2006, to December 31, 2016, focusing on individuals with HRCT findings indicative of UIP or suspected IPF. The Institutional Review Board approved the study, waiving informed consent (VGHKS16-CT12-03).
The 2011 American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society guideline established the definition for Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF). This diagnosis can be made without a surgical lung biopsy if high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) shows a definite usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern, characterized by subpleural and basal reticular opacities and honeycombing, and no inconsistent features, provided other interstitial lung disease causes like environmental exposure, connective tissue disease, or drug toxicity are absent. Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) is diagnosed when IPF and pulmonary emphysema are both evident on HRCT.
Dr. Chiu-Fan Chen visually assessed the degree of pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema using scoring systems. Fibrosis was identified by reticular opacity and honeycombing. Emphysema appeared as distinct regions of reduced attenuation, relative to healthy lung tissue, often with absent or very thin walls, and sometimes with multiple bullae.
To assess the extent of emphysema and fibrosis, the lungs were segmented into six distinct zones: upper, middle, and lower regions for each lung. Each of these zones was individually analyzed. The upper zone encompassed the area superior to the tracheal carina, while the lower zone extended below the inferior pulmonary vein. The middle zone occupied the space between these two defined regions. A semiquantitative scoring system was employed to quantify the presence of emphysema and fibrosis within each zone. Scores ranged from 0 (indicating no presence) to 4 (representing ≥75% involvement), with intermediate scores for percentages like <5%, 5-24%, 25-49%, and 50-74%. The cumulative score for all six zones yielded the total emphysema score and total fibrosis score. A total emphysema score exceeding 3 was considered clinically significant. Pulmonary hypertension was identified on CT scans by a pulmonary trunk diameter of 29 mm or greater, measured perpendicularly at its bifurcation.
Researchers gathered baseline clinical information for each IPF patient, including demographics, smoking history, room air oxygen saturation levels, forced vital capacity (FVC), diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO), existing health conditions (comorbidities), and prescribed medications. The main goal of the study was to determine patient survival time, while secondary objectives focused on identifying factors linked to IPF mortality. Survival duration was measured from the initial HRCT diagnosis date until the final follow-up, with all medical records reviewed up to March 31, 2019. To ensure patient privacy, all identifying details like names and medical record numbers were replaced with unique patient codes. The file containing clinical and imaging data, as well as the separate file linking codes to original identifiers, were password-protected and accessible only to Chiu-Fan Chen and Ruay-Sheng Lai.
Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 20.0. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For continuous variables, including age, BMI, FVC, DLCO, total fibrosis score, total emphysema score, and pulmonary trunk size, their distribution was assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test and histograms. Variables exhibiting a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using independent t-tests. Conversely, non-normally distributed variables were reported as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), with comparisons made using Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were summarized as numbers (percentages) and analyzed using chi-squared tests, or Fisher's exact tests when expected cell counts were below 5. Survival analysis employed the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences in survival were evaluated using the log-rank test. Cox regression analysis identified prognostic factors for IPF patients. Variables with a p-value under 0.1 in the univariate analysis were subsequently included in the multivariate analysis.
Our analysis began with 213 patients. Of these, 125 exhibited definite Usual Interstitial Pneumonia (UIP), 22 showed possible UIP, and 66 had HRCT features inconsistent with UIP (refer to Fig. 1). From the definite UIP group, 11 patients were excluded due to associated connective tissue disease (CTD). The remaining 114 patients were diagnosed with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF), all displaying characteristic honeycombing and reticular opacities on HRCT. Notably, 30 (26.3%) of these IPF patients also met the criteria for combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE). The study population with IPF exhibited a notable male dominance, with 86.8% of patients being men. The average age at diagnosis was 77.8 ± 9.4 years, with a median of 80 years (IQR 74-84). The age range was broad, from 43 to 97 years, though only one patient was under 50. Smoking history was prevalent, reported by 60.5% of the patients. Pulmonary function tests revealed a median predicted FVC of 70.5% (IQR 50.5-84, n=78) and a median predicted DLCO of 35% (IQR 25.5-53.3, n=30). At diagnosis, 64.5% of patients had a room air resting oxygen saturation of 95% or higher, as measured by pulse oximetry. When comparing the CPFE and IPF-alone groups, significant differences were observed. The CPFE group had a substantially higher smoking rate (90% vs 50%, p < 0.001), a greater predicted FVC (82% vs 59%, p < 0.001), and a lower total fibrosis score (8.5 ± 2.9 vs 10 ± 3.2, p = 0.022). However, baseline room air saturation, prevalence of lung cancer, and pulmonary hypertension were comparable between these two groups. The most frequent comorbidities across all IPF patients were pulmonary hypertension, hypertension, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. No significant differences were found in medication use, including steroids and N-acetylcysteine, between the CPFE and IPF-alone cohorts.
Table 1 presents the survival outcomes for IPF patients. The overall median survival for all IPF patients was 3.33 years, with some individuals surviving as long as 14.3 years, as illustrated in Fig. 2A. When comparing the CPFE and IPF-alone groups, Fig. 2B shows no significant difference in survival, with median survival times of 3.58 and 2.39 years, respectively (log-rank test p = 0.163).
Table 2 details the analysis of prognostic factors. While initial univariate Cox regression identified PH, lung cancer, total fibrosis score, and room air saturation below 90% as significant mortality indicators for IPF patients, emphysema did not show a notable link to survival. When subjected to multivariate Cox regression, only total fibrosis score (HR = 1.140, p = 0.004), lung cancer (HR = 4.637, p = 0.001), and initial room air saturation under 90% (HR = 5.433, p < 0.001) remained significant predictors of mortality.
Our research found that Taiwanese IPF patients have a survival rate similar to those in Western and other Asian nations, contradicting a prior Taiwanese National Health Insurance (NHI) study that reported a much shorter survival. In our study, CPFE patients exhibited higher smoking rates, better forced vital capacity (FVC), and less fibrosis. Importantly, no significant difference in survival was observed between CPFE and IPF-alone groups. Pulmonary hypertension (PH), room air oxygen saturation, and lung cancer prevalence were also comparable between these two groups. Ultimately, a higher baseline CT fibrosis score, presence of lung cancer, and room air saturation below 90% were identified as independent predictors of IPF mortality.
Our study found a median survival of 3.33 years for IPF patients, aligning with the 3 to 4-year range observed in the United States, Finland, and other Asian nations. This contrasts sharply with a prior Taiwanese National Health Insurance (NHI) study by Lai et al., which reported a significantly shorter median survival of only 0.7 to 0.9 years. This substantial discrepancy is likely attributable to several factors in the NHI study. The NHI study's lower reported IPF prevalence and incidence rates compared to other countries, coupled with an implausible sixfold increase in incidence between 1997 and 2007, suggest widespread underdiagnosis. This underdiagnosis, particularly in the early stages of the study, could stem from inaccurate ICD-9 coding and physician misdiagnosis. The inclusion of data from local hospitals, which may lack sufficient experience in IPF diagnosis and coding, further contributes to this likelihood. Moreover, the NHI study's own Kaplan-Meier curves indicated a median survival closer to 4 years, directly contradicting its stated conclusion of 0.7 to 0.9 years. The lack of explanation for this inconsistency renders the NHI study's conclusion questionable.
Patients in this study with IPF exhibited several demographic differences compared to those in other international studies. The average age at diagnosis was 77.8 years, notably older than the 60.3-73.5 year range reported elsewhere, with nearly half (48%) of the patients diagnosed between 80 and 89 years old. A strong male predominance was observed, with 86.8% of patients being men, exceeding the 59-85.7% range found in other IPF studies. Furthermore, 60.5% of the patients had a history of smoking, a rate considerably higher than Taiwan's general adult smoking prevalence (35.6% for men, 4.3% for women between 2005 and 2014).
Combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE) was initially defined by the presence of emphysema in the upper lung lobes alongside predominant pulmonary fibrosis, primarily IPF/UIP, in the lower lobes. This broad definition suggested CPFE as a heterogeneous condition. For instance, in Cottin et al.'s 2005 description of CPFE, only 51% of cases were typical IPF, and the syndrome could also encompass nonspecific interstitial pneumonias, connective tissue disease-related interstitial lung disease, and drug-induced interstitial lung disease, leading to varied study populations and outcomes. Cottin's study reported a median survival of 6.1 years for CPFE patients, with 47% experiencing pulmonary hypertension. However, Mitchell et al. later showed that IPF with emphysema exhibited more severe radiographic fibrosis and emphysema compared to non-IPF usual interstitial pneumonia with emphysema, even after accounting for confounding factors. This finding led to the recommendation that IPF with emphysema be considered a distinct disease entity, warranting focused research to enhance clinical analysis.
The impact of emphysema on IPF prognosis remains a subject of ongoing debate in clinical research. A review of existing literature, summarized in Table 3, reveals varied findings regarding CPFE and IPF survival. Four studies indicated a worse survival for CPFE patients compared to those with IPF alone, while three reported similar survival rates between the groups. Interestingly, one study even suggested better survival in CPFE. Our current research identifies fibrosis severity, the presence of pulmonary hypertension (PH), and lung cancer as key prognostic indicators for IPF. Cottin et al. previously emphasized the importance of accounting for confounding factors when assessing survival differences in CPFE and IPF, noting a potential link between CPFE and PH leading to poorer outcomes.
A review of existing literature identified only five studies that assessed both fibrosis scores and pulmonary hypertension (PH) in the context of combined pulmonary fibrosis and emphysema (CPFE). These studies presented varied findings regarding the prognostic role of emphysema. Mejía et et al. observed a higher prevalence of PH (90% vs 58%, p < 0.001) and greater fibrosis scores (p = 0.015) in CPFE patients compared to those with IPF alone. This likely contributed to the poorer prognosis seen in CPFE, while emphysema itself was not deemed a significant prognostic factor. Conversely, Sugino et al. reported comparable estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure (ePASP) between CPFE and IPF (30.8 vs 30.9 mmHg). However, their CPFE cohort exhibited a slightly lower fibrosis score (p < 0.001) but a substantially higher lung cancer rate (50% vs 14.5%, p < 0.001). They concluded that emphysema was not a significant prognostic factor, suggesting the elevated lung cancer rate in CPFE might explain its worse survival. Kohashi et al., in a study of biopsy-proven CPFE, found similar PH (20% vs 22.2%) and fibrosis scores between groups, yet CPFE patients had worse survival. Notably, lung cancer was absent at baseline. In their multivariate analysis, emphysema emerged as a significant poor prognostic factor, though PH data were incomplete for 32% of patients. Ryerson et al. identified higher ePASP (56.6 vs 39.6 mmHg, p = 0.008) and lower fibrosis scores (p = 0.003) in CPFE compared to IPF, but survival rates were similar between the two groups. Finally, the most recent study by Jacob et al. also concluded that emphysema was not a prognostic factor and was not linked to increased PH. This study noted a lower fibrosis extent (p = 0.02) and a higher lung cancer rate (9.5% vs 1.2%, p = 0.001) in CPFE.
In this study, CPFE patients exhibited a significantly lower fibrosis score compared to IPF-alone patients (p = 0.022). While differences in pulmonary hypertension (46.7% vs 58.3%) and lung cancer rates (16.7% vs 7.1%) were not statistically significant, the reduced fibrosis in CPFE might explain the observed, though non-significant, trend toward better survival in this group. Consistent with prior research, these findings indicate that emphysema does not independently predict IPF prognosis. Instead, patient survival appears to be primarily influenced by initial disease severity and coexisting medical conditions.
This study has several limitations. Its retrospective, single-center design meant some pulmonary function data were incomplete. The survival rates might be underestimated because patients transferred from other hospitals were included, and some participants were lost to follow-up (27.2% within one year, 39.5% within five years), preventing complete outcome tracking. Furthermore, the study exclusively focused on IPF patients exhibiting a usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern on high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), excluding those with atypical HRCT patterns. Consequently, the findings may not be generalizable to IPF patients lacking a typical UIP HRCT pattern.
This investigation marks the first in Taiwan to thoroughly examine both HRCT imaging and medical records for IPF patients. Our study's IPF diagnoses are considered dependable because all included patients exhibited a definite Usual Interstitial Pneumonia (UIP) pattern on HRCT. This specific HRCT pattern offers a positive predictive value exceeding 90% for histological UIP, eliminating the need for surgical lung biopsy. Ultimately, we concluded that IPF patient survival is primarily influenced by their initial health conditions and disease severity. Emphysema, notably, did not demonstrate a significant impact on IPF prognosis. The variability in CPFE outcomes across different studies likely stems from diverse patient groups.
