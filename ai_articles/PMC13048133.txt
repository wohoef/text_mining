This study investigated the role of long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) as potential prognostic and therapeutic targets in various cancers, specifically focusing on multiple myeloma (MM). Through analysis of the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, dysregulation of lncRNA urothelial carcinoma associated 1 (UCA1) and hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) mRNA was identified. These molecules were found to share a common target, miR-1271-5p. To explore their effects in MM, RNA expression profiles from the GEO database were downloaded and analyzed using R packages. The expression levels of these RNAs in MM tissue samples and cells were further validated using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Luciferase reporter assays were employed to confirm the binding interactions between UCA1/HGF and miR-1271-5p. Cell proliferation and apoptosis were assessed via CCK-8 assays and flow cytometry, respectively. Additionally, in vivo experiments were conducted to demonstrate tumor progression. The findings revealed elevated expression of lncRNA UCA1 and HGF in MM patient samples and cells compared to normal plasma cells. MiR-1271-5p was confirmed as a target of both lncRNA UCA1 and HGF, exhibiting a negative correlation with their expression. Downregulating lncRNA UCA1 and HGF was observed to inhibit cell proliferation and promote apoptosis in RPMI 8226 cells, a human MM cell line. Conversely, overexpression of miR-1271-5p led to decreased proliferation and increased apoptosis. In vivo studies further supported these findings, demonstrating that either downregulating or upregulating lncRNA UCA1 respectively repressed or enhanced MM tumor growth in xenograft models. In conclusion, lncRNA UCA1 was found to promote proliferation and inhibit apoptosis in RPMI 8226 cells by modulating miR-1271-5p and HGF. These investigations suggest that the lncRNA UCA1/miR-1271-5p/HGF axis could represent a novel therapeutic strategy for MM.

Multiple myeloma (MM), a malignancy of terminally differentiated B cells originating from the postgerminal center, ranks as the second most prevalent blood cancer in the United States. The disease's onset is marked by the transformation of long-lived plasma cells (PCs), which typically reside in the bone marrow for years, producing antibodies and maintaining immunological memory. This transformation progresses through distinct stages: initially, long-lived PCs develop into monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), which subsequently evolves into smoldering MM (SMM), and ultimately, full-blown MM. During this cellular transformation, significant genetic alterations occur, including IgH translocations, hyperdiploidy, secondary translocations, gene deletions, and chromosomal gains. Concurrently, the immunophenotype of MM cells changes, characterized by high expression of markers like CD138, CD56, CD28, and CD38, alongside reduced expression of CD19, CD27, and CD45. These specific immunophenotypic profiles are crucial for distinguishing normal PCs from MM cells using multiparametric flow cytometry. Despite advancements in treatment, such as FDA-approved molecular therapies like thalidomide (a first-line agent) and its more potent derivative, lenalidomide (a second-line agent), both acting via the E3 ubiquitin-ligase complex cereblon (CRBN), a complete cure for MM remains elusive. Given the complex genetic events underlying MM development, exploring RNA regulation presents a promising new avenue for therapeutic intervention, complementing existing treatments.
Various RNA types, such as long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), microRNAs (miRNAs), and messenger RNAs (mRNAs), are implicated in either promoting or suppressing numerous cancers. LncRNAs and miRNAs are categorized as noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs), which may or may not have limited protein-coding potential. LncRNAs are a significant group of ncRNAs, resembling mRNA transcripts produced by RNA polymerase II, and are characterized by their length exceeding 200 nucleotides. In contrast, miRNAs are shorter and function as negative regulators of mRNA expression, typically by binding to sequences within the 3'-untranslated region (UTR) of mRNAs. While many ncRNAs are known to influence tumor development, the roles of most ncRNAs in various diseases remain largely unexplored. Recent discoveries highlight that lncRNAs can suppress miRNAs by "sponging" them, thereby preventing miRNAs from modulating mRNA translation, or by mediating interactions between chromosomes and proteins.
Research has explored several lncRNAs in the context of Multiple Myeloma (MM). For example, lncRNA PDIA3P is known to interact with c-Myc, influencing MM cell proliferation. MALAT1 and PCAT1 lncRNAs have been identified as indicators of poor prognosis in MM, with MALAT1 also modulating MM progression through various molecular interactions. Scientists have also investigated how lncRNAs interact with miRNAs. LncRNA OIP5-AS1, for instance, targets miR-40, leading to KLF10 downregulation. Its absence results in miR-410 accumulation, which then regulates MM cell proliferation and apoptosis by activating the PTEN/PI3K/AKT pathway. LncRNA NEAT1 and linc00515 contribute to drug and chemoresistance, respectively; NEAT1 acts by targeting miR-193a, while linc00515 downregulates miR-140-5p. Additionally, lncRNA CRNDE inhibits MM cell growth by targeting miR-451. Despite these findings, the precise roles of many lncRNAs in MM are still unclear, necessitating further research to advance MM treatment.
Urothelial carcinoma associated 1 (UCA1), a long noncoding RNA, was initially discovered in human bladder carcinoma and is among the lncRNAs found to be dysregulated in Multiple Myeloma (MM). This lncRNA is situated on the positive strand of chromosome 19p13.12 and comprises three exons and two introns. Notably, the UCA1 sequence lacks conserved long open reading frames and includes multiple stop codons, meaning its full-length cDNA does not yield protein products in in vitro translation assays. While UCA1 exists in three isoforms (1.4, 2.2, and 2.7 kb), the 1.4 kb isoform is predominantly studied by researchers due to its high abundance across various cancers, including bladder, breast, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Given its diverse regulatory roles in different cancers, its specific function in MM warrants further investigation.
Hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) is a glycoprotein with multiple domains, naturally synthesized by mesenchymal cells as a single-chain precursor called pro-HGF. It functions as a ligand for the MET receptor, and their interaction can lead to mesenchymal-derived growth. Elevated HGF levels typically induce tumorigenic, metastatic, and progressive characteristics in human cells, an effect observed in both solid and blood tumors. Furthermore, HGF upregulation in the bone marrow of multiple myeloma (MM) patients has been identified as a prognostic indicator. While prior research suggests HGF's role in promoting cancer development from normal cells, its specific regulatory mechanisms in MM remain unclear.
This study utilized existing Multiple Myeloma (MM) data from the GEO database, focusing on lncRNA UCA1 and HGF mRNA due to their observed dysregulation in MM. In vitro experiments demonstrated that both lncRNA UCA1 and HGF target miR-1271-5p, influencing MM cell proliferation and apoptosis. Furthermore, lncRNA UCA1 exhibited comparable effects on MM progression in vivo.
From the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, lncRNA and mRNA expression profiles relevant to multiple myeloma (MM) were acquired. Specifically, lncRNA data originated from GSE109116, while mRNA data were sourced from GSE47552. The "limma" R package was employed to pinpoint dysregulated RNAs, defined by a log2|Fold Change| greater than 1 and an adjusted p-value less than 0.05. A heatmap illustrating the top 20 differentially expressed RNAs was then generated using the "pheatmap" R package. Subsequently, lncRNA UCA1 and HGF mRNA were identified for further investigation. To predict potential shared miRNA targets for UCA1 and HGF, the TargetScan 7.2, miRanda, and HMDD 3.0 databases were utilized for screening and filtering.
For this study, clinical samples were gathered from January 2017 to January 2018 at the Department of Hematology, Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University. This included bone marrow aspirates from 15 individuals diagnosed with multiple myeloma (MM) and 15 healthy donors. Ethical approval for the study was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, and all participants provided informed consent. Normal plasma cells were isolated using CD138− magnetic beads, while MM samples were purified using CD138+ magnetic beads, both with a Mini-MACS separator (Miltenyi Biotec GmbH, Germany). Additionally, the human myeloma cell line RPMI 8226 was acquired from the BeNa Culture Collection (Beijing, China). These cells were maintained in RPMI 1640 medium (BasalMedia, Shanghai, China) supplemented with 10% FBS, 2 mmol/L L-glutamine, and penicillin/streptomycin, incubated at 37°C in a 5% CO2 humidified environment.
RNA expression levels were quantified using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with U6 and β-actin serving as internal controls. This process involved synthesizing cDNA from total RNA, which was extracted following the manufacturer's instructions for TRIzol reagent. The cDNA was generated using the PrimeScript RT reagent kit with gDNA Eraser. Subsequently, transcript levels were determined via qRT-PCR, employing SYBR Green PCR Master Mix. Primer sequences for these qRT-PCR assays are detailed in a separate table.
To prepare for experiments, total RNA was first extracted and reverse-transcribed into cDNA. The resulting lncRNA UCA1 cDNA fragment was then amplified via RT-PCR. This amplified UCA1 was subsequently inserted into the pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-copGFP expression vector, utilizing BamHI and EcoRI restriction sites and a Cold Fusion kit. For gene silencing, a shRNA targeting lncRNA UCA1 (sh-UCA1) was synthesized by Ribobio, while an siRNA targeting HGF (si-HGF), miR-1271-5p mimics, and a negative control were acquired from Genepharma. All these constructs were introduced into RPMI 8226 cells using Lipofectamine 2000.
To investigate the interaction between miR-1271-5p and lncRNA UCA1, the complete cDNA sequence of lncRNA UCA1 was inserted into the pMIR-REPORT Luciferase vector, creating UCA1-wt, following analysis of their complementary sequences. A mutant version, pMIR-REPORT Luciferase-UCA1-mut (UCA1-mut), was custom-synthesized by BioSune. Similarly, the predicted binding region within the HGF 3´-UTR, along with its corresponding mutant, was cloned into the pMIR-REPORT Luciferase vector, yielding HGF-wt or HGF-mut. These reporter plasmids, along with either miR-1271-5p mimics or a control vector, were introduced into 293T cells using Lipofectamine 2000. The luciferase reporter assay was then performed using a Dual Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit, as per the manufacturer's guidelines.
For protein analysis, RPMI 8226 cell pellets underwent lysis for 30 minutes on ice using RIPA buffer, supplemented with PMSF (both from Beyotime, China). Equivalent protein quantities were subsequently resolved via 8% SDS-PAGE before being transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). These membranes were then blocked for one hour at room temperature with 5% nonfat milk in PBST. Following blocking, primary antibodies targeting HGF (rabbit polyclonal, ab83760, 1:1000; Abcam, USA) and the loading control α-tubulin (ab7291, 1:5000; Abcam, USA) were applied, and membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C. After two washes with PBST, horseradish peroxidase-labeled secondary anti-rabbit IgG antibodies (ab7090, 1:5000; Abcam, USA) were added. Finally, protein detection was achieved through chemiluminescence using an ECL kit (Beyotime, China).
To assess cell proliferation, the CCK-8 assay was employed. Cells were cultured in 96-well plates for 24, 48, or 72 hours. Subsequently, WST-8 solution was introduced, followed by a 3-hour incubation. Optical density readings were then taken at 450 nm using an EnSpire Reader.
Flow cytometric analysis, using Annexin V-FITC/PI and FlowJo Software, determined apoptosis. Early apoptotic cells were identified by Annexin V-FITC+/PI− staining, while late apoptotic cells also showed Annexin V-FITC+/PI−. The total apoptosis rate in this study combined both early and late apoptotic cell populations.
To establish a human MM xenograft model, twelve female CB-17 SCID mice, aged 6 to 8 weeks, were obtained from Vital River (Beijing, China). RPMI 8226 cells, prepared for injection, were categorized into four treatment groups: lncRNA UCA1 upregulation (UCA1 group), lncRNA UCA1 downregulation (sh-UCA1), an empty vector control (NC group), and an untreated control (blank group). Each mouse received a subcutaneous injection of 5 × 10^6 treated RPMI 8226 cells into its flank. Tumor volume measurements commenced on the day of injection and were recorded weekly. After 28 days, mice were anesthetized, and tumors were surgically removed. Tumor volume was determined using the formula: length × width^2/2. Following excision, tumors were weighed and then preserved at -80°C for subsequent analysis. The Animal Care Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University approved all experimental protocols.
Statistical analyses, including two-tailed Student's t-tests, Spearman correlation, and ANOVA, were conducted using GraphPad Prism 7.0. Results are expressed as mean ± SD, with p < 0.05 indicating significance. Each experiment was independently replicated at least three times.
Analysis of Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) data revealed significant dysregulation in multiple myeloma (MM). LncRNA expression data from GSE109116 (30 MM, 4 normal) and mRNA data from GSE47552 (41 MM, 5 normal) were utilized. Heatmaps highlighted the top 20 differentially expressed lncRNAs and mRNAs, specifically showing elevated levels of lncRNA UCA1 and HGF mRNA in MM patient samples (Fig. 1A, B). This overexpression of UCA1 and HGF was further confirmed in 15 MM samples and the RPMI 8226 cell line (Fig. 1C, D).
Computational analysis was performed to identify potential miRNA targets, as illustrated in a Venn diagram (Fig. 2A). This analysis integrated three datasets: miRNAs predicted to target lncRNA UCA1, miRNAs predicted to target HGF, and miRNAs documented in the HMDD v3.0 database. The overlap of these three datasets yielded five specific miRNAs: miR-18a-5p, miR-96-5p, miR-182-5p, miR-202-5p, and miR-1271-5p. Subsequently, the expression levels of these five miRNAs were measured in both MM patient samples and healthy controls. The findings showed that miR-202-5p was elevated in MM, while miR-1271-5p was reduced when compared to normal controls. No significant differences in expression were observed for the other miRNAs (Fig. 2B). Given that increased lncRNA UCA1 typically implies a decrease in its target miRNAs due to competitive inhibition, miR-1271-5p was chosen for more detailed study. Further investigation, using Spearman correlation analysis, demonstrated an inverse relationship between miR-1271-5p and both lncRNA UCA1 and HGF (Fig. 2C, D). Predicted binding sites for miR-1271-5p on lncRNA UCA1 are presented in Fig. 2E, and luciferase reporter assays confirmed miR-1271-5p as a target of lncRNA UCA1 (Fig. 2F). Similarly, the potential binding site for miR-1271-5p on HGF is shown in Fig. 2G, with luciferase reporter assays verifying miR-1271-5p as a target of HGF (Fig. 2H).
Downregulating lncRNA UCA1 in RPMI 8226 cells, confirmed by qRT-PCR, led to a notable increase in miR-1271-5p expression. This suppression of lncRNA UCA1 also decreased both HGF mRNA and protein levels. Subsequent assays revealed that this knockdown significantly inhibited cell proliferation and promoted apoptosis in these cells.
Transfection of RPMI 8226 cells with miR-1271-5p mimics led to confirmed upregulation of miR-1271-5p, as shown by qRT-PCR (Fig. 4A). This intervention did not affect lncRNA UCA1 expression (Fig. 4B). However, the mimics effectively suppressed HGF expression at both mRNA and protein levels when compared to the NC group (Fig. 4C–E). The cellular effects of miR-1271-5p mimics included a clear suppression of cell proliferation and an increase in apoptosis within RPMI 8226 cells (Fig. 4F, G). Notably, these changes, including the HGF inhibition, were significantly reversed by the upregulation of lncRNA UCA1 (Fig. 4A–E).
To investigate HGF's role in RPMI 8226 cell function, experiments involved either reducing HGF levels or increasing lncRNA UCA1. While HGF downregulation successfully decreased both HGF mRNA and protein expression (Fig. 5C–E), it did not impact the expression of lncRNA UCA1 or miR-1271-5p (Fig. 5A, B). Interestingly, when HGF downregulation was combined with lncRNA UCA1 upregulation, HGF expression returned to levels comparable to the control group (Fig. 5C–E). Similarly, HGF reduction alone inhibited cell proliferation, but this effect was negated by co-treatment with lncRNA UCA1 upregulation (Fig. 5F). Apoptosis, which increased with HGF downregulation, was subsequently suppressed when lncRNA UCA1 was overexpressed (Fig. 5G, H).
To validate the in vitro observations, an in vivo animal study was performed. RPMI 8226 cells, either with elevated lncRNA UCA1 (UCA1 group) or suppressed lncRNA UCA1 (sh-UCA1 group), were injected subcutaneously into mice. Tumor volume and weight were monitored weekly, and tumors were harvested on day 28. The sh-UCA1 group exhibited significantly reduced tumor growth, while the UCA1 group showed a notable increase in growth compared to the blank control (Fig. 6A–C), confirming lncRNA UCA1's role in promoting MM tumor development in living organisms. Further analysis revealed that lncRNA UCA1 expression was decreased in the sh-UCA1 group and increased in the UCA1 group (Fig. 6D). Conversely, miR-1271-5p was upregulated and HGF was downregulated in the sh-UCA1 group. The UCA1 group displayed the opposite expression pattern for miR-1271-5p and HGF (Fig. 6E–H). These results collectively demonstrate that lncRNA UCA1 fosters MM tumor growth in vivo by modulating miR-1271-5p and HGF.
Our studies confirmed that lncRNA UCA1 and HGF promoted cell proliferation and inhibited apoptosis in multiple myeloma, both in laboratory and living models. Conversely, miR-1271-5p exhibited opposing effects. The targeted relationship between lncRNA UCA1/HGF and miR-1271-5p was established. Notably, the impact of miR-1271-5p was negated when lncRNA UCA1 was simultaneously downregulated and miR-1271-5p was upregulated, suggesting the UCA1/miR-1271-5p/HGF axis's role in MM progression.
LncRNAs, which are often dysregulated in various cancers, play a significant role in cancer progression by indirectly modulating translation through their function as miRNA sponges. This study specifically focused on lncRNA UCA1, an oncogene previously implicated in numerous cancers. For instance, in bladder cancer, UCA1 has been shown to enhance cisplatin/gemcitabine resistance by activating the transcription factor CREB, which in turn promotes miR-196a-5p expression. It also drives invasion and migration by regulating the miR-145/ZEB1/2/FSCN1 pathway and promotes glycolysis through the mTOR-STAT3/miR-143-HK2 cascade. Beyond bladder cancer, UCA1's ability to increase drug resistance through its target miRNAs is also observed in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), gastric cancer (GC), and colorectal cancer (CRC). Furthermore, UCA1 contributes to increased cell proliferation and cancer progression in other malignancies, such as colorectal cancer (by inhibiting miR-204-5p) and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) (by inhibiting miR-216b and activating the FGFR1/ERK pathway). These diverse mechanisms highlight UCA1's role in cancer development by sponging different miRNAs and activating various signaling pathways, even within the same cancer type. Consistent with prior findings by Zhang et al., who reported UCA1 upregulation in MM patient samples and cell lines, our research similarly concluded that lncRNA UCA1 promotes MM development both in vitro and in vivo by sponging miR-1271-5p.
miR-1271-5p generally acts as a tumor suppressor across various cancers, including gastric cancer (GC), colorectal cancer (CRC), prostate cancer (PCa), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), pancreatic adenocarcinoma, and oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC). Its mechanism of action varies depending on the specific downstream molecules it targets. For instance, in CRC, miR-1271-5p inhibits proliferation and invasion by suppressing Capn4 or the metadherin/Wnt signaling pathway. Similarly, in prostate cancer, it curbs proliferation and invasion through the inhibition of DIXDC1 or ERG. However, miR-1271-5p's role is not universally suppressive. In non-small-cell lung cancer, it promotes increased proliferation and invasion by inhibiting HOXA5. A comparable pro-cancerous effect has been observed in breast cancer, where Yu et al. reported that miR-1271-5p enhances letrozole resistance by suppressing Erα. This highlights that miR-1271-5p can exhibit diverse functions across different cancer types, and even within the same cancer, its effects can vary between studies. For example, while one study indicated miR-1271-5p as an accelerant in breast cancer, another by Liang et al. found its suppression by circ-ABCBIO to have a negative impact on breast cancer proliferation and progression. In the context of multiple myeloma (MM), our findings demonstrate that miR-1271-5p acts as a repressor of proliferation and a promoter of apoptosis, specifically by inhibiting HGF. This aligns with another study by Xu et al., which showed miR-1271-5p reducing MM cell proliferation by suppressing the smoothened-mediated hedgehog signaling pathway. Thus, like lncRNA UCA1, miR-1271-5p influences cancer progression through multiple targets and signaling pathways.
Hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), initially identified in 1984 as a mitogenic protein in rat hepatocytes, was subsequently recognized for its crucial role in liver growth and regeneration. More recent research highlights HGF's capacity to stimulate proliferation and migration, impacting both normal and cancerous cells. For instance, in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), HGF contributes to gefitinib resistance and can drive epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and angiogenesis, though these effects can be counteracted by miR-206. Similarly, HGF promotes renal carcinoma proliferation via the HGF/c-Met pathway. In gastric cancer (GC), HGF-induced migration is inhibited by miR-16, and its growth-promoting effects are hampered by miR-26a/b. These findings collectively suggest that HGF primarily boosts cell proliferation and may induce EMT, leading to increased invasiveness and angiogenesis in cancer. Our study aligns with this, showing that HGF enhanced multiple myeloma (MM) cell proliferation and reduced apoptosis, consistent with earlier work by Kosai et al. (1998) on hepatocyte apoptosis. Furthermore, our investigation indicates that miR-1271-5p targets the 3´-UTR of HGF in MM, a mechanism supported by other studies where HGF's effects are modulated by its target miRNAs.
Our research indicates that lncRNA UCA1 and HGF promote MM cell proliferation and inhibit apoptosis. Conversely, miR-1271-5p negatively affects MM cells. LncRNA UCA1 directly regulates miR-1271-5p, showing a negative correlation in expression. In vivo, lncRNA UCA1 drives MM tumor growth by modulating miR-1271-5p and HGF. This lncRNA UCA1/miR-1271-5p/HGF axis presents a promising new therapeutic target for MM.
