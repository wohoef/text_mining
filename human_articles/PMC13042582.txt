AbstractMutations of the p53 gene are the most common genomic alterations associated with triple‐negative breast cancer (TNBC) and are reported in 60–88% cases. Despite the high incidence of such mutations, there is no consensus about the clinical application of p53 detection in breast cancer management. This study investigates the prognostic value of immunohistochemically detected p53 in TNBC patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy. We reviewed the clinicopathologic features of 1088 TNBC patients who received curative surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy. Immunohistochemically, nuclear staining of >10% was defined as p53 “positive.” Of the total 1088 TNBC patients, 709 (65.2%) had no lymph node metastasis (N0). Among the N0 patients, 408 (57.5%) were p53‐ positive (p53+), and 301 (42.5%) were p53‐ negative (p53‐). p53 + tumors showed a tendency for better breast cancer‐specific survival (BCSS, p = 0.052) and overall survival (OS, p = 0.079) compared to p53‐ tumors. In multivariate analysis, p53 + tumors showed significantly better BCSS (p53 + vs. p53‐; HR 2.8, 95% confidence interval: 1.1–7.3, p = 0.034); however, in TNBC patients with lymph node metastasis, there was no correlation between p53 status, clinicopathologic characteristics, and survival. Consequently, in TNBC patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy, immunohistochemical p53 expression was associated with better BCSS in N0 patients.

Triple‐negative breast cancer (TNBC) involves lack of expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor type 2 (HER2) and represents approximately 15% of all breast cancers (1). TNBC has aggressive biological characteristics, a short clinical course with early relapse, and low survival prognosis [[1], [2]].
The poor outcomes of TNBC also reflect a lack of therapeutic options, which are limited to conventional chemotherapy with supplemental adjuvant therapies. Despite concerted efforts to identify targeted agents for TNBC, there is no effective target therapy to date. Recent clinical trials of agents such as inhibitors of poly (ADP‐ribose) polymerase (PARP) or epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) were cause for optimism based on promising preclinical and phase I/II trial data, but they ultimately failed to demonstrate significant clinical activity [[3], [4], [5]].
The tumor protein p53 (TP53) is well known for its role as a tumor suppressor and as a regulator of the cell cycle. Mutations in TP53 are the most common genomic alterations in TNBC [6] and are reported in 60–88% of TNBC or basal like breast cancer (BLBC), but in only 13–26% of luminal type breast cancers [7]. Despite their high prevalence, TP53 mutations have not been exploited as prognostic parameters in the efficient management of breast cancer.
It is still uncertain whether p53 quantified using IHC reflects mutant or wild‐type TP53, although in breast cancer, a correlation of less than 75% was reported between IHC signal for p53 and mutant TP53 as confirmed by sequencing [8]. At present, immunohistochemistry (IHC) is the most commonly used method to evaluate p53 status in clinical institutions, and data obtained this way have appeared in numerous publications. Therefore, this study investigated the value of immunohistochemically detected p53 as a prognostic factor in adjuvant chemotherapy‐treated TNBC patients.
We retrospectively reviewed the medical records of TNBC patients diagnosed with invasive ductal carcinoma at Samsung Medical Center between January 2003 and September 2013. After excluding patients who had bilateral breast cancer or distant metastasis at initial work‐up, 1088 patients who underwent curative surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy were selected for this study. Patients who underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy were excluded.
We reviewed patient clinicopathologic characteristics of age at diagnosis, menopausal status, type of operation, TNM stage (American Joint Committee on Cancer Staging, 7th Edition), and expression of molecular biomarkers ER, PR, HER2, Ki 67, and p53.
Triple‐negative breast cancer was defined immunohistochemically as an ER‐, PR‐, and HER‐negative tumor. Using the Allred score (AS) for the evaluation of ER and PR status, AS ≤ 2 was defined as “negative,” while a HER2 evaluation score of 0 or +1 was defined as HER2 “negative.” For tumors with an equivocal score (+2) for HER2, silver in situ hybridization (SISH) or fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was performed. The cut‐off of Ki 67 labeling index was 14.0% using the recommendation from the St. Gallen 2011 consensus [9].
Immunohistochemical evaluation of p53 was performed using a mouse monoclonal anti‐human p53 (clone: BP53.12) antibody (Invitrogen/MD21704USA) at a 1:4000 dilution. The staining was performed using an autoimmunostainer (Leica Bond Polymer Refine detection kit/Leica Bond‐Max staining system), per the manufacturer's instructions. Nuclear staining >10% was defined as p53 “positive” [10]. All the diagnoses of p53 were made by board‐certified pathologists.
According to medical records, after curative surgery, 109 patients received four cycles of AC (doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 on day 1, repeated every 21 days); 358 patients received four cycles of AC (doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 and cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2 on day 1, repeated every 21 days), followed by four cycles of docetaxel (75 mg/m2 or 60 mg/m2); 588 patients received six cycles of FAC (5‐fluorouracil 500 mg/m2, doxorubicin 50 mg/m2, and cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m2 on day 1, repeated every 21 days); and 33 patients received six cycles of CMF (cyclophosphamide 100 mg/m2 PO days 1–14, methotrexate 40 mg/m2 and fluorouracil 600 mg/m2 on days 1 and 8, repeated every 28 days).
Differences in the frequencies of clinicopathologic variables were analyzed using Fisher's exact test and chi‐square test. Disease‐free survival (DFS) was defined as the time from surgery to the date of documentation of relapse, including locoregional recurrence and/or distant metastasis. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from surgery to the date of death. Breast cancer‐specific survival (BCSS) was classified using data on underlying cause of death code (ICD‐10). Survival curves were constructed using the Kaplan–Meier method. Hazard ratios were estimated using a Cox regression model for DFS/OS and BCSS in a multivariate analysis. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 23.0 (IBM Corp., NY, USA).
Median follow‐up duration of patients was 57 months (range, 4–140 months). Median patient age was 48 years (range, 21–78 years).
Of the total 1088 TNBC patients who underwent adjuvant chemotherapy, 623 (57.3%) had p53‐positive (p53+) tumors, and 465 (42.7%) had p53‐negative (p53‐) tumors. The relationships between clinicopathologic characteristics and p53 expression are shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences between p53 + tumors and p53‐ tumors in terms of median age, T stage, or N stage, but p53 + patients had a higher nuclear grade (NG) and a higher Ki67 level than p53‐ patients, (NG: 86.5% vs. 81.6%, p = 0.034; and Ki67: 96.9% vs. 92.8%, p = 0.032, respectively).
We evaluated the relationship between p53 expression and survival in TNBC patients (Fig. 1). Based on univariate analysis using the Kaplan–Meier method, p53 expression was not associated with DFS (p = 0.128), OS (p = 0.126), or BCSS (p = 0.053).
In 109 patients who received four cycles of AC, p53 expression was associated with significantly better DFS (p = 0.036), BCSS (p = 0.023), and OS (p = 0.023) (Fig. 2). However, there was no significant correlation between p53 expression and survival in patients who received other treatment regimens.
In the 1088 patients studied, 709 (65.2%) showed no LN metastasis (N0), and 379 (34.8%) exhibited LN metastasis. In N0 patients, 408 (57.5%) had p53 + tumors, and 301 (42.5%) had no p53‐ tumors. The p53 + tumors had shown a higher Ki67 level than p53‐ tumors (≥14.0%; 96.6% vs. 92.8%, p = 0.004), but there was no difference in Ki67 expression according to p53 status among TNBC patients with LN metastasis (Table 2).
In analysis between p53 expression and survival by LN metastasis status (Fig. 3), in N0 patients, p53 + tumors showed a trend for better BCSS (p = 0.052) and OS (p = 0.079) compared to p53‐ tumors by the Kaplan–Meier method. In multivariate analysis, p53 expression in N0 patients was associated with significantly better BCSS than absence of p53 expression (p53 + vs. p53‐: hazard ratio 2.8, 95% confidence interval: 1.1–7.4, p = 0.022) (Table 3). However, among TNBC patients with LN metastasis, there was no difference in clinicopathologic characteristics between p53 + tumors and p53‐ tumors and no association with either BCSS or OS (Supplement 1, 2).
The TP53 mutation is the single most common genetic alteration in breast cancer, yet its prognostic significance remains controversial. The relationship between p53 expression and prognosis has been reported variously [[11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16]]. In studies that were evaluated by IHC, Biganzoli et al. [11] showed a p53 expressing TNBC had worse overall and event‐free survival, but this study had small number of patients (n = 33) and higher rates of stage I, and there was no information of chemotherapy. Maeda et al. [12] showed that CK5/6 (−), androgen receptor (AR) (−), and p53 (+) TNBC subtype, presented the worst prognosis. On the other hand, patients with AR (+) and p53 (−) TNBC presented a good prognosis. However, in this study, low AR expression was associated with high clinical stage but the expression of CK5/6 and p53 were not correlated with clinicopathological features. Kashiwagi et al. [13] showed that p53 status was not a significant prognostic factor regardless of chemotherapy. These inconsistent results may be due to heterogeneity in patient characteristics, breast cancer subtypes, and chemotherapy regimens.
There are reports that suggest p53 expression might be a favorable prognostic factor in early TNBC. Coates et al. also found a significant correlation between p53 expression and better DFS and OS among patients with ER negative, node‐negative early breast cancer (both TNBC and HER2 subtypes) [15]. Jin et al. reported that p53 expression was significantly associated with decreased lymph node metastasis and improved patient survival [16].
We studied the significance of p53 expression in TNBC patients who underwent adjuvant chemotherapy and adjusted the multivariate analysis for chemotherapy regimen. We found that p53 expression was significantly associated with better BCSS, but only in patients with early‐stage TNBC with N0. The association of p53 with DFS, BCSS, and OS was not significant in patients with advanced TNBC with LN metastasis.
Several studies have explored p53 as a predictive factor for chemotherapy response. In a recent study with 198 TNBC patients, p53 expression was independently associated with pathologic complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (odds ratio: 3.961; p = 0.003). In addition, patients with higher expression of p53 and Ki67 also had a higher pCR rate (25.8%) than patients with lower p53 and Ki67 expression (5.2%) [17].
According to reports that predict sensitivity to chemotherapy agents, TP53 mutation is associated with resistance to anthracycline‐based chemotherapy in breast cancer [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. However, results have not been as convincing for taxane‐based chemotherapy. A large phase III clinical trial showed that, although TP53 status was prognostic for OS, it was not predictive of preferential sensitivity to taxanes [22]. Similarly, our data showed that p53 expression was associated with better BCSS in the AC treatment group, but not in patients treated with taxane‐based chemotherapy. There was no correlation between p53 expression and DFS, regardless of the chemotherapy regimen.
In this study, we used immunohistochemistry (IHC) to assess p53 expression. Our previous study found strong associations between the mutation status of TP53 and p53 protein expression [23]. A correlation less than 75% was reported between IHC signal for p53 and mutant TP53 as confirmed by sequencing [8]. It is still uncertain whether p53 quantified using IHC reflects mutant or wild‐type TP53. However, with the exception of cellular stress, wild‐type p53 is rapidly degraded and mutant P53 protein is less susceptible to degradation than wild‐type p53 protein [[24], [25]] most of the immunohistochemically detected proteins were thought to be mutant p53. In this regard, systematic and well‐controlled studies are needed to identify reciprocal relationships between IHC‐detected p53 protein expression and TP53 mutation status.
In TNBC, immunohistochemical p53 + tumors were associated with better BCSS in N0 patients, but not in patients with LN metastasis. These results suggest that p53 expression is a favorable prognostic factor in early TNBC treated with adjuvant chemotherapy. Further investigations are needed to confirm the prognostic value of p53 expression status in predicting patient response to chemotherapy in TNBC.
