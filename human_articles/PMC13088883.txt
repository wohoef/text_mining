AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the evaluating Member State (EMS), France, received an application from Syngenta Crop Protection AG to set import tolerances for benzovindiflupyr in several crops and in certain animal products. According to EFSA, the data are sufficient to derive maximum residue level (MRL) proposals for the imported crops under consideration, except for cucurbits with inedible peel. For coffee beans and liver of ruminants and horses, different risk management options were derived. Adequate analytical enforcement methods are available to control the residues of benzovindiflupyr in plant and animal origin commodities. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concludes that the uses of benzovindiflupyr notified for the products under consideration will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore are unlikely to pose a health risk to consumers.

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the evaluating Member State (EMS), France, received an application from Syngenta Crop Protection AG to set import tolerances for the active substance benzovindiflupyr in various crops from Canada, USA, Mexico, Paraguay and Brazil.
France proposed to set maximum residue levels (MRLs) for imported pome fruits, wine grapes, several root and tuber vegetables, several solanacea, cucurbits, pulses, sweet corns, several oilseeds and cereals, coffee beans, ginger and turmeric roots, muscle, fat, liver and kidney of ruminants. The requested modification of MRLs for imported soyabean and sugar cane was not assessed as the proposed MRLs are equal to the existing EU MRLs. France drafted an evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on 26 February 2016.
EFSA bases its assessment on the revised evaluation report submitted by the EMS, the draft assessment report (DAR) and its revised version, the Commission review report on benzovindiflupyr as well as the EFSA conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance benzovindiflupyr.
The toxicological profile of benzovindiflupyr was assessed in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 0.05 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.1 mg/kg bw.
The metabolism of benzovindiflupyr in primary crops was investigated in the fruit, cereal and pulse/oilseed crop groups following foliar applications. From these studies, the peer review established the residue definition for enforcement and for risk assessment as benzovindiflupyr, which is applicable to all the crops under consideration.
EFSA concludes that the submitted residue trials are sufficient to derive MRL proposals for the imported crops under consideration, except for cucurbits with inedible peel. For coffee beans, the submitted residue trials provide evidence that the MRL set in the country of origin is likely to be exceeded. EFSA derived three risk management options as regards MRL in coffee beans (option 1: no modification of the existing MRL, option 2: setting the import tolerance at the level of the country of origin and option 3: setting the import tolerance at the level calculated with the OECD calculator). Adequate analytical enforcement methods are available to monitor the residues of benzovindiflupyr in plant products at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg; the applicability of the methods to enforce MRLs in dried ginseng and turmeric roots needs to be confirmed.
For processed commodities, the same residue definition as for raw commodities is applicable. Processing studies were provided which allowed deriving robust processing factors for a number of processed products based on apples and grapes (apple juice, sauce, pomace, must, wine and raisins).
As the uses of benzovindiflupyr are for imported crops, investigations of residues in rotational crops are not required.
For food of animal origin, the peer review derived a residue definition for enforcement as benzovindiflupyr, and for risk assessment as the sum of benzovindiflupyr and monohydroxylated benzovindiflupyr, free and conjugated (SYN546039), expressed as benzovindiflupyr applicable to all products of animal origin. Considering the uses of benzovindiflupyr in feed products authorised in Europe and the residues on imported feed commodities and their by‐products, a modification of the existing MRLs is not required. The applicant requested to set import tolerances for animal products produce in the USA and Canada; thus, the import tolerances should reflect the livestock exposure resulting from benzovindiflupyr residues in feed items available in the countries of origin. Since a comprehensive overview of the authorised uses in the USA and Canada and the residues in feed is not available, EFSA is not in a position to verify the appropriateness of the tolerance/MRLs established in the countries of origin. A risk management decision is required whether it is acceptable to raise the existing MRL in liver of bovine, sheep, goats and horses to the level of 0.06 mg/kg established in the USA. Adequate analytical enforcement methods are available to monitor the residues of benzovindiflupyr in animal matrices at the validated LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg.
The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 2 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) considering the existing uses, the uses of benzovindiflupyr on the imported crops notified and the MRLs set at the LOQ. As regards liver of ruminants and horses, EFSA calculated two scenarios: scenario 1 is based on the existing EU MRLs while in scenario 2 exposure is calculated assuming the MRLs are set at the same level as in the countries of origin. In both scenarios, no consumer intake risks were identified. The highest chronic intake estimated accounted for 2.8% of the ADI (Dutch and German child diet) and the highest acute exposure was calculated to be 39% of the ARfD for pepper.
EFSA concludes that the uses of benzovindiflupyr notified for the products under consideration will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore are unlikely to pose a health risk to consumers.

Regulation (EC) No 396/20051 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) establishes the rules governing the setting of pesticide maximum residue levels (MRLs) in the European Union (EU). Article 6 of the Regulation lays down that commercially interested parties, such as manufacturers or importers, may submit an application requesting the setting of an import tolerance in accordance with the provisions of Article 7 of the Regulation.
The company Syngenta Crop Protection AG2 submitted an application to France, hereafter referred to as the evaluating Member State (EMS), to set import tolerances for benzovindiflupyr in various products from Canada, USA, Mexico, Paraguay and Brazil. This application was notified to the European Commission and the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and was subsequently evaluated by the EMS in accordance with Article 8 of the MRL Regulation.
The EMS submitted the revised evaluation report to the European Commission and to EFSA on 26 February 2016. The application was included in the EFSA Register of Questions with the reference number EFSA‐Q‐2016‐00164 and the following subject:
Benzovindiflupyr – Setting import tolerance of new MRL in various commodities
France proposed the following MRL values: 0.01* mg/kg sweet corns, peanuts;0.02 mg/kg potatoes, tropical root and tuber vegetables, Jerusalem artichokes, maize;0.04 mg/kg soyabeans, sugar canes;0.08 mg/kg cucurbits with edible peel;0.1 mg/kg wheat, rye, coffee beans,0.15 mg/kg rapeseeds, poppy seeds, linseeds, mustard seeds, gold of pleasure seeds, cotton seeds, ginger roots, turmeric roots;0.2 mg/kg pome fruits, pulses;0.3 mg/kg cucurbits with inedible peel;0.7 mg/kg tomatoes, aubergines;1 mg/kg peppers, okra;1.5 mg/kg wine grapes, barley, oats.
In addition, France proposed to modify MRLs for certain animal products: 0.02 mg/kg in muscle and kidney of ruminants, 0.03 mg/kg in fat of ruminants, 0.07 mg/kg in liver of ruminants and 0.01 mg/kg in liver of pigs.
EFSA proceeded with the assessment of the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the Regulation.
In accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA shall, based on the evaluation report provided by the EMS, provide a reasoned opinion on the risks to the consumer associated with the application.
The evaluation report submitted by the EMS (France, 2015b) and the exposure calculations using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) are considered as supporting documents to this reasoned opinion and, thus, are made publicly available.
In accordance with Article 11 of the Regulation, the reasoned opinion shall be provided as soon as possible and at the latest within 3 months (which may be extended to 6 months if more detailed evaluations need to be carried out) from the date of receipt of the application. If EFSA requests supplementary information, the time limit laid down shall be suspended until that information has been provided.
Benzovindiflupyr is the ISO common name for N‐[(1RS,4SR)‐9‐(dichloromethylene)‐1,2,3,4‐tetrahydro‐1,4‐methanonaphthalen‐5‐yl]‐3‐(difluoromethyl)‐1‐methylpyrazole‐4‐carboxamide (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance, which consists of two enantiomers in a 1:1 ratio, and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix B.
The details of the Good Agricultural Practices (GAPs) notified for benzovindiflupyr that triggered the MRL application are in Appendix A.
Benzovindiflupyr is a new active substance which was evaluated in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/20093 with France designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS). It has been approved for use as a fungicide by Regulation (EU) 2016/1774 which entered into force on 1 March 2016 and amended the Annex to Regulation (EU) No 540/20115. Risk mitigation measures and the submission of confirmatory information are foreseen in the implementing regulation. The representative uses evaluated in the peer review were foliar spray applications on wheat, rye, barley and oats. The draft assessment report (DAR) has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2015a).
The EU MRLs for benzovindiflupyr are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and are based on the MRL recommendations derived in the framework of the peer review (EFSA, 2015a). It is noted that the requested MRLs for imported soyabeans and sugar canes at the level of 0.04 mg/kg are already established in the EU MRL Regulation. Thus, EFSA does not see a need to reassess the residue situation for these two crops in the framework of this reasoned opinion.
Codex Alimentarius has established MRLs (codex maximum residue limits (CXLs)) at 0.05 mg/kg on soybeans and at the limit of quantification (LOQ) on products of animal origin (meat, fat, edible offal, milk, eggs). The CXLs were assessed by EFSA (EFSA, 2015b).
The MRLs/tolerances established in the United States6 (US), Canada7 (CA), Mexico8 (MX) and Brazil9 (BR) in the crops under consideration are reported below in brackets:
Pome fruits (US/CA: 0.2 mg/kg); wine grapes (US/CA: 1 mg/kg); potatoes, tropical root and tuber vegetables, Jerusalem artichokes, ginger, turmeric (US/CA: 0.02 mg/kg); tomatoes, peppers, aubergines, okra (US/CA: 1.5 mg/kg); cucurbits (US/CA: 0.3 mg/kg); pulses (US/CA: 0.2 mg/kg); sweet corn, peanuts (US: 0.01 mg/kg); cotton (US/MX; 0.15 mg/kg); other oilseeds (US/CA: 0.15 mg/kg); maize (US: 0.02 mg/kg); wheat, rye (US/CA: 0.1 mg/kg); barley, oats (US/CA: 1.5 mg/kg); fat of ruminants and horses (US/CA: 0.02 mg/kg); liver of ruminants and horses (US: 0.06 mg/kg; CA: 0.04); and coffee (BR: 0.03 mg/kg).
EFSA has based its assessment on the updated evaluation report submitted by the EMS (France, 2015b), the DAR and its revised version prepared under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (France, 2014, 2015a), the Commission review report on benzovindiflupyr (European Commission, 2015b), EFSA conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance benzovindiflupyr (EFSA, 2015a). The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Regulation (EU) No 546/201110 and the currently applicable guidance documents relevant for the consumer risk assessment of pesticide residues (European Commission, 1996, 1997a, b, c, d, e, f, g, 2000, 2010a,b, 2015a; OECD, 2011).
In the framework of the peer review under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, it was concluded that analytical methods based on the high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS), including the multiresidue Quick, Easy, Cheap, Effective, Rugged, and Safe (QuEChERS) method, were adequately validated to monitor residues in high water, high acid and high oil content commodities, dry/starch matrices and coffee beans at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2015a). Although sufficiently validated analytical enforcement methods are available for the four major matrix groups and coffee beans, the applicability of these methods to dried ginger and dried turmeric roots should be demonstrated.
The peer review in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 concluded that analytical methods based on the HPLC–MS/MS, including the multiresidue QuEChERS method, were adequately validated to enforce residues in meat, fat, liver, kidney, milk and eggs at the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (EFSA, 2015a).
Thus, EFSA concludes that sufficiently validated analytical methods for enforcing the proposed MRLs for benzovindiflupyr in food of animal origin are available.
The toxicological profile of the active substance benzovindiflupyr was assessed in the framework of the peer review under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (EFSA, 2015a; European Commission, 2015b). The data were sufficient to derive the toxicological reference values compiled in Table 1.
Toxicological studies on the metabolite SYN546039, which was included in the residue definition for risk assessment of products of animal origin, were assessed during the peer review (EFSA, 2015a). Its toxicity was considered as covered by the toxicological endpoints set for the active substance (France, 2014).
The metabolism of benzovindiflupyr in primary crops was evaluated in the framework of the peer review under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 in the fruit, cereal and pulse/oilseed crop groups (France, 2014, 2015a, EFSA, 2015a). An overview of the available metabolism studies is presented in Table 2.
Based on these metabolism studies, parent benzovindiflupyr was proposed as a global residue definition for monitoring and risk assessment after foliar applications (EFSA, 2015a). The current residue definition set in Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 is identical to the residue definition for enforcement derived in the peer review.
In the framework of the peer review, it was highlighted that the above studies do not investigate the possible impact of plant metabolism on the isomer ratio of benzovindiflupyr (EFSA, 2015a); further investigation on this matter would in principle be required. Since guidance on the consideration of isomer ratios in the consumer risk assessment is not yet available, EFSA recommends that this issue is reconsidered when such guidance is available.
For the crops under consideration, EFSA concludes that the residue definition for enforcement and risk assessment agreed during the peer review is applicable.
In support of the MRL application, residue trials from USA, Canada and Brazil (coffee) were submitted. In all trials, an adjuvant was added to the spray mixture. For certain crops (potatoes, tomatoes, etc.), the supervised field trials were conducted during a single season instead of at least two as required (European Commission, 1997b). Since the trials were located in different geographical regions representing different climatic conditions, EFSA is of the opinion that the trials are sufficiently representative for the uses and it is not necessary to request trials performed in a different year. Results from side‐by‐side bridging trials confirmed that emulsifiable concentrate (EC) and water‐dispersible granule (WG) formulations produce comparable residues. For the MRL calculation, EFSA selected the highest residue value of the bridging studies. In all trials, the samples were analysed for benzovindiflupyr and its metabolite SYN546039. The metabolite was at or close to the LOQ, except in barley grains (< 0.01–0.35 mg/kg).
It is noted that the food classification applicable in the EU (Regulation (EU) No 752/201411 differs from the Canadian and US food classification. For deriving the MRL proposals, EFSA identified the corresponding crop in the EU classification and applied the EU rules regarding the number of trials required and the possible extrapolation (guidance document SANCO/7525/VI/95, European Commission, 2015a).
A total of 18 (apple) and 12 (pear) GAP‐compliant residue trials were submitted. Two trials on apples and pears were found to be not independent (same location, same application dates and rates); the highest result of the respective pairs of trials was selected for the MRL calculation.
According to the guidance (European Commission, 2015a), results from trials on apples and pears can be extrapolated to the whole group of pome fruits. Since residue levels were statistically comparable (U‐test, 5%) and the single data set MRL values are within the neighbouring MRL category, the results were pooled to derive the MRL proposal of 0.2 mg/kg, which corresponds to the value established in the countries of origin.
A total of 17 GAP‐compliant residue trials were submitted. Applying the OECD MRL calculator, a MRL of 1.5 mg/kg is derived, which is higher than the value set in the countries of origin (1 mg/kg). Based on the previously used the EU methodology (European Commission, 1997g), a MRL proposal of 0.9 mg/kg (Rber calculation) is derived. Considering the EU policy not to set import tolerances higher than in the country of origin, EFSA proposes the MRL of 1 mg/kg, which is equal to the MRL established in CA/US.12
In total, 15 trials were submitted. In 12 of these trials, benzovindiflupyr was applied as an in‐furrow soil application followed by four foliar applications as described in the GAP. Interval between the soil treatment and the first spray application ranged from 55 to 95 days. Residues were slightly above the LOQ in five trials, including the one investigating residues beyond 14 days (0.015 mg/kg). In the remaining three trials, the single in‐furrow application and the four spray applications were applied separately in side‐by‐side plots. Residues were above the LOQ in one sample (0.015 mg/kg) after the foliar applications.
Although conducted with a combined experimental design, the trials are acceptable even in case of foliar applications, as the prior soil application at planting is not expected to significantly affect the magnitude of residues obtained after foliar applications. The MRL of 0.02 mg/kg is derived for potatoes, which corresponds to the value established in the countries of origin.
The following crops belong to the US crop group ‘tuberous and corm vegetables, subgroup 1C’: cassava roots, sweet potatoes, yams, arrowroots, ginger and turmeric roots and Jerusalem artichokes for which the MRL/tolerance of 0.02 mg/kg is set in USA and Canada. In the EU classification, they are listed under the group of tropical root and tuber vegetable (code 212000); for Jerusalem artichokes, a separate code is allocated in the group other root and tuber vegetables (code 213050) (for ginger and turmeric roots, see Section ‘Ginger, turmeric roots – GAP (see potato)’ below).
Since according to the guidance (European Commission, 2015a), results from trials on potatoes can be extrapolated to both groups (i.e. tropical root and tuber vegetables and other root and tuber vegetables), the data support a MRL proposal of 0.02 mg/kg for the group of tropical root and tuber vegetables and for Jerusalem artichokes. This level corresponds to the value established in the countries of origin.
According to the US food classification, ginger and turmeric roots are included in the group ‘tuberous and corm vegetables, subgroup 1C’ for which the MRL/tolerance of 0.02 mg/kg has been set in USA and Canada by extrapolation from residues on potatoes. In the EU classification, these two crops are listed in the group of spices, root and rhizome (code 0840020 and 0840030); thus, the MRLs are set for the dried product. The extrapolation from residues on potatoes is foreseen in Europe; however, an appropriate concentration factor for drying needs to be applied (European Commission, 2015a). EFSA used a dehydration factor of 7.513 to recalculate, based on the residue trials in fresh potatoes, the MRL of 0.15 mg/kg for dried ginger and turmeric roots.
From 12 GAP‐compliant residue trials on tomatoes, a MRL of 0.7 mg/kg is derived. According to the EU guidance document (European Commission, 2015a), results from trials on tomatoes can be extrapolated to aubergines.
In the countries of origin, the MRL for these crops is set at a level of 1.5 mg/kg. However, based on the result of the OECD calculator, a MRL of 0.7 mg/kg for tomatoes and aubergines is considered sufficient.
From nine GAP‐compliant residue trials on peppers, a MRL of 1 mg/kg is derived. According to the EU guidance document (European Commission, 2015a), results from trials on peppers can be extrapolated to okra.
In the countries of origin, the MRL for these crops is set at a level of 1.5 mg/kg. However, based on the result of the OECD calculator, a MRL of 1 mg/kg for peppers and okra is considered sufficient.
In total, 11 GAP‐compliant residue trials were submitted (six trials in cucumbers and five trials in courgettes). According to the EU guidance document (European Commission, 2015a), trials on cucumbers and courgettes can be combined to extrapolate the results to the whole group of cucurbits, edible peel. Based on the combined data set, a MRL proposal of 0.08 mg/kg is derived.
In the countries of origin, a MRL of 0.3 mg/kg is set for the whole group of cucurbits. However, based on result of the OECD calculator, a MRL of 0.08 mg/kg for cucurbits with edible peel is considered sufficient.
The applicant submitted six GAP‐compliant residue trials on melons. Since melons are classified as a major crop on a global level, six trials are not sufficient to derive an import tolerance for melons (European Commission, 2015a). EFSA agrees with the conclusion of the EMS (France, 2015b) that at least two additional trials on melons are required to derive a MRL proposal for melons and to extrapolate results to the whole group of cucurbits, inedible peel.
Based on 12 GAP‐compliant residue trials, a MRL proposal of 0.01 mg/kg (LOQ) is proposed. This value corresponds to the tolerance set in the country of origin.
Eight trials in peas and 12 trials in beans compliant with the GAP were submitted. According to the EU guidance document (European Commission, 2015a), trials can be combined to extrapolate results to the whole group of dry pulses. Although residue levels in peas and beans were statistically comparable (U‐test, 5%), the MRL proposals derived for the individual data sets differ by more than a neighbouring MRL class. Therefore, the data were not pooled (European Commission, 2015a).
Based on the residue data set for peas, using the OECD calculator, a MRL of 0.08 mg/kg for (dry) peas is derived.
Based on the residue data set in beans and using the OECD calculator, a MRL proposal of 0.3 mg/kg is derived which is higher than the MRL established in the countries of origin (0.2 mg/kg). Using the previous EU methodology for calculating MRLs, a MRL proposal of 0.2 mg/kg (Rmax calculator) is derived. Considering the EU policy not to set import tolerances higher than in the country of origin, EFSA proposes to set the MRL for pulses, except peas, at the level of 0.2 mg/kg equal to the MRL established in CA/US. For peas, the lower MRL of 0.08 mg/kg is proposed.
A total of 15 residue trials conducted with three foliar applications at a higher rate (95–104 g/ha) were submitted. At PHI, residues were below the LOQ. No trials were available that reflect the combined soil application followed by three foliar treatments as described in the critical GAP. However, EFSA agrees with the conclusion of the EMS that they are not required as the prior application at planting is not expected to impact the no‐residue situation observed after the foliar applications.
Results from these overdosed trials with residues below the LOQ support a MRL of 0.01 mg/kg (LOQ) for peanuts, which corresponds to the tolerance established in the country of origin.
A total of 13 GAP‐compliant residue trials on oilseed rapes were submitted. Two pairs of trials were found to be not independent (same location, same application dates and rate). Thus, only one result (i.e. the highest value from the duplicate trials) was selected for calculation of the MRL proposal. According to the EU extrapolation rules (European Commission, 2015a), results from trials on oilseed rapes can be extrapolated to the whole group, hence also to the oilseeds for which an import tolerance was requested by the applicant.
The data support a MRL proposal of 0.15 mg/kg for rapeseeds, linseeds, poppy seeds, mustard seeds, gold of pleasure seeds, which corresponds to the value set in the countries of origin.
Based on 16 GAP‐compliant residue trials on cotton, a MRL of 0.15 mg/kg is derived, which corresponds to the tolerance set in the USA.
Based on 24 GAP‐compliant residue trials on maize, a MRL of 0.02 mg/kg is derived, which corresponds to the tolerance set in the country of origin.
A total of 36 GAP‐compliant residue trials in wheat were submitted. Samples of grain were collected at standard maturity or between 28 and 54 days after last application. Based on this data set, a MRL of 0.1 mg/kg is derived for wheat which can be extrapolated to rye (European Commission, 2015a) and corresponds to the value established in the countries of origin.
A total of 20 GAP‐compliant residue trials in barley were submitted. Samples of grain were collected at standard maturity or between 22 and 41 days after last application. Based on this data set, a MRL of 1.5 mg/kg is derived for barley, which can be extrapolated to oats (European Commission, 2015a) and corresponds to the value established in the countries of origin.
Six residue trials conducted with three applications of the WG formulation at the dose rate of 50 g/ha were submitted.15 All trials were designed as decline studies. The last treatment was applied to the crop at growth stages of mono‐ and dicotyledonous plants (BBCH) 78‐83 which slightly deviates from the authorised GAP (BBCH 7216). The trials are acceptable since the application rate is within the ± 25%; the deviation of the residue trials regarding the growth stage is expected to lead to slightly higher residues compared to the GAP. At PHI, 21 days after last application, residues were below the LOQ except in three trials where residues of 0.02 and 0.07 mg/kg were detected.
Two additional overdosed trials conducted with three applications at higher rates of 150 g/ha (residue < LOQ) were proposed to complete the data set.
Using the OECD calculator, EFSA derived a MRL proposal of 0.1 mg/kg which is in line with the MRL proposal derived by the EMS (France, 2015b) but is higher than the MRL set in the country of origin (0.03 mg/kg). The available data provide some evidence that the MRL set in the country of origin may not be sufficient for the notified GAP. Thus, further risk management considerations are required to decide on the most appropriate MRL proposal.
The results of the residue trials, the related risk assessment input values (highest residue, median residue) and the MRL proposals are summarised in Table 3.
Benzovindiflupyr was found to be stable for at least 24 months at −18°C in commodities with high water, high acid and high oil contents and in dry commodities. In addition, it showed to be stable for at least 4 months at −18°C in coffee beans (EFSA, 2015a).
As the trial samples were stored for less than 24 months (3 months for coffee beans) under conditions for which integrity of the samples was demonstrated, it is concluded that the residue data are valid with regard to storage stability.
According to the EMS, the analytical methods used to analyse the residue trial samples have been sufficiently validated and were proven to be fit for the purpose (France, 2015b).
EFSA concludes that the submitted residue trials are sufficient to derive MRL proposals for the imported crops under consideration, except for cucurbits with inedible peel. For coffee beans, the submitted data suggest that the MRL set in the country of origin is not sufficient. Therefore, a risk management decision needs to be taken whether the request to set a MRL for imported coffee beans has to be rejected (option 1: no proposal) because the MRL in the country of origin is considered not appropriate. If it is agreed to set an import tolerance, two options are given to risk managers: setting the MRL at the level equal of the MRL in the country of origin (option 2: 0.03 mg/kg) or setting the MRL at the level calculated with the OECD calculator (option 3: 0.1 mg/kg). The risk assessment presented in Section 4 is not affected by the choice of the MRL proposal presented in option 2 or 3.
Studies investigating the nature of benzovindiflupyr residues under standard hydrolysis conditions were assessed during the peer review and showed the active substance to be hydrolytically stable under conditions representative for pasteurisation, boiling/cooking and sterilisation. Therefore, for processed commodities the same residue definition as for raw agricultural commodities (RAC) is applicable (EFSA, 2015a).
Studies investigating the magnitude of benzovindiflupyr residues in processed cereal, soyabean and sugar cane products were evaluated in the framework of the peer review and several processing factors (PF) were derived (EFSA, 2015a).
Additional studies were submitted with this MRL application (France, 2015b). The processing studies were conducted on apples, grape vines, potatoes, tomatoes, cotton, rapeseeds, peanuts, maize and coffee. All samples were analysed for benzovindiflupyr and its metabolite SYN546039. Studies with residues in the RAC at or close to the LOQ were disregarded for the calculation of the PF.
Overall, washing, peeling and processing into juice, jam and canning resulted in a reduction of benzovindiflupyr residues. Residues showed to concentrate in dehydrated products, pomace and in oils.
The number of processing studies was sufficient to derive robust PFs for apple juice, sauce, wet and dry pomace and canned apple fruits, red and white grape must and wines, grape juice and raisins. Indicative PFs were derived for tomato products (juice, paste, puree and canned and sun‐dried tomatoes), cotton, peanut and rapeseed meal and oil, cotton hulls and peanut butter. The PF derived for coffee need to be confirmed by storage stability data covering the whole period of storage of the samples used in the processing studies prior to analysis (1.5 months). The PFs on potato and maize processing products since derived from a single trial (France, 2015b) are not sufficient for any firm recommendation.
The PFs derived from the processing studies submitted in this MRL application are presented in Table 4 and the PFs on cereals derived during the peer review in Table 5.
The residues of benzovindiflupyr in rotational crops are not of relevance for the assessment of import tolerances and they are not considered in this reasoned opinion.
As the imported crops and their by‐products may be fed to livestock, the nature and magnitude of benzovindiflupyr residues in livestock is assessed in the framework of this application (European Commission, 1996).
The median and maximum dietary burdens for livestock were calculated using the animal feedstuff Table reported in the OECD guidance No 64 – Series on Pesticides No 32 (OECD, 2009) and the animal Model calculator developed by EFSA. EFSA used the expected residue concentrations for feed items derived in the framework of the peer review (soyabeans, barley, oats, rye and wheat straw17 (France, 2014, EFSA, 2015a) and in the framework of this MRL application (see Table 3). For processed products where no PF were available (Tables 4 and 5), default PFs were used to estimate the residue levels in the feed items. The input values for the dietary burden calculation are summarised in Table 6.
The results of the dietary burden calculation are summarised in Table 7. Comparing the calculated maximum dietary burden (MDB) with the MDB calculated in the framework of the peer review (EFSA, 2015a),18 it becomes evident that the results for ruminants are in a similar range. However, with the new dietary burden calculation the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg dry matter (DM) is exceeded also for pigs and poultry. Residues in cereal straw resulting from the existing European use are the highest contributing feed item in ruminants while in poultry and pigs the residues in imported barley and potato by‐products are the major source of exposure.
The metabolism of benzovindiflupyr in livestock was evaluated in ruminant (goats) and poultry (hens) in the framework of the peer review. Based on these studies, EFSA concluded on a residue definition for monitoring as parent benzovindiflupyr and for risk assessment as the sum of benzovindiflupyr and monohydroxylated benzovindiflupyr, free and conjugated (SYN546039), expressed as benzovindiflupyr for all products of animal origin. A conversion factor (CF) for risk assessment of 2 was proposed (EFSA, 2015a).
A livestock feeding study in cattle was assessed in the framework of the peer review (EFSA, 2015a). The study was carried out at the dose levels of 0.08, 0.43 and 0.80 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day (equivalent to 3.46, 16.41 and 32.45 mg/kg DM). Results were provided according to the residue definition for enforcement and for risk assessment. Given the rather low molecular weight (MW) CF of 1.04,19 the levels of SYN546039 (free and conjugated) residues were directly summed up without recalculation to benzovindiflupyr.
At the lowest feeding level of 0.08 mg/kg bw, representing 1.5–14N dose levels when compared to the maximum estimated intakes for ruminants and pigs, respectively (Table 7), residues in all matrices were at or below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. Furthermore, residue concentrations were below or at the LOQ in the muscle and kidney at the second feeding level of 0.43 mg/kg bw.
The samples from the cattle feeding study were analysed for benzovindiflupyr and its metabolite (free and conjugated) included in the residue definition for risk assessment. CFs for risk assessment were calculated for animal matrices. The derived CFs are in line with the CF of 2 established during the peer review (EFSA, 2015a).
The MRLs, highest residue (HR) and supervised trials median residue (STMR) calculated for animal products taking into account the estimated dietary burden in accordance with the OECD and FAO recommendations (OECD, 2013; FAO, 2016) are reported in Table 8.
EFSA concludes that considering only the uses authorised in Europe and the contribution of the crops and their by‐products which may be used as feed items at importation, a modification of the existing MRLs set in the MRL Regulation for ruminants, equine and other farmed terrestrial animals (0.02 mg/kg for fat, 0.03 mg/kg for liver and LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for remaining products) and for pig tissues (at LOQ) is not required.
For poultry, the dietary burden calculation using the new livestock feedstuff tables (OECD, 2009) exceeded the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg DM (see Table 7). Poultry feeding studies are not available. The metabolism study assessed in the framework of the peer review (EFSA, 2015a) provides indications on the expected residue levels in poultry products. The study was conducted at a dose rate of about 18 mg/kg DM, corresponding to 1.5 mg/kg bw (25N for poultry layer and 121N for broilers) over 14 consecutive days. The highest total radioactive residue attributed to benzovindiflupyr was observed in egg yolk, skin and subcutaneous fat (about 0.02 mg eq/kg). Based on these data, EFSA concludes that there is no need to set MRLs for poultry products in the EU legislation.
The applicant and the EMS requested to raise the existing EU MRLs as summarised in Table 9. In this table, the existing US/Canadian tolerance/MRL for the corresponding products are also reported.
The import tolerances for food of animal origin should reflect the livestock exposure resulting from the dietary exposure in the countries of origin, i.e. US and Canada. EFSA is not in a position to verify the appropriateness of the tolerance/MRLs established in the countries of origin. Therefore, a risk management decision is required whether the existing MRLs set for liver of bovine, sheep, goats and equine should be raised to the level of 0.06 mg/kg established in the US (option 2) or whether the MRLs should be at a level reflecting the EU uses and the residues in imported feed products (option 1: no change required). The risk assessment presented in Section 4 will investigate both scenarios.
The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 2 of the EFSA PRIMo. This exposure assessment model contains the relevant European food consumption data for different subgroups of the EU population20 (EFSA, 2007).
To calculate the chronic exposure, EFSA calculated two scenarios.
In scenario 1, EFSA used the STMR derived for the imported crops under consideration (Table 3) and the STMR values for soyabeans and sugar canes reported in the EFSA conclusion (EFSA, 2015a). For the remaining commodities of plant origin, the existing MRLs set at the LOQ in the MRL Regulation were used as input values. For products of animal origin, EFSA used the STMR values for fat and liver of ruminants resulting from the uses authorised in Europe and the contribution of the imported crops and their by‐products reported in Table 8; for the other products, EFSA used the existing MRLs multiplied by the CFs for risk assessment.
For scenario 2, the input values for liver of ruminants and equine were replaced by the MRL set in US multiplied by the CF for risk assessment.
The acute exposure assessment was performed for both scenarios only with regard to the commodities under consideration assuming the consumption of a large portion of the food items as reported in the national food surveys and that these items contained residues at the HR or the STMR (scenario 1) or the MRLs set for liver of ruminants and equine in the US as corrected for risk assessment purpose (scenario 2). A variability factor accounting for the inhomogeneous distribution on the individual items consumed was included in the calculation, when required (EFSA, 2007).
The input values used for the dietary exposure calculation are summarised in Table 10.
The estimated exposure was then compared with the toxicological reference values derived for benzovindiflupyr (Table 1). The results of the intake calculation using the EFSA PRIMo is a key supporting document and is made publicly available as a background document to this reasoned opinion.
In the risk assessment performed in the framework of the peer review, a correction factor of 2 was used for adjustment the input values to take into account the possible preferential metabolism of benzovindiflupyr isomers in plants and animals. This factor should account for the lack of knowledge whether the toxicological relevant effects are resulting from one property (EFSA, 2015a).
It is noted by EFSA that the current risk assessment was performed disregarding the possible impact of the isomer ratios due to plant or livestock metabolism. Considering, however, that the isomer ratio of the active substance is specified as 1:1 and that toxicological studies have been carried out according to these specifications (EFSA, 2015a), a change in isomer ratios in the residue might, in the worst case situation, lead to a duplication of the toxicological burden of the residue. As long as the exposure calculations represent less than 50% of the ADI or ARfD, a potential change in isomer ratios in the final residue will not be of concern for the uses assessed in the framework of this review.
A long‐term consumer intake concern was not identified in both scenarios for any of the European diets incorporated in the EFSA PRIMo. The highest chronic intake was calculated to be 2.8% of the ADI (Dutch and German child diet). The contribution of residues in the products under consideration to the total consumer exposure as a percentage of the ADI accounted for a maximum of 1.6% for milk and milk products (French toddler diet), 1.2% for apples (German child diet), 0.5% for tomatoes and barley (WHO Cluster diet B and Irish adult diet, respectively) and less for the remaining products.
An acute consumer risk was not identified in both scenarios in relation to the MRL proposals for the products under consideration. The highest acute consumer exposure was calculated to be 39% of the ARfD for peppers (German toddler diet), 25% of the ARfD for tomatoes and so on.
The replacement with the MRL of 0.06 mg/kg set for liver of ruminants and horses in USA (multiplied by the CF of 3 for risk assessment) in the calculation is not having any significant impact in the exposure assessment. In the chronic risk assessment, consumption data are reported only for bovine liver, with highest intake increasing from less than 0.01% (scenario 1) to 0.05% (scenario 2) of the ADI (British adult) and for sheep liver, increasing from 0.01% (scenario 1) to 0.08% (scenario 2) of the ADI (Irish adult diet). In the acute risk assessment, consumption data are reported only for bovine liver, with highest intake increasing from 0.3% (scenario 1) to 1.5% (scenario 2) of the ARfD (British infant diet).
EFSA concludes that the uses of benzovindiflupyr notified for the products under consideration will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore are unlikely to pose a health risk to consumers.
